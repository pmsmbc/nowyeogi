Everything in this guide is within a twenty-minute walk of Myeongdong Station, and most of it is free. Prices and hours are as of September 2026; check the official pages before you go.

## N Seoul Tower and Namsan

![N Seoul Tower rising above the autumn trees of Namsan](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

The tower on the hill above Myeongdong is the city's landmark and its best viewpoint. The **observatory** costs **₩29,000** for adults and **₩23,000** for children and seniors when you buy at the counter, and opens **10:00–22:30 on weekdays, 10:00–23:00 at weekends**, year-round. Online travel sellers routinely list the same ticket well below the walk-up price, so book ahead.

You do not have to pay to enjoy Namsan. The **base of the tower** is free and has the terraces, the padlock fences and most of the view. Many visitors go up the hill, look, and skip the observatory entirely.

### Getting up the hill

![A Namsan cable car climbing the hillside](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

- **Cable car** — about **₩15,000** round trip, running **10:00–23:00**. The base station is a steep ten-minute climb from Myeongdong; take the **free outdoor lift** next to the Pacific Hotel to cut out most of the slope.
- **Namsan shuttle bus** — cheap, pays with a transit card, drops you at the top.
- **On foot** — 30–40 minutes up a well-made path from the Namsan Park entrance. Pleasant in autumn, punishing in August.

Go up before sunset and come down after dark if you want both views in one trip.

## Myeongdong Cathedral

![The brick facade of Myeongdong Cathedral](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/04.webp)

The mother church of Korean Catholicism, finished in 1898, and the only quiet place in the neighborhood. The Gothic brick building sits on a small rise five minutes east of the main shopping street.

**Sightseeing hours are Tuesday to Sunday, 09:30–18:00**, and it is closed to tourists on Mondays, though the church stays open for prayer from early morning until late. There is an underground gallery and shops beneath the plaza. Entry is free. If a Mass is running, stay at the back and keep quiet.

## Namdaemun Market

![A narrow alley of stalls in Namdaemun Market](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

Five minutes west on foot, Namdaemun is Korea's largest traditional market: around ten thousand shops selling clothes, kitchenware, glasses, camera gear, ginseng, dried seafood and hardware, with food alleys threaded between them.

Hours vary by shop, but most retail stalls run roughly **09:00–18:00** while the wholesale trade works overnight. Sunday is quietest and many wholesale sections close. The market's own food alleys — braised hairtail, hand-cut noodles, hotteok — are cheaper and better than the street carts in Myeongdong.

**Sungnyemun (Namdaemun Gate)**, National Treasure No. 1, stands at the market's western edge and is free to walk around.

## Cheonggyecheon

The restored stream runs east to west along the northern edge of Myeongdong, about eight minutes' walk from the main street. Steps take you down to water level, where the city noise drops away and you can walk for kilometres past fountains, bridges and, in winter, a light festival. It is free, open all hours, and one of the better things to do on a hot evening.

## Shopping

### Olive Young Myeongdong Town

The flagship of Korea's cosmetics chain, and the single most efficient place to buy K-beauty: two floors, multilingual staff and signage, an in-store tax refund counter and luggage storage so you can shop without your bags.

**Spend ₩15,000 or more and show a physical passport, and the tax comes off instantly at the till.** Bring the passport itself; a photo does not count.

### Department stores and duty free

- **Lotte Department Store main branch** and **Shinsegae main branch** both sit on the western edge, each with duty-free floors and a tax refund desk.
- The **food halls in the basements** are excellent and a good rainy-day option.

### The street

Myeongdong-gil is cosmetics flagships, fast fashion and character-goods shops. Prices are fixed in the chains and negotiable almost nowhere, so shop on convenience rather than hunting bargains.

## How the tax refund works

Korea refunds VAT (about 10%) to foreign visitors on purchases over ₩15,000 per shop, per day.

1. **Instant refund at the till** — available at Olive Young flagships, department stores and many chains. Show your passport when you pay; the tax is deducted immediately. There is a cap on total instant refunds per trip.
2. **Refund at the airport** — get a tax-free form with your receipt, then use the self-service kiosks or the refund desks at Incheon before you check in. Keep the goods accessible in case of inspection.

Either way: carry your passport while shopping and keep the receipts.

## A half-day route

Cathedral (30 minutes) → Myeongdong-gil shopping and lunch (90 minutes) → Namdaemun Market (60 minutes) → free lift and cable car up Namsan for sunset (2 hours) → back down for street food.

## Practical notes

- **Street carts appear from about 16:00.** Mornings are for shopping, evenings for eating.
- **Sunday morning is the calmest time** in the whole neighborhood.
- **The Namsan lift and the base of the tower are free**; only the observatory and the cable car cost money.
- **Coin lockers** are in both Myeongdong and Euljiro 1-ga stations if you are between hotels.

## Wrapping up

Two hours gets you the cathedral, the market and the stream. Half a day adds Namsan and the tower. Everything else here is shopping, which expands to fill whatever time you give it.
