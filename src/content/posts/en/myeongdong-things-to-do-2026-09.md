Most first-time visitors see Myeongdong as a wall of cosmetics shop signs. Spread out a map, though, and within walking distance you will find **a Gothic cathedral consecrated in 1898, Seoul's original south gate, a market that traces itself back to 1414, a bank building finished in 1912 and a cable car that has been running since 1962**. The short version: almost everything here is free, and if you remember that **museums and the gate close on Mondays while Namdaemun Market closes on Sundays**, a full day plans itself.

Our [Myeongdong overview](/en/posts/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/) covers the neighbourhood and getting around; this post sticks to **what to see and buy**. All hours and prices are **as of 1 October 2026**. Prices are in won; ₩1,000 is roughly $0.75.

The administrative district of Myeongdong in Jung-gu takes in Myeongdong 1-ga and 2-ga, Chungmuro 1-ga and 2-ga and Namsan-dong. Visitors use the name more loosely, so this post covers:

| Hoehyeon-dong | Namdaemun Market, Sungnyemun Gate | Hoehyeon ([[line:4]]), Seoul Station ([[line:1]] [[line:4]]) |

| Pil-dong | Namsangol Hanok Village | Chungmuro ([[line:3]] [[line:4]]) |

N Seoul Tower technically sits in Yongsan-gu, but most people climb to it from the Myeongdong side, so it is included. Deoksugung Palace, Seoul Plaza and Cheonggye Plaza are in [things to do around Gwanghwamun and City Hall](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-things-to-do-2026-09/), and Sewoon Plaza and the Euljiro alleys in [things to do in Euljiro](/en/posts/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/), so they are not repeated here.

## Myeongdong Cathedral: Gothic, 1898

![The approach to Myeongdong Cathedral on a clear winter day: the Catholic Center building on the left, the red-brick Gothic church with its clock tower and spire on the rise to the right, people on the steps and plaza](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/04.webp)

The red-brick church on a small rise is **Myeongdong Cathedral**, seat of the Catholic Archdiocese of Seoul. According to the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), it was **the first large Gothic church built on the Korean peninsula and Korea's first parish** with a resident priest, and it was designated a **national historic site on 22 November 1977**. Construction began in August 1892 and the church was **consecrated on 29 May 1898**, designed and supervised by the French missionary priest Eugène Coste.

- The **crypt** holds relics of Catholic martyrs from the 19th-century persecutions, according to KTO. In 1991 the relics were re-housed and the entrance fitted with glass doors so pilgrims can pray there.
- Under the military governments of the 1970s and 1980s the cathedral was seen as **a centre of the democracy movement**.
- KTO notes that non-Catholics may attend Mass.

### Mass times and etiquette

The parish listing on Good News, the Korean Catholic Church's directory, gives these times:

| Day | Mass |
| --- | --- |
| Mon–Sat | 07:00, 10:00, 18:00 (plus 19:00 Wed–Fri) |
| Saturday evening (counts for Sunday) | 18:00, 19:00 |
| Sunday | 07:00, 09:00, 10:00, 11:00, 12:00, 16:00, 17:00, 18:00, 19:00, 21:00 |

The cathedral's own website returned errors while this was written, so we could not confirm these against an official notice. Check again before you go.

- KTO lists the **parish office as Tue–Sat 09:00–19:00, Sun 07:00–21:00, closed Monday**. Confession is heard Mon 13:00–17:00, Tue–Fri 11:00–20:00 and Sat–Sun 10:00–20:00.
- If Mass is under way, don't take photos or walk the aisles. Stand quietly at the back, take off your hat and silence your phone.
- Phone +82-2-774-1784. Parking is available, according to KTO.

The address is 74 Myeongdong-gil, within walking distance of both Myeongdong ([[line:4]]) and Euljiro 1-ga ([[line:2]]) stations.

## Myeongdong Theater: a national stage in the shopping street

At 35 Myeongdong-gil, right among the shops, stands the **Myeongdong Theater** of the National Theater Company of Korea. KTO says the building served as a cinema, concert hall and arts theatre from 1934 to 1973, closed in 1975, and reopened in **2009 as a 588-seat drama theatre** after three years of restoration. The facade was kept; the interior was rebuilt.

### October–November programme

From the National Theater Company's official site:

| Show | Dates | Times | Tickets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Oedipus* (ANTHROPOLIS III) | until 18 Oct | Weekdays 19:30, weekends and holidays 15:00, **no shows on Tuesday** | R ₩60,000, S ₩45,000, A ₩30,000 |
| *Iokaste* (ANTHROPOLIS IV) | 28 Oct–21 Nov | Weekdays 19:30, weekends 15:00, **no shows on Tuesday** | R ₩60,000, S ₩45,000, A ₩30,000 |
| Myeongdong Humanities (lectures, Korean) | Tue 6 and 13 Oct, 3, 10 and 17 Nov, 8, 15 and 22 Dec | 19:30 | ₩10,000 |

- Both plays are for **ages 16 and over**, and you need ID showing your date of birth to collect tickets. Bring your passport.
- **English subtitles run every Thursday and Sunday**, except Sunday 4 October for *Oedipus* and Sunday 8 November for *Iokaste*.
- *Oedipus* runs 120 minutes and *Iokaste* 130 minutes, both without an interval.

## N Seoul Tower and Namsan

![A silver cable car climbing over autumn-tinted Namsan forest, with N Seoul Tower on the peak behind](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/08.webp)

### Observatory prices and hours

From N Seoul Tower's official site:

| Ticket | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Adult (13+) | ₩29,000 (about $22) |
| Child (3–12) | ₩23,000 |
| Senior (65+, with ID) | ₩23,000 |

- Open **weekdays 10:00–22:30, weekends and public holidays 10:00–23:00**, every day of the year.
- **Last entry is 30 minutes before closing.**
- Enquiries +82-2-3455-9277.

### What you can see for free

![The viewing deck below N Seoul Tower, railings packed with love locks and a heart sculpture, with Seoul's skyline and distant mountains beyond](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/07.webp)

You do not need an observatory ticket to reach **the plaza at the tower's base and the love-lock viewing deck**. As the photo shows, the city spreads out below the railings. Decide on the observatory once you are up there.

Namsan itself is the other attraction. According to Seoul's parks office, Namsan Park **opened on 10 September 1968**, covers about 2.9 km², and is 84.7% forest. **Private cars and taxis have been banned since 2005**, and tour buses since August 2021, so the summit is reached by cable car, bus or on foot.

### Three ways up

**1. Namsan Cable Car**

| Ticket | Individual | Group (30+) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adult return | ₩15,000 | ₩13,500 |
| Adult one-way | ₩12,000 | ₩11,000 |
| Child return | ₩11,500 | ₩10,500 |
| Child one-way | ₩9,000 | ₩8,500 |

- **10:00–23:00, every day**; last ticket 30 minutes before closing. Bad weather can stop it or close it early.
- Child fares cover **36 months to 12 years**; seniors and national merit holders pay the child fare with proof.
- It opened on 12 May 1962 and covers **about 600 m** from the Hoehyeon-dong station to the summit station, carrying up to 48 people per car.
- To reach it, leave Myeongdong Station ([[line:4]]) by **Exit 3** and follow **the road to the right of the Pacific Hotel**, about 10 minutes, mostly uphill.
- The Hankook Ilbo (8 January 2026) reported **1.74 million riders in 2024**, nearly triple the 630,000 of 2021, which is why weekend evening queues get long.

**The free inclined lift: Namsan Oreumi**

The **Namsan Oreumi** (남산오르미) cuts out most of that climb. According to N Seoul Tower's transport page, you board it **in front of the monument at the entrance to Namsan Tunnel 3**, and it reaches the cable car station in two minutes.

- **09:00–23:00, free**
- **Every Monday it stops from 09:00 to 14:00 for inspection**, so on Monday mornings you walk.
- It also stops in strong wind or heavy rain.
- Seoul's parks office points out that the Oreumi **does not go to the summit**. From the top you still need the cable car, a 30-minute walk or the bus.

**2. Namsan circular buses 01A and 01B**

Two bus routes run to just below the tower. Both start at the **Namsan Yejang Bus Transfer Parking Lot**, close to Myeongdong Station Exit 3.

| Route | Main stops | Frequency | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [[bus:yellow:01A]] | Chungmuro Stn & Hanok Village → Dongguk Univ. Stn → National Theater → **N Seoul Tower** → Namsan Library → Baekbeom Square → **Sungnyemun & Namdaemun Market** → Gwanghwamun Stn → Gyeongbokgung → Anguk Stn → **Myeongdong Cathedral** → back to start | every 6–9 min | 06:30–23:00 |
| [[bus:yellow:01B]] | Chungmuro Stn → Dongguk Univ. Stn → National Theater → **N Seoul Tower** → Namsan Library → **Namsan Cable Car** → back to start | every 15–18 min | 06:30–23:00 |

- Routes are from N Seoul Tower's transport page; frequency and hours from Seoul's news site (April 2024). Both now run as "Haechi buses" painted with Seoul's mascot, mint for 01A and pink for 01B.
- Pay with a transport card. Seoul says the Climate Card (기후동행카드) unlimited pass is accepted.
- [[bus:yellow:01A]] loops through Namsan, Namdaemun, Gyeongbokgung and Myeongdong, linking most of this post.

**3. On foot**

Several paths climb Namsan's north slope from Myeongdong. Going up from the Hoehyeon side you pass **Baekbeom Square** and the **Ahn Jung-geun Memorial Hall**.

- **Baekbeom Square**: KTO says it stands on the site of a Japanese colonial-era Shinto shrine; a 10 m statue of independence leader Kim Gu was erected in August 1969. Always open.
- **Ahn Jung-geun Memorial Hall**: opened on the same shrine site in 1970 and rebuilt in 2010, honouring the independence activist who assassinated Itō Hirobumi. The building is a bundle of 12 glass columns symbolising his finger-cutting pledge, and it holds his calligraphy, designated a Treasure. **Free; closed Mondays** (the following day if Monday is a holiday), 1 January, Lunar New Year and Chuseok days, and Labour Day.

On Namsan's west side, off Sowol-ro, the **Namsan Sky Forest Path** opened in October 2025. Seoul's news site describes it as a **1.45 km barrier-free boardwalk** from Sowol Garden near Namsan Library to the Namsan fitness area, open free all year and usable by wheelchairs and strollers. It won Seoul's 2026 landscape grand prize.

### Autumn colour from late October

The Seoul Tourism Organization says **Namsan is at its reddest from late October to early November**, which is when the tower is framed by leaves as in the first photo. More spots are in our [Seoul autumn foliage guide](/en/posts/seoul-autumn-foliage-2026-09/).

### The Namsan gondola is not running

Some travel sites still say a gondola will run from Myeongdong Station to the summit "from spring 2026". **As of October 2026 there is no gondola and no opening date.**

- The plan was an **832 m line from Yejang Park, near Myeongdong Station, to the summit**, with 25 ten-person cabins carrying over 2,000 people an hour (Bizhankook, Supple).
- The cable car's operator, Hankook Ropeway, sued over Seoul's planning decision, and after a court suspended it **construction stopped in October 2024**.
- **On 17 September 2026 the Seoul High Court also ruled** that the city's rezoning was unlawful (Newspim). Seoul says it is considering an appeal to the Supreme Court and a change to the parks law.

So this autumn, the three options above are the only ways up.

## Sungnyemun Gate and the guard ceremony

![Sungnyemun gate on a sunny day: a two-storey wooden pavilion on a stone base pierced by an arched gateway, restored wall sections and lawn on either side, office towers behind](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/19.webp)

At the west end of Namdaemun Market stands **Sungnyemun**, the main south gate of Joseon-era Seoul, better known as Namdaemun ("Great South Gate"). KTO records that it was first built in 1396, rebuilt in February 1398, and made a **National Treasure on 20 December 1962**. It is the largest of Seoul's four great gates, and its name board is written **vertically**, which is unusual.

- A **fire on 10 February 2008** burned part of the lower pavilion and destroyed the upper one. It reopened after a **restoration ceremony on 4 May 2013**.
- The connecting city walls were torn down in 1907 for a visit by the Japanese crown prince; sections on both sides were rebuilt in 2006.
- Open **09:00–18:00** (last entry 17:50), **closed Monday**, no parking.

The **Sungnyemun gate guard ceremony** re-enacts the Joseon military ritual of opening, guarding and closing the city gates. KTO's 2026 listing says it runs **daily except Monday, 10:00–15:40**: the guards line up, the gate is ceremonially opened, the watch changes several times, and the day ends with the closing ritual. Enquiries +82-2-3789-7402.

The Deoksugung and Gwanghwamun guard changes are covered in [things to do around Gwanghwamun](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Namdaemun Market: 612 years of trading

![An alley in Namdaemun Market strung with flags of many countries, shops with striped awnings selling clothes and goods on both sides, shoppers walking](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

According to the National Archives of Korea, the market began in **1414**, when the state leased shop space to merchants along the road near the south gate. A tax office built in 1608 drew more traders, and in 1897 nearby markets were consolidated during road works. Major fires in 1954 and 1968 led to modern market buildings. The Nongmin Shinmun counted 2026 as the market's **612th year**.

KTO puts its scale at:

- **close to 10,000 shops** fanning out from Sungnyemun
- **about 1,700 kinds of goods**: clothing, accessories, kitchenware, folk crafts, food, farm and fishery produce
- **300,000 visitors a day** on average
- both wholesale and retail, drawing shopkeepers from the provinces and abroad

### When to go

- **The market is largely closed on Sundays.** Seoul's city media list Sunday as the market's day off, with weekday hours "varying by shop".
- Hours differ by building; the market's own website lists them building by building. Weekday daytime or Saturday is the safe choice.
- Buildings post separate closures for national holidays; this year's Chuseok notice went up on 18 September.
- From March to June 2026 the city repaved 150 m of the north side and 200 m of the main alley in stone and added **signs to nine specialist alleys and a common design to seven entrance gates**, the Nongmin Shinmun reported, so finding your way should be easier than before.

### What to buy

The market's website sorts goods into clothing and fashion accessories, accessories and sundries, children's clothing, kitchenware and tableware, imported goods, food and stationery, farm and fishery produce, flowers and crafts, and jewellery, watches and cameras. Shops selling the same thing cluster in the same building, so decide what you want and head straight there.

For food, KTO mentions the hairtail stew alley, the knife-noodle alley and vegetable hotteok. See [where to eat in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-food-2026-09/) for more.

Hoehyeon Station ([[line:4]]) is right next to the market, and it is an easy walk west from Myeongdong.

## Bank of Korea Money Museum: a 1912 bank hall

![The central hall of the Bank of Korea Money Museum, chandeliers hanging below a domed ceiling and arched windows, display cases on the ground floor](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/14.webp)

The stone building at 39 Namdaemun-ro is the **Bank of Korea Money Museum**. KTO says construction began in 1907 and **finished in 1912**; under Japanese rule it was the head office of the Bank of Chosen, and from 12 June 1950 the head office of the Bank of Korea. Gutted during the Korean War, it was restored in 1958 and designated a national heritage building in 1981. It opened as a museum on **12 June 2001**, the bank's 50th anniversary.

- **Tue–Sun 10:00–17:00**, last entry 16:40, **free**
- **Closed** Mondays, Lunar New Year and Chuseok holidays, Labour Day, election days, and 29 December–2 January
- **Guided tours** daily at 11:00, 14:00 and 15:00 (Korean, about 30 minutes)
- Groups of 10 or more book a day ahead. Hourly entry is capped at 100 on weekdays and 50 at weekends.
- No parking
- Maps and guides in English, Chinese and Japanese can be downloaded from the official site.

The permanent galleries fill 13 rooms on two floors, covering how money is made and circulated and how to spot counterfeits. As the photo shows, the high-ceilinged banking hall survives.

## Stamp Museum: two floors under the Central Post Office

![A glass wall case at the Stamp Museum lined with dozens of miniature red, yellow and blue postboxes](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/17.webp)

On **basement level 2** of the Seoul Central Post Office (Post Tower) on Sogong-ro is the **Stamp Museum**, run by Korea Post. KTO says it opened as "Stamp Culture Nuri" and took its current name in 2013.

- **09:00–16:50, free**
- **Closed Mondays and public holidays.** In October that means the 3rd (National Foundation Day), the 5th (substitute holiday) and the 9th (Hangeul Day).
- The official site says visits run through the Naver reservation system, so check the booking page first.
- 5 minutes' walk from Myeongdong Station ([[line:4]]) **Exit 5**, or 10 minutes from Euljiro 1-ga ([[line:2]]) Exit 6
- +82-2-6450-5600

The museum's homepage currently features *Dancheong of Korea*, a stamp issue dated 1 October 2026.

## Namsangol Hanok Village: free, with tours in four languages

![Namsangol Hanok Village: tiled hanok roofs layered behind a stone-and-earth wall, with Namsan's forest rising behind](/images/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/11.webp)

### Tax refunds, straight from the regulation

Foreign visitors can reclaim VAT at shops displaying a "Tax Free" sign. The rules are set by the Special Regulation on VAT and Individual Consumption Tax for Foreign Tourists; the current version on the National Law Information Center took effect on **2 January 2026**.

**Instant refund** (you pay the tax-free price at the till)

| Condition | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Per transaction | **under ₩1,000,000** including tax |
| Running total during your stay | instant-refund purchases **up to ₩5,000,000** |
| Minimum spend | **₩15,000** per transaction |
| Export | goods must leave Korea **within 3 months** of purchase |

- The instant-refund limits doubled on **1 January 2024** from ₩500,000 per purchase and ₩2.5 million in total, and the minimum fell from ₩30,000 to ₩15,000 (Korea.net).
- The regulation lets shops deduct the refund operator's fee, so you get back less than the full 10%.
- If you hold goods you failed to take out of Korea within three months, later purchases no longer qualify for instant refunds.
- You need your **physical passport** at the till.

**Refund on departure**

If you are over the instant-refund limit or the shop does not offer it, keep the sales confirmation (tax-free receipt) and claim at the airport when you leave. Article 9 requires you to show customs the receipt **together with the goods**, so pack them where you can reach them.
