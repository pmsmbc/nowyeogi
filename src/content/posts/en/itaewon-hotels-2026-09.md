Itaewon has fewer hotel rooms than any other area in this series, and they sit at opposite ends of the price range. What it offers instead is the easiest English-speaking base in Korea and the best food variety in the city.

Prices are per-night bands for a double room in low-to-mid season, from booking-site averages as of September 2026. Add 20–40% in April–May and September–October.

## Pick your slope

![Hannam-dong and N Seoul Tower seen from across the Han River](/images/itaewon-hotels-2026-09/01.webp)

Itaewon is built on the side of a mountain. Where you book decides how much climbing you do.

- **Itaewon-ro (the main road)** — flat, central, loudest. Everything within five minutes, including the noise until 02:00 at weekends.
- **Hannam-dong (east, Hangangjin Station)** — quieter, more expensive, the Leeum museum and good cafés. Gently sloped.
- **Gyeongnidan-gil and Haebangchon (west, uphill)** — guesthouses and small hotels, cheaper, and genuinely steep. Ten to fifteen minutes uphill from the station.
- **Namsan slope (north)** — the Grand Hyatt, and effectively taxi-only.

If you have a suitcase, book on or near Itaewon-ro or in Hannam. The hill neighborhoods are lovely and hard work with luggage.

## Luxury: ₩350,000 and up

- **Grand Hyatt Seoul** — on the Namsan slope above Itaewon, with the best view of any hotel in the city, a large outdoor pool in summer and an ice rink in winter. It is isolated: you will take taxis to and from, which is fine because they are cheap.
- **Mondrian Seoul Itaewon** — in Hannam, design-led, with a rooftop bar and a strong food and drink programme. The most fashionable hotel in the neighborhood.

Both are destinations in themselves rather than places to sleep between sightseeing.

## Mid-range: ₩130,000–250,000

- **Boutique hotels along Itaewon-ro** — a handful of small properties, modern, 18–25㎡ rooms, well located. Quality and soundproofing vary, so read recent reviews.
- **Serviced residences in Hannam and Yongsan** — better value for longer stays, with kitchenettes.
- **Business hotels near Samgakji and Noksapyeong** — plain but convenient, and one stop from the main strip.

This band is thin in Itaewon compared with Myeongdong or Gangnam. If nothing appeals, staying in [Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-hotels-2026-09/) and visiting Itaewon for dinner is a reasonable plan.

## Budget: ₩50,000–110,000

- **Guesthouses in Haebangchon** — the long-running expat neighborhood has a cluster of small guesthouses, often in converted houses with rooftop terraces and good views. Cheap, friendly, and uphill.
- **Small hotels off Itaewon-ro** — basic rooms above shops and restaurants. Central and noisy.
- **Hostels** — a few, mostly aimed at long-stay visitors rather than tourists.

Hongdae still has more and better budget beds — see our [Hongdae hotel guide](/en/posts/hongdae-hotels-2026-09/).

## Getting there from the airport

- **AREX to Seoul Station**, then [[line:4]] one stop to Samgakji, then [[line:6]] two stops to Itaewon. About 90 minutes.
- **Airport bus 6010** serves Hannam, useful if you are staying on that side with luggage.
- **Taxi** — ₩65,000–85,000, 60–75 minutes.

Full fares in our [Incheon Airport to Seoul guide](/en/posts/incheon-airport-to-seoul-2026-09/).

## The transport trade-off, honestly

[[line:6]] is the weakest of Seoul's main lines. It does not reach Myeongdong, Jongno or Gangnam directly, so most journeys involve a transfer and 30–40 minutes.

That matters if your itinerary is palaces and shopping. It matters much less if you are here to eat, drink and visit the museums, all of which are local.

## What to check before you book

- **The gradient**, not just the distance. Look at the map in satellite view; contour lines matter here.
- **Which street the room faces.** The main strip is loud until 02:00 on Friday and Saturday.
- **Whether the building has a lift.** Many small hotels and guesthouses on the slopes do not.
- **Distance to Itaewon, Noksapyeong or Hangangjin station** specifically, since "Itaewon" is used loosely by listings.
- **Breakfast** is rarely worth paying for; the neighborhood is full of better options.

## Who should stay here

**Yes**: repeat visitors; anyone anxious about the language barrier; travellers who want halal or vegetarian food easily; people who plan to spend evenings out; museum-focused trips.

**No**: first-time visitors doing the palace-and-shopping circuit; anyone with mobility limits, because of the hills; travellers on a tight budget, who will do better in Hongdae.

## Wrapping up

Book Hannam if you want quiet and design, the main road if you want convenience, and Haebangchon if you want cheap and do not mind the climb. Then treat the neighborhood as an eating and drinking base rather than a sightseeing one.
