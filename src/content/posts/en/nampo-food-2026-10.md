Eating in Nampo-dong (남포동) comes down to how you work three markets. **Jagalchi is where you buy downstairs and eat upstairs**, **Gukje Market's food alley is where you sit on a stool for chungmu gimbap and bibim dangmyeon**, and **Bupyeong Kkangtong Market is the one that turns into a night market at 19:30**. In between are a dumpling-soup shop that opened in 1948, a pork-soup house in its third generation since 1954, and a street of jokbal restaurants that formed in the 1980s.

The short version: **lunch at Jagalchi's second floor or a milmyeon shop, a snack at BIFF Square, dinner at the night market or the jokbal alley.** Cross one bridge and you are among the warehouse cafes of Yeongdo. Every shop here has a name and address confirmed by Visit Busan, the Korea Tourism Organization, the MICHELIN Guide or the Jung-gu district office, and prices and hours are **as of October 2026**. For the city-wide picture, see [What to Eat in Busan](/en/posts/busan-food-2026-09/); this guide goes shop by shop.

## Jagalchi: buy on the ground floor, eat on the second

![Rows of mackerel laid out on green trays at an outdoor stall at Jagalchi Market, with a vendor in a pink apron standing behind a 'Korean mackerel' sign](/images/nampo-food-2026-10/04.webp)

**52 Jagalchihaean-ro.** Visit Busan lists it as open **05:00 to 22:00** and closed on the **first, third and fifth Tuesday** of each month. It is a six-minute walk from Nampo Station exit 2 or Jagalchi Station exit 10 on [[line:busan1]]. There are the open-air stalls along the waterfront and the indoor building with the gull-wing roof; the building is the easier place to start.

The sequence works like this.

1. Pick your fish or shellfish from a **ground-floor stall**. The stalls are numbered and there is an electronic seafood price board above the aisle.
2. Carry it up to a **second-floor restaurant**, known as a *chojangjip*. The overhead sign reads "2F Raw Fish Restaurant" in English, Chinese and Japanese.
3. The restaurant slices it and lays out lettuce wraps, dipping sauces and side dishes. **You pay the stall for the fish and the restaurant for the table.**

![The ground-floor live fish hall inside the Jagalchi Market building: numbered stalls with tanks and fish, an overhead sign reading '2F Raw Fish Restaurant' in English, Chinese and Japanese, and an electronic seafood price board](/images/nampo-food-2026-10/05.webp)

**Ask the table charge before you sit.** It is not in any official listing and varies by restaurant. A November 2025 round-up on the review site Siksin put it at about **₩5,000 (about $3.75) per person**, with wraps and sauces refillable. A December 2025 visitor reported being charged **separately for steaming crab and clams** on top of the table charge, and turning the bones into *maeuntang*, a spicy stew, also costs extra. Prices per kilogram follow the day's market, so the board and the stall signs are the only guide.

If haggling sounds tiring, there are **fixed-price restaurants**. One of them, Sujeong Hoetjip, published its menu in April 2026: a **₩20,000 sashimi set** per person including sashimi, spicy stew, rice and side dishes, and **assorted sashimi platters at ₩60,000 (two people), ₩80,000 (three) and ₩100,000 (four)**, with stew and rice usually ordered separately for platters. That is one restaurant's listing; check the menu wherever you end up.

### Sindonga Market: dried seafood and grilled hagfish

Right next door at **42 Jagalchi-ro**, Sindonga Seafood Market is organised floor by floor. The official site lists **B1 as beef-intestine and grilled hagfish (kkomjangeo) restaurants, 1F live fish, 2F dried seafood shops and 3F a large sashimi hall**. The Korea Tourism Organization dates the building to 1980. Anchovies, kelp and seaweed make the easiest **edible souvenirs** on the second floor, and the basement is where to go for hagfish in the evening.

## Gukje Market food alley: chungmu gimbap and bibim dangmyeon

![The 'GUKJEMARKET Changseon Market' entrance arcade of Gukje Market, lined with tool and lighting shops, with the covered market lane continuing inside](/images/nampo-food-2026-10/12.webp)

Gukje Market grew out of the trade in American military surplus after the Korean War. Visit Busan lists it as **09:00 to 20:00, closed Sundays** (individual shops vary), seven minutes on foot from Jagalchi Station exit 7 on [[line:busan1]]. The food alley sits on the Changseon-dong side at **36 Junggu-ro**, and the Korea Tourism Organization describes it as an **open-air food lane with more than 60 years of history**.

- **Chungmu gimbap** — small rice rolls with no filling, eaten with tangy squid salad and radish kimchi.
- **Bibim dangmyeon** — boiled sweet-potato glass noodles tossed in sauce, a Busan speciality. Visit Busan also lists it among the things to try near BIFF Square.
- Also on the stalls: **tteokbokki, cold noodles, sundae (blood sausage) and patbingsu (shaved ice with red beans)**.

You sit on low stools around the stall, so do not expect table service. Each stall keeps its own hours.

## BIFF Square: seed hotteok and 18beon Wandangjip

**BIFF Square (4 Nampo-gil)** is the street where the Busan International Film Festival began. Visit Busan's Nampo-dong guide names **seed hotteok, tteokbokki with dumplings, bibim dangmyeon and skewers** as the things to eat here, and notes the popular stalls already have queues. *Ssiat hotteok*, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, is a griddled sugar-filled pancake split open and stuffed with **peanuts, pumpkin seeds, almonds and walnuts**; it became famous nationwide after appearing on the variety show *2 Days 1 Night*. There is nowhere to sit, so you eat standing. A January 2026 guide to the Nampo-dong stalls puts hotteok at **₩2,000 to ₩3,000**.

Beside the square, **18beon Wandangjip (31 BIFF Gwangjang-ro)** has, per the Korea Tourism Organization, been making *wandang* **since 1948**. Wandang are thin-skinned dumplings simmered in a clear broth with bean sprouts. The dining room is a large basement, and you can watch staff folding the dumplings in the open kitchen. The menu is wandang, buckwheat noodles and shrimp wandang noodles, with sets adding gimbap or inari sushi. **10:30 to 20:00 (last order 19:30), open daily**, no parking.

## Bupyeong Kkangtong Market: the one that changes at 19:30

![A covered lane in Bupyeong Kkangtong Market with orange plastic stools outside a snack shop on the left and red stools and a cooking counter under a 'dangmyeon' sign on the right](/images/nampo-food-2026-10/01.webp)

**48 Bupyeong 1-gil.** It adjoins Gukje Market and you cross from one to the other without noticing, but in Visit Busan's phrasing **Gukje is the everything market and Kkangtong is the food market**. The name, "tin-can market", comes from the American canned goods sold here after the war. It is a 9–10 minute walk from Jagalchi Station exit 3 on [[line:busan1]].

By day, the **fish cake lane** is lined with *eomuk* in every shape, and the tteokbokki shops sell **muldeok** (rice cake in broth) and **yubu jeongol**, a fried-tofu pouch filled with glass noodles and vegetables served in hot broth; Visit Busan calls these two the market's long-standing specialities. **Old-style whole fried chicken** is the thing people carry home.

![Shoppers walking a lane of Bupyeong Kkangtong Market under a colourful arched roof, blue shop signs hanging in a row, and imported snacks and liquor stacked outside a shop on the left](/images/nampo-food-2026-10/02.webp)

**Bupyeong Kkangtong Night Market opened in 2013 as Korea's first permanent night market.** Visit Busan lists it as **daily 19:30 to 24:00**, closing only on national holidays. At the appointed time, wheeled carts roll in and take the middle of the lane. The food mixes countries: grilled whole pork belly, okonomiyaki, kebabs, banh xeo. The lane is narrow and crowded, so **keeping to the right** is the house rule. Prices vary by cart and parking is in nearby paid lots.

Inside the market, **Yangsanjip (30 Junggu-ro 47beon-gil)** is a pork-soup house the Korea Tourism Organization describes as **run by the same family since 1954, now in its third generation**. The *suyuk baekban*, a boiled-pork set, comes with a small brazier so it stays warm, with chive salad and kimchi alongside. **Weekdays 10:00 to 20:00, weekends 09:00 to 20:00 (last order 19:30), closed Tuesdays**, no parking.

## Bupyeong-dong jokbal alley: chilled trotters with jellyfish

According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the jokbal alley runs **from the Gukje Market crossroads to the Bupyeong intersection** and grew from a single small shop in the 1980s into a cluster. Its signature dish is **naengchae jokbal**: reviews describe chilled sliced pork trotters topped with jellyfish salad and vegetables, dressed in a mustard sauce.

- **Busan Jokbal (13-1 Gwangbok-ro)** — jokbal, naengchae jokbal and *jangyuk* (soy-braised pork), in **portions for one to four people**. The Korea Tourism Organization calls the naengchae jokbal the popular order and describes it as tangy and clean-tasting. **10:00 to 24:00, open daily**, takeaway available, no parking.
- **Bupyeong Jokbal (19-2 Gwangbok-ro)** — jokbal, naengchae jokbal and a spicy griddled version. **11:00 to 24:00, open daily**, everything available to take away.

Neither restaurant's prices appear in official listings, so read the menu board.

## Milmyeon, naengmyeon and kalguksu: old shops off Gwangbok-ro

**Halmae Gaya Milmyeon (56-14 Gwangbok-ro)** is a milmyeon shop under five minutes' walk from Nampo Station. Milmyeon is Busan's wheat-flour take on cold noodles, invented by wartime refugees who could not get buckwheat. Reviews collected on Trip.com and refreshed in October 2026 give **10:30 to 21:30**, irregular days off, **₩8,000 for mul (broth) or bibim (sauced) milmyeon and ₩5,000 for four mandu**. A cup of warm broth arrives when you sit down, and several reviews mention counter seats for solo diners.

On the same stretch, **Wonsan Myeonok (56-8 Gwangbok-ro)** is, per the Korea Tourism Organization, a **naengmyeon house with 70 years of history in its third generation**, making its broth from Korean beef, pork and chicken and also serving large mandu, skate sashimi, boiled pork and hotpot. **11:00 to 21:30, closed only on the day of Seollal and Chuseok.** It is on the Jung-gu office's list of exemplary restaurants, updated in October 2025; the same list includes **Jungang Momil Udong (9-1 Jungang-daero 49beon-gil)** in Jungang-dong and **Nampo Samgyetang (16 Nampo-gil)**.

West of Kkangtong Market, **1969 Buwondong Kalguksu (3 Gudeok-ro 22beon-gil)** is listed in the **MICHELIN Guide Busan**. The guide says the shop has moved several times while keeping its traditional flavour, and that the menu is **kalguksu and mandu only**: house-made noodles with a firm chew in a clear, savoury broth, plus a spicy, tangy Busan-style *bibim kalguksu*. **A larger portion costs nothing extra**, and it opens **10:50 to 19:30 daily**. Head north from there and you reach Bosu-dong Book Street (67-1 Daecheong-ro).

## Bongnae-dong, Yeongdo: cafes in old warehouses

Cross Yeongdo Bridge from Jagalchi and you reach the Bongnae-dong wharf, where a few **old port warehouses have become cafes**. **Momos Coffee Yeongdo (160 Bongnaenaru-ro, 1F)** is the roastery and coffee bar of Busan's Momos Coffee; the company's official site lists this as its registered address. Check the official Instagram for hours. A little further along is **Mumyeong Ilgi (178 Bongnaenaru-ro)**, which GQ Korea describes as a neglected warehouse turned cafe that hosts live music and flea markets and sells house-baked cakes and a bulgogi baguette.

## Notes for overseas visitors

- **Signs inside Jagalchi are in English, Chinese and Japanese**, and Visit Busan has English pages for every market here.
- **Carry some cash.** Restaurants take cards, but some market stalls and night-market carts do not.
- **Vegetarians: seed hotteok is the safe choice.** Pork soup, milmyeon, jokbal and the fish market all involve meat or fish.
- **From Gimhae Airport**, take the [[line:gimhaelrt]] to Sasang, change to [[line:busan2]], change again at Seomyeon to [[line:busan1]] and get off at Jagalchi. **From Busan Station**, Jagalchi is three stops on [[line:busan1]].
- **Map apps:** Naver Map or KakaoMap are far more accurate here than Google Maps. Search shop names in Korean (they are in the place cards below).

## Practical notes

- **Closing days differ.** Jagalchi on the 1st, 3rd and 5th Tuesday; Gukje Market on Sundays; Yangsanjip on Tuesdays; Wonsan Myeonok on the day of Seollal and Chuseok.
- **See the fish in the morning.** Jagalchi opens at 05:00.
- **The night market starts at 19:30.** Go earlier and the carts are not there.
- **Parking is public or paid lots only.** None of the restaurants in this guide have parking.
- **No tipping**, including at the markets.

## Wrapping up

A sashimi lunch on Jagalchi's second floor, a hotteok at BIFF Square and a lap of the Kkangtong night market cover the bones of Nampo-dong eating. With a second day, add the jokbal alley and the Yeongdo warehouse cafes.
