The Joseon royal tombs (조선왕릉) are a UNESCO World Heritage set of 40 burial grounds scattered around Seoul and Gyeonggi-do, and most of the year they are quiet parks that close before dark. For nine days each autumn the gates stay open after sunset for concerts, a musical and night walks. The **2026 World Heritage Joseon Royal Tombs Festival** opens with a ceremony at Yungneung and Geolleung in Hwaseong on Friday 16 October and runs **from Saturday 17 to Sunday 25 October** at 12 tombs, up from 9 last year. This is the seventh edition.

The short version: **split your plan into the evening programs that need a reservation (the royal tomb musical, the night-time Royal Stage concerts and the Donggureung night tour) and the daytime programs you can simply walk into (Joseon Detective, the Book Garden, the Culture Lounge).** Evening tickets went on sale from 15 September through Ticketlink for Korean residents and Creatrip for foreign visitors. Daytime programs only need the regular tomb ticket, ₩1,000 (about $0.75).

This post reflects what was published as of 22 October 2026. Four days remain, and most of the performances fall on the weekend of 24–25 October.

## The basics: 12 tombs, 9 days

The festival is run by the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center of the Korea Heritage Service together with the Korea Heritage Agency. The official site lists these 12 venues.

- **Western Gyeonggi**: Yungneung and Geolleung (Hwaseong), Seooreung (Goyang), Paju Samneung, Gimpo Jangneung
- **Eastern Gyeonggi**: Donggureung (Guri), Hongneung and Yureung (Namyangju), Sareung (Namyangju)
- **Seoul**: Seolleung and Jeongneung (Gangnam), Taereung and Gangneung (Nowon), Uireung (Seongbuk)
- **Further out**: Yeongneung and Nyeongneung (Yeoju), Jangneung (Yeongwol)

The main stage is **Yungneung and Geolleung**, where the opening ceremony and the musical take place. The easiest venue from central Seoul is **Seolleung and Jeongneung**, five minutes from a subway station in Gangnam. Monday 19 October was the weekly closing day for the tombs, although a few reservation-only programs, such as the special opening of the burial mounds at Donggureung and Seooreung, ran that day.

## The royal tomb musical

"Peach Blossoms: A Promise Kept Beyond Sorrow" is a 60-minute music drama staged on a temporary stage at Yungneung. It tells the family story of King Yeongjo, his son Crown Prince Sado and Sado's son King Jeongjo, whose tombs are right there. Performances are on 17, 18, 24 and 25 October at **19:30–20:30**. According to the Korean press, Choi Jae-rim plays Prince Sado, Song Yong-tae plays Yeongjo and Ku Jun-mo plays Jeongjo. English subtitles are provided for foreign visitors.

Tickets are **₩15,000** (about $11) according to the Financial News report, and Creatrip lists the foreigner ticket at $11.29. Yungneung and Geolleung normally close at 18:00, so an evening inside the tomb grounds is something you can only do during the festival.

## The Donggureung night tour and the daytime programs

**The Donggureung Night Tour** "The Kings' Dreams" is a guided walk through Donggureung after dark. It runs from 13 to 25 October (not on Mondays) with departures at 19:00, 19:30 and 20:00, by reservation. Press reports put the fee at ₩10,000 (about $7.50). Donggureung is the largest cluster of Joseon royal tombs, nine of them starting with the tomb of the dynasty's founder King Taejo, so you could do a daytime loop and come back for the night walk.

![Leaning pine trees on either side of the Donggureung spirit gate and the stone path leading to the ritual hall](/images/joseon-royal-tombs-festival-2026-10/05.webp)

Several daytime programs need nothing more than the tomb entry ticket.

- **Joseon Detective: The Royal Tomb Chronicles** — an outdoor escape-room style mission game in which you play a court historian hunting for words missing from the royal annals. Walk-in, 17–25 October, 10:00–17:00, at Yungneung-Geolleung, Seooreung, Gimpo Jangneung, Donggureung, Taereung-Gangneung and Seolleung-Jeongneung.
- **Royal Tomb Book Garden** — an outdoor reading space, 10:00–17:00, at Seooreung, Donggureung, Seolleung-Jeongneung and Yeongneung.
- **Royal Tomb Culture Lounge** — craft sessions such as bookmarks, building blocks, traditional tea bags, gold-leaf work and key rings. Some items took reservations on Ticketlink: a biscuit collage at Taereung-Gangneung, lotus-root digging at Gimpo Jangneung (24–25 October) and the "forest breath" walk at Seolleung-Jeongneung (20–23 October).
- **Stories of the Royal Tombs by the Tomb Keeper** — a re-enactment of a Joseon tomb keeper's daily rounds, twice a day (11:00 and 15:00) at Seooreung, Donggureung and Yeongneung.
- **Contemporary pansori "Two Hearts, One Longing"** — a sung story of King Danjong and Queen Jeongsun, 14:00 on 17–18 October at Sareung and 24–25 October at Yeongwol Jangneung.
- **Ancestral rites experience** — set the ritual table with food in ceremonial vessels (13:30), then put on the officiant's robes and take part in the rite (15:30). Taereung-Gangneung on 17–18 October, Seooreung on 24–25 October. Free, but reservation-only and it filled up early.
- **Royal Tomb Humanities Lectures** — press reports name the novelist Kim Yeon-su at Seolleung-Jeongneung on 18 October and the Go player Lee Se-dol at Yungneung-Geolleung on 25 October.

There are exhibitions too. The **Royal Tomb Sound Gallery** "Sounds of Nature, Resonance of Rituals", an immersive sound and media-art show, runs from 30 September to 25 October at the National Intangible Heritage Training Center in Gangnam, Seoul (closed Mondays, 10:00–17:00). At Yungneung and Geolleung the **Ancestral Rites Exhibition** shows models of the ritual food and the actual vessels, and **The King's Garden** uses light and objects to express King Jeongjo's devotion to his father. An academic seminar on the royal tombs and their guardian temples takes place at Yongjusa temple in Hwaseong on Friday 23 October at 14:00.

## Tickets and prices

- **Where to book**: Ticketlink for Korean residents, Creatrip for foreign visitors. Sales opened on 15 September at 14:00 with the opening ceremony, then the musical (15:00), Royal Stage (16:00) and the Donggureung night tour on 16 September at 15:00. As of 22 October the Ticketlink page lists the musical, Royal Stage, the tomb keeper stories, the night tour and Culture Lounge items as bookable, while the opening ceremony, the Sareung pansori and the special mound openings are sold out or closed.
- **Tomb admission**: ₩1,000 for ages 25–64. Visitors 24 and under, 65 and over, and anyone wearing hanbok get in free, according to the Korea Heritage Service. The agency plans to announce new palace and tomb fees in November, to apply from 1 January 2027, so prices may change next year.
- **Program fees**: opening ceremony free; musical ₩15,000; Donggureung night tour ₩10,000 (both per the Financial News); special mound opening and ancestral rites experience free. The Ministry of Culture's festival listing simply says "some performances and activities charge a fee".
- **Contact**: festival hotline +82-1522-2295 (palace and tomb program call centre), operations +82-70-7462-1106. Both are Korean-language lines. For English, the national tourist line 1330 can help.

## Practical notes

- **Closing day**: the Joseon royal tombs close every Monday. If Monday is a public holiday they open and close on the next weekday instead.
- **Evening entry**: last entry at most tombs is around 17:00. The musical, Royal Stage and the night tour are run separately for ticket holders, so if you also want to walk the grounds in daylight, go in before last entry first.
- **Parking**: Yungneung and Geolleung use the public car park by the entrance, and Donggureung, Seooreung and Hongneung-Yureung have car parks according to the tourism office. Seolleung-Jeongneung is downtown, so take the subway.
- **What to wear**: the grounds are long stretches of lawn and dirt path, so comfortable shoes help, and late-October nights are chilly, so bring a jacket.
- **Mobile stamp tour**: during the festival a location-based app lets you collect stamps at all 40 Joseon royal tombs, with a prize draw after the festival.
- **For foreign visitors**: the musical has English subtitles, Creatrip handles bookings, and the special opening of the Seolleung-Jeongneung burial mounds on 12 October was reserved for foreign visitors only.

## Wrapping up

Looking only at the final weekend of 24–25 October, Saturday evening has the musical at Yungneung and the Yang Bang-ean concert at Hongneung at the same hour, and Sunday has the 14:00 concert at Hongneung, the 15:00 concert at Yeongneung and the ancestral rites experience at Seooreung.
