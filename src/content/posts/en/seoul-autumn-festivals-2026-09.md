Seoul packs its festival calendar into autumn. From the middle of September to the end of October there is something on almost every weekend, and most of it is free. What follows is the 2026 line-up in date order, with locations and the nearest stations. One note first: the Seoul International Fireworks Festival, the biggest event of the season, was held on 4–5 September this year and has already passed. Everything below is still ahead. Details are current as of 10 September 2026, and organisers do move dates.

![Silver grass covering Haneul Park under an autumn sky, with the landfill gas flare tower behind](/images/seoul-autumn-festivals-2026-09/01.webp)

## 19–20 September — Seoul Street Arts Festival

This year the festival left Seoul Plaza for the river. The stage is **Ttukseom Hangang Park and the Seoul Forest area**, with 20 productions giving 62 performances over two days. Nine of them come from abroad — France, Lithuania, Chile, Slovenia and Brazil.

The headline piece is *Girafes* by the French company Off: seven eight-metre red giraffes walking the riverbank as an outdoor opera. Everything is free to watch, though some participatory programmes take advance bookings. Organised by the Seoul Foundation for Arts and Culture.

![Tents pitched under shade trees on the lawn at Ttukseom Hangang Park](/images/seoul-autumn-festivals-2026-09/07.webp)

## 24–27 September — Chuseok, when the palaces are free

Chuseok, the Korean harvest holiday, falls on 25 September this year, with the holiday period running 24–27 September. Under the government's holiday measures, **the four grand palaces, Jongmyo Shrine and the Joseon royal tombs are open free of charge** across those four days. That applies to standard daytime entry; separately ticketed programmes such as Gyeongbokgung's evening viewing keep their own rules and fees. Check the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center notice before you go.

Two practical consequences for visitors. Some restaurants and shops close for the holiday, since Chuseok is when families travel to their hometowns. And the palaces, museums and riverside parks are at their most crowded precisely then.

## 2–5 October — Gangnam Festival

Now in its fifteenth year, the festival has expanded from Yeongdong-daero onto **Dosan-daero**. The main events cluster on 3–5 October.

- 3 October: a grand parade along the 2 km stretch from Cheongdam Intersection to Dosan Park Intersection, followed by a family concert.
- 4 October: a K-pop concert.
- 5 October: the Gangnam International Peace Marathon around Bongeunsa-ro.

## 7–11 October — Autumn Royal Culture Festival

Five days across Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, Changgyeonggung and Jongmyo Shrine, all at once. Daytime brings exhibitions, tours and hands-on programmes; after dark the palaces stay lit for performances. Usefully, **there are sessions and booking quotas reserved for overseas visitors**, so this is the one to look at first if Korean-language booking pages have been a barrier.

Deoksugung also runs its own programme, *Night at Seokjojeon*, from 9 September to 18 October. It requires a reservation.

![Visitors in hanbok crossing the wide courtyard in front of Heungnyemun Gate at Gyeongbokgung](/images/seoul-autumn-festivals-2026-09/04.webp)

## 17–23 October — Seoul Silver Grass Festival

The 25th edition runs for a week at **Haneul Park** in World Cup Park. Expect craft booths (making bouquets from the grass), busking on a central stage and an outdoor photography exhibition — but the signature is the nightly lighting show across the grass fields.

Haneul Park is a hill, as the name suggests, so allow time for the climb. Arriving around sunset gets you the daylight view and the illuminations in one trip.

![A path cut through tall silver grass at Haneul Park with wind turbines in the distance](/images/seoul-autumn-festivals-2026-09/02.webp)

## 23–25 October — Hanseong Baekje Festival

Baekje, one of Korea's Three Kingdoms, kept its capital in what is now the Songpa district for 493 years. The festival marks that period at the **88 Lawn in Olympic Park**, with opening and closing performances, hands-on booths and food stalls. A historical street procession and a walk along the Mongchontoseong earthen fortress walls are the parts specific to this one.

## 29–31 October — Jeongdong Night Walk

Autumn closes with a night stroll. Historic buildings along Deoksugung-gil and Jeongdong-gil open after dark, with guided tours and history programmes. 29 October is a preview evening; the official opening is a palace concert in front of Junghwajeon Hall at Deoksugung at 19:00 on 30 October.

## Practical notes

- **Getting there**: Ttukseom Resort Station on [[line:7]] for the street arts festival; World Cup Stadium Station on [[line:6]] for the silver grass; Mongchontoseong Station on [[line:8]] or Hanseong Baekje Station on [[line:9]] for Olympic Park; Gyeongbokgung Station on [[line:3]] for the palace festival; City Hall Station on [[line:1]] or [[line:2]] for Jeongdong.
- **Cost**: nearly all of the above is free. Only the palace night programmes and a few ticketed performances cost anything.
- **Booking**: participatory programmes fill quickly. Check each festival's official site for when reservations open, and look for the English page — several of these have one.
- **Weather**: October in Seoul swings widely between afternoon and night. The outdoor evening events need a jacket.
- **Confirm before you travel**: dates shift even after announcement. Re-check on festival.seoul.go.kr during the week you visit.

## Wrapping up

The season runs from the Han River on 19 September to Jeongdong on 31 October. If you only have room for one, the silver grass festival and the night walk both guarantee a strong evening; the Royal Culture Festival is the better daytime choice and the easiest to join without Korean. In November the Seoul Lantern Festival takes over along Cheonggyecheon Stream.
