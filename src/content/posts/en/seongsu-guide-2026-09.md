If you have one free day in Seoul and want to spend it in a single neighbourhood rather than ticking off landmarks, Seongsu-dong (성수동) is the current answer. A large riverside park, a dense grid of café streets, a rotating calendar of brand pop-ups and a working shoemaking district all sit within two subway stops of each other.

Information here is as of September 2026. Hours and prices change, so check official pages before you go.

## From factories to café street

Seongsu-dong is in Seongdong-gu, on the north bank of the Han River, east of the city centre. Until fairly recently it was a light-industrial area: shoe workshops, print shops, car repair garages, warehouses. Through the 2010s, operators began taking over those red-brick warehouse and factory buildings, keeping the shells and exposed structure and rebuilding only the interiors. The result is distinctive enough that the Seoul city government's own media outlet calls the district **"the Brooklyn of Seoul."**

![A red-brick warehouse building with a wide open doorway on Seongsu's café street](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/05.webp)

The building most often credited with starting it is **Daelim Changgo** (대림창고). Built in the 1970s as a rice mill and used as a warehouse from the 1990s, it now runs as a gallery café. **Cafe Onion Seongsu**, in a converted 1970s building, works on the same principle. Reviews of Seongsu cafés tend to talk about the rooms rather than the menus — high ceilings, bare concrete, original beams left in place.

## Seoul Forest

The western edge of the neighbourhood runs into Seoul Forest, a park that opened in 2005 and is divided into four zones: a cultural arts park, an experiential learning centre, an ecological forest and a wetland ecology area. **Entry is free and the park is open year-round.**

![Two spotted deer standing beside dry hay in the Seoul Forest deer enclosure](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/04.webp)

Only the individual facilities have fixed hours. According to the park's official information, the ecological forest is open 5:30am–9:30pm, the Insect and Plant Garden 11am–4pm (closed Mondays and Tuesdays), and the visitor centre 10am–6pm, closing at 5pm in winter. The butterfly garden runs May to October and the ground fountains May to September. The deer enclosure sits right on a main path, which makes it an easy stop if you are travelling with children.

## Yeonmujang-gil and the café streets

The densest run of cafés, galleries and concept stores is along **Yeonmujang-gil** (연무장길). The name comes from a Joseon-era military training ground that once stood in the area. Shops line the street itself, and the signage gets smaller and the spaces more independent the further you turn into the side alleys.

![A Seongsu side street where a glass building faces a brick one, with outdoor café tables](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/06.webp)

Walking only the main road means missing most of it. Seongsu rewards turning two or three corners off your route rather than navigating point to point.

## Pop-up stores

Pop-ups are what pushed Seongsu to its current status. Brands rent an empty building or a dedicated space for anything from a few days to a few weeks and fit it out like an exhibition — often free to enter, usually built around photo spots and limited merchandise. Fashion and beauty labels, luxury houses and K-pop agencies all use the district, and **weeks with more than ten pop-ups running at once are common.**

![A ground-floor pop-up store in Seongsu with character artwork and a courtyard with parasols](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/07.webp)

Because the line-up changes weekly, there is no fixed list to plan around. Check a pop-up listing site or the brands' Instagram accounts a few days before you go, and check whether a reservation is needed — popular ones book out, and Korean pop-ups frequently use free timed-entry reservations rather than walk-in queues. Permanent anchors such as the Musinsa Megastore Seongsu mean there is still something to see on a quiet week.

## The shoemaking district

Walk toward Seongsu Station and the tone changes. This is the largest concentration of handmade shoe workshops in Korea, and the workshops are still operating. A retail space under Seongsu Station sells the local makers' shoes and takes custom orders, and the nearby **Shoe Theme Park** marks the trade with oversized shoe sculptures.

![Brightly striped shoe-shaped sculptures in Seongsu's Shoe Theme Park](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/09.webp)

These alleys run parallel to the café streets, and seeing both explains the neighbourhood better than either does alone.

## Practical notes

- **Subway**: [[line:2]] **Seongsu Station** for the café streets and the shoe district, [[line:2]] **Ttukseom Station** (exit 8, about 10 minutes' walk) for Seoul Forest, and [[line:suinbundang]] **Seoul Forest Station** (exit 4, about 2 minutes) for the park itself. Picking the right station saves a lot of walking.
- **From Incheon Airport**: the Airport Railroad to Hongik University Station takes roughly 50 minutes, then [[line:2]] to Seongsu takes about 30 minutes. Allow an hour and a half with the transfer. Fares and ticket options are in the [Incheon Airport to Seoul guide](/en/posts/incheon-airport-to-seoul-2026-09/).
- **Bus**: [[bus:2224]] and [[bus:2413]] link Seoul Forest, Ttukseom Station and Seongsu Station.
- **Parking**: Seoul Forest has 211 spaces, open 24 hours, at ₩200 per five minutes (₩2,400 per hour, about $1.80). Parking around the café streets is genuinely scarce — take the subway.
- **When it is busy**: weekend afternoons and the opening week of a popular pop-up. Weekday mornings are quiet, but many cafés do not open until around 11am.
- **A route that works**: start at Seoul Forest Station, walk the park, then head east through Yeonmujang-gil and finish at Seongsu Station. That is roughly 2–3km with no backtracking.

## Wrapping up

Seongsu is not a neighbourhood with one thing to see. The appeal is the mix itself — an intact factory shell with a fashion flagship inside it, a shoe workshop two doors from a gallery café — which makes it better suited to a half-day walk than a tight itinerary. With Seoul Forest attached to one end, a decent-weather day fills itself.
