On 5 October 2026 Seongdong-gu's district-run creative industries festival, **Creative × Seongsu 2026**, opened across Seongsu-dong. It runs until the 11th, using S-Factory, Seoul Forest, Yeonmujang-gil and Seongsui-ro as its stage (PPSS and Simin Ilbo, September 2026). Five months earlier the same streets saw the opposite. Seongsu has already proved it can pull a crowd; the question now is how to move one safely.

In short: Seongsu-dong is **a riverside district zoned for light industry in the 1960s, where shoe workshops, printers and car repair shops gathered; a converted warehouse opened as an arts space in 2011; Korea's first anti-gentrification ordinance followed in 2015; and corporate headquarters moving in during the 2020s turned it into Seoul's "pop-up capital".** This guide rebuilds that story, and today's numbers on visitors, vacancies, station use and construction, from district records, Korean Wikipedia and dated reporting. ## Seongsu at a glance

"Seongsu" means slightly different things to different people. Administratively it is two legal neighbourhoods (*dong*) of Seongdong-gu: **Seongsu-dong 1-ga and Seongsu-dong 2-ga**. In Korea a *gu* is a city district with its own district office and a *dong* is a neighbourhood inside it; *1-ga* and *2-ga* simply mean "section 1" and "section 2". In everyday use "Seongsu" also takes in Seoul Forest and the shopping streets around Seongsu and Ttukseom stations, and property reporting often adds the redevelopment zone along the Han River.

To the north are the elevated Line 2 tracks and the Wangsimni area; to the east, across the Jungnangcheon, is Gwangjin-gu's Konkuk University area.

## From Ttukdo to Seongsu: the name and the land

### Ttukdo-myeon, Goyang County

According to Korean Wikipedia, the Seongsu area belonged to **Ttukdo-myeon in Goyang County, Gyeonggi Province, from 1914**, was **brought into Seoul's Seongdong-gu in 1949**, and was **split into Seongsu-dong 1-ga and 2-ga in 1950**. "Ttukseom" is not actually an island; it was the name for the broad flats between the Han and the Jungnangcheon, and it survives in **Ttukseom Station** on Line 2 and in **Ttukseom Hangang Park** on the Gwangjin-gu side. One 2026 report notes that a food programme at this year's Creative × Seongsu borrowed its theme from "Ttukdo-ri", the area's old name (Inven, 2026).

Korean Wikipedia gives two theories for the name *Seongsu* (聖水, "holy water"). One says it combines a character each from **Seongdeok Pavilion** and the **Ttukdo waterworks**; the other says it refers to the clean, precious Han River water drawn here for drinking. We could not find an official record that settles it.

### A 1908 waterworks, a 2005 park

The Korea Tourism Organization describes the site of Seoul Forest as **Seoul's first water-supply intake, set up in 1908**, and says the park **opened in 2005** as a large urban forest on that land and the former Ttukseom sports park. It fits neatly with the "waterworks" theory of the name. Facilities and opening hours inside the park are covered in [Seongsu things to do](/en/posts/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/).

Sources phrase the zoning date slightly differently. Yonhap (November 2016) gives **1964**; other articles say only "the 1960s" or "the early 1960s". We follow the source that names a year and list it under what we could not confirm.

Shoemaking is still here. The Korea Tourism Organization says Seongsu has **about 350 finished-shoe makers and about 100 component and materials suppliers**, and **more than 70% of Korea's handmade shoe manufacturers**, with design, production, shipping and sales all happening inside the neighbourhood. That looks smaller than the 650 businesses counted in 2013 (Aju Business Daily), but the two figures count different things, so they are not directly comparable.

The wooden shapes on the racks outside are **shoe lasts**, the forms shoes are built around. Many shops on this street are workshop and showroom in one. Public sales halls and shoe-related sights are in [Seongsu things to do](/en/posts/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## 2011: a warehouse becomes an arts space

Almost every account names the same starting point: **Daelim Warehouse** (Daelim Changgo). According to Financial News (September 2024), the building was a rice mill until the 1970s and a warehouse from 1990, and in **2011** it became an arts and events space with its original shell left intact. The Korea Tourism Organization calls it the original of the warehouse cafes now scattered along the cafe street. Aju Business Daily (January 2026) describes the change as **grassroots urban regeneration that started before any district policy**. Since September 2024 the building has housed **Musinsa Store Seongsu@Daelim Changgo**, a fashion store (Financial News, September 2024), so today it is better thought of as a shop than a cafe.

## Gentrification: the 2015 ordinance and the sustainable development zone

When a neighbourhood gets popular, rents rise and the small shops that made it popular get pushed out. That is gentrification, and Seongdong-gu tackled it earlier than most.

| When | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| September 2015 | Ordinance passed. The district and many reports call it **Korea's first anti-gentrification policy** | Aju Business Daily 2016, Asia Economy 2023 |
| 2016 | Seoulsup-gil, Bangsongdae-gil and Sangwon-gil designated as **sustainable development zones** | Aju Business Daily 2016 |
| Rules inside the zone | New large-company and franchise outlets restricted; landlords and tenants sign rent-stability agreements | Asia Economy 2023 |
| **1 August 2023** | Zone **expanded 8.6 times to the whole of Seongsu-dong**, except the riverside redevelopment zone and new apartment sites | Asia Economy 2023 |
| Why | Seongdong-gu had the highest rent increase of Seoul's districts in 2022 (42% over 2020) | Asia Economy 2023 |
| Effect | Businesses with agreements stayed **79 months** on average, against **52 months** without | Asia Economy 2023 |

It has clear limits. Chosun Ilbo's property section (February 2026) pointed out that the ordinance proclaimed in September 2015 **is not legally binding**, so it cannot actually stop a landlord evicting a tenant. It works through agreements and incentives, such as extra floor area for new buildings whose owners sign a rent agreement.

![A small cafe with an orange entrance open right next to a building carrying a vertical sign for a leather company](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/04.webp)

A small cafe opening right next to a leather company's sign. This is what the ordinance is trying to protect: workshops and small shops holding their ground side by side. With corporate station branding, flagship stores and high-rise development all arriving at once, that balance is being tested.

### A new district head

The policy was led by **Chung Won-oh**, Seongdong-gu's district mayor since 2014 (three terms). In the local elections of 3 June 2026 he ran for Mayor of Seoul and **lost by 0.6 percentage points**, 48.34% to Oh Se-hoon's 48.94% (News Claim). Seongdong-gu's new district mayor, **Yoo Bo-hwa, took office on 1 July 2026**; her first signed order set up a task force to speed up redevelopment and reconstruction (Mega Economy). We found no announcement changing the anti-gentrification policy, but the new administration is clearly prioritising redevelopment.

**Seongsu's record is a string of "keep it and change it" decisions.** Keep the rice-mill building and turn it into an arts space and a shop; pass an ordinance to protect brick buildings; and now allow a development zone and towers right next to them.

## How to do it

Seongsu is **small enough to walk**. We suggest going from Seoul Forest in the west to the Seongsu Station end in the east.

1. **Morning**: get off at [[line:suinbundang]] Seoul Forest and walk the park. It is open every day; facility hours are in [Seongsu things to do](/en/posts/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/).
2. **Late morning**: the red-brick houses of **Atelier-gil**, north-east of the park.
3. **Lunch**: around Ttukseom Station or on Yeonmujang-gil. How to choose is in [Seongsu food](/en/posts/seongsu-food-2026-09/).
4. **After 2pm (weekends)**: **Yeonmujang-gil** with its traffic restrictions. Most pop-ups last days or weeks, so check just before you go; tips in [Seongsu things to do](/en/posts/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/).
5. **Late afternoon**: the **handmade shoe street** near Seongsu Station, under the elevated tracks.
6. **Evening**: on weekdays Exit 3 at Seongsu Station is at its worst from 5 to 7pm. Heading back via Ttukseom or Seoul Forest station may be easier.

## Crowds and etiquette

- **Watch for big event announcements.** As 1 May 2026 showed, free giveaways and limited editions can draw far more people than expected. Seongdong-gu's "Seongsu Live" shows conditions in real time.
- **Car-free hours still have cars.** Residents, workers and delivery vehicles can drive in during the 2pm–6pm weekend window.
- **This is still a working district.** Do not block shoe workshops, printers or repair shops for photos, and do not photograph people at work.
- **Keep your voice down in residential lanes.** Atelier-gil and the back streets of Yeonmujang-gil are people's homes.
- **Company offices are not attractions.** Don't wait outside or block the entrances of SM, Musinsa or other headquarters.

## Where to stay

There are very few large hotels in Seongsu itself. By the count in [Seongsu hotels](/en/posts/seongsu-hotels-2026-09/), Seongsu-dong 1-ga and 2-ga have **one registered tourist hotel (Hotel Poco Seongsu, 80 rooms)**; the rest are hostels and homestay-type rentals. Across all of Seongdong-gu there are seven tourist hotels with 220 rooms and no five-star (Aju Business Daily, September 2026). Some hotels with "Seongsu" in their names are actually across the stream in Gwangjin-gu, so check the address. The Sampyo development includes 130 hotel rooms, but it is not due until 2032 (Consumer Times). Options by station, prices and the risks of unregistered rentals are in [Seongsu hotels](/en/posts/seongsu-hotels-2026-09/); comparisons with other parts of Seoul are in [Where to stay in Seoul](/en/posts/seoul-where-to-stay-2026-09/).

## Who it suits

**It suits you if:**

- You are curious about converted factories and warehouses, and **how a city district changes**
- You want to walk between pop-ups and fashion and beauty flagships
- You want Seoul Forest and cafes in the same half-day
- You want a base on Line 2 with direct trains to Gangnam and Hongdae

**It may not suit you if:**

- Palaces and hanok are your goal: try the [Bukchon guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/) or the [Jongno guide](/en/posts/jongno-guide-2026-09/)
- You find crowds hard: avoid weekend afternoons and big event days
- You want a large hotel within walking distance: options inside Seongsu are limited

## In short

- **Boundary**: Seongsu-dong 1-ga (Seoul Forest, Atelier-gil, Ttukseom Station) and 2-ga (Yeonmujang-gil, the shoe street, Seongsu Station) in Seongdong-gu.
- **History**: Goyang County's Ttukdo-myeon from 1914, part of Seoul from 1949, semi-industrial zoning in 1964, Daelim Warehouse in 2011, the anti-gentrification ordinance in September 2015.
- **Companies**: SM in May 2021, Musinsa in September 2022. Development zone expanded about fourfold to 2.05 km² in January 2026.
- **Numbers**: about 70 million visitors in 2024, 883 pop-ups in 2025, 2.5% vacancy (Q4 2025) or 4.5% (Q2 2026), 102,489 daily users at Seongsu Station.
- **Watch out**: Seongsu Station has four exits; Exit 3-1 is due by the end of 2026. The 1 May 2026 Pokémon event was stopped after 160,000 people came.
- **Weekends**: Yeonmujang-gil traffic restriction is Saturday and Sunday 2pm–6pm in 2026 (until 29 November).

In one line: **Seongsu kept its factories and still drew the crowds, and now it is learning how to let those crowds walk safely.**
