Seongsu-dong is **a factory district that cafes moved into**. It did not turn into one or the other. It is still both.

This is what the Korea Tourism Organization wrote when it put the Seongsu cafe street on the **Korea Tourism 100 Selection (2025-2026)** list.

> **The car repair shops, the printing works and the handmade shoe street are all still busy**, and as global restaurant brands and concept-store cafes moved in among the clattering factories, an atmosphere found only in Seongsu-dong took shape.

This post is the overview for a first visit.

![A narrow lane between red-brick houses, with a new high-rise block lit up at the end of it](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/03.webp)

## Where Seongsu-dong is

East Seoul, north of the Han River. The administrative district is Seongdong-gu.

Three axes are worth remembering.

- **Yeonmujang-gil** - the cafe street, south of Seongsu Station
- **Achasan-ro** - the handmade shoe street, the road linking Seongsu Station and Ttukseom Station
- **Ttukseom-ro** - Seoul Forest and the Seoul Waterworks Museum

This is how the Seongdong-gu tourist guidebook describes the cafe street.

> Yeonmujang-gil in Seongsu-dong is a street full of the vintage atmosphere that red brick gives, where **spaces converted out of old factories and warehouses** have been reborn with a new face.

There is one more to add: **Atelier-gil**, a lane of workshops and cafes in among the red-brick houses.

For what it is worth, when Seongdong-gu explains its red-brick policy it cites **Brooklyn in New York and the Aka Renga warehouses in Yokohama** as overseas comparisons. "The Brooklyn of Korea" is not a phrase bloggers invented; it is the district office's own wording.

## Getting there from the airport

**The Incheon Airport limousine [[bus:6013]] stops inside Seongsu-dong.**

- The official route is written as "Myeonmok / Gunja Station / Konkuk University Station / **Seongsu-dong**"
- The Seongsu-dong stops are **Seongsu 119 Fire Station, Seongsu E-Mart and Gyeongil Elementary School**
- **17,000 won for an adult (about $12.75), 12,000 won for a child**

**Routes 6001 and 6002, which come up constantly in other Seoul travel posts, do not stop anywhere in Seongdong-gu.** Get that mixed up and you will get off in the wrong part of the city.

If you would rather come by rail, take [[line:arex]] **as far as Hongik University Station and change to [[line:2]] there.** That is a single transfer all the way to Seongsu Station.

## Which exit to take

This is where people get lost in Seongsu-dong. **There are three stations and each one leads to a different neighbourhood.**

| Destination | Station and exit |
| --- | --- |
| **The cafe street (Yeonmujang-gil)** | [[line:2]] Seongsu Station, **exit 3** |
| The handmade shoe street | [[line:2]] Seongsu Station, exits 1 and 2 |
| Shoe Theme Park | [[line:2]] Seongsu Station, exit 4 |
| Ttukdo Market | [[line:2]] Seongsu Station, exit 3, then 15 minutes on foot |
| **Seoul Forest** | [[line:2]] Ttukseom Station, **exit 8** |
| Seoul Forest and the Waterworks Museum | [[line:suinbundang]] Seoul Forest Station, exit 3 or exit 4 |

For Seoul Forest Station, **the official sources contradict each other**. The Seoul Metropolitan Government says exit 4, Seongdong-gu says exit 3, and the Seoul Tourism Organization says exits 3 and 4.

## When to go

![The orange entrance of a small cafe standing right beside a leather factory sign](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/02.webp)

Seongsu-dong gets very crowded. These are Seongdong-gu's official figures.

- Visitors: **about 46 million in 2020, about 70 million in 2024** (up 52%)
- **883 pop-up stores** held in 2025

The district office has **96 of its 100 crowd-detection CCTV cameras concentrated in Seongsu-dong**, monitored around the clock.

So there are three things to pass on.

**One: 1pm to 5pm at the weekend is the best time to walk.** Seongdong-gu runs what it calls a Seongdong-style pedestrian safety street and **blocks vehicles from entering Yeonmujang-gil** during those hours. The plan for 2026 is to raise this to 78 times a year.

**Two: you can check how crowded it is before you set off.** Seongdong-gu puts out real-time information through the **Seongsu Live YouTube channel** and through kiosks and display boards on the street.

**Three: avoid Mondays.** The indoor facilities at Seoul Forest (the insect and plant garden, the butterfly garden) and the Seoul Waterworks Museum **all close on Mondays**. If you are only here for cafes it makes no difference, but that removes half of what there is to see.

**Seongdong-gu officially classifies Halloween and Christmas as crowd-concentration periods.**

## A one-day route

Seongdong-gu publishes two official themed routes. Here they are as written.

**Seongsu Brooklyn** - the galbi alley, then Atelier-gil, Seoul Forest, Understand Avenue and the Seoul Waterworks Museum

**Seongsu Cafes** - a route linking 11 cafes along Yeonmujang-gil, 3 to 10 minutes' walk between stops

For a first visit, mixing the two works better.

1. **[[line:2]] Seongsu Station, exit 3** - the Yeonmujang-gil cafe street (1-2 hours)
2. **Cross over towards Achasan-ro** for the handmade shoe joint sales outlets and Shoe Spot Seongsu (30 minutes, free)
3. **Walk to Seoul Forest** - it is within walking distance of the cafe street
4. **The Seoul Waterworks Museum** - next door to Seoul Forest, free, 09:00-18:00
5. Dinner in the meat restaurant alleys around **Sangwon-gil**

**Seoul Forest and the Waterworks Museum were originally one and the same filtration plant site.** The insect and plant garden at Seoul Forest is the old rapid filtration building, and the gallery garden is the old sedimentation basin. Fittingly for a neighbourhood where factories became cafes, a water treatment plant became a park and a museum.

## What it costs

**Most of what there is to see is free.**

- Seoul Forest - free
- The Seoul Waterworks Museum - free
- The five public handmade shoe facilities - all free

In practice the only thing you spend money on is **eating and drinking**. Going by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's official "good price" list, the floor for lunch in Seongsu-dong is **5,500 to 9,000 won** (roughly $4 to $6.75), and there are cafes on that list selling an americano at 3,000 won. The detail is in the [food and cafe post](/en/posts/seongsu-food-2026-09/).

## What to know before you go

![A CLOSED sign in pink letters on the peeling wall of an old building](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/04.webp)

**Shops change often.** This is structural, not accidental.

Seongdong-gu's own official Seongsu Cafes route page lists 11 shops in order, but **there are no addresses, no opening hours and no last-updated date.** In other words the district office is not guaranteeing that those shops are still trading.

**So do not come with one particular shop as your target.** Aim at the street and go into whatever is open on the day. That is also why this post names almost no shops.

**The same goes for pop-up stores.** There are 883 a year and most of them run for a few days to a few weeks. Check just before you go.

### Tourist information here is thin

Plainly stated.

- **There is no Seongsu-dong course in the Seoul city walking-tour programme**
- **My Store SS inside Seongsu Station is an unstaffed touchscreen**, and its hours are not in any official source
- **The English and Japanese on the Seongdong-gu website are a Google auto-translate widget**

The places where you can actually ask a person are the **Seoul Forest visitor centre** (09:00-18:00, open year-round) and the **1330 tourist interpretation line**.

### Do not ride a scooter into Seoul Forest

**Electric kick scooters and Segways are banned from Seoul Forest**, with a **50,000 won fine** (about $37). A lot of visitors get around on them these days, but not inside the park. Tents and drones are banned too.

## Where to sleep

**There is not a single registered tourist hotel in Seongsu-dong.** The five registered tourist accommodation businesses in Seongdong-gu are all in Wangsimni and Yongdap-dong.

So staying in Seongsu-dong means, in practice, staying in someone's home. **Unlicensed lodging is a real issue here**, so read the [accommodation post](/en/posts/seongsu-hotels-2026-09/) before you book.

## Who this neighbourhood suits

![A glass high-rise seen across an empty lot where demolition and construction are under way](/images/seongsu-guide-2026-09/05.webp)

**It suits you if:**

- You like cafes and bakeries
- You are interested in spaces made out of converted old buildings
- You like walking. Everything worth seeing is within walking distance
- You need somewhere with a direct ride to **both Gangnam and Hongdae** with no transfer

**It does not suit you if:**

- You came for palaces or hanok, in which case [Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-guide-2026-09/) is your neighbourhood
- You dislike crowds, in which case avoid the weekend
- You want well-developed signage and interpretation, which is weak here

## Wrapping up

- **The airport bus is [[bus:6013]].** Routes 6001 and 6002 do not stop in Seongdong-gu.
- **Exit 3 at Seongsu Station is the cafes, exit 8 at Ttukseom Station is Seoul Forest.**
- **Yeonmujang-gil is closed to cars from 1pm to 5pm at weekends** - the best time to walk it.
- **On Mondays the Seoul Forest indoor facilities and the Waterworks Museum are all closed.**
- **Most sights are free.** The only real spending is on food.
- **Even shops on the district office's official course disappear.** Aim at the streets, not at shops.
- **There are zero tourist hotels in Seongsu-dong.**

In one line: **Seongsu-dong is a neighbourhood you go to for the cafes, and what stays with you is the factory still running next door to one.**
