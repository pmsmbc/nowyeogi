Eating at Gwangalli (광안리) runs on a clock. By day it is the upper-floor cafes on the beach road that look straight at Gwangan Bridge. At sunset it is the live-fish market and the shellfish grills at the Millak-dong end. At night it is takeaway chicken on the sand and pork soup shops that never close.

The short version: the MICHELIN Guide Seoul & Busan 2026 lists **two Bib Gourmands within walking distance of the beach, the pork soup house Anmok and the cold noodle house 100.1 Pyeongnaeng, plus two Selected restaurants, Jin Dweji Gomtang and Eonyang Bulgogi Busanjip**. Raw fish works on the Jagalchi model: buy at **Millak Fishermen's Live Fish Market**, then pay a table charge to eat it at a *chojangjip*. Prices and hours are **as of October 2026**, taken from the Korea Tourism Organization's TourAPI database ("KTO"), official MICHELIN pages, dated articles and menu listings on the Korean restaurant site Polle. Anything about flavour comes from those write-ups and reviews.

## Cafes that face the bridge

Almost every building on Gwanganhaebyeon-ro, the beach road, has a cafe upstairs. Two names come up repeatedly for an open view of the bridge.

**Cup&Cup** is on the 4th floor at 177 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro. Reviews say it spreads over two floors with an open rooftop terrace, and that the roof beats the window seats. The menu lists an iced americano at **₩7,000 (about $5.25)** and a latte at ₩7,500. Sources disagree on hours, giving both 10:00–22:00 and 11:00–21:00, so check a map app on the day.

**Ttubeokseu Gwangan** is on the 7th floor at 239 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro, directly facing the sand behind floor-to-ceiling glass. The menu runs from a ₩6,000 americano to ₩10,000 highballs: coffee by day, the lit bridge and a drink by night.

For dinner with the view, **The Able Gwangan** (29 Millaksubyeon-ro) is an ocean-view restaurant serving pasta, pilaf and steak, listed by KTO at 11:00–22:00 (last order 21:15) with free parking.

![Gwangan Bridge under a pink sunset sky, white towers and cables stretching across the water](/images/gwangalli-food-2026-10/10.webp)

Beachfront drinks cost around ₩7,000, more than a Seoul chain, and the good seats go quickly on weekend afternoons.

## The Millak fish market and the table charge

**Millak Fishermen's Live Fish Market (민락어민활어직판장)** is at 60 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro 312beon-gil, the eastern end of the beach. KTO describes rows of raw-fish counters selling sea bream, flounder, rockfish and sole, with spicy stew from the bones, eaten against the night view of the bridge. Hours vary by stall; a January 2023 listing gives 09:00–22:40, closed the second and fourth Tuesday.

The system is the Jagalchi one. You pick a fish from the tank, the vendor slices it, and you carry it upstairs or to a nearby **chojangjip**, which lays out side dishes, lettuce wraps and sauces and charges a **table fee** per person. The 2023 listing quotes ₩5,000 a head and a recent review ₩12,000 for two; fish starts around ₩20,000 per kilo and moves with the season. Settle the table fee and the price of adding stew before you sit, and carry some cash, since negotiated prices come out cheaper that way.

At the western end, **Namcheon Beach Market** (77 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro 15beon-gil) is a covered neighbourhood market from 1989 with around 200 stalls, many of them live fish and chojangjip. It is the quieter place to do the same thing.

## Grilled shellfish in Millak-dong

The shellfish grills cluster along the waterfront by Millak Waterfront Park and the lanes behind it. A grill goes on your table and scallops, pen shells and prawns are cooked in front of you; the current staple is a three-tier grill topped with cheese.

**Jogae Daepyo Gwangan** (Unit 204, 2F, 16 Gwangnam-ro 94beon-gil) lists its signature cheese grill at **₩35,500** and the three-tier version at ₩37,500, per person, so two people start around ₩70,000. It takes Naver reservations, and reviews say weekend evenings run a queue with reservation holders going in first.

## What MICHELIN picked

Four entries in the MICHELIN Guide Busan 2026 have Suyeong-gu addresses on this route. A Bib Gourmand means a full meal for ₩45,000 or less; MICHELIN shows prices only as ₩ bands.

**Anmok** (3 Gwangnam-ro 22beon-gil) is the pork soup Bib Gourmand. MICHELIN describes a gukbap unlike the familiar version and suggests tasting it first without chilli paste or salted shrimp. Hours are 11:30–15:00 and 17:00–21:00, closed Wednesday, reservations by phone only. A May 2026 article lists dwaeji gukbap at **₩10,000**. Counter seating makes it an easy solo meal.

**100.1 Pyeongnaeng** (29 Namcheonbada-ro 10beon-gil) opened in March 2024 and holds a Bib Gourmand for Pyongyang-style cold buckwheat noodles. MICHELIN singles out a delicate, understated broth, with plate bulgogi and dumplings for a drinking table. A June 2025 GQ Korea piece gives 11:30–21:00 with a 15:30–17:30 break; Naver reservations work, and daily quantities are limited.

**Jin Dweji Gomtang** (2F, 4 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro 284beon-gil) is a Selected restaurant opened in 2024: a U-shaped counter, a ten-item menu and a clear, mild soup of Berkshire K black pork. Reviews mention early sell-outs in the evening.

**Eonyang Bulgogi Busanjip** (32 Namcheonbada-ro) is Selected, in the ₩₩ band: seasoned beef grilled over a grate rather than simmered the Seoul way, from a shop about thirty years old that uses only female hanwoo. Open 11:00–21:30 daily, with parking.

## Where locals eat

**Suyeong Dwaeji Gukbap** (6 Muhak-ro), near Suyeong Station on [[line:busan2]], makes a milky broth from pork leg bones only and a popular suyuk set with sliced pork jowl. Open 08:00–22:40 with a 15:50–16:50 break, every day, with its own car park.

**Gwangalli Daegyo Milmyeon** (239 Gwangnam-ro) makes Busan's cold wheat noodles from a dough of wheat, sweet potato starch and mugwort, pressed to order and topped with chicken. KTO lists 10:00–20:00, break 15:00–17:00, last order 19:50.

**Gaemijip Gwangalli** (9 Gwangnam-ro 130beon-gil) does nakgopsae, the Busan stir-fry of octopus, pork intestine and prawn, at **₩15,000** on the menu. A reviewer who sat straight down at 17:00 found a long queue on the way out.

## Millac The Market

![The front entrance of Millac The Market, graffiti on red brick walls and a wide staircase leading up](/images/gwangalli-food-2026-10/04.webp)

**Millac The Market** (56 Millaksubyeon-ro 17beon-gil) is the complex next to Millak Waterfront Park: a busking square, an ocean-view stand and a food and drink zone of Korean and international brands, open **10:00–24:00 every day** per KTO. Many tenants are time-limited pop-ups, so check first if you want a particular brand. Parking is ₩4,000 for the first hour and ₩1,000 per 15 minutes after that, per the sign at the entrance.

## Eating and drinking on the beach

![A boat-shaped sculpture and breakwater at Millak Waterfront Park, with Gwangan Bridge behind](/images/gwangalli-food-2026-10/07.webp)

**Millak Waterfront Park is a no-alcohol zone.** Suyeong-gu designated it from 1 July 2023, with a **₩50,000 fine** for drinking. The old routine of market fish and soju on a mat in the park is over; eat at a chojangjip or take the fish back to your room.

On the sand itself people do sit with takeaway chicken and convenience-store beer. A KNN report from August 2025, though, describes rising resident complaints about noise and litter as the crowds grew, and the district fines littering. Clear your spot and take your rubbish with you.

The **Gwangalli M Drone Light Show** runs every Saturday, twice a night, at **19:00 and 21:00 from October to February**, for about 12 minutes and free. The beach restaurants fill at once before and after, so eat early and watch, or watch and then go for soup.

- **Subyeon Choego Dwaeji Gukbap, Millak main branch** (9-32 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro 370beon-gil) — 24 hours, every day. A May 2026 article lists pork jowl gukbap at ₩13,000 and plain pork gukbap at ₩11,000. No parking.
- **Halmae Jaecheopguk** (8 Gwangnam-ro 120beon-gil) — 07:00–21:00, every day. KTO calls it a shop with more than fifty years behind it; the clear clam soup is the breakfast cure, with a set at ₩12,000.

## Practical notes

- **Waitlists.** Most Busan restaurants run their queue on the Tabling app, which is Korean-only and needs identity verification tied to a Korean phone contract, so tourists cannot usually join remotely. Use the tablet at the door: switch it to English and enter a number for SMS alerts. **Catch Table Global** supports English, Japanese and Chinese, accepts overseas cards and signs you up by email, for the restaurants it covers.
- **Bookings and cards.** Anmok takes reservations by phone only; 100.1 Pyeongnaeng and Jogae Daepyo use Naver. MICHELIN marks Anmok, 100.1 Pyeongnaeng and Eonyang Bulgogi Busanjip as accepting credit cards. At the fish market, a negotiated price is easier in cash.
- **Avoiding pork.** Raw fish, grilled shellfish and the beef-only Eonyang bulgogi are the options; pork soup and milmyeon both use meat broth. Halal-certified restaurants are far easier to find in Seoul, covered in our [Itaewon food guide](/en/posts/itaewon-food-2026-09/).
- **Getting there.** Gwangan Station exit 5 on [[line:busan2]] is a 13-minute walk to the sand; Geumnyeonsan Station is closer for the Namcheon-dong places. The fish market, shellfish grills and Millac The Market are at the eastern end. From Gimhae Airport take [[line:gimhaelrt]] to Sasang and change to [[line:busan2]]; from Busan Station take [[line:busan1]] to Seomyeon and change.
- **Closing days.** The fish market closes the second and fourth Tuesday per the 2023 listing; Anmok closes Wednesday.

## Wrapping up

A Gwangalli day is the Cup&Cup rooftop at noon, raw fish at the Millak market as the bridge lights come on, the Saturday drone show, and 24-hour pork soup afterwards. For one proper bowl of dwaeji gukbap go to Anmok; for cold noodles, 100.1 Pyeongnaeng.
