Gangneung is the east coast beach city that Seoul can reach in two hours, and it has built its modern identity on coffee rather than seafood. That sounds like a marketing line. It is not: there is a stretch of beach with about thirty cafés on it, a coffee museum, and an annual coffee festival.

Prices and times are as of September 2026.

![A giant coffee cup sculpture on the sand at Anmok Beach with a boardwalk leading to it and blue sea behind](/images/gangneung-guide-2026-09/01.webp)

## Getting there from Seoul

**By KTX** on the Gyeonggang Line, Seoul Station to Gangneung takes **about two hours**.

On the fare, a caution. Korea's two high-speed operators merged on 1 September 2026 and fares fell by roughly ten percent, and Korail's booking system does not expose a public fare table to check against. Look up the current price when you book rather than trusting an older figure.

**By express bus**, there are two options and they are not the same.

| From | Time | Deluxe fare |
|---|---|---|
| Seoul Express Bus Terminal | 2h 50m | ₩24,600 |
| Dong Seoul Terminal | 2h 30m | ₩26,100 |

Dong Seoul is faster and slightly dearer. Premium class runs ₩31,900 and ₩33,900 respectively, and there are late-night services at a surcharge. Seoul Express runs 24 departures a day, Dong Seoul 12.

## Getting around

**Gangneung Station is in the city centre**, which is unusual for a Korean provincial KTX stop and makes the city easy to work. Many bus routes start or finish there.

**City buses** cost **₩1,530 by card** for a standard service and ₩1,700 in cash, so tapping saves ten percent. **T-money works**, along with Cashbee, Mybi and several others. You get **two free transfers within an hour** with a card, though not with cash and not on the same route number.

**The SEA TEA city tour bus** is the useful one. It runs the 23 km coastal route from **Anmok Coffee Street to Jumunjin Beach**, calling at Songjeong, Gangmun, Gyeongpo, Sacheon, Yeongok, Yeongjin and Jumunjin fish market. It leaves **every 40 minutes from 08:00**, and it charges **the same as an ordinary city bus** with transfers valid. For a coastal day this beats a rental car.

**Specific routes**: Anmok is served by the SEA TEA bus and routes 200, 212, 220, 222 and 223. Gyeongpo Beach is routes 202, 202-1 and 202-2. Jeongdongjin is bus 112.

## Anmok Coffee Street

![The beachfront at Anmok with cafés lining the shore road](/images/gangneung-guide-2026-09/02.webp)

**Anmok (안목해변)** is a short beach with a road behind it and roughly thirty cafés along that road. It is **free and open at any hour**.

The origin story is better than most. The beach was known for its coffee **vending machines** in the early 1980s, of all things, and from the 1990s independent roasters moved in and turned an accidental reputation into a real one. Gangneung is now, with Jeonju, one of Korea's UNESCO Creative Cities of Gastronomy.

Come for a morning coffee facing the sea. It is the single most characteristic thing to do in the city.

## The rest of the city

**Gyeongpo (경포)** is the big beach, with the **Gyeongpodae pavilion** on the hill above it, free and open at all hours.

**Ojukheon (오죽헌)** is the birthplace of the scholar Yi I and his mother Shin Saimdang, the two faces on Korean banknotes. **₩3,000** for adults.

**Jeongdongjin (정동진)** is the sunrise village south of the city, reachable by bus 112 or by a 17-minute local train.

**Chodang (초당두부마을)** is a village of tofu restaurants, and **Jungang Market** is the covered market in the centre with a fish market in the basement.

Full details are in our [things to do guide](/en/posts/gangneung-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## What to eat

Two things define Gangneung's table.

**Chodang sundubu (초당순두부)** is soft tofu curdled with **seawater** instead of a chemical coagulant, which is the local peculiarity. The practice is attributed to a sixteenth-century magistrate, and the first dedicated tofu restaurant in Chodang village opened in 1986.

**Coffee**, obviously, at Anmok or at the roasters in town.

Beyond those, **gamja ongsimi**, grated potato dumplings, and **mulhoe**, cold raw fish soup, are the Gangwon dishes to look for. Our [food guide](/en/posts/gangneung-food-2026-09/) has the detail.

## When to go

**Summer** is beach season. Gyeongpo opened on **4 July in 2026** and the beaches closed in late August, so the supervised window is roughly two months.

**October is the better month for everything else**, and 2026 has an unusually dense run of festivals:

- Gangneung Bread Festival, **9 to 11 October**
- Gangneung Noodle Festival, **15 to 18 October**
- **18th Gangneung Coffee Festival, 21 to 25 October**

**June** brings the **Gangneung Danoje Festival**, **15 to 22 June 2026**, a traditional festival designated a national intangible cultural property in 1967 and the city's biggest annual event.

**31 December** is the sunrise festival at Jeongdongjin, which fills every room on the coast. Book months ahead if that is the plan.

## Two days that work

**Day one.** Arrive late morning, coffee at Anmok, Gyeongpo Beach and the pavilion in the afternoon, tofu dinner at Chodang.

**Day two.** Ojukheon in the morning, Jungang Market for lunch, then the SEA TEA bus up the coast to Jumunjin, or the train down to Jeongdongjin.

## Practical notes

- **The station is central.** You can walk into town from the train.
- **Tap, do not pay cash.** Card fares are ten percent cheaper and only cards get transfers.
- **The tour bus is a normal bus fare.** There is no premium for the coastal route.
- **Book far ahead for New Year.** Jeongdongjin's sunrise is a national event.
- **Beaches are supervised only in season**, roughly July and August.

## Wrapping up

Gangneung works as a two-day trip and it is unusually easy: fast train, central station, cheap coastal bus. Drink the coffee, eat the tofu, and let the SEA TEA bus do the driving.
