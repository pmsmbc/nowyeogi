There is more to see around Ikseon-dong (익선동) than one hanok lane. Within a 10–15 minute walk you also have **Seosulla-gil, the lane that follows the stone wall of Jongmyo Shrine**; **Donhwamun-ro, the street in front of Changdeokgung that the district has nicknamed "Gugak-ro" (Traditional Music Street)**, with several free music venues; **Nakwon Arcade**, with hundreds of instrument shops and a cinema that charges over-55s ₩2,000 (about $1.50); and **the site of Dansungsa**, where the first Korean film was screened.

The short version: **wander the hanok lanes any time, walk Seosulla-gil at the weekend (car-free Saturday and Sunday 10 am–10 pm), and avoid Mondays and Sundays for the museum and library on Gugak-ro.** This October, Seoul Donhwamun Traditional Theater has **several free concerts**, plus a free outdoor show on Saturday 17 October.

The neighbourhood's history (the 1920s–30s "urban hanok", the scrapped redevelopment, how Seosulla-gil was rebuilt) and the etiquette for its residential lanes are in our [Ikseon-dong guide](/en/posts/ikseon-guide-2026-09/). The rules for Changdeokgung and Jongmyo are in [things to do in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/). This page is about **what to see, when, and in what order**. All hours and prices are **as of 2 October 2026, from each venue's official information**.

## The Ikseon-dong hanok lanes

![A hanok shop in an Ikseon-dong lane, a French flag beside its blue door and a stone-paved alley running on under the tiled eaves](/images/ikseon-things-to-do-2026-09/12.webp)

The Seoul Hanok Portal calls Ikseon-dong **"a hanok island in the city"** and says **118 hanok built before the 1930s** remain. The city designated the area a hanok cluster in 2018.

### What to look for

- **The width of the lanes and the line of the roofs.** These are not aristocratic houses like those in Bukchon but **"urban hanok"** built on small plots. The city magazine *Seoul Sarang* (June 2026) describes them as practical houses on plots of about 33 m², with a main wing and a servants' wing, and glass doors added to the wooden floor.
- **The site of Ojinam.** According to the Seoul Hanok Portal, **34-8 Ikseon-dong** was home to Ojinam, one of the three best-known *yojeong* (high-end banquet houses) of the 1970s–80s. Talks leading to the 4 July 1972 North–South Joint Statement were held there. It was demolished in 2010 when a tourist hotel was built; its timbers were moved to Buam-dong and rebuilt as the cultural centre **Mugyewon**. A hotel stands on the Ikseon-dong site today, and the district's walking route stops there as "the site of Ojinam".
- **The site of Nudonggung**, the residence and shrine of King Cheoljong's father (Seoul Hanok Portal). Nothing of it survives; a developer later bought the land and divided it into the small plots that shape the lanes today. The full story is in our [Ikseon-dong guide](/en/posts/ikseon-guide-2026-09/).

### Correcting a number

The previous version of this page was titled "62 of the 75 Hanok Are Shops". It was based on the Seoul Fire & Disaster Headquarters' list of **fire-prevention priority districts**. In the version registered on 5 August 2026, the row for "Ikseon-dong Hanok Village (around 166 Ikseon-dong)" reads:

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Construction | Wooden tiled hanok etc., total floor area 8,544.57 m² |
| Buildings (shops) | **75 (62)** |
| Dwellings | **13** |
| Year built | 1920 |
| Designated | 21 August 2018 |

But the same table gives Insadong's main street **30 buildings and 40 shops**. The shop count can be higher than the building count, so it counts **shops, not buildings that are shops**. The 13 is counted in **households**, not buildings. What the table actually says is that **the district around 166 Ikseon-dong has 75 hanok buildings, 62 shops and 13 homes**. "62 of the 75 hanok are shops" misread it, and we have corrected it.

The area is different too. The Seoul Hanok Portal counts **118 hanok** and **around 330 businesses (2018)** for Ikseon-dong as a whole; the fire statistics cover one block.

### Walking the lanes

![A narrow Ikseon-dong alley with a tiger banner on a hanok wall, overhead wires and people walking further in](/images/ikseon-things-to-do-2026-09/21.webp)

- **There is no ticket and no opening time.** The Korea Tourism Organization lists hours and closing days simply as "different for each shop" and says there is no parking.
- Jongno-gu's walking-route page warns that **"some of the alley routes are homes where residents actually live."** The 13 households in the fire statistics are those people. The district's specific requests (no loudspeakers, don't look into homes) are quoted in our [Ikseon-dong guide](/en/posts/ikseon-guide-2026-09/).
- Across the road to the south, **Donui-dong** has a cluster of *jjokbang*, tiny single-room dwellings rented by people on very low incomes. The same fire table lists "Donui-dong jjokbang area" separately with **89 buildings and 85 households**. It is a place where people live, not a sight; please keep your camera down.
- The only way to see **inside** an Ikseon-dong hanok is to go into a café or restaurant ([Ikseon-dong food](/en/posts/ikseon-food-2026-09/)) or stay in one ([where to stay in Ikseon-dong](/en/posts/ikseon-hotels-2026-09/)).

## Seosulla-gil: car-free until 10 pm at weekends

Seosulla-gil (서순라길) is **the lane running along the west wall of Jongmyo Shrine**. The Korea Tourism Organization describes it as the road from **150-3 Jong-ro (Jongno 3-ga) to 26 Gwonnong-dong**; the name means "the lane west of the Sullacheong", the Joseon-era post whose patrol guards (*sullagun*) watched over the shrine. Dong-A Ilbo (February 2026), citing the city, gives its length as **820 m**.

### Car-free hours

According to a Jongno-gu press release of 22 December 2025, **from Saturday 20 December the car-free hours on Seosulla-gil were extended to 10 am–10 pm on Saturdays and Sundays**, four hours longer than before (it used to end at 6 pm).

| Day | Car-free |
| --- | --- |
| Mon–Fri | No (traffic runs) |
| **Sat–Sun** | **10:00–22:00** |

The district said the reason was safety: visitors wanting photos against the shrine wall kept stepping into the road among the cars. **On weekdays the narrow lane has traffic**, so take your wall photos from the pavement. There is no parking.

### You cannot get into Jongmyo from Seosulla-gil

Seosulla-gil runs **outside** the shrine wall and has no gate. To enter Jongmyo, walk round from the southern end of the lane to the **main gate (Oedaemun)**, which is **220 m from Exit 8 of Jongno 3-ga Station** (Seoul Tourism Organization, cited in our [Jongno guide](/en/posts/jongno-guide-2026-09/)).

The Jongmyo basics, matching [things to do in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/):

- **Closed Tuesdays.** October 09:00–18:00 (last entry 17:00); November 09:00–17:30 (last entry 16:30). Adults ₩1,000.
- **Monday, Thursday and Friday are timed entry with a guide**; **Wednesday (Culture Day), Saturday, Sunday and public holidays are walk-in.**
- **Free every Wednesday from 7 October**, and **free with walk-in entry every day from 29 October to 15 November.** **On Tuesday 6 October every royal palace and tomb site is closed.**
- During the autumn Royal Culture Festival (7–11 October) it is also walk-in every day.

There is even a connection with the lane's name. A new event at this year's autumn festival, the **Jongmyo gatekeepers' patrol ceremony**, runs **from 15:30 for about an hour on 9–11 October** around the main gate and Jongmyo Plaza (per our [Jongno sights page](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/)).

### What to see along the lane: jewellery

Seoul's Future Heritage description says that **when the jewellery industry moved here in the 1970s, many homes in the area became jewellery shops and workshops.** That is why Seosulla-gil is lined with jewellers' studios and boutiques. Time Out's write-up, as quoted by Segye Ilbo, describes the lane as full of craft studios, jewellery workshops and wine bars.

The public place you can walk into is the **Seoul Jewelry Support Center**, a city-funded centre for the jewellery trade set up in 2015. Its website lists two buildings:

| Building | Address | What's there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building 1, Seoul Jewelry Lab | 89-8 Seosulla-gil | Prototyping room, gem-testing equipment, photo studio (by booking, for people in the trade) |
| **Building 2, Seoul Jewelry Showroom** | **83 Seosulla-gil** | **Pop-up showroom on floors 1–2**, B2B showroom in the basement |

- **Tuesday–Saturday 11:00–19:00, closed Sundays, Mondays and public holidays.** The October notice says it is closed on **3 and 9 October** (public holidays) and **4–5 October** (regular closing days).
- The lab equipment is for trade members. For a look round, the **floor 1–2 showroom** is the place.
- From 1 to 15 September the centre ran "Ring Week in Seoul", linking 22 shops along Seosulla-gil and Donhwamun-ro (Seoul Shinmun). This year's edition is over.

The northern end of Seosulla-gil meets Yulgok-ro. According to Dong-A Ilbo, the city put part of Yulgok-ro underground and laid **about 8,000 m² of green space** on top, **opened in July 2022**, reconnecting Jongmyo and Changgyeonggung with woodland after the road cut them apart in the colonial period. From the top of Seosulla-gil you can walk up through it towards Changdeokgung and Changgyeonggung.

## Donhwamun-ro: free venues on "Gugak-ro"

**Donhwamun-ro** runs south from Donhwamun, the main gate of Changdeokgung Palace, to Jong-ro. In May 2021 Jongno-gu gave **520 m of it, from 96 Yulgok-ro to 46 Donhwamun-ro, the honorary name "Gugak-ro"** (*gugak* means Korean traditional music; Asia Economy). The reasons it gave were the music landmarks that once stood here: the royal court music bureau of the colonial era, the Undang Inn run by gayageum master Park Gwi-hui from 1958 to 1989, Ojinam, and the hall of the Joseon Vocal Music Society (1937–43). Today three music venues stand on the street.

**Donhwamun Gate itself is closed for repairs until July 2027.** Changdeokgung's entrance is the west gate, Geumhomun; prices and the **Monday** closing day are in [things to do in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/).

### Seoul Museum of Korean Folk Music: free, closed Mondays

![The front lawn of the Seoul Museum of Korean Folk Music, with a name sculpture in front of a hanok building and office blocks behind](/images/ikseon-things-to-do-2026-09/53.webp)

- **96 Yulgok-ro**, opposite Donhwamun. According to Dong-A Ilbo it was built on former petrol-station land the city bought on Donhwamun-ro and opened in **2019** as **Korea's first museum devoted to folk song (*minyo*)**.
- **Tuesday–Sunday 09:00–18:00 (last entry 17:30), closed Mondays and 1 January, free** (Seoul Museum of History).
- The Korea Tourism Organization says it holds folk songs recorded from **more than 20,000 people in 904 villages across 139 cities and counties**, with a permanent gallery, listening room, video room and archive. The listening room, set in a hanok space, plays representative songs from every province and is meant for first-time listeners, including foreign visitors.
- The museum website lists a **2026 special exhibition (18 June 2026 – 30 May 2027)** and **related programmes on 17–31 October**; details were still marked "registration upcoming" when we checked.
- 5 minutes' walk from [[line:3]] Anguk Station Exit 4, or 8 minutes from Jongno 3-ga Exit 6. No parking. +82-2-742-2600.

### Urisori Library: a public library of traditional-music recordings

- According to the Jongno Cultural Foundation it opened in **December 2017** as a **library specialising in gugak**. The Korea Tourism Organization gives the address as **47 Samil-daero 30-gil (Ikseon-dong)**, inside the local community centre building.
- **Monday–Saturday 09:00–18:00, closed Sundays and public holidays.** About 40 reading seats.
- As of the end of 2025 it held **6,522 books, 1,016 CDs, 121 LPs, 69 DVDs and 473 audio files**. LPs and audio files are for use in the library only.
- Borrowing is for registered Seoul residents, but anyone can read and listen. We could not confirm whether there is help in languages other than Korean.

## Myo-dong and Bongik-dong: Dansungsa and the jewellery district

![Black-and-white photo of the old Dansungsa cinema, its name in Chinese characters above a facade with round windows](/images/ikseon-things-to-do-2026-09/81.webp)

### The site of Dansungsa, birthplace of Korea's Film Day

According to the National Archives of Korea, **Dansungsa opened in June 1907** as a two-storey wooden theatre in Jongno 3-ga and became a full-time cinema in 1918. On **27 October 1919** it screened *Righteous Revenge* (*Uirijeok Guto*), the first film made with Korean money, and **27 October became Korea's Film Day**. Na Un-gyu's *Arirang* (1926) and *Seopyeonje* (1993), which ran for 194 days, also premiered here. The photo above shows the Dansungsa building in **1937**, according to its Wikimedia Commons description.

Today the site is the **Dansung Gold Building (26 Donhwamun-ro, Myo-dong)**.

- After the cinema went bankrupt in 2008 and was sold at auction, the building was renamed on 1 September 2016 and a jewellery centre opened on the ground floor (National Archives).
- Seoul Shinmun (24 October 2019) reported that, to mark 100 years since *Righteous Revenge*, a **Dansungsa Film History Museum opened on 23 October 2019 in about 1,400 m² on the second basement floor**, showing **some 5,500 items** including 1930s posters, flyers, scripts and equipment, and bricks from the 1934 cinema building. But the report said it would open **free once a week, to school groups only**. **We could not confirm whether individual visitors can get in now.**
- The building's website gives opening hours of **10:00–22:00** and lists a "jewellery history hall" and a "film history hall". Jongno-gu's walking route B also stops at the "Jongno jewellery street (Dansung Gold jewellery centre's jewellery history hall)".

Film Day falls on Tuesday 27 October. We found no announcement of an event at the site.

### Daegaksa temple

**Daegaksa** (87 Yulgok-ro 10-gil, Bongik-dong) was founded in **1911 by the monk Yongseong (Baek Yong-seong)**, according to Korean Wikipedia. He was one of the **33 national representatives who signed the 1 March 1919 Declaration of Independence**, and on 12 December 1945 the temple hosted a welcome for Kim Gu and other members of the returning Provisional Government. The present building dates from 1986. Jongno-gu's walking route A includes it between the library and the jewellery centre. We could not find official visiting hours; inside the main hall, keep quiet and don't photograph people praying.

## Nakwon Arcade: instrument shops and a ₩2,000 cinema

Nakwon Arcade (428 Samil-daero) stands at the western edge of Ikseon-dong, towards Exit 5 of Jongno 3-ga Station. Its history is in our [Jongno guide](/en/posts/jongno-guide-2026-09/); here are the two places you can go into.

### Nakwon Musical Instrument Arcade

- **Seoul Future Heritage 2013-044.** The listing describes it as **a large specialist instrument market since 1970**, which became an instrument arcade proper when piano shops moved in after the 1979 tidy-up of Tapgol Park's walls. In the 1970s–80s it also worked as a job market where musicians found gigs.
- The arcade's own website says **around 300 instrument shops fill the 2nd and 3rd floors**: guitars, orchestral instruments, Korean traditional instruments, electronic instruments and sound equipment (KTO).
- **Hours vary by shop.** The Korea Tourism Organization lists 10:00–18:00 "varies by shop". The arcade's notices show it can also close as a whole, for example for a summer break in early August.
- The ground floor is a road that runs under the building.

### Hollywood Classic: the silver cinema

- On the **4th floor of Nakwon Arcade**. Seoul's Future Heritage listing for the Hollywood Theater (2013-285) says it opened in **1969** with the arcade as a 1,200-seat single-screen cinema, became a three-screen multiplex in 1997 and an art-house cinema in 2005, and in **2009 turned into a "silver cinema" for older people**. Today it has **one 300-seat screen, the "Nangman" (Romance) theatre**.
- **It shows classic films.** A September 2026 report (Singlelist) on a special screening for older audiences there confirms it is still operating.
- **Prices**: per the Korea Tourism Organization's accessible-travel site (revised May 2025), **₩2,000 for 55 and over, ₩7,000 general, ₩5,000 youth**. In a May 2024 Segye Ilbo interview, the owner said the 55-plus price had stayed **₩2,000 since 2009** and would stay so "until the building is torn down and the cinema disappears".
- The same article says there are four or five screenings a day and 500–600 visitors daily. The snack bar sells traditional snacks such as grilled rice cakes and old-style instant coffee.
- Screening times are posted on the Korean booking service Dtryx under "허리우드클래식". Phone +82-2-3672-4232.
- Jongno-gu's walking route B includes it as the "silver cinema (Hollywood Theater)". Most of the audience is elderly, so **ask before taking photos inside.**

Note that **the Seoul Art Cinema (Cinematheque) is no longer here.** After the Hollywood Theater and Seoul Cinema, it moved in **March 2022 to Kyunghyang Art Hill in Jeong-dong** (Sports Kyunghyang). Older guides still put it in Nakwon Arcade.

South of the arcade, the **roughly 100 m from Tapgol Park's north gate to Nakwon Arcade** was redesigned by the city in 2016 as **"Rakhui-geori"**, an older-people-friendly street (Mediaus), with old-style lettered signs, large-print menus and walking-stick racks. Tapgol Park is in [things to do in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## What we got wrong

**First, "62 of the 75 hanok are shops" misread the statistics.** The bracketed number in the fire-service table is the **number of shops**, not of buildings; in the same table Insadong has 30 buildings and 40 shops. The 13 dwellings are **households**, so they can't simply be subtracted from 75. And the figures cover only **the block around 166 Ikseon-dong**, not the whole neighbourhood.

**Second, "you can walk all the lanes in 30 minutes" had no source.** With Seosulla-gil, Gugak-ro and Nakwon Arcade, the area easily fills half a day or more, so we removed it.

**Third, we presented Donhwamun simply as "a Treasure within walking distance".** **The gate is closed for repairs until July 2027 and the palace entrance is Geumhomun.**

**Fourth, we gave Jongmyo only as "220 m from Exit 8".** We left out the **Tuesday closing**, timed entry on Monday, Thursday and Friday, and the free days in October–November, and didn't mention that **there is no gate into Jongmyo from Seosulla-gil**.

**Fifth, we missed Seosulla-gil's car-free weekends**: **10:00–22:00 on Saturdays and Sundays** since 20 December 2025.

**Sixth, we skipped the free venues on Gugak-ro** (the folk-music museum and Urisori Library) and the theatre's programme. We had copied the names on the district's walking route without checking hours or prices.

**Seventh, the photos didn't match the neighbourhood.** The cover was a platform at Jongno 3-ga Station and the body photo a hanok room in the National Folk Museum. They are now the Ikseon-dong lanes, the Gugak-ro venues and the old Dansungsa.

## Quick summary

- **Seosulla-gil**: 820 m along Jongmyo's west wall, **car-free Saturday and Sunday 10:00–22:00**. No gate into the shrine.
- **Jongmyo**: closed Tuesdays, timed entry Mon/Thu/Fri, **free on Wednesdays from 7 October and 29 October–15 November.**
- **Gugak-ro (Donhwamun-ro)**: **Folk Music Museum free (closed Mon)**, **Urisori Library free (closed Sun)**, **several free October concerts at Seoul Donhwamun Traditional Theater and the free courtyard show on 17 October.**
- **Seoul Jewelry Showroom**: 83 Seosulla-gil, Tue–Sat 11:00–19:00.
- **Nakwon Arcade**: about 300 instrument shops on floors 2–3; **Hollywood Classic** on the 4th, ₩2,000 for 55+.
- **Site of Dansungsa**: Korea's first film screened here on 27 October 1919; now the Dansung Gold Building.
- The old headline **"62 of 75 hanok are shops" misread the data**: the block around 166 Ikseon-dong has 75 hanok, 62 shops and 13 homes.
