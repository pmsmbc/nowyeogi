Most people do Jeonju as a day trip, and most people are wrong about that. The Hanok Village is a working district that fills with visitors between eleven and five and empties afterwards, and the evening is the better half.

Staying the night is the single decision that changes this trip. Where you stay matters less.

This guide does not quote nightly rates. Jeonju's accommodation is dominated by small independent operators who do not publish prices, and the city's own directory lists none.

## Hanok stays inside the village

The distinctive option, and the one to take if the point of the trip is the village.

These are **traditional houses converted to guest accommodation**, usually a room or two opening onto a courtyard, with ondol floor heating and often floor bedding rather than a bed. The city runs a **dedicated booking portal** for them, and the Hanok Village's own site links to it.

Properties listed on the official portal include **Gaeunchae**, **Gallery Stay**, **Gyodong Myeongga**, **Gyodong Sallae**, **Gwigeoraesa** and **Geumwondang**. The city's wider accommodation directory carries **101 listings** across traditional stays, registered tourist accommodation and other categories, with a filter for pet-friendly properties.

**Suits**: couples, anyone who wants the village at night and early morning, travellers who want the experience rather than the amenities.

**Downsides to expect**: floor sleeping is normal rather than exceptional, walls in a hanok are thin, bathrooms are sometimes shared, and many operators take bookings by phone. Rooms are also small by hotel standards. None of this is a complaint about quality, it is simply what a converted traditional house is.

## Near Jeonju Station

The practical option. Jeonju Station is about **25 minutes from the village by bus** on routes 112, 79, 508, 536, 542, 545, 546, 101, 119 and 999, or a **₩7,000 to ₩8,000 taxi**.

Book here if you have an early train, if you want a conventional hotel room with a proper bed and a lift, or if you are travelling with luggage that would be awkward in a hanok courtyard.

**Downsides**: you commute to everything, and the area around the station is ordinary.

## Downtown and Gaeksa

**Gaeksa (객사)** is Jeonju's main commercial and nightlife district, north of the Hanok Village. It has the density of restaurants, bars and shops that the village lacks after dark, and it stays open later.

Consider it if you want city amenities at night and a short bus or taxi ride to the sights during the day. It is also where the film festival concentrates in late April and early May.

## Near the express bus terminal

Worth knowing rather than recommending. The **express bus terminal is closer to the Hanok Village than the railway station is**, at about **15 minutes by bus** on routes 79, 999 and 1994, or a ₩5,000 to ₩6,000 taxi.

If you are arriving from Seoul by bus, which is cheaper than the train, this saves a transfer.

## What to check before you book

- **Whether it is a bed or floor bedding.** Many hanok stays use traditional bedding and do not say so prominently.
- **Whether the bathroom is private.** In a converted house it often is not.
- **How the booking is made.** Several village properties take phone bookings only.
- **Heating and air conditioning.** Hanok are cold in January and hot in August.
- **Festival dates.** The Jeonju International Film Festival runs 29 April to 8 May 2026 and fills the city.

## Who should stay where

**One night, here for the village** — a hanok stay inside it. This is the answer for most people.

**Early train out, or travelling with a large suitcase** — near Jeonju Station.

**Want restaurants and bars at night** — Gaeksa.

**Arriving by bus from Seoul** — near the express bus terminal.

## Why the overnight matters

![The lotus pond and wooden bridge at Deokjin Park in Jeonju](/images/jeonju-hotels-2026-09/01.webp)

The Hanok Village has no gate and no closing time. It is open twenty-four hours because it is a residential district, not an attraction.

What that means in practice is that the streets you queued through at two in the afternoon are quiet and lit at nine in the evening, and almost empty at seven in the morning. Day trippers never see either.

Add the **Nambu Market night market**, which runs **Friday and Saturday from 17:00 to 23:00**, and the argument for a Friday or Saturday overnight becomes fairly strong.

## Wrapping up

Book a hanok inside the village for one night, arrive in time for dinner, and walk the streets after the coaches have gone. The room will be small and you will probably sleep on the floor. That is the point.
