There is no place called Hongdae on a Seoul map. The name is short for **Hongik Daehakgyo**, Hongik University, and it covers the streets in front of the campus across four neighbourhoods of Mapo-gu: Seogyo-dong, Donggyo-dong, Sangsu-dong and Hapjeong-dong.

The name has made it into official paperwork twice. On **2 December 2021** the Seoul city government designated the **Hongdae Culture and Arts Special Tourist Zone** (1.13 km²). On **13 May 2023** Mapo-gu launched the **Red Road**, a roughly 2 km route painted red through the district. What makes the story interesting is underneath: the backbone of that red route is a **railway built in 1929 to haul coal**, and the tourist zone was first blocked by **the very artists and shopkeepers it was meant to promote**.

This is the overview post for our Hongdae series. Sights, food and hotels have their own guides: [things to do in Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-things-to-do-2026-09/), [where to eat in Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-food-2026-09/) and [where to stay in Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-hotels-2026-09/). Yeonnam-dong to the north and Mangwon-dong to the west are covered separately in the [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong guide](/en/posts/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/).

## Hongdae at a glance

Here are the four neighbourhoods inside the tourist zone, plus the two next door. Korean neighbourhoods are called *dong*; the name origins come from Mapo-gu's own explanation.

| Neighbourhood | Name origin (Mapo-gu) | What's there for visitors | Nearest station |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Seogyo-dong** | "Lower village of the small bridges" over a branching stream | Walking Street, Eoulmadang-ro, the club strip, most of the Red Road | Hongik Univ., Sangsu |
| **Donggyo-dong** | "Upper village" of the same stream | North side of Hongik Univ. Station, Gyeongui Line Book Street | Hongik Univ. |
| **Sangsu-dong** | The uppermost riverside village, or "the new village" | Side streets around Sangsu Station, toward the Danginri power plant | Sangsu |
| **Hapjeong-dong** | A "clam well" in a village by the old Yanghwa ferry | Around Hapjeong Station, Yanghwajin and Jeoldusan | Hapjeong |
| Yeonnam-dong | Outside the zone | Gyeongui Line Forest Park | See the [Yeonnam guide](/en/posts/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/) |
| Mangwon-dong | Outside the zone | Mangwon Market, Mangwon Hangang Park | See the [Yeonnam guide](/en/posts/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/) |

According to Mapo-gu, Seogyo-dong and Donggyo-dong share one origin. A stream coming down from the Yeonhui-dong valley split into many branches here, crossed by **lots of little bridges** (*jandari* in old Korean). The lower and upper villages became Seogyo-jeong and Donggyo-jeong in a 1936 reorganisation and took their present names in 1946. The street name **Jandari-ro** in Seogyo-dong still carries the old word. Hapjeong was written with the character for "clam" until the colonial era, when it was swapped for an easier one.

## The street that was a railway: the Danginri Line

Hongdae's big pedestrian streets did not start life as streets. This is the history of the **Danginri Line** as told by the Hongik University student paper (September 2020).

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| **20 September 1929** | Rail line opens from Seogang Station to the Danginri power plant, carrying anthracite coal plus some passengers |
| November 1930 | Danginri Unit 1 built by Gyeongseong Electric, 10,000 kW (Hankyung Magazine) |
| Early 1970s | Passenger trains end |
| **1980** | The plant changes fuel, freight stops, and the line closes |
| 1990s | The old track bed becomes car parks |
| Today | The same strip is the **Eoulmadang-ro** area |

There were four stations: Danginri, Bangsongso, Segyori and Seogang. That is why Eoulmadang-ro, today Hongdae's busiest walking street, **runs in one long line south toward the power plant**: it follows the old track.

![The southern stretch of Eoulmadang-ro seen from above on a clear day: shops with blue, red and green awnings line a brick-coloured walkway, and a tall chimney rises against the sky at the far end](/images/hongdae-guide-2026-09/08.webp)

### The power plant went underground

The Danginri plant at the end of the line is still a power plant. Hankyung Magazine (March 2023) describes it as **Korea's first thermal power station**, opened in November 1930. Since November 2019, two large LNG combined-cycle units have run **underground beneath the city**, with a park on top.

The retired Units 4 and 5 are being turned into the **Danginri Cultural Creation Power Station**. A government announcement in March 2024 (Financial News) gave **2026** as the opening target. At the time of writing we could not find an official opening date. Opening details will go into the [things to do in Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-things-to-do-2026-09/) guide once announced.

## One university, one club

### 1955: Hongik University arrives

Korean Wikipedia's history of Hongik University says it opened in Yongsan in 1946, was scattered across several buildings in central Seoul after the Korean War, and **moved to its present hillside campus in April 1955**. "Hongdae-ap", in front of Hongdae, starts there. Even [[line:6]] Sangsu Station carries "Hongik University" as its secondary name.

### 5 April 1995: Club Drug

Hongdae's reputation as Seoul's indie neighbourhood has a date attached. Newsis (March 2025) reports that the music industry treats **a Kurt Cobain memorial gig at Club Drug on 5 April 1995** as the birth of Korean indie. The punk band Crying Nut started on that stage, and in May 1996 "Street Punk Show" gigs were held outdoors in Hongdae and Myeongdong.

What followed, from news reports:

| When | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| March 2001 | **Club Day** starts: one ticket gets you into many clubs | Newsis 2015 |
| January 2011 | Club Day stops after its 117th edition | Newsis 2015 |
| 27 February 2015 | Ten live venues revive it as **Live Club Day** | Newsis 2015 |
| 28 February 2025 | 71st Live Club Day; tickets ₩35,000 (about $26) | Newsis 2025 |

The 2025 report says the event now runs **roughly every two months** rather than monthly, organised by a live-club cooperative, and notes that Hongdae's live venues have been closing or changing use one by one. Venues and busking rules are in [things to do in Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Becoming a tourist zone took five years

Itaewon became Seoul's first Special Tourist Zone in 1997. Hongdae got there much later, and only after **a fight inside the neighbourhood**. (A Special Tourist Zone is a designation under Korea's Tourism Promotion Act that relaxes some regulations and unlocks city funding.)

### 2015: Seoul names the problem

In **November 2015**, Seoul announced a **gentrification action plan** and listed **Sinchon–Hongdae–Hapjeong** as one of six target areas (Etoday). The aim was to slow the rent rises pushing out long-standing shops and artists.

### 2016: "It will lose its character"

The Hankook Ilbo reported on 15 November 2016 that when Mapo-gu prepared to ask Seoul to designate Seogyo-dong, Sangsu-dong and part of Hapjeong-dong as a tourist zone, **about 300 artists formed a committee to oppose it**. The same report said rents around Hongdae rose **5.4%** in the third quarter of that year, and **18.7%** in Hapjeong-dong. Their argument: hotels, duty-free shops and chain stores were already replacing independent businesses, and a tourist zone would finish the job.

### 2021: Seoul's seventh

Mapo-gu drew up a new plan in June 2020 and held residents' briefings. On **2 December 2021 Seoul designated 1.13 km² of Seogyo-dong, Donggyo-dong, Sangsu-dong and Hapjeong-dong as the Hongdae Culture and Arts Special Tourist Zone** (Seoul Shinmun). It was Seoul's **seventh**, after Itaewon; Myeongdong–Namdaemun–Bukchang; Dongdaemun Fashion Town; Jongno–Cheonggye; Jamsil; and Gangnam. An eighth, around the Express Bus Terminal and Sebitseom in Seocho-gu, followed in December 2024 (SBS).

In December 2023, Etoday reported that Mapo-gu had commissioned a study on **extending the zone toward the Yanghwajin foreign missionary cemetery, Mapo Saebit Culture Forest, Mangridan-gil and Mangwon Hangang Park**. We could not find an official notice confirming the extension.

![A Hongdae side street at night with a red stripe painted along the road edge. Ahead is the pink-neon rooftop bar building 'PINK'S' and a phone-case stall; a banner on the building reads, in Korean, 'Tenants and landlords both on the road to ruin'](/images/hongdae-guide-2026-09/03.webp)

Look at the banner on the building in the middle of this photo. It reads, roughly, "**tenants and landlords both on the road to ruin**", above the red stripe of the Red Road. Citing Seoul's commercial-district data, Kukmin Ilbo (March 2024) reported that **converted rents in Seogyo-dong peaked in Q1 2023 at ₩190,553 per 3.3 m²** and that shoppers had drifted to Hapjeong, Yeonnam, Sangsu and Mangwon. That is the background to the tourist zone and the red paint.

## The Red Road: two kilometres of red

### Where it runs

On **13 May 2023** Mapo-gu launched the **Red Road** (Aju Business Daily): about **2 km from Gyeongui Line Forest Park through Hongdae to the Danginri power plant**, divided into numbered sections. It runs in **almost the same direction as the old Danginri railway**.

| Section | Character (as reported) |
| --- | --- |
| R1–R2 | Busking and street performance. Upgrade finished 27 April 2024 |
| R3–R4 | Mixed. R4 hosts the on-site command post at Halloween and year-end |
| R5–R6 | Art and culture |
| R7 | Walkway on toward the Han River |

R1–R2 overlaps the **Hongdae Walking Street**, about 500 m of Eoulmadang-ro that the Seoul city government rebuilt **between October 2016 and July 2017** (Korea Tourism Organization). The 2024 works removed a tiered stage to open up the space, **moved the Mapo Tourist Information Center to R2 (140 Eoulmadang-ro) and added a public toilet** (Asia Economy). **A second information centre and toilet opened at R6 on 10 September 2024** (Seoul&).

### Car-free and scooter-free

**Cars**: according to Seoul Shinmun in December 2023, only **R2–R4 were car-free, Friday to Sunday**, and Mapo-gu was discussing with police a plan to make all of R1–R6 car-free at weekends from 2024. We could not confirm the current days and hours from an official notice. On weekdays, assume cars can use the red-painted road.

**E-scooters**: this one is clear. From **16 May 2025** Seoul and the Seoul Metropolitan Police made the Red Road **Korea's first "scooter-free street"** on a trial basis (Seoul city).

- Hours: **12:00–23:00 every day**
- Covers: e-scooters, self-balancing devices and bikes that can move on motor power alone
- Penalty: **₩30,000 fine and 15 licence points** (₩60,000 and 30 points in school zones)
- **Made permanent from 30 September 2026** (Newspim)

If you rode a shared scooter to Hongdae, park and end the rental before you reach the Red Road.

## Crowd control at Halloween and year-end

Since the Itaewon crowd crush of October 2022, Mapo-gu runs a separate crowd-safety operation in Hongdae every Halloween and year-end. Reported figures:

| When | Peak crowd at one time | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25 December 2024 | 134,000 | Cited by Herald Economy in October 2025 |
| **31 October 2025 (Halloween)** | **140,000** | Intensive response 20:00–05:00 |
| 20 December 2025 | 120,000 | Year-end staff raised from 770 to 820 |
| 31 December 2025 | 88,000 | Operation extended to 04:00 on 1 January |

The 2025 Halloween operation, from Herald Economy and Hankyung:

- From **24 October** into early November, with street checks every night 20:00–01:00
- 226 district officials, 120 safety stewards and 270 volunteers; **1,804 people in total** including police and fire services
- **An on-site command post and first-aid station at Red Road R4**, **AI crowd-density monitoring at nine points**, and five emergency message boards
- **Exit 9 of Hongik Univ. Station** and the club strip were the priority zones
- Outcome: no serious incidents; 140 illegal-parking, 100 personal-mobility and 48 pavement-obstruction cases dealt with

As of 1 October 2026 the plan for this year's Halloween has not been announced. On the last weekend of October and on December weekend nights, **using [[line:6]] Sangsu or Hapjeong instead of Exit 9** is the easy way to avoid the worst of it.

## A route that works

Walk from Hongik Univ. Station in the north toward Danginri in the south, **following the Red Road's section numbers**. You will be tracing the old railway the whole way.

1. **Late morning** — Exit 6 for the Gyeongui Line Book Street, then round to Eoulmadang-ro near Exit 9. Pick up a map and event info at the R2 Mapo Tourist Information Center.
2. **Afternoon** — Buskers on the Walking Street (R1–R2), then south through R3–R4. Busking rules and venues are in [things to do in Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-things-to-do-2026-09/).
3. **Evening** — Dinner in the lanes near Sangsu or around Hapjeong. How to choose is in [where to eat in Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-food-2026-09/).
4. **Night** — Clubs and live venues. The Exit 9 area is a crowd-management zone on weekend nights, so consider Sangsu or Hapjeong for the trip back.

## Summary

- **Hongdae is not an official place name** but the area in front of Hongik University across Seogyo, Donggyo, Sangsu and Hapjeong. The university arrived in April 1955.
- The Eoulmadang-ro area sits on the **Danginri Line, a coal railway to the power plant from 1929 to 1980**.
- **Club Drug, 5 April 1995** is counted as the start of Korean indie; Club Day (2001–2011) returned as Live Club Day in 2015.
- **Tourist zone**: about 300 artists objected in 2016; **designated 2 December 2021, 1.13 km², Seoul's seventh**.
- **Red Road**: launched 13 May 2023, **about 2 km from Gyeongui Line Forest Park to the Danginri power plant**, with information centres at R2 and R6.
- **Scooter-free street**: 12:00–23:00 daily, ₩30,000 fine, **permanent from 30 September 2026**.
- In 2025 Hongik Univ. Station ([[line:2]]) averaged **153,298** entries and exits a day; Halloween peaked at **140,000** people. On busy nights, use Sangsu or Hapjeong.

In one line: **Hongdae is a coal railway that became car parks and then a walking street, and now that it has been painted red and named a tourist zone, its biggest job is moving the crowds through safely.**
