Hokkaido looks compact on a map of Japan, but it is a big island. Sapporo to Hakodate takes close to four hours by limited express, and Furano and Biei are about two and a half hours out. For a first trip, the plan that works best is simple: **base yourself in Sapporo**, do Otaru as a day trip, then add either Furano and Biei or Hakodate depending on the season. This guide collects the numbers you need to make that call. Every fare and opening time below is as of November 2026, taken from JR Hokkaido and the official pages of each attraction and city. Prices are in yen; at roughly ¥150 to the US dollar, ¥1,000 is about $6.70.

## Getting there: New Chitose is the gateway

Most international flights land at **New Chitose Airport**, south of Sapporo. From Seoul Incheon, Korean Air, Asiana, Jin Air, Jeju Air, T'way, Eastar Jet and Parata Air all fly nonstop in about 2.5 to 2.8 hours. Hokkaido Airports says the Incheon route alone runs 91 round trips a week at this winter's peak (October 25, 2026 to March 27, 2027), and Air Premia has announced a four-times-weekly service from December 2, 2026. From Busan, Air Busan, T'way, Jin Air and Eastar Jet take about 2 hours 20 to 35 minutes.

**Hakodate Airport**, in the south, currently has no flights from Korea, but Jeju Air plans to restart a daily Incheon service on December 1, 2026 (leaving Incheon 13:10, arriving 15:30). That makes an open-jaw trip possible: fly into Hakodate and out of Sapporo. The shuttle bus from Hakodate Airport toward Hakodate Station takes about 20 minutes and costs ¥700.

## From New Chitose to Sapporo and Otaru

Take the JR **Rapid Airport** train from the station under the terminal. Sapporo Station is ¥1,230 one way for adults, 36 minutes on the fastest special rapid and about 37 on a regular rapid. In the daytime there are six trains an hour, so you rarely need a timetable. With heavy bags, the reserved u-seat car costs ¥2,230. Some of the same trains run through to Otaru: ¥2,040 and as little as 73 minutes from the airport.

Sapporo's subway starts at ¥210 for adults (up to ¥380 depending on distance). A transit IC card works on JR, the Sapporo subway and Hakodate's trams alike.

Whether a **JR pass** pays off depends on your route. Bought abroad in advance, the Hokkaido Rail Pass costs ¥22,000 for 5 days, and the 4-day Sapporo–Furano Area Pass, which covers Otaru, Asahikawa, Furano and Biei, costs ¥11,000. A single Sapporo–Hakodate return by limited express already costs ¥19,540, so if Hakodate is on your list, the 5-day pass comes out ahead. Since March 2026 every JR Hokkaido limited express is reserved-seat only, so pass holders still need to book a seat.

## Where to stay, and the new lodging tax

First-timers do well between **Sapporo Station and Odori**. Airport trains and long-distance expresses leave from Sapporo Station, and underground walkways link it to Odori and Susukino, which helps in winter. If you stay a night in Hakodate, the area **around Hakodate Station**, next to the morning market, keeps your walks short.

Since April 1, 2026, Hokkaido has charged an **accommodation tax** per person per night, combining a prefectural and a city portion:

| Room rate per person | Sapporo | Otaru | Hakodate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under ¥20,000 | ¥300 | ¥300 | ¥200 |
| ¥20,000–49,999 | ¥400 | ¥400 | ¥400 |
| ¥50,000 and up | ¥1,000 | ¥700 | ¥1,000 (¥2,500 from ¥100,000) |

Most hotels collect it separately at check-in, so the total may differ from your booking screen.

## Sapporo: Odori Park and the night view

![The red steel Sapporo TV Tower rising beyond flowerbeds in Odori Park on a clear day](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/14.webp)

**Odori Park** runs east to west through the middle of the city, with the **Sapporo TV Tower** at its eastern end. The observation deck costs ¥1,200 for adults and ¥600 for elementary and junior high students, open 9:00–22:00 (last entry 21:50). The classic shot is the long view west down the park.

![Front of the Sapporo Clock Tower, a white wooden building with a red roof and clock](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/21.webp)

A few blocks north is the **Sapporo Clock Tower**, a school building from the city's pioneer era. Entry is ¥350 for adults and free for high school students and younger with student ID. It opens 8:45–17:10 (last entry 17:00) and closes January 1–3.

For the night view, go up **Mt. Moiwa**. The ropeway plus a mini cable car to the summit costs ¥2,100 return for adults, or ¥1,400 for the ropeway only. Hours are 10:30–22:00 from April to November and 11:00–22:00 from December to March, with the last ride up at 21:30.

Split your eating between morning and evening. **Nijo Market**, about 7 minutes on foot from Odori Station, opens 7:00–17:00 (varies by shop), which makes it a natural breakfast stop for a seafood rice bowl. Evenings center on **Susukino**, the nightlife district to the south. The **Sapporo Beer Museum** now charges even for self-guided visits (¥1,000 for adults, 11:00–18:00). Tasting glasses are ¥450–550, and a guided tour with two tastings is ¥2,000.

![Snow sculptures and crowds in Odori Park during the Snow Festival, seen from above](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/24.webp)

In winter, try to time your trip for the **Sapporo Snow Festival**. The 77th edition runs February 4–11, 2027, at three sites: Odori, Susukino and Tsudome. Hotels fill early, so book ahead.

## Otaru: the canal and Sakaimachi-dori

![Visitors walking along Otaru Canal past stone warehouses on a sunny day](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/03.webp)

Otaru is 33 to 46 minutes from Sapporo by JR for ¥800, an easy half or full day out. **Otaru Canal** is 8 to 10 minutes on foot from JR Otaru Station. Old stone warehouses line its 1,140 meters, and 63 gas lamps are lit from dusk until midnight.

![Otaru Canal in the evening with gas lamps lit and brick warehouses reflected in the water](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/01.webp)

South of the canal, **Sakaimachi-dori** is lined with glass workshops and sweet shops. Near its far end, the **Otaru Music Box Museum** main building is open 9:00–18:00 (upper floors until 17:45) with no regular closing day. For lunch, many visitors head to **Sushi-ya Dori**, a street with about 15 sushi restaurants. A good order is to walk the streets by day and come back to the canal as the lamps go on.

## Hakodate: the ropeway night view and the morning market

![Night view from Mt. Hakodate of the city lights pinched between two bays](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/06.webp)

From Sapporo, the **Hokuto** limited express takes roughly 3.5 to 4 hours, at ¥9,770 one way with a reserved seat, so plan on staying overnight rather than doing a day trip. Hakodate's signature sight is the **night view from Mt. Hakodate**, where the city's lights narrow into a waist between two bays.

The **Mt. Hakodate Ropeway** reaches the summit in about 3 minutes. A return ticket is ¥1,800 for adults (¥1,200 one way), and it runs from 10:00. The last ride up is 21:30 from April 20 to September 30 and 20:30 from October 1 to April 19. From November 9, 2026 to April 12, 2027, the mountain road is closed to buses and cars, so in winter the ropeway is the only way up. The official site posts the day's sunset time, the time it calls "night view time" and live crowd levels; check before you go and aim to ride up about 30 minutes before sunset.

![A lane of seafood stalls under red awnings at Hakodate Morning Market](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/23.webp)

Next morning, **Hakodate Morning Market** is a minute's walk from JR Hakodate Station. Stalls open around 5:00 from May to December and around 6:00 from January to April; hours vary by shop, but the market is open every day.

![The star-shaped moat and green grounds of Goryokaku Park seen from Goryokaku Tower](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/18.webp)

In the afternoon, ride up **Goryokaku Tower** (¥1,200 for adults, 9:00–18:00) to look down on the star-shaped fort of **Goryokaku**. Down by the harbor in **Motomachi**, pair the straight slope of **Hachimanzaka**, a minute from Suehiro-cho tram stop, with the **Old Public Hall of Hakodate Ward** (¥300 for adults). The **city tram** is the easiest way around: ¥250–300 for adults by distance, or ¥800 for a tram-only one-day pass if you ride several times.

## Furano and Biei: lavender and the Blue Pond

![Rows of purple lavender at Farm Tomita's Lavender East with the Daisetsuzan peaks in the distance](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/07.webp)

To reach Furano and Biei, take a limited express from Sapporo to Asahikawa and change to the JR Furano Line. Biei takes 2 hours 24 minutes for ¥6,320, Furano 2 hours 41 minutes for ¥6,760. Local trains alone cut the fare to the ¥3,000 range but take over three hours. The Norokko sightseeing train, which used to stop at a seasonal station right by the lavender fields, retired after its September 2026 runs.

Furano's best-known stop is **Farm Tomita**, a lavender farm about 25 minutes on foot from Naka-Furano Station. It is busiest in summer, when the lavender blooms; November hours are 9:30–16:30.

![Gentle Biei hills striped with sunflowers, wheat fields and woods](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/28.webp)

Biei is known for rolling hills where fields join like a patchwork quilt. Photo spots such as the Ken and Mary Tree and the Seven Stars Tree draw crowds, but **most of these hills are private farmland**. The Biei Tourism Association repeatedly asks visitors to stay off the fields and shoot only from the road. After years of tourists trampling his crops, the farmer who owned the famous Philosophy Tree cut it down in 2016.

![Emerald water and dead tree trunks at Shirogane Blue Pond under a blue sky with white clouds](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/19.webp)

**Shirogane Blue Pond** is about 22 minutes by Dohoku Bus from JR Biei Station. The fare was ¥800 under the October 2024 timetable, and a later timetable change has been announced, so confirm locally. If you drive, parking rose to ¥1,000 for cars in July 2026. Many visitors combine it with nearby **Shirahige Falls**, about 30 meters high.

![Blue Pond glowing blue under night illumination with tree trunks reflected in the water](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/11.webp)

From October 22, 2026 to April 21, 2027, the pond is lit from dusk until 21:00. In midwinter, though, it freezes and is covered with snow, so the summer blue is not visible.

## What to eat: soup curry, jingisukan, ramen and seafood

![Soup curry with a chicken leg, eggplant, carrot, green pepper, potato and boiled egg, with rice](/images/hokkaido-guide-2026-11/26.webp)

According to Hokkaido's tourism organization, **soup curry** began at a Sapporo café in the 1970s, and the city now has more than 200 specialist shops. You choose your spice level and broth when you order. **Jingisukan**, lamb grilled on a domed iron plate, is a local staple. The Sapporo Tourism Association calls Sapporo the birthplace of miso ramen, and Susukino's **Ganso Sapporo Ramen Yokocho** alley packs in about 17 shops. Hakodate is known for clear-broth **shio (salt) ramen**, and Otaru for the sushi on Sushi-ya Dori.

## Practical notes

- **Visas**: Many nationalities, including South Koreans, can enter Japan visa-free for short stays of up to 90 days. Check JNTO for your passport.
- **Visit Japan Web**: Register immigration and customs details in advance and pass through with a QR code.
- **Tax-free shopping changed on November 1, 2026**: You now pay the tax-inclusive price in store, scan your passport at an airport kiosk for customs confirmation when you leave, and the store then refunds the tax. Purchases of ¥5,000 or more (before tax) per store per day qualify, and you must leave Japan within 90 days of purchase.
- **Weather**: Japan Meteorological Agency normals (1991–2020) put Sapporo's January average at −3.2°C and August at 22.3°C. Snow lies on the ground from November to March, with a normal maximum depth of 97 cm. Bring shoes with non-slip soles in winter.
- **Car rental**: A car makes Biei's hills and the Blue Pond much easier, but if you are not used to snow, avoid winter driving and use buses or local tours instead.
- **Basics**: Japan runs on 100V electricity, and there is no time difference with Korea.

## Wrapping up

For a first Hokkaido trip, fly into New Chitose, spend three nights in Sapporo, give Otaru a day, then add Furano and Biei in summer or the Snow Festival and Hakodate in winter. Rather than squeezing in everything, work out the travel times first.
