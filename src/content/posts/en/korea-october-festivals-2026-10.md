October is when Korea holds the most festivals. Pull every event running on at least one day of October 2026 out of the Korea Tourism Organization's festival and events database and you get **more than 200 entries**. This guide picks the twelve that a visitor could actually fit into a trip, and checks dates, fees and access against official sources.

The short answer first. If you can travel outside Seoul for only one, make it the **Jinju Namgang Yudeung (Lantern) Festival, 3–18 October**. If you're staying in Seoul, it's the **Royal Culture Festival in the palaces, 7–11 October**. Everything here is as of 1 October 2026, and organisers can change dates.

## Check this first: October public holidays

There are three public holidays in October 2026.

- **Saturday 3 October** — National Foundation Day (Gaecheonjeol)
- **Monday 5 October** — substitute holiday for National Foundation Day
- **Friday 9 October** — Hangeul Day

Because Foundation Day lands on a Saturday, Monday becomes a day off. Korea's rules on public holidays give the next weekday off when a national day falls on a weekend. Hangeul Day is a Friday, so no substitute there.

That makes **two three-day weekends: 3–5 October and 9–11 October**, and Koreans travel on both. The Jinju lanterns, the Baekje festival and the Punggi ginseng festival **all open on 3 October**, and the King Jeongjo royal procession in Suwon is on **Sunday 4 October**. Book trains and rooms outside Seoul for that first weekend early.

The quieter window is **Monday 12 to Friday 16 October**: the holidays are over, but the Jinju lanterns and the Busan film festival are still running.

## The Seoul fireworks have already happened

Many "October in Korea" lists still put the Seoul International Fireworks Festival on Yeouido in early October. **In 2026 it was held on Saturday 5 September.** The organiser's traffic notice gives the road closure as "5 September 2026 (Sat) 15:00–24:00". Don't plan an October trip around it.

If you want fireworks, two are left: **Yeosu's night-sea fireworks on 31 October** and the **Busan Fireworks Festival on Saturday 7 November**. Seats and booking for Busan are in our [Busan Fireworks Festival guide](/en/posts/busan-fireworks-2026-09/).

## 24 September – 4 October · Andong International Mask Dance Festival

![A troupe in bright costumes dancing on the mask dance stage between two giant mask sculptures, the stands full of people](/images/korea-october-festivals-2026-10/07.webp)

Already under way, and it **ends on Sunday 4 October**. This weekend is the last.

Troupes performing Korea's mask dances, which are on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list, share the grounds with mask dance groups from abroad. This year's theme is "Memory of the Mask, Dance of Everyone". There are Korean and international mask dance performances, open-air madang plays, the World Mask Play Contest, street parades and mask-making and dance workshops.

- **Venues** — Maskdance Park, Jungangseon 1942 Andong Station (the old station site), and the old town (Ungbu Park, Munhwa Park, the food street)
- **Hours** — 10:00–22:00
- **Fee** — free entry; performances in the mask dance hall and some workshops are paid
- **Closing ceremony** — Sunday 4 October at 20:30 on the Jungangseon 1942 main stage, with the final of the mask play contest

The official FAQ says the festival's "Festival Love Coupons" work like cash in participating shops in the old town nearby. Check the official notices for how to get them.

**Getting there** — Andong Station is served by KTX-Eum trains on the Jungang Line, with departures from Cheongnyangni Station in Seoul. Check times and fares on Korail for your date.

## 1–5 October · Ground Forces Festival, Gyeryong

Once a year the Korean Army opens the Gyeryongdae runway. Tanks and self-propelled guns on display and to ride in, special forces parachute jumps, and the **Black Eagles aerobatic team on Saturday 3 October only, 16:30–16:40**.

- **Hours** — 09:00–17:00 (to 21:00 on 1 October only)
- **Fee** — free entry and parking
- **Getting there** — Gyeryong Station on the Honam Line, then a free shuttle bus

Timetables and shuttle routes are in our [Ground Forces Festival guide](/en/posts/gyeryong-ground-forces-festival-2026-10/).

## 1–5 October · Gimje Horizon Festival

A **farming culture festival** centred on Byeokgolje, an ancient reservoir embankment in Gimje. The KTO listing says it is the only festival among the government's honorary representative culture and tourism festivals built around traditional farming, and describes Byeokgolje as Korea's oldest and largest irrigation work.

The highlights are the national farmers' music (nongak) contest, a twin-dragon play based on the Byeokgolje legend, and a tug-of-war for a good harvest. Hands-on activities include cooking rice in an iron pot over a wood fire, a zipline over a paddy-field artwork, and kite flying.

- **Venue** — 442 Byeokgolje-ro, Buryang-myeon, Gimje
- **Hours** — 09:00–21:00
- **Fee** — free (some activities paid)
- **Phone** — 063-540-3172

Byeokgolje is in Buryang-myeon, outside central Gimje. If you're going by public transport, check for a festival shuttle first; we couldn't find an official shuttle notice.

## 3–18 October · Jinju Namgang Yudeung (Lantern) Festival

![Lanterns spread across the whole Namgang River and a riverside promenade packed with visitors](/images/korea-october-festivals-2026-10/02.webp)

The **longest-running festival of the month, and the best one after dark**. Large lanterns float on the Namgang River below Jinju Fortress. Jinju City says it was chosen as one of the Ministry of Culture's **global festivals** for 2026.

The city's account of its origin: during the Imjin War siege of Jinju Fortress in the 1590s, lanterns were floated on the river to signal to the militia outside the walls and to send word to families. From 1949 it was a "lantern contest" inside the Gaecheon Arts Festival, and it became **a festival of its own in 2000**.

- **Venue** — Jinju Fortress and the Namgang River (626 Namgang-ro)
- **Hours** — 13:00–23:00
- **Fee** — free
- **Programme** — lanterns on the water and on land, a drone light show, fireworks over the river, a night parade; hanging a wish lantern, floating your own lantern, crossing the pontoon bridge, river cruises
- **Running alongside** — the **75th Gaecheon Arts Festival, 9–18 October**, and the Korea Drama Festival

Go between **9 and 18 October** and you catch both. The Gaecheon Arts Festival started in 1949 and is described as Korea's first regional all-arts festival.

**Getting there** — high-speed trains run to Jinju Station, but the station is in Gajwa-dong, a different part of town from the fortress. Intercity buses from Seoul Nambu Terminal arrive at Jinju Intercity Bus Terminal on Namgang-ro, close to the festival. More in our [Jinju travel guide](/en/posts/jinju-guide-2026-09/).

## 3–11 October · Baekje Cultural Festival (Gongju and Buyeo)

![Figures of Baekje people, an elephant, a camel and mounted soldiers on floats on the Geum River in Gongju, a pontoon bridge behind](/images/korea-october-festivals-2026-10/12.webp)

This is the **72nd** edition; it traces back to a "Baekje Grand Rite" held in Buyeo in 1955. Baekje was one of Korea's Three Kingdoms, and its two old capitals **each run their own festival grounds on the same dates**.

| | Buyeo | Gongju |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theme | Beautiful Baekje, Shining Sabi | Bronze Mirror: the Light of 1,500-year Baekje |
| Where | Buyeo town centre (Jeongnimsa Temple Site, Seoktap-ro, Gwanbuk-ri) | Geumgang Singwan Park, Mirseom island, Gongsanseong Fortress, old town |
| Hours | 10:00–22:00 | 11:00–22:00 |
| Fee | Free | Free |

Buyeo moves the festival into its **town centre** this year: a ceremony re-enacting the move of the capital to Sabi, historical processions, a night parade through the streets, and media art projected at the Jeongnimsa Temple Site. Gongju takes as its theme a bronze mirror excavated from the tomb of King Muryeong.

**Getting there** — Buyeo has no railway station, so it's a bus trip. Gongju has a KTX station, but it is a long way from town. We couldn't confirm bus routes and times from Seoul in official sources. If you want both towns, check the connection between them too.

## 4–11 October · Suwon Hwaseong Cultural Festival

![The walls of Hwaseong Fortress in Suwon lit at night, with a painted pavilion on top](/images/korea-october-festivals-2026-10/25.webp)

In 1795 King Jeongjo travelled to Hwaseong for eight days to mark his mother Lady Hyegyeong's 60th birthday. The festival recreates that journey from the court record of it, around the UNESCO-listed Hwaseong Fortress and Hwaseong Haenggung Palace.

The highlight is the **King Jeongjo Royal Procession on Sunday 4 October**, staged jointly by Seoul, Gyeonggi Province, Suwon and Hwaseong. The Seoul leg starts with a departure ceremony at **Gyeongbokgung**; the Suwon leg is a parade through the fortress city, and the Hwaseong leg has modern and traditional sections.

- **Hours** — 12:00–21:00
- **Fee** — free; some pre-booked programmes are paid
- **For visitors from abroad** — a "Global Village" with a foreigners' lounge and traditional games and tea
- **Note** — the KTO listing says there are **no food stalls inside the festival grounds**; eat at nearby restaurants

KTO lists the festival as 4–11 October, but the official site's timetable already has programmes on **Saturday 3 October**. If you're going on the 3rd, check that day's schedule on the site. Traffic closures for the procession are posted on the site in English, Chinese and Japanese.

**Getting there** — Suwon Station is on [[line:1]] and [[line:suinbundang]], and KTX trains stop there too. Hwaseong Haenggung is some way from the station, so take a bus or taxi for the last stretch.

## 6–15 October · Busan International Film Festival (BIFF)

The **31st** edition, ten days centred on the **Busan Cinema Center** in Centum City.

Ticket prices from the 2026 ticket catalogue:

| Type | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Regular screenings, Cine Class | ₩10,000 (about $7.50) |
| Actors' House, Master Class | ₩15,000 |
| Midnight Passion | ₩20,000 |
| Opening and closing ceremonies | ₩30,000 |

- **Booking** — regular seats are sold **online at biff.kr**, 17 September to 15 October, up to two tickets per screening.
- **At the venue** — only seats that didn't sell out online, or returns, are sold at BIFF ticket booths during the festival, and only for that day's and the next day's regular screenings.
- **Entry** — no entry from 15 minutes after the start. Screenshots of mobile tickets and booking text messages are not accepted.
- **Cancelling** — up to 60 minutes before the screening; free up to six days before.

**Getting there** — the Busan Cinema Center is near Centum City Station on [[line:busan2]]. The rest of Busan's October line-up is in our [Busan autumn festivals guide](/en/posts/busan-festivals-2026-09/).

## 7–11 October · Royal Culture Festival, Seoul

![Gyeonghoeru Pavilion at Gyeongbokgung at night, its lit stone pillars mirrored in the pond](/images/korea-october-festivals-2026-10/27.webp)

The easiest October festival if you're based in Seoul. Run by the Korea Heritage Service and the Korea Heritage Agency, it's billed as **Korea's biggest heritage festival**, now in its **12th year**, with the theme "The Palace Awakens the Arts".

- **Venues** — Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, Changgyeonggung and Jongmyo Shrine
- **Fee** — each palace's normal ticket. Gyeongbokgung is ₩3,000, and **free if you wear hanbok**
- **Eve concert** — Tuesday 6 October, 17:00, at Heungnyemun Gate square in Gyeongbokgung: a special edition of the KBS2 music show *Immortal Songs*. Watching is free; audience places are applied for on the KBS website
- **Bookable by foreigners only** — the Gyeongbokgung hanbok banquet, and "Waking the Palace in the Morning"
- **Walk-in programmes** — a palace games room, processions, a stamp tour, a time-travel programme at Changgyeonggung and more

The spring edition this year drew a record of about 725,000 visitors. The autumn edition adds more exhibitions, tours and workshops by day and performances against the palace halls by night.

## Week by week

**Week 1 (1–5 Oct, three-day weekend)** — the last weekend of Andong's mask dances; Gyeryong and Gimje close; Jinju and Baekje open. The royal procession is on the 4th. The busiest week for regional festivals.

**Week 2 (6–11 Oct, Hangeul Day weekend)** — BIFF opens, the palaces host the Royal Culture Festival, and the Gaecheon Arts Festival joins the Jinju lanterns. Baekje and Suwon close on the 11th.

**Week 3 (12–18 Oct)** — weekdays after the holidays. A good time for the Jinju lanterns (close 18th) and BIFF (closes 15th) with smaller crowds. Seoul Design Week, Wow Book and Ganggyeong overlap at the weekend.

**Week 4 (19–25 Oct)** — the Silver Grass Festival (to the 23rd), Gangneung coffee, Gwangju kimchi; Seoul Design Week runs to the 25th.

**Last week (26–31 Oct)** — fewer big festivals, fireworks in Yeosu on the 31st, and the start of proper autumn colour. The Korea Forest Service forecasts peak foliage at Seoraksan on 20 October and Songnisan on 28 October; see our [Korea autumn foliage guide](/en/posts/autumn-foliage-2026-09/) and [Seoul autumn foliage guide](/en/posts/seoul-autumn-foliage-2026-09/).

## Practical notes

- **Book trains for the holiday weekends first.** Regional festivals cluster on 3–5 and 9–11 October. Since 1 September 2026, SRT has been merged into KTX under one operator; book on Korail.
- **Bring money to "free" festivals.** Entry is free; workshops, food and some shows are not. Some grounds, like Suwon's, have no food stalls at all.
- **Many of these are night festivals.** Jinju runs to 23:00 and Baekje to 22:00. You'll be standing outdoors for hours, so bring a layer.
- **Watch Monday closures.** 5 October is a public holiday, but whether museums open on a holiday Monday varies. In Jinju, the National Jinju Museum and the Lantern Exhibition Hall close on Mondays.
- **About "culture and tourism festival" status.** Korea's Ministry of Culture designates selected regional festivals for support. We mention it only where an official source states it (Gimje as an honorary representative festival, Jinju as a global festival).

## Wrapping up

October in Korea is a month you can plan around festival dates alone. In short:

- **One trip out of Seoul** — the Jinju lanterns, 3–18 October; go between the 9th and 18th to add the Gaecheon Arts Festival.
- **Staying in Seoul** — the Royal Culture Festival 7–11 October, Seoul Design Week 14–25 October, the Silver Grass Festival 17–23 October.
- **Pairing with Busan** — BIFF, 6–15 October. Jinju and Busan a day apart is a realistic plan.
- **Last chance this weekend** — the Andong Mask Dance Festival (to 4 October), the Ground Forces Festival and Gimje Horizon Festival (to 5 October).
