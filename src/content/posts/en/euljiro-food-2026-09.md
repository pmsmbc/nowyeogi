Two eating neighbourhoods overlap in Euljiro: **restaurants more than 70 years old**, and **outdoor street tables** set out on the road.

Only one of the two appears on the government lists. Let me start with why.

![A heaped plate of whelks dressed in chilli-powder sauce with shredded spring onion, two coils of thin noodles beside it](/images/euljiro-food-2026-09/05.webp)

## The nogari alley

**The city designated it Seoul Future Heritage in 2015**, certificate number 2015-029. This is the original text of the designation.

> The Euljiro nogari alley took shape from the 1980s, and the number of customers coming to it grew explosively in the wake of the IMF financial crisis.

> In the nogari alley, where some ten beer halls sit together, the crowds in the evening are such that an empty seat is hard to find even at the outdoor tables. **When customers sit down, draught beer and nogari come out by the head count without anything being ordered.**

That last sentence matters. **You sit down and the drink and the snack arrive before you order.** That is how this alley works, and it can take a first-timer by surprise.

**Nogari is a juvenile pollack.** That is the Standard Korean Dictionary definition; it is dried, grilled and served with beer.

The alley is **around 19 Euljiro 13-gil in Jung-gu**, **3 to 5 minutes on foot** from [[line:2]] Euljiro 3-ga Station.

The **"some ten beer halls" in the designation text is a count from the 2015 survey**. No official figure for the number today could be found.

### The tables on the road are permitted

This is not a practice that gets quietly overlooked. There are two official documents.

- The Jung-gu street environment division's **road-occupancy permit plan for the Euljiro nogari alley and others** (2021)
- The Seoul city pedestrian environment division's **notice of street-stall inspection results and requested measures for the Euljiro nogari alley** (2024)

**The district office manages it through road-occupancy permits and the city inspects it.** The body of both documents is closed, so the permitted area and conditions could not be confirmed, but it is clearly something the administration keeps an eye on.

Which means **there may be days with outdoor tables and days without.**

## Why no beer hall appears on the government lists

This is the interesting part.

**The nogari alley is not among the seven restaurant streets designated by Jung-gu.** The golbaengi alley is. **It is not among the ten designated shopping streets either.**

The reason is in the rules. This is the exclusion clause on the Jung-gu page for the "model restaurant" designation.

> Businesses excluded from applying: **businesses dealing mainly in alcohol, such as beer halls and soju bars**, and businesses handling objectionable foodstuffs

**A business led by alcohol is not eligible to apply in the first place.** It is not about the food being poor. The scheme rules them out.

The city takes a different view, though. This is how it described Euljiro in a press release in August 2026.

> Euljiro ... its distinctive industrial landscape of densely packed print shops, ironworks and workshops joined by cafes, craft studios and cultural spaces, has acquired **the nickname "Hipjiro"** and is drawing attention as one of Seoul's leading cultural and tourist destinations.

> In particular, **with a food trade and a culture of outdoor street tables forming around the golbaengi alley and the nogari alley**, it has emerged as an inner-city night-tourism destination popular with younger people and foreign visitors.

**"Hipjiro" is a word the city uses in official press releases.** But this is a mention as the object of policy, not a designation.

**Andongjang is the oldest Chinese restaurant in Seoul.** It opened in 1948 and is in its third generation; the "Andong" of the name comes from a place name in Shandong, China.

The note on **Wooraeok** is a good one.

> The restaurant is said to have been renamed Wooraeok because the family **"came back again"** after fleeing during the Korean War.

**Sunheungok serves only two things**, braised oxtail and oxtail soup. It opened in 1949.

**Munhwaok** is one of the rare cases where an official city document includes directions.

> Come out of exit 4 at Euljiro 4-ga and walk a little way and you will see a sign reading "Munhwaok" in **red lettering on a yellow ground**.

### Oraegage

There is also the **Oraegage certification**, which the city extended to food in 2024. Twelve places in Jung-gu were selected, and the Euljiro ones among them include **Ojangdong Hamheung Naengmyeon, Buminok and La Cantina**.

## Know the difference between the two naengmyeon

![Cold buckwheat noodles in clear broth in a brass bowl, topped with sliced boiled beef and half a boiled egg](/images/euljiro-food-2026-09/02.webp)

Confuse these two in Euljiro and you will end up eating something other than what you meant to.

**Pyongyang naengmyeon** is buckwheat noodles in a clear meat broth. The description at Wooraeok puts it exactly.

> Into a broth made by simmering whole cuts of Korean beef cow rump and leg for four or five hours, **buckwheat noodles and nothing else at all** are placed

Many people find it bland. That is what it is supposed to taste like.

![Cold noodles in a brass bowl under a thick spoonful of red sauce](/images/euljiro-food-2026-09/01.webp)

**Hamhung naengmyeon** is a different thing. The noodles are chewy, drawn from potato and sweet potato starch, and you **mix them with a red sauce**; many places top them with raw skate. Ojang-dong is where that is done.

This is Jung-gu's official account.

> Starting with Heungnamjip, which opened in 1952, the number grew to **as many as six restaurants in the 1970s, but at present Heungnamjip, Ojang Naengmyeon and Sinchang Myeonok** are the ones receiving customers.

**Six down to three** is a decline the district office itself records.

**An americano at 1,800 won** (about $1.35), in the middle of central Seoul.

### It is a floor, not an average

An important caveat. The condition for the "good price" designation is **an item priced below the average for the surrounding trading area**. By definition, only places cheaper than their neighbours get on it.

So it is right to say **you can eat for this much in Euljiro**, and wrong to say **this is what Euljiro costs on average**. Pyongyang naengmyeon at an old restaurant does not cost this.

**No comparison with past prices was possible.** The database has no function for retrieving prices as of an earlier date.

## Some places are already gone

This may be the most important part of the post.

**Euljiro is in the middle of redevelopment**, and there are places still named on official lists that are no longer trading. Only what can be confirmed in city documents is set down here.

**Sangol Makguksu, Euljiro main branch** - this is written into the Seoul Future Heritage text itself.

> **Closed during 2018 when works dragged on during a refurbishment because of a cultural heritage survey** and ... the Euljiro main branch is making preparations to open again.

**On the official city record it has been closed since 2018**, and when it might reopen is unconfirmed.

**A business described as the original of the nogari alley** - there is a record from 2022 of a petition requesting a meeting with the mayor being handled through three separate city departments. It was a dispute over surrender of the building. The body of the document is closed so the outcome could not be confirmed, but **it is not in a state where it can be written up as a place that will be there when you arrive.**

### Seoul Future Heritage does not protect a business

This is easy to misunderstand, and the city has stated it outright.

> **The Seoul Future Heritage conservation programme does not establish a regulatory mechanism for the uniform preservation of heritage.**

> Future heritage ... is preserved voluntarily by the owner, which **distinguishes it from cultural properties whose preservation is made mandatory by law.**

What the designation actually amounts to is **a certificate, and a plaque fixed to the building if the owner agrees**. It has no power under the scheme to protect a tenant or to stop redevelopment.

So this post does not suggest that you go with a particular restaurant as your target. **Go with an alley as your target, and go into whatever is open that day.**

## Things worth knowing

**Every restaurant and bar is entirely non-smoking**, since 1 January 2015, whatever the floor area. Outdoor tables are no exception.

**There is no tipping.** The price on the menu is the final amount, VAT included.

**Refusing a card payment is illegal.** That said, at a very old place the terminal may not accept a foreign-issued card. It is safer to carry a little cash.

## What could not be verified

**The current number of businesses in the nogari alley could not be confirmed.** The only official figure is the "some ten" from the 2015 designation.

**No comparison with past "good price" figures was possible.** The database has no retrieval by reference date.

**Bangsan Market is widely known as a market for baking and confectionery supplies, but Jung-gu's official material lists it as a "printing and packaging" market.** No official basis for the baking supplies could be found, so it is not written here.

**The specific area and conditions of the road-occupancy permits** could not be confirmed either, as the document is closed.

## Wrapping up

- **The nogari alley is Seoul Future Heritage 2015-029.** Sit down and the beer and nogari arrive without an order.
- **The outdoor tables are subject to road-occupancy permits**, and the city inspects them.
- **No beer hall appears on the government restaurant lists because the scheme excludes alcohol-led businesses.**
- **Pyongyang naengmyeon is clear and mild; Hamhung naengmyeon is red and chewy.** Ojang-dong is the Hamhung kind.
- **On the "good price" list, an americano is 1,800 won and a stew 7,000 won.** That is a floor, though.
- **Some places on the official lists have closed.** Aim at alleys, not shops.
