If Haeundae is the Busan of today, Nampo-dong (남포동) is where Busan became Busan. A market that grew out of the 1945 liberation, a bridge where Korean War refugees went looking for lost relatives, and the bank building of the wartime capital all sit within a 20-minute walk of each other. This guide is only about **the order to walk them in from Nampo Station**. Gamcheon, Huinnyeoul and Taejongdae themselves are covered in our [Busan things-to-do guide](/en/posts/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/), so here you get just the directions. Hours and prices are as of October 2026, from official listings and dated news reports.

- **Saturday 14:00** is the only timed event in this neighbourhood. If your trip includes a Saturday, plan backwards so you are at Jagalchi or the bridge at that hour.

## Getting from Busan Station to Nampo-dong

From Busan Station on [[line:busan1]], Nampo is **two stops** (via Jungang), about 5 minutes on the train. Jagalchi is one stop further, and Toseong, the station for Gamcheon, is one more.

Walking is a real option. From the station plaza, follow Jungang-daero south past the **40 Steps** and you reach Nampo-dong in about 30 minutes. If you have no luggage and the weather is fine, this is a better first impression of the old town than the subway. More on the 40 Steps below.

## Start at Nampo Station: BIFF Square, Gukje Market, Kkangtong Market

Come up from Nampo Station and you are on **BIFF Square**, a 428-metre pedestrian street named after the Busan International Film Festival, which held its first edition here in 1996. Film-maker handprints are set into the pavement. The festival has since moved to Centum City, but the street food stayed, including the **ssiat hotteok** (seed-stuffed sweet pancake) stalls.

One block north is **Gukje Market**. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, it began right after liberation in 1945 as a trading ground for goods left behind by departing Japanese residents and brought in by returning Koreans; after the war, US military supplies flowed in too, and it earned the nickname "the market that has everything". Today each alley has its own trade: vintage clothing, cosmetics, a food alley, homewares. The 2014 Korean film "Ode to My Father" (국제시장) is set here. Shops generally run **09:00 to 20:00** and the market closes on the **first and third Sundays**.

Attached to Gukje on the west is **Bupyeong Kkangtong Market**, whose name ("tin-can market") comes from the US army canned goods that flowed through it after the war. By day, 08:00 to 20:00, it is an ordinary covered market. After dark it becomes the **Kkangtong Night Market**: stalls open from **around 19:30** and run late. Closing times differ between listings, with about 23:30 the most common, so arriving before 21:00 is the safe bet. Fish cakes, stuffed tofu pouches and Busan's bibim-dangmyeon (seasoned glass noodles) are all here in one row. The stalls prefer **cash**.

![Inside the Bupyeong Kkangtong Market arcade, shop signs in rows under a colourful roof with shoppers walking through](/images/nampo-things-to-do-2026-10/09.webp)

## Bosu-dong Book Alley

Cross Daecheong-ro from Kkangtong Market and you are in **Bosu-dong Book Alley**. When Busan became the temporary capital during the Korean War, a refugee couple started selling used books from a cardboard mat here; students walking to tent schools on the nearby hillside passed through daily, and more bookshops followed. It is the oldest second-hand bookshop street in Korea, and each shop specialises, in old volumes, textbooks, comics or foreign-language books.

The alley holds a small **Book Alley Culture Center** and a children's library. Shop hours vary house by house, so **go in daylight**. A sign at the entrance tells the story of the Good Books Cooperative founded here in 1978, a root of Busan's democracy movement.

![Entrance to Bosu-dong Book Alley, stacks of used books outside a shop and a small photo studio](/images/nampo-things-to-do-2026-10/22.webp)

## Busan Modern and Contemporary History Museum: free, closed Mondays

Five minutes east along Daecheong-ro from the book alley is the **Busan Modern and Contemporary History Museum**, split across two buildings.

- The **annex** (104 Daecheong-ro) was built in 1929 as the Busan branch of the Oriental Development Company, a colonial-era institution. After liberation it housed US forces and then the American Cultural Center, was returned to Korea in 1999 and served as the Busan Modern History Museum from 2003. It reopened as the annex in March 2023 as a combined reading room and archive.
- The **main hall** (112 Daecheong-ro) is the **former Bank of Korea Busan branch**. The permanent exhibition on floors 3 and 4 covers Busan's modern and contemporary history, floor 2 holds special exhibitions, and floor 1 has the bank archive and a cafe.

The museum lists **09:00 to 18:00 with last entry at 17:00**, closed **Mondays** (the following day if Monday is a holiday) and 1 January, and it is **free**. Exhibit texts carry English, Chinese and Japanese alongside Korean, which makes it the first stop to recommend to overseas visitors. Seeing the port opening, the colonial years and the refugee-capital era in one pass changes how you look at the bridge and the 40 Steps afterwards.

![Permanent exhibition at the Busan Modern and Contemporary History Museum with black-and-white photos of the wartime refugee capital](/images/nampo-things-to-do-2026-10/15.webp)

## Yongdusan Park and Busan Tower

The hill directly above the museum is **Yongdusan Park**. An **escalator** from the Gwangbok-ro shopping street carries you up, so there are no stairs to climb. The park is **free and always open**, with a statue of Admiral Yi Sun-sin, a bell pavilion and a flower clock.

At the top stands **Busan Tower**, a 120-metre observation tower. The operator renovated it in late 2021 and branded it "Diamond Tower", but signs and maps still mostly say Busan Tower. It runs **10:00 to 22:00**, ticketing until 21:30, every day. Admission at the time of writing is **₩12,000 (about $9)** for adults and ₩9,000 for children and over-65s. From the deck you see the north and south harbours, Yeongdo Bridge and Jagalchi in one sweep, and in the evening the windows play fireworks-festival footage.

You do not need the tower for a view: the park's own terrace looks over the south harbour and Yeongdo. Save money by stopping at the park, or go up the tower about 30 minutes before sunset for night views.

## The 40 Steps: on the walk from Busan Station

The **40 Steps** in Jungang-dong linked the hillside shanties of Korean War refugees to the docks below. It served as a meeting point for separated families, and a bronze accordion player now sits halfway up. The staircase is always open, and the 450 metres from the KB bank branch through the steps to the culture center is laid out as the **40 Steps Culture and Tourism Theme Street**.

The **40 Steps Culture Center** (49 Donggwang-gil) is a small free museum of 1950s refugee photographs and household objects. Hours are **weekdays 10:00–18:00, weekends 10:00–17:00**, closed Mondays, national holidays and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays. You pass it naturally when walking from Busan Station; from Nampo-dong, the nearest station is Jungang on [[line:busan1]].

## Jagalchi Market rooftop deck: free, 09:00–21:00

**Jagalchi Market**, two minutes from Jagalchi Station on [[line:busan1]], is covered as a place to eat in our [Busan food guide](/en/posts/busan-food-2026-09/). Here it is only about the **roof**. According to a Busan Ilbo report, you take the lift to the **7th floor and climb one more flight of stairs** to a free observation deck. It is open **09:00 to 21:00**, with two telescopes, wooden benches and a "seagull mailbox" that delivers your postcard six months later. Yeongdo Bridge, Busan Bridge, Namhang Bridge and the Yeongdo shipyards are straight ahead, and on a clear day Tsushima is said to be visible. With around 50 visitors a day, it is quiet.

The market itself runs **05:00 to 22:00** and closes on the **1st, 3rd and 5th Tuesdays** of each month plus the day of and after major holidays. On closure days the roof is off limits too, so avoid Tuesdays.

## Yeongdo Bridge lift: every Saturday at 14:00

Ten minutes east along the waterfront from Jagalchi is **Yeongdo Bridge**. Opened in 1934, it was Korea's first bascule bridge, and during the Korean War it was where refugees agreed to "meet at the bridge". A row of fortune tellers set up underneath for families seeking news of the missing, and the bridge appears in the famous 1950s song "Be Strong, Geum-sun". The lift stopped in 1966 and was restored on 27 November 2013.

![The red deck of Yeongdo Bridge raised into the sky, with a bronze statue of a refugee family in front](/images/nampo-things-to-do-2026-10/05.webp)

The Busan Facilities Corporation raises the Nampo-side span **every Saturday at 14:00 for about 15 minutes**; a June 2026 report confirms the same schedule. Traffic on the bridge is stopped 10 minutes before, so be at **Yurari Square** (the waterfront plaza at the west end of the bridge) by about 13:40. Lifts are cancelled in extreme heat or bad weather. In 2026 there were also night lifts at 20:00 every Saturday in August, but that was a summer programme; in October only the afternoon lift runs.

## Across the bridge to Yeongdo: Huinnyeoul and the coastal trail

Cross Yeongdo Bridge and you are on Yeongdo island. **Huinnyeoul Culture Village** is reached by bus 7, 71 or 508 from Nampo Station exit 6 to the Baengnyeonsa stop. White houses line a cliff-top lane with anchored cargo ships below; drop down to the **Jeoryeong Coastal Trail**, walk along the water and climb back up, and it is about an hour. The village is free and always open.

![Cliff-top lane at Huinnyeoul Culture Village with white houses, the sea and Namhang Bridge in view](/images/nampo-things-to-do-2026-10/12.webp)

## Getting to Gamcheon Culture Village

This is the nearest postcard view to Nampo-dong. Take [[line:busan1]] to **Toseong Station, exit 6**, then village bus Saha 1-1 or Seogu 2 up to the village entrance, 10 to 15 minutes. Toseong is two stops from Nampo. The walk up is steep, so take the bus. The information centre and shops run 09:00 to 18:00 until the end of October and close at 17:00 from November. People live here, so do not photograph into windows. The route inside the village is in our [Busan things-to-do guide](/en/posts/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Songdo Marine Cable Car

With a spare half day, add the **Songdo Marine Cable Car**, about 10 minutes by taxi from Nampo-dong. Official fares rose on 15 April 2026: a round trip is **₩19,000 (about $14)** for an adult in the Air Cruise cabin (₩14,000 for children) and **₩24,000** in the glass-floored Crystal Cruise (₩18,000 for children); one way is ₩15,000 and ₩19,000. It runs **09:00 to 21:00** with ticketing closing 30 minutes before, and shuts early in bad weather.

## One day or two

**One day (a Saturday)**

1. 09:00 Busan Station, walk via the 40 Steps to Nampo-dong (30 min)
2. 10:00 History museum, main hall and annex (1 hour)
3. 11:30 Bosu-dong Book Alley, then lunch while browsing Gukje Market
4. 13:30 Jagalchi Market rooftop deck
5. 14:00 Yeongdo Bridge lift, from Yurari Square or the rooftop
6. 15:00 Escalator up to Yongdusan Park
7. Evening: Kkangtong Night Market, then Busan Tower after dark

On a non-Saturday, drop steps 4 and 5 and give BIFF Square and Gukje Market more time.

**With two days**: on the second morning take the train to Toseong for Gamcheon, then after lunch take the bus from Nampo to Huinnyeoul and the coastal trail, with the Songdo cable car if time remains.

## Practical notes

- **Almost everything is free.** The only paid items are Busan Tower (₩12,000) and the Songdo cable car (from ₩19,000).
- **Check the day of the week first.** The museum and the 40 Steps center close on Mondays, Jagalchi on the 1st, 3rd and 5th Tuesdays, Gukje on the 1st and 3rd Sundays. The bridge lift is Saturdays only.
- **One subway line covers it.** [[line:busan1]] runs Busan Station, Jungang, Nampo, Jagalchi, Toseong in that order, and every gap is walkable.
- **From Gimhae Airport**, take [[line:gimhaelrt]] to Sasang, change to [[line:busan2]] to Seomyeon, then [[line:busan1]] to Nampo. Allow a little over an hour.
- **Cash for market stalls**; shops, the tower and the cable car take cards.
- **Hills and stairs are everywhere.** Yongdusan has an escalator; Huinnyeoul and Gamcheon do not. Wear comfortable shoes.

## Wrapping up

Nampo-dong is less about what to see than the order to see it in. Start with the refugee-capital story at the museum, walk through the markets, watch the bridge go up at two on Saturday, and climb the tower at dusk, and the day reads as a single story.

Gamcheon, Huinnyeoul and Taejongdae get full coverage in our [Busan things-to-do guide](/en/posts/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/), and what to eat around here is in [Nampo-dong and Jagalchi food](/en/posts/nampo-food-2026-10/).
