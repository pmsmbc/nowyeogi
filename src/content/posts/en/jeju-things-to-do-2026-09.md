Almost everything worth seeing on Jeju was made by the volcano: the crater cones, the lava tubes, the columnar cliffs, the waterfalls. Because circling the island eats time, the practical approach is to **split it into an east day and a west day** rather than trying to link sights across the middle.

Information here is as of September 2026. Hours and fees change, so check official pages before you go.

## Seongsan Ilchulbong (east)

A UNESCO World Heritage site, and the island's signature view. It is only 180m high, but it formed about 5,000 years ago in an underwater eruption, which is why it looks nothing like Jeju's other cones. The crater at the top is 600m across.

![Seongsan Ilchulbong seen across a field of blue hydrangeas at blue hour](/images/jeju-things-to-do-2026-09/07.webp)

The climb is a stepped path, about an hour up and down. Official opening hours change with the season: 4:30am–8:00pm from May to August (last tickets 7:00pm), 5:00am–7:00pm in March, April, September and October (last tickets 6:00pm), and 6:00am–6:00pm from November to February (last tickets 5:00pm). **It closes on the first Monday of each month**, moving to the next day when that Monday is a public holiday.

![Seongsan village and its harbour seen from the summit of Ilchulbong](/images/jeju-things-to-do-2026-09/09.webp)

The name means "sunrise peak," but catching that means staying nearby. The view over the sea and the village holds up perfectly well in daylight.

## Hallasan

South Korea's highest mountain. Both trails that reach the summit **require a reservation**, allocated first-come on the official Hallasan booking site, so book once your dates are fixed.

- **Seongpanak trail**: 9.6km, about 4 hours 30 minutes each way — allow nine hours return. 156 parking spaces at the trailhead
- **Gwaneumsa trail**: 8.7km, about 5 hours each way, and steeper

There are hard cut-off times. If you have not passed the Jindallaebat checkpoint by 12:30pm (April–September) or 11:30am (October–March), you are not allowed to continue to the summit, which makes an early start essential. If a nine-hour day is too much, the Eorimok and Yeongsil trails are shorter and need no reservation.

## Manjanggul lava tube (east)

A tunnel left behind by flowing lava, with a section open to walk. Hours are 9:00am–6:00pm with last entry at 5:00pm, and it **closes on the first Wednesday of each month**. It stays cool inside all year, so bring a layer even in August.

## Hyeopjae Beach (west)

Shallow, bright water and white sand — the beach that fills Jeju photo feeds. Open at all times, year-round, with parking.

![Aerial view of Hyeopjae Beach with turquoise shallows and white sand](/images/jeju-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

Biyangdo island sits offshore, and the sand runs on into neighbouring Geumneung Beach. From Aewol it is under twenty minutes by car.

## Cheonjiyeon Falls (south)

The most accessible of Jeju's waterfalls, inside Seogwipo town, reached by a flat walking path from the car park.

![Cheonjiyeon Falls dropping from a forested cliff into a dark pool](/images/jeju-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

It is open 9:00am–10:00pm (last entry 9:20pm), unusually late, and the falls are lit at night — which makes it a good evening stop. Most itineraries pair it with Jeongbang and Cheonjeyeon falls in the same area.

## Daepo columnar joints (Jungmun)

Cliffs of hexagonal basalt columns formed as lava cooled, viewed from a platform above, so there is very little walking involved.

![Waves breaking against the hexagonal basalt columns of the Daepo coast](/images/jeju-things-to-do-2026-09/06.webp)

Open 9:00am–5:30pm, year-round. If you are staying in Jungmun you can walk there.

## Practical notes

- **Routing**: one day east (Ilchulbong, Manjanggul, Udo), one day west (Hyeopjae, Hallim), one day south (Jungmun and the Seogwipo waterfalls) keeps driving down.
- **Closing days**: Ilchulbong on the first Monday, Manjanggul on the first Wednesday. Check these against your dates.
- **Weather**: Jeju's weather turns over quickly. Hallasan and the oreum cones give you nothing on a cloudy day, so keep them flexible.
- **Admission**: most sites charge, but the amounts change often enough that they are not listed here — check each official page before visiting.
- **Udo island**: ferries leave from Seongsan port. Sailing times and the rules on bringing a car across change frequently, so confirm on the day.

## Wrapping up

With one day, the eastern loop of Ilchulbong and Manjanggul gives you the most. With two, add the west-coast beaches; with three, Hallasan or a section of the Olle walking trail. The single most useful planning habit on Jeju is leaving generous gaps between sights for the drive.
