Few capitals let you step off a subway train and start climbing a real mountain. Seoul does, and in late October those mountains change colour before the street trees downtown. The short version: **for a proper hike, pick Bukhansan, Dobongsan or Gwanaksan; for a walk under two hours, pick Inwangsan, Ansan or Achasan.** This guide pulls together information from the Seoul Metropolitan Government, the Korea National Park Service and the Korea Tourism Organization. Hours are as of 16 October 2026.

![The southern slopes of Bukhansan covered in red, orange and yellow leaves under a clear sky, with a grey granite ridge in the distance](/images/seoul-mountain-foliage-2026-10/14.webp)

## When to go

The 2026 foliage map, released on 8 October by the Korea Tourism Organization with the Korea National Arboretum, expects **leaves around Seoul to peak from late October**. Seoul's own foliage guides list Bukhansan as one of the first places in the city to turn. A sensible plan is mountains in the last week of October and city parks and palaces in early November. For the palaces and ginkgo streets, see our [Seoul autumn foliage guide](/en/posts/seoul-autumn-foliage-2026-09/).

All six mountains below are free to enter. Korea's national parks charge no admission fee, and the city mountains have no gates at all.

## Bukhansan: Baegundae, the highest point in Seoul

Baegundae, the top of Bukhansan, stands at 836 m. Of the many routes, the Korea Tourism Organization recommends the **one starting from Bukhansan Ui Station** for people who don't hike often. It runs past the entrance to Doseonsa temple, over Harujae pass, around Insubong peak and through the Baegun gate in the old fortress wall. The tourism body describes it as the shortest and least difficult route, taking 2–3 hours. Take the [[line:uisinseol]] to Bukhansan Ui Station.

The final stretch, roughly 300 m from the gate to the summit, is a rock scramble where you hold onto fixed ropes. A Seoul city hiking report advises boots with good grip and non-slip gloves. Trail entry hours are 04:00–17:00 in the summer season and 04:00–16:00 in winter. On the summit rock there is an inscription said to have been carved in 1919 during the March 1st independence movement; it is a designated Seoul cultural heritage item.

## Dobongsan: granite peaks above autumn valleys

From Exit 1 of Dobongsan Station ([[line:1]] and [[line:7]]), follow the strip of restaurants for about 20 minutes to the Dobongsan Visitor Center. The route Seoul's city media recommends crosses the bridge on the left and heads past Neungwonsa temple and Dobong Seowon, a former Confucian academy, toward Sinseondae. Madang Rock, just before the top, is the natural rest stop.

![Granite peaks of Dobongsan rising above pine trees, with layers of mountain ridges behind](/images/seoul-mountain-foliage-2026-10/02.webp)

The highest peak, Jaunbong (739.5 m), is off-limits because it is too dangerous. The highest point you can actually stand on is **Sinseondae at 726 m**. The last climb has few footholds and you pull yourself up on iron bars, so turning back at Madang Rock is a reasonable option if your knees or grip are weak.

## Gwanaksan: the trail starts at the station

Gwanaksan is the easiest to reach. The entrance to Gwanaksan Park is right outside Gwanaksan Station on the [[line:sillim]]. On the station's basement level is the city-run **Hiking Tourism Center, Gwanaksan branch**, open Tuesday to Sunday 09:00–18:00 (closed Mondays). It rents hiking boots, clothing, poles and gloves to foreign visitors and to Koreans hiking with them, which is useful if you arrived in Seoul with only sneakers.

![A wooden deck staircase with railings climbing through leaf-covered forest on Gwanaksan](/images/seoul-mountain-foliage-2026-10/04.webp)

The center suggests three day routes:

- Course A: Gwanaksan Park to Samgeori spring, 3.4 km, 1 hour 30 minutes
- Course B: Engineering Building (Seoul National University) to Yeonjudae, 4.7 km, 3 hours 30 minutes
- Course C: Jaunam ridge to Yeonjudae, 4.3 km, 3 hours

The summit, Yeonjudae, is 629 m. Yeonjuam, a hermitage near the top, is said to date to 677 during the Silla kingdom. One section is nicknamed "kkaltak gogae", Korean slang for a pass so steep it leaves you gasping.

## Inwangsan: autumn colour along the city wall

On Inwangsan you follow the Seoul City Wall (Hanyangdoseong), the Joseon-era fortress that once ringed the capital. The Inwangsan section runs **4 km from the Donuimun gate site to Changuimun gate**, passing rock formations such as Chima Rock and the hillside dedicated to the poet Yun Dong-ju. The stairs are steep, but the views over Seochon, Gyeongbokgung and Gwanghwamun are among the best in the city.

![The Seoul City Wall winding along the Inwangsan ridge, with downtown Seoul, Namsan and N Seoul Tower beyond](/images/seoul-mountain-foliage-2026-10/08.webp)

Coming down toward Changuimun, you can stop at the Yun Dong-ju Literary Museum (119 Changuimun-ro, Jongno-gu), open 10:00–18:00, closed Mondays, free.

## Ansan: a step-free forest loop

Ansan (296 m) in Seodaemun-gu starts from Exit 5 of Dongnimmun Station on [[line:3]]. Walk along the wall of the old Seodaemun Prison toward Muakjae, then climb the stairs to the right of the Korean Provisional Government Memorial Hall. The summit beacon site is about 1.5 km away, roughly 40 minutes.

![View from a lookout on Ansan over apartment blocks toward the mountain ridges of Bukhansan and Inwangsan](/images/seoul-mountain-foliage-2026-10/11.webp)

If you'd rather skip the climb, the **Ansan Jarak-gil** is a 7 km loop around the mountainside built as a barrier-free trail, so wheelchairs and strollers can use it. The metasequoia grove is its best-known stretch. Seoul's city media suggests finishing at Yeongcheon Market across the road, open since 1960, for twisted doughnuts or tteokbokki.

## Achasan: river views in 40 minutes

At 295.7 m, Achasan is the lowest of the six. From Achasan or Gwangnaru Station on [[line:5]], it is a 20–25 minute walk to the trailhead; the usual way from Gwangnaru Exit 1 or 2 passes two schools and enters through Achasan Ecological Park.

![A flat rock near the Achasan pavilion looking out over green forested ridges and a hazy city](/images/seoul-mountain-foliage-2026-10/19.webp)

According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the top is about 40 minutes up and gives a 360-degree view from Jamsil to Namsan, Bukhansan and Dobongsan. The mountain once held a fortress guarding the Han River during the Three Kingdoms period, so it works as a history walk too.

## Practical notes

- **Cost**: free at all six.
- **Transport**: every route starts on foot from a subway station. Parking is hard on autumn weekends, so take the train.
- **Timing**: on Bukhansan and Dobongsan, start in the morning so you are down well before the trail entry cut-off (16:00 in winter).
- **Gear**: boots and gloves are essential for the rock sections on Baegundae and Sinseondae. If you have none, rent them at the Gwanaksan center or the Hiking Tourism Center near Bukhansan Ui Station.
- **Crowds**: reviews often say Gwanaksan is quieter if you set off on a weekday between 9 and 10 a.m.

## Wrapping up

Choose by time and fitness. With a full day, go for Baegundae or Yeonjudae; with half a day, the Inwangsan wall; with two hours or less, the Ansan loop or Achasan.
