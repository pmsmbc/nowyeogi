Busan's attractions are spread along 30 km of coast, so the useful skill here is grouping them by area rather than by ranking. Hours and prices are as of September 2026 and come from official listings.

## Haedong Yonggungsa

![Haedong Yonggungsa temple halls built on the rocks above the sea](/images/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

A Buddhist temple built directly onto the rocks above the water in Gijang, northeast of the city. Most Korean temples sit inland in the mountains, which is what makes this one unusual and why it is on every Busan itinerary.

**Free**, open **04:30 to 19:20** with last entry at 18:50, every day of the year.

Getting in means going down and back up **108 stairs**, so it is not step-free. From [[line:busan2]] Haeundae Station exit 7, take bus 181 or 100. From Osiria Station on [[line:donghae]], take bus 139.

Go at opening or in the last two hours. Between 11:00 and 15:00 the stairs become a slow-moving queue.

## Gamcheon Culture Village

Pastel houses stacked in terraces up a steep hillside in Saha-gu, laid out by refugees who settled here in the 1950s. It was a poor hillside district until an arts-led regeneration project in the 2010s, and it now draws well over a million visitors a year.

**Free.** The information centre and shops run **09:00 to 18:00** from March to October and close an hour earlier in winter, but the lanes themselves are ordinary public streets and never close.

From [[line:busan1]] Toseong Station exit 6, take village bus Saha 1-1 or Seo-gu 2 up the hill.

This is a residential neighbourhood, not an open-air museum. People are living behind those doors, so keep noise down and do not photograph into windows.

## Huinnyeoul Culture Village

![Sea view framed between two houses at Huinnyeoul Culture Village, with cargo ships anchored offshore](/images/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

A single lane of white houses running along a cliff edge on Yeongdo island, looking out at the cargo ships waiting to enter the port. Quieter than Gamcheon and, for many visitors, the better of the two.

**Free** and always open. From [[line:busan1]] Nampo Station exit 6, take bus 7, 71 or 508 to the Baengnyeonsa stop.

The **Jeoryeong Coastal Trail** runs along the base of the cliff below the village, connected by steep staircases. Walking out along the lane and back along the shore takes about an hour.

## Taejongdae

![The yellow Danubi tourist train parked at the Taejongdae park entrance](/images/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

A forested headland at the southern tip of Yeongdo with cliffs, a lighthouse and a view out to sea. Entry to the park is **free**.

**Important as of September 2026**: the **Danubi tourist train is suspended**. The Busan Tourism Organization stopped it on 4 July 2026 for an emergency safety inspection and has not announced a restart date. A **free shuttle** runs in its place from Tuesday to Sunday, with boarding passes issued at the Danubi ticket office between 09:10 and 17:30.

Without the train the loop is about 2 km of hilly road on foot. Check the operator's notice board before you go.

From [[line:busan1]] Busan Station exit 7, take bus 66, 88 or 101.

## Haeundae Blue Line Park

A disused coastal railway between Mipo and Songjeong, reopened as two tourist rides on the same track.

**The Beach Train** runs the full 4.8 km with six stops. One way from Mipo or Songjeong costs **₩10,000**, a round trip **₩14,000**, and an all-station one-day pass **₩16,000**.

**The Sky Capsule** is the photogenic one: small four-seat pods on an elevated rail between Mipo and Cheongsapo, about 30 minutes each way. It is priced **per capsule, not per person**, at **₩50,000 for two**, ₩55,000 for three and ₩60,000 for four.

Booking is the part people get wrong. Online reservations open **every Tuesday at 07:00 Korean time**, and they release the week beginning **four weeks later**. You cannot book online for the same week. A small same-day allocation is sold at the ticket office and usually goes within the first hour.

Both rides operate in rain but stop for typhoons.

## BUSAN X the SKY

The observation deck in the LCT Landmark Tower at Haeundae, 411.6 m tall. Open **10:00 to 21:00** with last admission at 20:30.

Adult admission is around **₩29,000**. Official listings disagree, with the city tourism site quoting ₩27,000 and the national tourism site ₩29,000, so budget the higher number. Nearest station is Jung-dong on [[line:busan2]].

## Gwangalli and the free drone show

Gwangalli Beach faces the **Gwangan Bridge**, which is lit with colour-changing lighting after dark. That alone is the main reason to come in the evening.

On top of that, the **Gwangalli M Drone Light Show** runs **every Saturday, all year, and it is free**. There are two performances a night, at **20:00 and 22:00** from March to September and an hour earlier from October to February. Each lasts about 12 minutes and uses roughly a thousand drones. You can watch from anywhere along the beach.

Shows are cancelled for rain or strong wind, announced the day before or on the day. [[line:busan2]] Gwangan Station exit 5 is about 13 minutes on foot.

## Two more worth the trip

**Beomeosa Temple** sits on the slope of Geumjeongsan in the north of the city. **Free**, **08:00 to 17:00**, and one of the three great temples of the Yeongnam region. From [[line:busan1]] Beomeosa Station exit 5 or 7, take bus 90 rather than walking up. It is an active monastery, so dress modestly.

**Songdo Marine Cable Car** crosses 1.62 km of open water in the west of the city. A round trip is **₩19,000** for the solid-floor cabin or **₩24,000** for the glass-floor one. Songdo Beach below it opened in 1913 and was Korea's first public beach.

## Grouping it into days

**Day one, the east**: Yonggungsa in the morning, then the Sky Capsule from Mipo, then X the SKY at dusk, then Gwangalli for the bridge and the drone show if it is Saturday.

**Day two, the west**: Gamcheon in the morning, Jagalchi market for lunch, then Yeongdo for Huinnyeoul and Taejongdae in the afternoon.

That leaves Beomeosa and Songdo for a third day, or drop them.

## Practical notes

- **Nearly everything here is free.** The paid items are the Sky Capsule, X the SKY and the cable car.
- **Book the Sky Capsule four weeks out** or plan to queue on the day.
- **Bus numbers matter.** Several of these sites need a short bus hop from the metro, and the walk is steep enough to regret.
- **The villages are people's homes.** Gamcheon and Huinnyeoul both ask visitors to keep quiet after dark.
- **Typhoon season runs into September.** Coastal rides and shows are cancelled at short notice.

## Wrapping up

Two temples, two cliff villages and one very photogenic pod ride will fill two full days without costing much. Add the Saturday drone show if your dates allow, because it is free and genuinely good.
