What you look at in Euljiro is not a sight. It is **a workplace**. You walk through alleys where presses are turning and forklifts are carrying paper.

And a good part of it is **being demolished right now**. That is what this post wants to tell you first.

![The front of Sewoon Sangga, lighting shops lined up beneath a Makercity SEWOON sign with the elevated walkway running out to either side](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/06.webp)

## Sewoon Sangga

**Korea's first mixed-use residential and commercial building, opened in 1968.** The architect was Kim Swoo-geun.

This is how the Seoul Tourism Organization introduces it.

> Sewoon Sangga, which opened in 1968, was the country's first mixed-use residential and commercial building and **the mecca of the electrical and electronics industry** through the 1970s and 1980s, a place that has lived through both a brilliant heyday and the dark years of decline.

The history of the site is unusual too. In **1945 the Japanese cleared it as a firebreak** and left it empty against the risk of air raids. Shacks went up after the war, and in **1966 it became the first inner-city redevelopment site in the country**; two years later Sewoon Sangga stood there.

The shops inside still sell electrical and electronic components, lighting, audio equipment and CCTV.

### The elevated deck is coming down

![A steel-framed pedestrian bridge crossing above Euljiro, cars and buses passing underneath](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/07.webp)

This part matters. If you have read online that you should go and walk the elevated deck at Sewoon Sangga, **that information may be out of date.**

- On **26 December 2024** the city's urban regeneration committee **resolved that the Dasi Sewoon regeneration project be concluded**. That is a formal end.
- On **26 March 2026** a Seoul city fact briefing stated flatly that the current policy is **to "demolish the old and dangerous concrete arcade blocks"** and create a green axis of about 136,000 square metres.

Here is the city's own description of the deck.

> The elevated walkway around Sewoon Sangga is installed along **a section of roughly 1km**, running from Sewoon Sangga to Cheonggye and Daerim Sangga, Sampoong Sangga and the PJ Hotel, and on to Inhyeon and Jinyang Sangga ... and includes **the decommissioning of the roughly 250m of steel-framed walkway installed on either side of Sampoong Sangga and the PJ Hotel**.

**The 250m section has been approved for removal, and the rest goes in stages as the arcade blocks become parkland.** In other words, **the walkway will not survive.**

The 5,670 square metre park at Sampoong Sangga had its implementation plan gazetted on 17 July 2025, and **the target for breaking ground is the first half of 2026**.

**How much of the deck you can actually walk today could not be confirmed.** Check the signs on site before you go.

## The alley the city describes as being demolished

It is not often you see wording like this on an official heritage page.

**The Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley** is designated Seoul Future Heritage 2016-044. It runs about **350m** along Euljiro 17-gil and 15-gil in Ipjeong-dong, Jung-gu, and took shape in the 1960s.

This is what the page says.

> In 2021 the Sewoon 3-2 zone in which the Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley sits completed its management and disposal authorisation ... **at present demolition is under way across the area for redevelopment**

**It is designated heritage, and it is being demolished.** As the food post also notes, **Seoul Future Heritage designation carries no duty of preservation.** The city itself states that it "does not establish a regulatory mechanism for uniform preservation".

The Inhyeon underground shopping arcade is likewise recorded as **"temporarily closed for the public-space works at Sewoon Sangga"**.

## The alleys that are still working

Talking only about demolition would give the wrong impression. **A lot of these alleys are still at work.**

The route the city put together for its 2025 Seoul Future Heritage public walk is a good reference.

> Euljiro golbaengi street - Euljiro nogari street - **Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley - Euljiro lighting street - Cheonggye tool market - Sewoon Sangga - the machinery and metalwork alley - Inhyeon Market - Chungmuro printing alley**

Held to their axes, they go like this.

- **The lighting street** - on the main Euljiro road. A whole block selling nothing but lighting, from chandeliers to LEDs
- **The tool market** - on the Cheonggyecheon side. Drills, cutters and hose out on the pavement
- **The printing alley** - between Euljiro and Chungmuro. They will take an order for a single box of business cards
- **The machinery and metalwork alley** - where the lathes and mills are running

**You will have no reason to go into the shops.** The trade is wholesale and made-to-order. Looking as you walk is how this neighbourhood is enjoyed.

### When you take photographs

**These are people's places of work.** It is better to ask before photographing someone at work, or their machine, head-on. The alleys are narrow and forklifts and handcarts come through constantly, so standing in the middle of one is dangerous.

## Bangsan Market

It has "market" in the name, but it is classified as something else.

In the city's traditional market records (as of 28 August 2026), **Bangsan Market is listed not as a registered market but as a "shopping street"**. The **Bangsan General Market** right next to it **is a registered market**.

The distinction has a practical effect. This is the city's explanation.

> Within the categories of traditional market, those other than "registered" and "recognised", such as **shopping streets**, ... **do not qualify for the additional income deduction rate that applies to traditional markets**

Bangsan Market appears in Jung-gu's official material as a **"printing and packaging"** market. It is widely known online as a market for baking and confectionery supplies, but **no official basis for that could be found.**

## Is "Hipjiro" an official term

Half so. This is what the city wrote in a press release in August 2026.

> Euljiro ... its distinctive industrial landscape of densely packed print shops, ironworks and workshops joined by cafes, craft studios and cultural spaces, has acquired **the nickname "Hipjiro"**

**It is a term that turns up in official Seoul city press releases**, but the city itself calls it a nickname. It is not an administrative name.

That press release covered an on-site inspection of night tourism, and the route inspected was **"the print shop and ironworks alleys, the Euljiro golbaengi alley, and the nogari outdoor-table street"**. Which is to say those three are what the city regards as Euljiro's tourism assets.

## What could not be verified

**How much of the elevated deck is currently open could not be confirmed.** The demolition policy and the approved removal of the 250m section are confirmed in official documents, but how far you can walk today is a separate question.

**How much of the Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley is still standing** could not be confirmed either. The city's document says only that demolition is under way.

**Whether Bangsan Market sells baking and confectionery supplies** has no official basis that could be found.

**Not every exit number could be verified.** Only the numbers written in the table above are officially confirmed.

## Wrapping up

- **Sewoon Sangga is Korea's first mixed-use residential and commercial building**, from 1968, designed by Kim Swoo-geun.
- **The Dasi Sewoon project formally ended in December 2024**, and current policy is to demolish the arcade blocks and create a green axis.
- **The elevated deck will not survive.** Removal of 250m of it is already approved.
- **The city writes that demolition is under way in the Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley.**
- **The lighting, tool, printing and metalwork alleys are still working.** Look as you walk.
- **Bangsan Market is a shopping street, not a registered market.**

In one line: **Euljiro is a neighbourhood with a lot you will not see unless you see it now.** But please understand as well that what is going is not a tourist attraction. It is people's places of work.
