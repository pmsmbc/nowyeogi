Nothing in Euljiro has a ticket booth. The sights are a 1968 electronics market, printing alleys where three-wheeled trucks carry paper, close to 300 lighting shops and a packaging-supplies market, and every one of them is **a working business**. That leads to one practical rule: **go on a weekday or a Saturday, during the day.** On Sundays and public holidays Sewoon Sangga and most of the trade buildings shut, so the streets are still there but the things worth seeing are behind shutters.

History and transport for the area are in our [Euljiro guide](/en/posts/euljiro-guide-2026-09/); this page is only about **what to see and how to see it**. Times and prices are **as of 1 October 2026**.

Euljiro the road runs from City Hall to Dongdaemun History & Culture Park. This page covers **Euljiro 1-ga to 5-ga and the back streets** (Ipjeong-dong, Sallim-dong, Supyo-dong, Jugyo-dong, Bangsan-dong, Inhyeon-dong and so on), **the Sewoon buildings**, and **the stretch of Cheonggyecheon stream alongside Euljiro**.

## Sewoon Sangga: "the world's energy", 1968

![The front of Sewoon Sangga: a 'SEWOON PLAZA' sign under the upper-floor apartments, a 'SEWOON' sign over the entrance, and glass-railed elevated walkways running out to both sides above a plaza with pedestrians and delivery trucks](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/14.webp)

According to the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), Sewoon Sangga opened in **1968** as **Korea's first all-in-one electronics market**; the name is usually glossed as "where the world's energy gathers". The site has an unusual past.

- In 1945 the Japanese colonial authorities kept it empty as a **firebreak** against Allied bombing.
- After the Korean War, people who had lost their homes built shacks there, and in **1966 it became Korea's first downtown redevelopment zone**.
- Seoul Shinmun (21 August 2026) notes that architect **Kim Swoo-geun** designed it as **eight linked buildings** running about 1 km south (Hyundai, Sewoon, Cheonggye, Daelim, Sampung, the Pungjeon Hotel, Sinseong and Jinyang), with shops below and apartments above: Korea's first mixed-use complex.

KTO adds that TG Sambo Computer, the word-processor maker Hancom and the intercom firm Commax are said to have started here.

### What you can see today

Makercity Sewoon, the city's regeneration programme for the buildings, says **the Sewoon, Cheonggye and Daelim buildings trade Monday to Saturday, 09:00–19:00, and close on Sundays and public holidays.** Individual shops vary. KTO lists 09:00–20:00, so don't plan on a late-evening visit.

- **What's sold**: per KTO, electrical and electronic parts, wiring, semiconductors, audio gear, CCTV, arcade machines, karaoke systems and lighting, plus repair and fabrication workshops.
- **Shared spaces**: the Makercity Sewoon map marks Sewoon Hall, the rooftop, the Sewoon Electronics Museum, a tech book lounge and a partner lounge. These broadly follow the market's hours, though each space differs a little.
- **Sewoon Makers Cube**: small tenant units on the walkway decks. The first phase (Sewoon–Cheonggye–Daelim) opened in September 2017, the second (Sampung–Inhyeon–Jinyang) in June 2022, and a new tenant call went out in June 2026.
- **Sewoon Map** (map.sewoon.org): an online directory of **about 4,200 businesses** around Sewoon, Cheonggyecheon and Euljiro. If you're hunting for a specific part, search here first.

### Don't count on the rooftop

The "Sewoon rooftop viewpoint" that turns up in many travel articles has **no official notice saying it is open** as of writing. Maeil Business reported on 3 September 2023 that the rooftop had been **closed for six months** over a subsidy dispute between the city and the building's association, and an OhmyNews citizen-reporter piece on 27 August 2025 said it was "already closed". We found no announcement of a reopening since. Don't make the trip just for a photo looking down on Jongmyo.

### How much of the elevated walkway is left?

The **elevated walkway** that runs at third-floor level from Sewoon Sangga south to Jinyang Sangga was built as part of the regeneration programme. Seoul's press release of 12 September 2024 puts it at **about 1 km**; it was joined up end to end in 2022 at a cost of **₩110.9 billion** (about $83 million), per the Hankyoreh.

The plan for it has changed several times.

| Date | What happened | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 Sep 2024 | Seoul announces a hearing on removing the **~250 m steel bridge section** on both sides between Sampung Sangga and the PJ Hotel; actual footfall was **11%** of forecast overall and **6.7%** on that section | Seoul press release |
| Dec 2024 | Regeneration committee approves the change and closes the programme | Chosun Biz |
| 16 Jan 2025 | Seoul says removal will be **timed with the Sampung Sangga park**; section 285 m (570 m counting both sides), cost ₩2.3 billion | Hankyoreh |
| Feb 2025 | Sampung–PJ Hotel section removed from the footpath register | Herald Business |
| From May 2026 | **Removal of that 287 m section planned** | Herald Business |

In short: the northern decks linking the Sewoon, Cheonggye and Daelim buildings are still part of the working market, while **the Sampung–PJ Hotel section was due to come down from May 2026**. We could find **no official confirmation** that work has started or exactly where the walkway now ends, so follow the signs on site. Reported lengths for the section differ (250 m, 285 m, 287 m).

### The buildings are meant to become a park

The city's long-term plan is to take the Sewoon buildings down in stages and run a green corridor from Jongmyo to Namsan.

- **Phase 1, 5,670 m² around Sampung Sangga**: implementation plan notified on 17 July 2025, with **construction targeted for the first half of 2026**, and a 1,500-seat musical theatre planned underground (Seoul Shinmun).
- **Overall**: eToday (29 April 2026) describes about **136,000 m²** of green space, three times the size of Gwanghwamun Square, with **completion targeted for 2035**.
- The row over building heights in **Sewoon District 4**, across the road from Jongmyo, a UNESCO World Heritage site, is set out in our [Euljiro guide](/en/posts/euljiro-guide-2026-09/).

Kyunghyang Shinmun (December 2025) quoted a city official as saying it was "still too early" to talk about relocating traders in the main building. For now, the market trades as usual.

## Cheonggyecheon along Euljiro: six bridges

Cheonggyecheon is the stream that divides Jongno-gu from Jung-gu. Per KTO, 5.84 km of it was uncovered and restored between July 2003 and September 2005 with 22 bridges. It is **open at all times**, though access can be closed in heavy rain for safety.

These are the bridges along the Euljiro stretch, with details from the Seoul Facilities Corporation, which manages the stream:

| Bridge | Location | Worth knowing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Samilgyo | Samil-ro | Named after the road that commemorates the 1919 March First independence movement |
| Supyogyo (temporary) | Between Supyo-dong and Gwansu-dong | A stand-in bridge on the site of the original stone one |
| Gwansugyo | Cheonggye 3-ga (Donhwamun-ro) | Named for the office that once measured the stream's water level; an arch bridge in a traditional hall style |
| Sewoongyo | In front of Sewoon Sangga | 50 m wide, designed around light as a nod to the lighting market |
| Baeogae Bridge | Cheonggye 4-ga | Named after Baeogae, a hill that once stood at Jongno 4-ga |
| Saebyeok ("Dawn") Bridge | In front of Bangsan Market | A membrane roof modelled on market awnings, 10 m wide |

### The real Supyogyo is in Jangchungdan Park

![Supyogyo, a long stone bridge on rows of square stone piers with a carved stone railing, set among green trees and grass](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/51.webp)

You'll see the name Supyogyo on the stream between Euljiro 2-ga and 3-ga, but the historic stone bridge isn't there.

- According to the Korea Heritage Service, Supyogyo was built in **1420**, and takes its name from the **supyo**, a water-level gauge that stood beside it. It was designated Seoul tangible heritage on 18 June 1973 and is now listed at **Jangchung-dong 2-ga, Jung-gu**.
- It was dismantled when the stream was covered over in 1959, stored in Sinyeong-dong, and moved to **Jangchungdan Park in 1965**. Korean Wikipedia notes that when the stream was restored in 2005 the bridge could not go back because its length no longer matched the new channel, so a temporary bridge stands on the original site.

To see the real thing, go to Jangchungdan Park near [[line:3]] Dongguk University Station. It's outside Euljiro, so it pairs better with a Namsan day.

Further east along the stream are Pyeonghwa Market and the Jeon Tae-il bridge, covered in [things to do in Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/).

## Tools, lighting and furniture streets

Between Euljiro 3-ga and 4-ga, the main road and the lanes toward the stream are grouped by trade.

### Cheonggye lighting market: about 300 shops

KTO says around Euljiro 3-ga and 4-ga stations and the Sewoon–Cheonggye buildings there are **about 300 lighting shops**, of which **some 20 deal only in specialist lighting** for TV studios, theatre stages and shop windows. In the 1960s it was Seoul's only lighting market and supplied the whole country. Shops range from tiny bulb wholesalers to large showrooms, and many take **custom orders** for a particular space, so individual shoppers looking for interior fittings are welcome too.

### Euljiro steel furniture street: office furniture

KTO describes it as a street of **commercial and office furniture** (around 34-1 Changgyeonggung-ro). Many shops have their own workshops, and buying one or two office pieces here can be **about 10% cheaper** than at a neighbourhood shop. Most are small makers specialising in a single item such as chairs or desks.

### Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley: heritage being demolished

North of Euljiro 3-ga, **about 350 m of Euljiro 15-gil and 17-gil** is the Seoul Future Heritage site **Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley** (2016-044). The heritage page says small steel shops, precision machinists and mould makers packed in after the stream was covered in 1961, with craftsmen of whom it was said "give them a drawing and they'll build you a tank". It appears in the films *Pietà*, *The Thieves* and *Cold Eyes*.

The same page records **a redevelopment approval in 2021 and demolition under way in 2024**. Future Heritage status doesn't oblige anyone to preserve a site. We couldn't confirm how much is left; if you find hoardings, stay outside them.

## The printing alleys, between Euljiro and Chungmuro

![A three-wheeled truck loaded with bundles of paper drives down the middle of a printing alley; a vertical sign for a printing firm hangs on the right and a man pushes a hand trolley further back](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/16.webp)

Print shops run south from Euljiro 3- and 4-ga down to Chungmuro. KTO splits them like this:

- **Chungmuro side**: commercial and digital printing and plate-making, with many firms on newer equipment.
- **Euljiro side**: the traditional heart of the trade, with plate-making and machinery side by side and many long-established firms doing analogue printing and machine servicing.

KTO's Inhyeon Market entry explains that "Chungmuro printing alley" is the umbrella name for the whole district across **Inhyeon-dong, Euljiro and Pil-dong**. The centre is around 14 Toegye-ro 37-gil (Chungmuro 4-ga). There is no parking.

![A printing press seen through a shop window, with sheets of colourful printed images coming off the rollers](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/18.webp)

Most of what's worth seeing is visible from the street: presses running behind glass, forklifts and three-wheelers moving pallets of paper, and signs naming each process (laminating, embossing, foil stamping) lane after lane.

![A narrow staircase in a printing-alley doorway fitted with rails, with a wooden pallet platform waiting at the bottom; boxes of A4 copy paper are stacked by the window next to it](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/32.webp)

You'll also spot rail lifts laid over staircases to haul pallets of paper to upper floors, a practical answer to heavy stock in narrow buildings.

### Walking through someone's workplace

- **Work vehicles have priority.** Forklifts, three-wheelers and hand trolleys come through constantly; step to the edge to take photos.
- **Ask before photographing people or the inside of a workshop.** The glass is a shop window, not an exhibit.
- **Leave pallets and parcels by doorways alone.** They're waiting for delivery.
- Makercity Sewoon's filming rules for events say **pedestrians may not, in principle, be held back**, and filming is **banned anywhere unsafe or not meant for walking**, such as the roofs of the Makers Cube units. The same line works for independent visitors.

## Inhyeon Market: 2 m wide, 202 m long

![An Inhyeon Market lane: small wood-fronted restaurants line both sides of a narrow path under green awnings and hand-lettered signs](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/21.webp)

A lane market hidden inside the printing district. KTO says it grew up on its own in the late 1950s and early 1960s and squeezes **110 shops into a lane 2 m wide and 202 m long**. The site is also said to be where Prince Inseong, seventh son of King Seonjo of the Joseon dynasty, once lived. Address: 192-1 Inhyeon-dong 2-ga; hours vary by shop.

It's mostly small restaurants, which makes it an easy lunch stop between print shops. Dishes and specific places are in [Euljiro food](/en/posts/euljiro-food-2026-09/).

## Bangsan Market: printing and packaging

![The entrance to Bangsan Market under a blue sky, with an arched sign over the road reading 'Packaging and printing town, Bangsan Market' and commercial buildings behind](/images/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/24.webp)

North of Euljiro 4- and 5-ga, facing Saebyeok Bridge on the stream. The Bangsan Market Merchants' Association calls it **a cluster for printing and packaging** and "the mecca of Korea's packaging and printing industry", and the Seoul Facilities Corporation describes the area around the bridge as a market for "printing and paper".

- **What's sold**: the association's trade list covers shopping bags, boxes of every kind, plastic packaging, tape, labels, stickers, promotional goods, wallpaper and flooring, printing materials and print processes. **Bakery, meat and food supplies** also appear as a category.
- **Address**: around 18-1 Euljiro 33-gil. Jung-gu lists **Bangsan Jonghap Market** next door (20 Dongho-ro 37-gil) as a separate market.
- **Fair**: the association's history shows a printing and packaging fair in recent autumns (1 November 2023, 16 October 2024, 27 October 2025). No 2026 date had been announced at the time of writing.

People come here for gift boxes or small runs of branded bags. Most shops also sell wholesale, so expect to buy by the pack rather than by the piece. We couldn't confirm the market's trading days from an official source.

## Admiral Yi's birthplace: a free guided walk

Euljiro has an official walk with a culture and tourism guide: **Yi Sun-sin Road (Birthplace of a Hero)**, launched by Jung-gu in 2026. It starts from the fact that Admiral Yi Sun-sin, the 16th-century naval commander, was born in this part of the city.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Route | Myeongbo intersection → Yi Sun-sin birthplace marker → birthplace site → Jungbu Market → Hullyeonwon Park → Hadogam site (Dongdaemun History Museum 1398) |
| Length | 1.3 km, about 80 minutes |
| Runs | **Wed, Fri, Sat at 10:00 and 14:00** |
| Not run | July–August and December–February |
| Booking | Online or by phone, **at least five days ahead**; goes ahead with four or more people |
| Fee | Free |
| Contact | Jung-gu Sports & Tourism Division, +82-2-3396-4644 |

October and November are in season; it pauses from December, so book before the end of November if you want to do it this year. The meeting point is near [[line:2]] [[line:3]] Euljiro 3-ga Station. The official page doesn't say which languages the guides speak, so assume Korean unless they confirm otherwise.

Jung-gu's older Euljiro routes, "Eulji Yuram" and "New Eulji Yuram", no longer appear in its list of walking tours.

### Myeongbo Art Hall

At the same intersection, **Myeongbo Art Hall** is, per KTO, the Myeongbo cinema of 1957 **rebuilt as a performance venue in 2008** (47 Mareunnae-ro, open year-round, +82-2-2274-2121). As of September 2026 its Raon Hall is running the comedy play *Matryoshka* **until 3 January 2027**, for ages 13 and up, 100 minutes long. It's in Korean.

## The Euljiro underground walkway: 2.8 km

On a wet or cold day you can walk most of Euljiro underground. Seoul's Mediahub (February 2021) describes the walkway from [[line:2]] Euljiro 1-ga Station through Euljiro 3-ga and 4-ga to Dongdaemun History & Culture Park as **2.8 km long, the longest underground walkway in Korea**, with small galleries and photo spots along the way.

Between City Hall and Euljiro 1-ga stations there is also **a 335 m space hidden for more than 40 years**. Seoul Shinmun (3 September 2026) says it probably dates from the early 1980s, when the two stations at different depths were linked; it was first shown to the public in September 2023 and **opened as an exhibition space on 24 August 2026**, entered next to the Euljiro 1-ga ticket gates and closed on Mondays. The opening show (BIGBANG's 20th-anniversary "COSMOS") ended on 27 September; we couldn't confirm the next exhibition or ticket prices.

## Euljiro at night: "Hipjiro" and Nogari Alley

"Hipjiro" (hip + Euljiro) is a nickname rather than an official name, but Seoul uses it. A city press release on 14 August 2026 says Euljiro, where old downtown industry meets new culture, has earned **the nickname "Hipjiro"**. The night-tourism route that city advisers walked that evening was **printing and ironworks alleys → Euljiro golbaengi (whelk) alley → Nogari street with its outdoor tables**, and the city said it would improve night-time pedestrian safety and multilingual signage.

- **Euljiro Nogari Alley** is Seoul Future Heritage 2015-029. The heritage page says it began with **Eulji OB Bear in 1980**, grew to about ten beer halls, and boomed during the 1997 financial crisis. *Nogari* is dried young pollock, the classic snack with draught beer. The traders hold a nogari festival every May. KTO points to the lane off Euljiro 3-ga Station exit 4.
- In the evening, plastic tables spill out onto the street. The lanes after the print shops close are the neighbourhood's night-time sight.

The bars, the menu and the story of Eulji OB Bear's move are in [Euljiro food](/en/posts/euljiro-food-2026-09/).

## Quick summary

- **Sewoon Sangga**: Korea's first all-in-one electronics market (1968), address in Jongno-gu. **Mon–Sat 09:00–19:00, closed Sundays and holidays.**
- **The rooftop has no official reopening notice.** Don't go just for the view.
- **Elevated walkway**: northern decks still in use; the Sampung–PJ Hotel section was scheduled to come down from May 2026.
- **Cheonggyecheon along Euljiro**: six bridges from Samilgyo to Saebyeok Bridge. The real Supyogyo is in Jangchungdan Park.
- **About 300 lighting shops**, the steel furniture street, and the Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley under demolition.
- **Printing alleys**: plate-making and machinery on the Euljiro side, commercial and digital on the Chungmuro side, with **Inhyeon Market** in between.
- **Bangsan Market**: printing and packaging supplies.
- **Yi Sun-sin Road**: free guided walk, Wed/Fri/Sat 10:00 and 14:00, book five days ahead, through November.
- **Euljiro underground walkway**: 2.8 km, the rainy-day option.
