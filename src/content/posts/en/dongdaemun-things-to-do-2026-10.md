Most visitors think of Dongdaemun as a place to shop. Look at a map, though, and within one subway stop you will find **a Zaha Hadid building, a Joseon-era city gate and wall, a colonial-era quarry, a 400-year-old shrine and the site of Nam June Paik's childhood home**. The short version: almost everything worth seeing here is free, and **as long as you avoid Monday** a full day plans itself.

Our [Dongdaemun overview](/en/posts/dongdaemun-guide-2026-10/) explains how the area grew; this post sticks to **what to see and do**. All hours and prices are **as of 1 October 2026**.

![Seen over tall grass on the Heunginjimun Park hill, Heunginjimun gate inside its curved outer wall, with DDP's silver roof, the Pyeonghwa Market sign, office towers and a blue sky behind](/images/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/38.webp)

## Where "Dongdaemun" means in this post

There is no administrative district actually called Dongdaemun. The gate stands in Jongno 6-ga, Jongno-gu; DDP is in Euljiro 7-ga, Jung-gu; and Dongdaemun-gu, confusingly, is a separate district further east. This post covers **everything within walking distance of the gate**.

## DDP: the building is the attraction

![A walkway outside DDP on a clear day, curving grey concrete railings and a grassy mound with Dongdaemun shopping towers beyond](/images/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/09.webp)

**Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP)** opened on 21 March 2014. According to DDP's own introduction it was designed by Zaha Hadid, is clad in **45,133 exterior panels, each a different shape**, and has had **126 million visitors** since opening.

DDP describes itself as an open plaza during the day, so you can walk the paths around the building, Eoullim Plaza and the indoor public spaces without buying any ticket.

- Open **10:00-20:00**. It closes only on **1 January and on Seollal and Chuseok days** (the Lunar New Year and harvest festival holidays). Some spaces keep different hours depending on the programme.
- **It is open on Mondays**, unlike most museums in the area.
- At Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station ([[line:2]] [[line:4]] [[line:5]]), **Exit 1** is closest to Eoullim Plaza and the Museum, **Exit 2** to the Design Lab and the park entrance.
- The **DDP Visitor Center** is on Eoullim Plaza (B2), to the right outside Exit 1, open 10:00-20:00. It has event information and lends wheelchairs and strollers (strollers for babies aged 6-36 months, return by 19:30).
- Paid lockers are near Exit 1, on Design Lab level 1 and inside the Art Hall.
- Parking is under the Design Lab (B1-B3): **₩400 per 5 minutes** (₩4,800, about $3.60, an hour), up to ₩50,000 a day.

## Dongdaemun History & Culture Park: a city wall under a stadium

![The Igansumun water gate in Dongdaemun History and Culture Park, two stone arches under a section of city wall, with a floodlight tower from the old stadium and buildings behind](/images/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/10.webp)

The park next to DDP is the site of the old **Dongdaemun Stadium**. According to the Seoul city media hub (July 2024), it opened in 1925 as Gyeongseong Stadium, became Seoul Stadium after liberation in 1945 and Dongdaemun Stadium in January 1985, and closed with a farewell event in **2008**, after 83 years.

KTO says that clearing the stadium for the park uncovered the city wall's **Igansumun water gate**, a bastion, the sites of Joseon military offices, **44 building foundations and about 1,000 artefacts**. Igansumun ("two-span water gate") let a stream pass under the wall; as the photo shows, it has **two arches** side by side, and you can see it in the open park. One of the stadium's floodlight towers still stands behind it.

The three indoor galleries are run as a branch of the Seoul Museum of History.

| Venue | What's inside |
| --- | --- |
| Dongdaemun History Museum | The excavation and its finds, including Joseon white porcelain and buncheong ware |
| Dongdaemun Stadium Memorial | Newspapers, flyers, tickets and a model of the stadium |
| Excavation site gallery | Remains of wells, ondol fireplaces and defence structures |

- Indoors: **Tuesday to Sunday 10:00-18:00**, last entry 17:30, **closed 12:00-13:00 for lunch**.
- **Closed Mondays**, 1 January and on Seollal and Chuseok days. **Free.**
- No flash, tripods, food or drink.

The longer story of how a stadium became a park and DDP is in our [Dongdaemun overview](/en/posts/dongdaemun-guide-2026-10/).

## Heunginjimun Gate and the Seoul City Wall Museum

**Heunginjimun**, the gate that gave Dongdaemun ("Great East Gate") its name, is the eastern main gate of the eight in Seoul's city wall. KTO records that it was built in 1396 when the wall went up, rebuilt in 1453, and that **the present gate dates from 1869**. It is a two-storey gate tower, five bays wide and two deep, wrapped in a semicircular outer wall called an **ongseong** that gave the gate a second line of defence. Seoul's media hub also singles out the ongseong as the gate's defining feature.

- KTO lists it as **open at all times, every day**; there is no parking.
- Two minutes from Dongdaemun Station ([[line:1]]) **Exit 10**.
- The hill north of the gate is **Heunginjimun Park**, built on the site of a former hospital. Walk up along the wall and you look down on the gate and DDP; the first photo in this post was taken from there.

### Seoul City Wall Museum

![A gallery in the Seoul City Wall Museum, a model of Heunginjimun with its curved outer wall inside a long glass case and a tiled-roof graphic projected on the wall](/images/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/16.webp)

On floors 1-3 of the Seoul Design Support Center inside Heunginjimun Park, the **Seoul City Wall Museum** (Hanyangdoseong Museum) is a good first stop before walking the wall. The city's media hub says it uses documents, models and video to explain how the roughly 18.6km wall was built, how stonework differs from period to period, the four main and four minor gates, and how the wall was restored.

- **09:00-18:00**, last entry 17:30, **free**
- **Closed Mondays and 1 January.** KTO's listing adds that it opens if a Monday is a public holiday; if you plan to go on Monday 5 October (a substitute holiday), call first: 02-724-0243.
- About 317m from Dongdaemun Station **Exit 1**
- Only 29 parking spaces; public transport is recommended.

## The Naksan section of the city wall: 3.2km, 90 minutes

![The city wall path in Naksan Park, a paved trail beside a crenellated stone wall, with the peaks of Bukhansan and hillside houses in the distance](/images/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/21.webp)

The path that follows the wall north from Heunginjimun is the **Naksan section of the Seoul City Wall Trail**. Naksan, at 124m, is the lowest of the four mountains the wall runs over, which makes this a good first section to try.

From the official Seoul City Wall website:

- **Hyehwamun to Gwanghuimun, 3.2km, about 1 hour 30 minutes**, difficulty two stars out of five
- Route: Hyehwamun → Hansung Univ. Station Exit 4 → wooden steps → lane behind the Catholic university → Jangsu Village → Naksan Park → Naksan summit → Ihwa Village → Seoul City Wall Museum → Heunginjimun Park → Heunginjimun
- Access: Hyehwamun is 3 minutes from Hansung Univ. Station ([[line:4]]) Exit 4; Heunginjimun is 2 minutes from Dongdaemun Station ([[line:1]]) Exit 10
- **Open 24 hours**
- **Jangsu Village and Ihwa Village are residential**, and visitors are asked to keep quiet passing through.

One option is to **start at Hansung Univ. Station and walk down to Heunginjimun**, so you finish at the museum and the restaurants around Dongdaemun.

**Naksan Park** (41 Naksan-gil) opened in July 2002. KTO says the hill was nicknamed "camel mountain" for its humped shape, and also Tarak-san after a royal dairy farm that supplied milk to the palace. The park is always open; the **Naksan Exhibition Hall** at its entrance runs 09:00-17:00, and there are 29 parking spaces.

### Seasonal events at the gate

Seoul holds events marking the 24 traditional seasonal divisions in Heunginjimun Park. At the "Baengno" event on 5 September, reports describe a street procession with traditional musicians, a wall walk where you meet costumed Joseon characters, AI-generated Joseon-style portrait postcards and a wall-shaped embroidery keyring workshop, all free. **We could not find official dates for an October event.**

## Changsin-dong: a quarry lookout and Nam June Paik's childhood home

Northeast of the gate, on the slopes of Naksan, is **Changsin-dong**. According to the Dong-A Ilbo (March 2023) it is **Korea's biggest cluster of garment workshops**, small factories that moved up from the Pyeonghwa Market area in the 1970s, and in 2014 it became **Seoul's first urban regeneration project**. The paper describes motorbikes loaded with fabric streaming through its narrow lanes. **This is a working neighbourhood**, so walk accordingly.

### Quarry Observatory

KTO explains that a **quarry operated here during Japanese colonial rule**, supplying granite for buildings such as the Government-General building, the old Seoul Station and the Bank of Korea; the sheer cliffs are what remains. Mining stopped after the Korean War and migrants and refugees built a village below. On top of the cliff stands a long rectangular building: the **Quarry Observatory**.

- 51 Naksan 5-gil
- **Tue-Thu and Sun 10:00-20:00, Fri-Sat 10:00-22:00, closed Mondays** (KTO listing)
- No parking
- It reopened on 20 June 2023, run by the Changsin-Sungin urban regeneration cooperative, which residents co-own (Edaily).
- KTO directions: Changsin Station ([[line:6]]) Exit 1, or the Jongno 03 village bus ([[bus:village:03]]). It is uphill all the way, so wear comfortable shoes.

### SeMA Nam June Paik House

The Seoul Museum of Art opened this small memorial in March 2017 on **the site of the house where the video artist Nam June Paik (1932-2006) lived from 1937 to 1950**. It occupies a 1960s single-storey hanok (traditional Korean house), restored without trying to fake a period look, the museum says.

- **Tuesday to Sunday 10:00-19:00**, last entry 30 minutes before closing, **free**
- **Closed Mondays and 1 January**
- Three minutes on foot from Dongmyo Station ([[line:1]] [[line:6]]) **Exit 8** or Dongdaemun Station ([[line:1]] [[line:4]]) **Exit 3**
- No parking, and no wheelchair or stroller loans
- The NJP Café by the courtyard is run by a local cooperative.
- According to the museum's site, "Megatron Rhapsody" has been on show since 17 June 2025.

### The Iumpium sewing museum has closed

Older travel guides to Changsin-dong still list the **Iumpium Sewing History Museum**. **It closed on 28 February 2023.** The Dong-A Ilbo reported that it opened in 2018 as a flagship of the regeneration project but never drew the hoped-for visitors. We found no announcement of a reopening, so leave it off your plan.

## Dongmyo: Guan Yu's shrine and the flea market

![The main hall of Donggwanwangmyo shrine, red wooden lattice doors and pillars under Chinese-character signboards, a stone incense stand in the courtyard](/images/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/25.webp)

Dongmyo Station ([[line:1]] [[line:6]]) is named after **Donggwanwangmyo**, "Dongmyo" for short, a shrine to **Guan Yu**, the general from the Chinese classic Romance of the Three Kingdoms. KTO says it was built in **1601** after Emperor Shenzong of Ming China sent money and a signboard in his own hand following the Imjin War, and restored in 1739. Of the east, west and north shrines built at that time, only this one survives. It is designated a Treasure.

According to the Seoul city media hub, the main hall holds a **2.5m statue of Guan Yu cast from about 2.4 tonnes of copper**. The layout and look follow Chinese shrine architecture and differ noticeably from Korean buildings of the period.

- **March-October 09:00-18:00, November-February 09:00-17:00, open every day**
- Free; no parking
- It reopened in 2022 after three years closed for COVID-19 and repairs.

### Dongmyo Flea Market

![Dongmyo flea market street on a cloudy day, people browsing clothes and shoes laid out under colourful parasols, low buildings with discount-store signs behind](/images/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/46.webp)

The market spills out from the shrine wall into the lanes of Sungin-dong. A Seoul city media hub piece (15 May 2026) sums it up:

- Around 243 Nangye-ro, right outside **Dongmyo Station Exit 3**
- **Partly open on weekdays, fully open all day at weekends**
- Clothes at ₩1,000, ₩2,000 and ₩5,000; second-hand military jackets and denim; old cameras and radios; used books, magazines and antiques
- **Many items have no price tag**, so prices are agreed with the seller.
- Once mostly an older crowd's market, it now draws people in their 20s and 30s thanks to social media and the vintage trend.

There are no official hours, so a weekend daytime visit is the safest bet.

## East along Cheonggyecheon: used books and Jeon Tae-il Bridge

Head down from Dongdaemun Station to the stream and **Pyeonghwa Market** stretches along Cheonggyecheon. KTO describes it as a garment wholesale market where clothes go from design to delivery within 72 hours.

### Used bookshops on Pyeonghwa Market's ground floor

Second-hand bookshops line the **ground floor** of Pyeonghwa Market. An Asia Economy report (May 2022) said the Cheonggyecheon area had **more than 120** at its 1980s peak and, according to the local booksellers' association, **12** remained. That figure is from 2022; we could not confirm how many open today or their hours.

### The Jeon Tae-il statue on Beodeul Bridge

On **Beodeul Bridge** (also called Jeon Tae-il Bridge) in front of Pyeonghwa Market stands a **bust of Jeon Tae-il**. A cutter in a Pyeonghwa Market garment workshop, he set himself on fire there on 13 November 1970, demanding that the Labour Standards Act be obeyed. In 2005 Seoul named the area Jeon Tae-il Street and placed the statue and a plaque on the bridge.

His memorial hall is about a 30-minute walk west along the stream, at 105 Cheonggyecheon-ro (free, closed Mondays).

## Fashion Town: shopping as sightseeing

![A fabric aisle in Dongdaemun Jonghap Market, rolls of cloth and swatches stacked to the ceiling under green shop signs](/images/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/27.webp)

The malls south and east of DDP were designated the **Dongdaemun Fashion Town Special Tourist Zone on 23 May 2002**. The zone's association puts its area at **about 586,000m²**; Jung-gu District counts **30 malls with some 30,000 shops**.

What trips visitors up is that **retail malls and wholesale malls keep completely different hours**.

| Place | Type | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doota Mall | Retail | **Daily 10:30-24:00** (some shops differ) |
| Hyundai Outlet Dongdaemun | Retail | Mon-Thu 10:30-21:00, Fri-Sun 10:30-21:30 |
| Dongdaemun Jonghap Market | Fabric and trimmings | Fabric and garment supplies 08:00-18:00, accessory supplies 09:30-19:00 |
| apM and other night wholesale malls | Wholesale | Generally **20:00-05:00** |
| DIOT | Wholesale (day market) | Midnight to noon; **closed from Friday noon to 23:50 Sunday** |

- Many night wholesale malls **close on Friday and Saturday nights and reopen on Sunday night**, Fashionnet reported in March 2022, when several Dongdaemun wholesale malls moved to a five-day week for the first time in about 60 years.
- The Korea Textile News (28 January 2026) reported that apM, apM PLACE and apM Luxe still run 20:00-05:00, and that DIOT surveyed its merchants in January about changing hours. **We could not confirm the outcome.**
- So **if you plan a "Dongdaemun night market" stroll for a Friday or Saturday night, most wholesale malls may be shut.** For retail, Doota stays open until midnight.
- **Dongdaemun Jonghap Market**, opened in December 1970, specialises in fabric and garment supplies. It connects directly to Dongdaemun Station through underground passage 9, and its aisles are stacked to the ceiling with bolts of cloth, as in the photo.

How the wholesale market grew is covered in the [Dongdaemun overview](/en/posts/dongdaemun-guide-2026-10/).

## A day by time of day (Tuesday to Saturday)

**Morning: start on the wall.**
From Hansung Univ. Station ([[line:4]]) Exit 4, see Hyehwamun and follow the Naksan section downhill through Naksan Park to Heunginjimun Park, where the City Wall Museum opens at 09:00. The hilltop frames the gate and DDP in one view.

**Lunch: around Dongdaemun Station.**

**Afternoon: Changsin-dong or Dongmyo.**
Walk to Dongmyo for the shrine and flea market (better at weekends), then three minutes to the Nam June Paik House. With energy left, climb Changsin-dong to the Quarry Observatory.
