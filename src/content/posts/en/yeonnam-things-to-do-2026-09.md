Almost nothing in Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong needs a ticket. You walk a park laid on an old railway line, cut through a covered market and sit on the Han River bank. The one place that charges is **Seoul Battleship Park**, three decommissioned navy ships moored in Mangwon Hangang Park, at **₩3,000 (about $2.25)** for adults. In October you can add the **Seoul Silver Grass Festival** at Haneul Park (17–23 October), two stops away on [[line:6]].

What you do need to know are the **rules and the clock**. "Yeontral Park" is a designated no-smoking and "drinking-clean" zone, park-approved busking happens in three slots and ends at 7 pm, Seoul Battleship Park closes on Mondays and **switches to a 6 pm closing from 1 November**, and Haneul Park locks its gates at 9 pm from October. This page covers **what to see, when and how**. For the neighbourhood's history (how the railway became a park, the 1984 Mangwon flood), see the [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong guide](/en/posts/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/). Times and prices are **as of 7 October 2026, from official pages and dated news reports**.

## Gyeongui Line Forest Park, Yeonnam section: 1.2 km of railway park

![Gyeongui Line Forest Park, a path lined with ginkgo trees and paved with wooden boards and stone like old railway sleepers, with a small station-style shelter on the right](/images/yeonnam-things-to-do-2026-09/02.webp)

The **Gyeongui Line Forest Park** is a **6.3 km linear park** running from Mapo-gu to Yongsan-gu. According to the Seoul Parks site, it was built section by section from March 2012 and **completed in 2016** on the old Yongsan Line rail bed after the trains moved underground. The backstory is in the [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong guide](/en/posts/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/).

The Yeonnam-dong section is the stretch locals nicknamed **"Yeontral Park"** (a play on Central Park).

- **Runs from the Yeonnam intersection to Hongik Univ. Station**
- **About 1.2 km, roughly 35 minutes on foot** (Seoul Parks). Mapo-gu's tourism site gives **1.3 km**.
- Seoul Parks lists the highlights as a **lawn, a ginkgo avenue, a water channel recreating the old Segyocheon stream, and preserved track**. The water channel is **860 m** long.
- Shelters shaped like small railway stops line the route, a reminder that trains once ran here.
- **There is no park car park.** The city asks visitors to use public transport.

In October and November the ginkgo avenue is the draw: Seoul Parks singles out its long row of ginkgo trees as the section's signature.

![Gyeongui Line Forest Park, a straight paved path under tall shady trees beside a lawn, with a bed of tall grasses on the left](/images/yeonnam-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

### Busking: three slots a day, finished by 7 pm

The park's approved busking spot is the **roughly 200 m stretch from exit 3 of Hongik Univ. Station to Yeonnam police box**.

| | Busking hours |
| --- | --- |
| Slot 1 | 11:00–13:00 |
| Slot 2 | 14:00–16:00 |
| Slot 3 | 17:00–19:00 |

- Performers book slots **free, first come first served, through the Seoul public service reservation site**.
- That means the park's official performances **end at 7 pm**. Music after dark is not park-approved.
- The park sets **six busking etiquette rules** for performers: keep people and nature safe, keep the volume low, sing decent lyrics, keep shows small, no religious, commercial or political purposes, and tell the park's story. Knowing them helps you tell an approved show from an unapproved one.

Hongdae's main busking zone on Hongdae Walking Street has its own booking system and a 10 pm rule; that is covered in [things to do in Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-things-to-do-2026-09/).

### Drinking and smoking

Photos of people drinking convenience-store beer on the Yeonnam lawn are everywhere, but the **Gyeongui Line Forest Park is one of 22 city-run parks designated a "drinking-clean zone"** (Seoul city card news).

- **What is banned: drinking and then causing others serious discomfort through loud noise or bad smells**
- Fine **up to ₩100,000 (about $75)**
- The city states plainly that **drinking in the park is not banned in itself**.

Smoking is stricter. In June 2019 the city said the Gyeongui Line Forest Park is a **city-designated no-smoking area** with a **₩100,000 fine**, and that **heated tobacco and e-cigarettes are included** (Chosun Ilbo, 11 June 2019). At the time the city ran enforcement in the busy Yeonnam section **at night as well**.

The park is surrounded by homes, which is the real reason for the rules. For directions in the crowded Yeonnam section, the park suggests asking staff at the **Yeonnam guard post outside Yeonnam police box**, which also stocks leaflets. The park leaflet can be downloaded from the Seoul Parks site in **Korean, English, Japanese and Chinese**.

### Filming rules (not about your phone)

The park's filming guidance applies to **permitted shoots** such as TV or advertising.

- Permitted hours: **weekdays only (not Saturday or Sunday), 08:00–21:00**
- **No filming zone: exit 3 of Hongik Univ. Station to the Yeonnam police box junction**, the same stretch as the busking area
- No amplifiers or loudhailers, no fixed sets, no blocking residents, no promotions that gather crowds.

None of this affects holiday snaps, but it explains why you rarely see film crews on that stretch.

### Lanes beside the park: craft shops and Dongjin Market

The Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) describes the lanes near the Yeonnam section as the **"Yeonnam-dong craft street"** (around 18 Seongmisan-ro 28-gil): small workshops and select shops making and selling ceramics, candles, leather goods and handmade accessories. Opening hours differ by shop and most workshop classes need booking, so check each shop's notice first.

The lanes around **Dongjin Market**, inside Yeonnam-dong, host two music events in October (see the calendar below).

## Mangwon Hangang Park: 7.4 km of riverbank and a sunset spot

![A slope in Mangwon Hangang Park on a clear day, stepped paths among pine trees and, beyond them, the wide Han River and a red arched bridge](/images/yeonnam-things-to-do-2026-09/16.webp)

The Hangang Park Headquarters gives it as **7.4 km long, 422,347 m²**, on the north bank between Wonhyo Bridge and Seongsan Bridge. It has lawns, walking paths, sports facilities, a **7.2 km cycle path**, two convenience stores and 11 toilets. Both the city and KTO note water sports on this stretch: windsurfing, water-skiing and motorboats.

### Sunset near the Mangwon pier

In September the city published a **"Han River sunset map"** of **23 spots** in four zones (Herald Economy, Seoul Economic Daily). The **Gangseo–Nanji–Mangwon–Yanghwa zone** includes the **area near the Mangwon Hangang Bus pier**, where the sun sets over the western horizon beyond the river bridges.

The city's own news site also notes that the **bow and stern decks of ROKS Seoul** at Seoul Battleship Park bring you close to the river and suit a visit timed for sunset. The park closes at 7 pm on October weekdays and at 6 pm from November, so check the day's sunset time before counting on it.

**Mangwon is not on the Hangang Park HQ's list of riverside bike-rental shops**; the nearest on the north bank is downstream at Nanji. In Mangwon the practical choice is **Ttareungyi**, Seoul's public bike-share.

- Day pass: **₩1,000 for 1 hour, ₩2,000 for 2 hours** per ride
- Over time: **₩200 per 5 minutes**
- For 24 hours you can **re-rent as often as you like as long as each ride stays within the time limit** (Seoul city media).
- Foreign visitors choose the foreigner option in the app or website and pay with an **overseas-issued credit card**, one pass per card (Ttareungyi FAQ).

### Hangang Bus from Mangwon pier

The **Hangang Bus**, the city's river ferry service, stops at **Mangwon pier**. Since all routes resumed on 1 March 2026, services split at Yeouido into an eastern leg (towards Jamsil) and a western leg (towards Magok); Mangwon is on the western leg.

- Weekly Donga reports **40 sailings a day, 11:00–21:30, weekdays and weekends**. There are no sailings before 9 am.
- At weekends Yeouido pier uses **numbered queue tickets**, and the last boats can fill early, the same report says.
- The city is pushing a **₩6,000 weekend and holiday adult fare from April 2027**, keeping weekdays at ₩3,000 and adding ₩2,000 for optional advance booking (Dailian, from a 29 September public hearing). It does not affect autumn 2026 trips.
- Timetables and temporary skipped stops change often. **Check the Hangang Bus website on the day.**

There is **no pool at Mangwon**, and the Hangang Park HQ has announced that all Han River outdoor pools have closed for 2026.

## Mangwonjeong: the pavilion with two names

Mangwon-dong takes its name from this pavilion, though its address is in **Hapjeong-dong** (23 Donggyo-ro 8an-gil). It is registered as the **"Site of Mangwonjeong"**, a **Seoul Metropolitan City Monument** designated on 18 June 1990.

Putting KTO data and Seoul city media together:

- **1424**: **Grand Prince Hyoryeong**, King Sejong's elder brother, built a retreat here.
- The following year King Sejong stopped by to inspect farming just as welcome rain fell, and named it **Huiujeong**, "pavilion of happy rain".
- **1484**: **Grand Prince Wolsan**, King Seongjong's elder brother, rebuilt it and renamed it **Mangwonjeong**, "pavilion for gazing far".
- Under King Yeonsan it was enlarged to seat over a thousand people and renamed Suryeojeong, then reverted to Mangwonjeong after he was deposed the same year.
- It vanished after the **Great Flood of 1925** and later river works, and was rebuilt between **June 1988 and October 1989**.

That is why **"Mangwonjeong" hangs outside and "Huiujeong" inside**. Seoul city media say you can see the Han River and apartment blocks across it from the pavilion, with the Gangbyeon expressway passing right in front.

- The same article describes a low gate at the end of an alley and **a two-storey pavilion at the top of steep steps**.
- **KTO data lists it as always open, every day.** It is managed by Mapo-gu.
- About 870 m from exit 8 of Hapjeong Station, and **about ten minutes' walk from Mangwon Market**.

## Haneul Park: the Seoul Silver Grass Festival, 17–23 October

![Haneul Park in autumn, a dirt path lined with rope fencing running through silver grass in white bloom, three wind turbines in the distance](/images/yeonnam-things-to-do-2026-09/10.webp)

Two stops west of Mangwon on [[line:6]] (Mapo-gu Office, then World Cup Stadium) lies **World Cup Park**. According to the city, it sits on **Nanjido, where Seoul buried its rubbish for 15 years from 1978 to 1993**; stabilisation began in 1996 and the park **opened in May 2002**. It is split into five theme parks: Peace Park, Haneul Park, Noeul Park, Nanjicheon Park and Nanji Hangang Park.

**Haneul Park** ("sky park") is the grassland on top of the second landfill mound.

- **About 190,000 m²** (city). Herald Economy (25 September) describes a gentle plateau **98 m above sea level**.
- The mound is divided into four sectors in an X shape along drainage ridges; **silver grass and cogon grass were planted in the tall-grass sector to the north**.
- From the top, the city says, you see Bukhansan to the north, Namsan and the 63 Building to the east, the Han River to the south and Haengju Fortress to the west.
- Facilities: a visitor centre, **five wind turbines**, 20 viewpoints, five toilets, the **Haneul Stairs** and the "Bowl Holding the Sky" observation structure.
- By late September visitors were already coming for the silver grass and kochia, Tour Korea reported (28 September).

### 25th Seoul Silver Grass Festival

From the city's notice of 28 September:

- **Title: "Silver Grass, Comfort Offered by Nature"**
- **Dates: Saturday 17 to Friday 23 October 2026, 10:00–21:00**
- Venue: Haneul Park, World Cup Park
- Enquiries: Western Parks Leisure Center, 02-300-5574
- The city warns that **traffic is extremely heavy** and asks visitors to use public transport.

World Cup Park says Haneul Park is normally **closed at night to protect wildlife but opens in the evening during the festival**. Herald Economy reports that lights go on around the festival period and that **crowds thin out from late October to early November** once it ends, a good alternative if you want the grass without the festival crush.

### Metasequoia path and Noeul Park

- **Haneul Park metasequoia path**: according to KTO, metasequoias were planted in 1999 along a **450 m eastern path and a 900 m southern path**, with a garden strip and a four-season flower path added in 2023. Always open.
- **Noeul Park** ("sunset park") covers about 340,000 m² on the first landfill mound. The city calls it the place to see **"Seoul's most beautiful sunset"**, with sculptures, viewing decks, a campsite and a park-golf course. Shuttle route 2 runs to the top.
