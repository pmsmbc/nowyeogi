How much should a taxi from Incheon Airport into Seoul cost? The short answer: a regular mid-size taxi in daytime runs about **₩70,000 (about $52)** to Myeongdong, Seoul Station or Yongsan Station and about **₩60,000** to Hongdae, plus a bridge toll of ₩2,000–3,200. Know those numbers and most overcharging attempts fall apart on the spot. This guide is built from the airport operator's and Seoul city government's official pages: fare estimates by destination, the flat-rate International Taxi, surcharges and tolls, which stand to queue at in each terminal, and the phone numbers to call when a fare looks wrong. Figures are as of October 2026.

![Silver and orange Seoul taxis lined up on a Dongdaemun street at night](/images/incheon-airport-taxi-2026-10/01.webp)

## The short version

- **Regular mid-size taxi** (metered): Hongdae about ₩55,000–65,000, Myeongdong, Seoul Station and Yongsan about ₩65,000–75,000. Tolls are paid separately.
- **International Taxi** (flat rate, foreign visitors only): Hongdae ₩75,000, Myeongdong, Seoul Station and Yongsan ₩85,000. Tolls included, price fixed in advance.
- A **night surcharge** of 20–40% applies between 22:00 and 04:00. There is **no out-of-city surcharge** from the airport to Seoul.
- Never follow anyone who approaches you inside the terminal asking "Taxi?". Board only at the marked stands, in a car that switches on the meter.
- If the fare looks wrong, note the plate number, keep the receipt, and call **1330** (24-hour, English) or **120** (Seoul city).

## Fare estimates by destination

Seoul's official fare table sets the mid-size taxi at **₩4,800 for the first 1.6 km**, then ₩100 per 131 m, plus ₩100 per 30 seconds whenever the car crawls below 15.72 km/h. The airport is roughly 50–60 km from central Seoul, so distance alone comes to nearly ₩50,000, and time charges in traffic push the total into the ₩60,000–70,000 range. Travel guides' 2026 estimates land in the same band.

| Destination | Regular mid-size (daytime, toll extra) | International Taxi flat rate (toll included) | Deluxe or large flat rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hongik Univ. Station, Hongdae (Mapo-gu, Zone B) | about ₩55,000–65,000 | ₩75,000 | ₩110,000 |
| Myeongdong (Jung-gu, Zone C) | about ₩65,000–75,000 | ₩85,000 | ₩120,000 |
| Seoul Station (Jung-gu / Yongsan-gu, Zone C) | about ₩65,000–75,000 | ₩85,000 | ₩120,000 |
| Yongsan Station (Yongsan-gu, Zone C) | similar to Seoul Station | ₩85,000 | ₩120,000 |

Yongsan Station sits in the same Zone C as Seoul Station at a similar distance, so expect the same metered range. Journey times depend on traffic: about 45–55 minutes to Hongdae and 60–70 minutes to Myeongdong or Seoul Station, stretching to 90 minutes in rush hour. Typing your destination into Kakao T or Uber shows an estimated fare before you ride, which makes a handy reference point.

**Passengers pay the toll**, and the amount depends on which bridge the driver takes. According to the transport ministry, the **Incheon Bridge** toll dropped from ₩5,500 to **₩2,000** for cars on 18 December 2025, and the **Yeongjong Bridge** on the airport expressway has charged **₩3,200** since October 2023. The final fare is the meter plus that toll. Since December 2025 Seoul taxi receipts print "Meter Fare" and "Toll fee" in English on separate lines, so you can check the two parts.

## The International Taxi flat rate

Seoul has run the **International Taxi** service for foreign visitors since 2009, and between the airport and Seoul it charges a **flat zone fare**. The city's table lists Zone A (Gangseo-gu) ₩70,000, Zone B (Mapo, Seodaemun, Eunpyeong, Yeongdeungpo, Guro, Yangcheon) ₩75,000 and Zone C (Jung, Jongno, Yongsan, Dongjak, Gwanak, Geumcheon) ₩85,000, with large and deluxe cars at ₩100,000, ₩110,000 and ₩120,000. **Tolls are included**, so nothing is added at the end.

Book on the website (English, Japanese, Chinese) or app **at least 24 hours ahead**, or call 1644-2255. Walk-up requests are taken at the information desks in both terminals. The dedicated stands are T1 4C and T2 3C. Foreign credit cards, Korean cards, transit cards and cash are all accepted. It costs ₩10,000–20,000 more than the meter, but there is no haggling and the drivers speak a foreign language. Seoul's page notes that International Taxis on the meter add a 20% surcharge, so from the airport the flat rate is the better deal.

## When surcharges apply

The **night surcharge** for Seoul and Incheon regular taxis is 20% from 22:00 to 23:00 and 02:00 to 04:00, and 40% from 23:00 to 02:00. The base fare rises to ₩5,800 and ₩6,700 respectively, and distance and time charges go up by the same share. Land at 23:00 and a ₩70,000 daytime ride to Myeongdong can come to ₩90,000-plus. Gyeonggi taxis add 30% from 23:00 to 04:00; deluxe and large taxis add 20% from 22:00 to 04:00.

**There is no out-of-city surcharge from Incheon Airport to Seoul, Incheon, Goyang, Gwangmyeong, Gimpo or Bucheon.** Both the airport operator and Seoul city say so. A driver adding 20% "because we're leaving the airport area" has no basis for it. It is one of the classic overcharges: in a January 2026 press release Seoul described penalising a driver who applied the out-of-city surcharge on a Hongdae to airport run.

## Taxi stands by terminal

In both terminals the stands are on the **kerb outside the first-floor arrivals hall**. The airport's own list:

- **Terminal 1, 1F**: 5C, 6C and 6D regular taxis (Seoul, Gyeonggi and Incheon cars queue in separate bays), 5D Incheon taxis, 7C deluxe, 8C large (9-seat), 4C International Taxi, 1C and 14C pre-booked taxis
- **Terminal 2, 1F**: 7C Seoul taxis, 5D Incheon taxis, 6D Gyeonggi taxis (Bucheon, Gwangmyeong, Gimpo, Goyang), 5C out-of-area taxis, 7D deluxe and large, 3C International and Smart taxis, 1C and 10C pre-booked taxis

![Deluxe taxi stand 8C on the first floor of Incheon Airport Terminal 1](/images/incheon-airport-taxi-2026-10/07.webp)

If you are heading into Seoul, join the **Seoul taxi** queue. Seoul, Incheon and Gyeonggi taxis all work the airport, but Seoul cars carry "서울" (Seoul) on the roof light and licence plate and charge straight off the Seoul fare table. The airport website shows how many taxis are waiting at each terminal in real time, which helps if the queue looks long.

## How to spot a scam or illegal taxi

The airport's taxi page carries a blunt warning: call-vans posing as taxis tout for passengers and overcharge them, and it happens often. A Channel A report from November 2025 filmed unlicensed private cars with a taxi sticker whose drivers approached arriving passengers inside the terminal with "Taxi? Where to?", ran without a meter, took cash only, and quoted ₩130,000 to Yeouido. One driver caught by inspectors claimed his car was "an Uber". Such operators face up to two years in prison or a ₩20 million fine under the passenger transport law, and Seoul pays a ₩200,000 reward per report.

Walk away if any of these apply:

- Someone approaches you **inside the terminal or at the exit** rather than waiting at a stand
- No roof light, no yellow licence plate, no **driver ID card** displayed inside the car
- The driver offers a "flat price" and **does not start the meter** (the only legitimate flat rates are official schemes like the International Taxi)
- **Cash only**, or the card reader is "broken"
- An extra charge for the **out-of-city surcharge** or for luggage on a trip to Seoul

A legitimate taxi starts the meter when it pulls away, takes cards, and hands you a receipt. Keep a map app open and you will see at once if the route wanders.

## Hotlines and what to keep

To file a complaint you need the **plate number**, the **date and time**, the **receipt** (printed automatically for card payments; ask for one if you pay cash), and ideally photos of the meter and route. If you hailed through an app, the ride record in the app is the strongest evidence.

| Situation | Number | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tourist complaints and interpretation | **1330** (+82-2-1330 from abroad) | Korea Tourism Organization, 24 hours, English, Chinese, Japanese · can connect you to the tourist police |
| Overcharging or refusal by a Seoul taxi | **120** (02-120 from a mobile or outside Seoul, +82-2-731-2120 from abroad) | Seoul Dasan Call Centre, Korean 24 hours · foreign-language agents weekdays 09:00–18:00 |
| Complaint about an Incheon taxi | **032-120** | Incheon Michuhol Call Centre, press 2 for transport |
| Unlicensed vehicle, threats, refusing to let you out | **112** | Police |
| Touting or stand problems at the airport | **1577-2600** | Incheon Airport customer centre, 07:00–22:00 |

Foreign visitors can also use Seoul's **QR complaint survey**. Since 19 June 2025 the city has handed out business cards with an English, Chinese and Japanese survey QR code in the departure halls of Incheon and Gimpo airports and at tourist information centres, and the same code is on stickers inside taxis. Answers are matched against the vehicle's plate and meter log and lead to fines. Seoul counted 487 reports from foreigners in the first six months, overcharging the most common. Penalties for overcharging run from a ₩200,000 fine for a first offence to ₩400,000 plus a 30-day suspension, then ₩600,000 plus loss of the taxi licence. A report to 120 gets a result by text or post within 15–30 days.

## Practical notes

- **Apps**: Kakao T and Uber (branded UT in Korea) both work at the airport. The estimated fare and route are logged, which settles most disputes. Uber charges the card on your existing account.
- **Sanity check**: anything over ₩100,000 to central Seoul in daytime is suspect. Even with the 40% night surcharge, Myeongdong comes to roughly ₩100,000.
- **Luggage**: a mid-size taxi's boot takes two large suitcases at most. For three or four people with bags, a 9-seat large taxi or the International Taxi's large car is easier.
- **Payment**: regular taxis take cards, transit cards and cash, and most foreign cards go through.
- **Alternatives**: the all-stop AREX train reaches Hongik Univ. Station and Seoul Station for at most ₩5,350, and airport limousine buses [[bus:6001]] and [[bus:6002]] cost ₩17,000. Travelling alone, that is a tenth of the taxi fare or less.

## Wrapping up

From Incheon Airport to central Seoul, a daytime taxi is ₩60,000–70,000 plus a few thousand won in tolls, with surcharges at night. If a quote is far above that or the meter stays off, get out, photograph the plate, and call 1330 or 120.
