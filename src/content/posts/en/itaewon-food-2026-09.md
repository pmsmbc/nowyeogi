Itaewon is where Seoul goes for food that isn't Korean, but "Itaewon" covers three quite different eating areas: the **halal streets below the mosque on Usadan-ro**, the hillside cafés of **Gyeongnidan-gil and Haebangchon** above Noksapyeong Station, and a handful of **MICHELIN-listed restaurants** scattered between them.

The short version: **the MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026 lists seven places here (Mosu with two stars, Soul with one, ALT.a as a Bib Gourmand and four Selected restaurants); the Korea Tourism Organization's Muslim-friendly list has 22 Itaewon restaurants, but it dates from December 2021; and the cheapest proper meal is ₩8,000 (about $6) stew or noodles at Bogwangjeong, a government "good-price" business.** Every place below comes from MICHELIN, the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), the Korea Muslim Federation, the Ministry of the Interior and Safety or Seoul city sources, and anything about food or atmosphere is taken from those write-ups.

## What this guide covers

This guide covers the administrative neighbourhoods of **Itaewon 1-dong and 2-dong** and **Yongsan 2-ga-dong**, which is where Haebangchon sits. On the map, that is the stretch of Itaewon-ro between Itaewon and Noksapyeong stations, Usadan-ro, the northern end of Bogwang-ro, Hoenamu-ro and Gyeongnidan-gil (Noksapyeong-daero), and Sinheung-ro in Haebangchon.

**Hannam-dong has its own guide: [Where to eat in Hannam-dong](/en/posts/hannam-food-2026-09/).** The MICHELIN restaurants near Hangangjin Station (Soseoul Hannam, Escondido, Youhan and others), UN Village and Dokseodang-ro, and Hannam's one good-price restaurant are there.

One boundary is blurry. **Seoul Central Mosque (39 Usadan-ro 10-gil) is legally in Hannam-dong**, and the Korea Muslim Federation describes it as being on "Usadan-ro (Hannam-dong)". But KTO calls the area "Itaewon Islamic Street" and "Itaewon Usadan-gil", and most halal restaurants are on the climb up from Itaewon Station, so **the whole halal list is kept in this guide.** Arabesque on Itaewon-ro 227 also has a Hannam-dong address. Going the other way, the MICHELIN-Selected lamb restaurant Kyoyang Siksa at Itaewon-ro 238 is in Hannam-dong and appears in the Hannam guide.

## MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026: seven places in Itaewon, Haebangchon and Gyeongnidan-gil

MICHELIN announced its 2026 Seoul stars on **5 March 2026**. Checking every Seoul restaurant on the official site against its address gives seven in Itaewon-dong and Yongsan-dong 2-ga.

**The ₩ symbols are bands, not prices.** MICHELIN's Korean pages gloss ₩ as roughly "don't hesitate" and ₩₩₩₩ as "for a very special occasion". In Korea, the Bib Gourmand marks places where MICHELIN says you can eat for ₩45,000 (about $34) or less per person. "Selected" means the restaurant is in the guide without a star or Bib.

### Mosu: the new two-star on Hoenamu-ro

**4 Hoenamu-ro 41-gil**, up the hill from Noksapyeong. Mosu was one of the **new two-star restaurants** in 2026. MICHELIN singles out the abalone taco, sesame tofu and burdock tarte as signatures, praises the sourdough, and notes the converted two-storey house: an open kitchen on the ground floor and sunset light upstairs. Wine pairing is part of the offer.

### Soul and Egg & Flour: two listings in one Haebangchon building

**35 Sinheung-ro 26-gil** holds two MICHELIN entries.

- **Soul** (basement, One Star) — MICHELIN describes it as a bold fine-dining project by chef Yoon Dae-hyun with his wife and fellow chef Kim Hee-eun, drawing on the ingredients and everyday flavours of modern Korea. The building is split into several spaces for different styles of dining.
- **Egg & Flour** (Selected) — named for the two ingredients of fresh pasta, it makes its own capellini, reginette, tagliatelle, rigatoni and pappardelle. MICHELIN mentions window seats looking over Haebangchon at dusk. **MICHELIN's Korean page puts it on the third floor and the English page on the second.**

### ALT.a: a vegan Chinese Bib Gourmand

**109 Bogwang-ro**. Everything is made with plant ingredients and meat substitutes. MICHELIN's write-up mentions sweet-and-sour "pork", fried dishes in yuringi style and a vegan jjamppong (spicy seafood-style noodle soup), and calls it a good restaurant "both as a vegan place and as a Chinese restaurant". It carries the lowest ₩ band and is tagged as good for solo diners. The street next door, around 109-1 Bogwang-ro, is what KTO promotes as the **Itaewon Antique Furniture Street**.

**None of the eight new 2026 Bib Gourmands are in this area**; ALT.a is a continuing listing.

### Three Selected restaurants on Hoenamu-ro and Noksapyeong-daero

- **Chez Simon** (3F, 66 Hoenamu-ro) — a French restaurant that moved from Samcheong-dong to Seogyo-dong and then to Itaewon. MICHELIN says the couple who run it close for part of each year to travel and research ingredients, and there is **only one set course**.

## Usadan-ro halal restaurants: the KTO list (as of December 2021)

### Seoul Central Mosque and Itaewon Islamic Street

The halal cluster grew up around **Seoul Central Mosque**. According to the Korea Muslim Federation (KMF), the government provided the land in May 1969, Saudi Arabia and other Muslim countries funded construction, work began in **October 1974**, and the mosque **opened on 21 May 1976** as the first in Korea.

KTO calls the area **Itaewon Islamic Street**. Take exit 3 of Itaewon Station on [[line:6]] and walk up the lane past Bogwang Elementary School: Arabic signs start to outnumber Korean and English ones, and around the mosque you find an Islamic school, **halal butchers and grocers**, halal restaurants and Islamic bookshops. KTO describes the same streets as **Itaewon Usadan-gil**, with Pakistani, Turkish, Egyptian, Lebanese and Indian restaurants, and young artists and start-ups arriving from the early 2010s.

### KTO's four categories

KTO's English site sorts Muslim-friendly restaurants into **four groups**:

- **Halal Certified** — certified by an accredited halal body such as the Korea Muslim Federation (KMF)
- **Self-certified** — all food is halal, as declared by the Muslim owner
- **Muslim Friendly** — some halal dishes; **alcohol may be sold**
- **Pork Free** — no halal menu, but no pork; alcohol may be sold

The same page warns that **hotel buffets in the Muslim Friendly group may still serve dishes containing pork**.

### Checking halal status today

**The list is nearly five years old.** Kuki News reported that KTO ran the categorisation programme only until 2021.

KMF's certification work is now handled through the **Korea Halal Industry Promotion Institute** website, which links to a public spreadsheet of **KMF-certified companies**. In the copy downloaded on 6 October 2026, **five of the 1,122 rows** were in the "restaurant" category, and **none of the restaurant names in the table above appeared**. The sheet gives company names only, without addresses, so it does not show where those five are.

So treat the table as **"places KTO categorised in 2021", not "places certified now"**. If certification matters to you, look at the certificate on the wall and check the **issuing body and expiry date**. The institute's fee table lists certification in one-year (new) and three-year (renewal) terms, so certificates do expire.

## Haebangchon Sinheung Market

Haebangchon ("liberation village") began after **liberation in 1945**, when people displaced from the North, Koreans returning from abroad and later war refugees settled on the slope below Namsan (KTO). **Sinheung Market** is its market.

- It served as the neighbourhood's daily-goods market **from 1953** and declined from the 1990s (Seoul city media, 2023).
- In the **early 1960s** shacks were replaced with cement buildings, giving the market its continuous slate roof.
- Seoul designated Haebangchon an urban regeneration area in **December 2015**. In **November 2016**, 44 building and land owners and 46 tenants signed an agreement to **keep rent rises within inflation for six years** (Seoul Metropolitan Government, 2021).
- The new roof that replaced the old slate, called **"Cloud", won the grand prize at the 42nd Seoul Architecture Awards** (Seoul city media, 2024).

Seoul's 2021 release described a mix of cafés, leather workshops and older shops that still look like the 1970s and 1980s. **Hours vary by shop.**

Places in Haebangchon that official sources name:

- **Soul** (One Star) and **Egg & Flour** (Selected) — 35 Sinheung-ro 26-gil
- **Cueva Matera** (28-1 Sowol-ro 20-gil) — KTO calls it the most distinctive café in Sinheung Market. You enter through a wardrobe door into a cave-like room modelled on the cave dwellings of Matera in Italy; there are nine flavours of dacquoise and cheesecake. 12:00–20:00
- **Haebangchon Hongo** (89 Sinheung-ro) — a charcoal brazier grill. KTO says there is a single-diner set of beef, chicken neck, mushrooms, sausage, grilling cheese and a highball. From 17:00; closed Tuesdays
- **Myeonmaru** (86 Sinheung-ro) and **Namsan Bulpan** (10 Sowol-ro 20-gil) — good-price listings

Haebangchon is steep. KTO notes that an **inclined lift** has been installed beside the area's well-known 108 Steps.

## Good to know

**Don't go by the word "halal" alone.** In KTO's system, Muslim Friendly and Pork Free places **may sell alcohol**. The certificate's issuer and expiry date tell you more than a sign.

**Usadan-ro and Haebangchon are residential.** The lanes are narrow and steep; keep the noise down late at night. KTO itself describes Usadan-gil as a district of old houses and alleys.

**Book ahead where noted.** MICHELIN says Chez Nous opens for dinner only and needs a reservation.

## What we got wrong

The version published on 14 September 2026 contained a lot that had no source. Corrections:

- It said **Muslim Friendly and Self-certified restaurants use no pork or alcohol**. That does not match KTO's definitions: Self-certified means the owner declares all food halal, and **Muslim Friendly places may sell alcohol**. The definitions now follow KTO's wording.
- The flat statement that **halal restaurants never sell alcohol and you cannot bring your own** had no source and has been removed.
- It implied the Korea Muslim Federation is the only certifier and that the 2021 categories still apply. KTO says "an accredited body such as KMF", and **the current KMF certified list does not contain those restaurant names**; both points are now stated.
- **Price ranges** such as ₩12,000–18,000 for döner, ₩15,000–22,000 for curry sets, ₩8,000–12,000 for a pint and ₩18,000–28,000 for Hannam brunch had no source and are gone. The only prices here are published good-price figures.
- Claims that **Korean craft beer started in Itaewon**, that every place takes cards and that small shops close on Mondays were also unsourced and have been removed.
- Hannam-dong brunch and coffee moved to the [Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-food-2026-09/).
- The craft-beer photo showed a bar in Britain. Photos are now generic food shots, labelled as such, and KTO's own photos of Itaewon Islamic Street.
- The earlier [Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-food-2026-09/) said the Korea Muslim Federation website was unreachable. It loaded this time, which is how the mosque history and certified list were checked.

## What we could not confirm

**Which Itaewon restaurants hold halal certification now.** The five restaurant entries on the KMF list have names only, no locations, and none match the 2021 list.

**Whether all 22 restaurants from 2021 are still open.** Only Petra has a recent KTO update (December 2025).

**Usmania's address.** On the booklet page it overlaps with the neighbouring entry, so it is left blank.

**Egg & Flour's floor.** MICHELIN's Korean and English pages disagree.

**Menu prices and exact hours at the MICHELIN restaurants.** Check with each restaurant.

**Seoul Future Heritage, Seoul Oraegage (long-running shops) or Baeknyeon Gage (century shops) designations for restaurants here.** The earlier version found that Yongsan's Oraegage shops in this area are not restaurants; the other two lists were not cross-checked.

**Myeonmaru's neighbourhood.** Its published address has no dong; by street number it is grouped with Haebangchon.
