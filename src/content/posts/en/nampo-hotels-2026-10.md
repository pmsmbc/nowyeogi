Staying in old-town Busan means choosing the harbour and the markets over the beach. There is no five-star cluster here as in Haeundae; instead there are three quite different bases within a couple of metro stops of each other: business hotels you can walk to from the KTX platform, mid-range hotels that have stood beside the markets since the 1970s, and a tall bridge-view hotel across the water on Yeongdo. This guide splits the area into **Busan Station, Nampo-dong with Jungang-dong, and Yeongdo**, lists the hotels we could verify, and explains how to reach each one. Room rates swing too much by date to quote; transport fares are as of October 2026.

![Nampo-dong seen from above, with the Jagalchi Market building and fishing boats on the water to the left, dense blocks along the main road and the white Busan Tower on the hill to the right](/images/nampo-hotels-2026-10/20.webp)

All three sit on [[line:busan1]]. From Busan Station the stops run Jungang, Nampo, Jagalchi and Toseong; Nampo is two stops away.

## Busan Station: the business hotel row

Cross the road from the station plaza and the hotels are in one block, a few minutes' walk from the KTX and SRT platforms and with the international ferry terminal also on foot. This is the base for a trip built around trains and boats.

**Ramada Encore by Wyndham Busan Station** (10 Jungang-daero 196beon-gil, Dong-gu) is the biggest. Korea Tourism Organization data lists **439 rooms**, and the top floor has a rooftop infinity pool and lounge facing Busan Harbor Bridge. Check-in is 15:00 and check-out 11:00.

In the next lane, **Toyoko Inn Busan Station 1** (12 Jungang-daero 196beon-gil) runs the Japanese chain's stripped-down formula: no extras and a lower price. Note that **Toyoko Inn Busan Station 2** is not next door; it is at 125 Jungang-daero in Jung-gu, by Jungang Station, so check the address when you book. Across the main road, Choryang-dong (Chinatown and the old Texas Street) holds most of the cheaper hotels and guesthouses; the Texas Street lanes take on a bar-district feel after dark, so check which lane a listing sits on.

### The ferry terminal and the cruise pier

**Busan Port International Passenger Terminal** (206 Chungjang-daero, Dong-gu) is, by the Busan Port Authority's own directions, **10 to 15 minutes** from Exit 10 of the station building by moving walkway and the harbour walking deck. Ferries to Fukuoka, Tsushima, Shimonoseki and Osaka sail from here. Large cruise ships berth instead at the **Yeongdo Cruise Terminal** in Dongsam-dong (17 Haeyang-ro 301beon-gil), a pier for one ship of up to 220,000 GT. The port authority's directions are bus 101 or 88 from outside Busan Station to Dongsam-samgeori plus a 15-minute walk, or Line 1 to Nampo and a taxi; with luggage, the taxi is the realistic choice.

## Nampo-dong and Jungang-dong: markets at the door

![The pedestrian Gwangbok-ro in Nampo-dong under a blue sky, with the Arirang Street arch and rows of café and shop signs](/images/nampo-hotels-2026-10/18.webp)

**Commodore Hotel Busan** (151 Jung-gu-ro, Yeongju-dong) opened in July 1979 and was designed on a Joseon palace theme; the roofline is visible from well down the hill. The tourism data lists **311 rooms**. It is up a slope, and the hotel itself recommends a taxi from Exit 7 of Busan Station, about 8 to 10 minutes. The reward is a harbour view at night; the cost is needing a taxi back from the markets late.

**Hotel Foret Premier Nampo** (54-1 Gudeok-ro) sits between Jagalchi and Nampo stations, about six minutes' walk from either. Gukje Market, Jagalchi and Yongdusan Park are all on foot, which makes it the easiest choice for a market-centred trip.

Below Yongdusan Park are two small survivors, **Busan Tourist Hotel** (23 Gwangbok-ro 97beon-gil, open since 1974) and **Towerhill Hotel** (20 Baeksan-gil, opened as the Tower Hotel in 1975 and refitted in 2013). Neither is large, but Gwangbok-ro and Gukje Market are at the door.

![The statue of Admiral Yi Sun-sin in Yongdusan Park with the white Busan Tower rising behind it against a clear sky](/images/nampo-hotels-2026-10/04.webp)

The downsides are real. Daytime is crowded with market traffic, the streets empty after about ten at night, there is no beach, and Gwangalli or Haeundae is close to an hour by metro. Yeongju-dong and Donggwang-dong are built on slopes, so with big luggage check whether a hotel is on flat ground.

## Yeongdo: the view hotel across the bridge

![Yeongdo Bridge from above, with a tall dark-glass hotel tower at the Yeongdo end of the bridge and a harbour full of fishing boats behind it](/images/nampo-hotels-2026-10/30.webp)

**Lavalse Hotel** (82 Bongnaenaru-ro, Yeongdo-gu) is the tall tower standing where Yeongdo Bridge lands on the island. Tourism data lists **381 rooms**, most of them sea-facing, and the hotel says that depending on room type the view takes in Namhang Bridge, Yeongdo Bridge, Busan Bridge or Busan Harbor Bridge. Its directions give 10 minutes on foot from Nampo Station and about 40 minutes by car from Gimhae Airport. The Nampo-dong markets are one bridge away; Huinnyeoul Culture Village and Taejongdae are a bus or taxi ride.

![The coastal path at Huinnyeoul Culture Village, white houses above the sea and the Namhang Bridge in the distance](/images/nampo-hotels-2026-10/22.webp)

Yeongdo is quieter than Nampo-dong and late-night dining is thin. The reason to choose it is what is outside the window.

![The two illuminated towers and cables of Busan Harbor Bridge at night, with Lotte Mall Gwangbok and the port lights in the foreground](/images/nampo-hotels-2026-10/23.webp)

## Practical notes

### Gimhae Airport to Busan Station and Nampo-dong

- **Light rail and metro**: from the [[line:gimhaelrt]] Airport station ride to Sasang, change to [[line:busan2]] for Seomyeon, then [[line:busan1]] to Busan Station or Nampo. Busan metro fares with a transit card are ₩1,600 (about $1.20) for zone 1 and ₩1,800 for zone 2, and the light rail uses the same structure. Since 19 September 2025 there is no transfer surcharge between Busan, Gimhae and Yangsan services: change within 30 minutes of tapping out and you pay only the highest single fare. Budget ₩1,800 and 50 minutes to an hour
- **Limousine bus**: after the January 2026 reorganisation, **Airport Limousine 2** (Seomyeon and Bujeon) costs ₩6,000. Nothing runs direct to the old town, so change at Seomyeon to [[line:busan1]]
- **Taxi**: the Commodore quotes about 35 minutes from the airport and the Lavalse about 40
- **From Seoul**: KTX from Seoul Station takes around two and a half hours

### Getting around the area

- **Gamcheon Culture Village**: Exit 6 of Toseong Station on [[line:busan1]], then village bus Saha 1-1 or Seo-gu 2. Nampo-dong is the closest base for it
- **Busan International Film Festival** runs 6 to 15 October 2026. Nampo-dong gets busier on BIFF Square event days, though it is the Haeundae and Centum City hotels that fill up first
- The Commodore, Busan Tourist Hotel and Towerhill are on a hill or in narrow lanes, so take a taxi with big suitcases; the Lavalse's bridge view depends on the room type, so ask when booking. Hotel signage here is in Korean and English, and the Toyoko Inns also handle Japanese

## Wrapping up

The three parts of old-town Busan have clear personalities. If your trip revolves around trains and ferries, stay **by Busan Station**; if it is about markets and lanes, stay in **Nampo-dong**; if the view matters most, cross to **Yeongdo**. For how this area compares with Haeundae and Seomyeon, see [Where to Stay in Busan](/en/posts/busan-hotels-2026-09/), and for what to do once you are here, [Things to Do in Nampo-dong and Jung-gu](/en/posts/nampo-things-to-do-2026-10/).
