A three-metre shark has been living in a landscaped canal a short walk behind Busan Station for almost a month. Locals have named it **Bukangi (부캉이)**, and over the Chuseok holiday it drew 100,000 visitors in a single day. This guide covers what kind of shark it is, where in the park to look, how to walk there from the station, and why you should not go after dark. Everything is as of 13 October 2026 and comes from official statements and Korean press reports. The shark could swim out to sea at any time, so check the news before you make the trip.

![North Port Waterfront Park seen from above: the plaza, the canal, a footbridge and Busan Harbour Bridge in the distance](/images/busan-bukhang-shark-2026-10/04.webp)

## What Bukangi is

The shark was first spotted on 18 September 2026 in the artificial canal of North Port Waterfront Park (북항 친수공원). From its shape alone it could have been a spinner shark or a blacktip, and on 6 October the National Institute of Fisheries Science confirmed it as a **blacktip shark** (*Carcharhinus limbatus*) after analysing close-up footage: a clear ridge between the two dorsal fins settles the question. It measures about 3 m, judged against fixed objects nearby, and the shape of the underside shows it is female.

![A blacktip shark swimming side-on in blue water, with black-tipped fins](/images/busan-bukhang-shark-2026-10/06.webp)

Blacktips are 70 to 100 cm at birth and grow to about 4.2 m. The species is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List, and it was only recorded in Korean waters for the first time in 2019. On 12 October a video showing something red hanging from the shark's belly set off rumours that it had given birth; the institute explained it as a shark everting part of its intestine to rinse it, a normal behaviour.

## Why it is still in the canal

The authorities have tried twice to move it out to sea. On 29 September they sprayed jets of water on the surface to drive it with vibration, without effect. On 2 October they stretched a net 50 m long and 6.8 m deep across the canal near footbridge 4 and pushed towards footbridge 6, the seaward exit. The net snagged on the canal floor and the shark would not head out. The next day's high tide lifted the water enough for Bukangi to swim back over the net, and the net was then removed. The current plan is to observe it and wait for it to leave on its own. At a National Assembly hearing, lawmakers raised concerns that the shallow canal is prone to temperature swings and called for a proper rescue protocol.

## Where to look

The canal runs through the middle of the park, with several numbered footbridges crossing it. As of 4 October the shark was circling **between footbridges 4 and 5**. Visitors line the railings along the canal or watch from the bridges. It moves around, so in practice you follow the crowd.

![Close-up of a blacktip shark's head and dorsal fin, with a small remora under its gills](/images/busan-bukhang-shark-2026-10/08.webp)

There is no admission charge, but expect company. On 24 September the park counted 102,000 visitors, fifty times a normal holiday, and the running total passed 900,000 in early October. Passenger numbers at Busan Station on the first weekend of October were up 47 percent on a month earlier. Weekday mornings are the easiest time to get a spot at the railing.

## Do not go at night

To prevent accidents, **the canal area is closed from 18:00 to 06:00 every day**. The park is known for its night lighting, but evening viewing is off while the shark is there.

Some people have entered after closing and shone large torches and phone flashes on the water. On 7 October the shark was startled by a strong light, turned sharply and was found with a graze on its snout. Staff from the Busan Facilities Corporation, which runs the park, have no legal power to stop this, so residents and YouTubers have formed volunteer patrols to ask people to switch lights off. Even by day, do not throw food or anything else into the water.

## About the park

North Port Waterfront Park sits in the North Port redevelopment zone and is reached from Busan Station along an **elevated pedestrian walkway**. The address is 164 Yi Sun-sin-daero, Dong-gu. It has a wide lawn plaza, walking paths and a chain of footbridges over the canal, with views across to Busan Harbour Bridge and Yeongdo island. After dark the whole park is lit in colour, which has made it one of the city's newer night-view spots. In August 2026 the canal zone was opened for a stand-up paddleboard festival. The park is managed by the Busan Facilities Corporation; the canal is public water under the Busan Port Authority.

Nearby are the Choryang Ibagu-gil hillside lanes, the old port road and Choryang Chinatown. Dong-gu district runs a stamp tour linking the park with these old-town sights, so a natural plan is to see the shark and then walk up into Choryang for lunch.

## Practical notes

- **Getting there**: walk from KTX Busan Station along the pedestrian bridge. By metro, use Busan Station or Choryang Station on [[line:busan1]].
- **Parking**: there is a car park but it fills early on busy days and the surrounding roads jam. The city has sent out alerts asking people to use public transport.
- **Hours**: the canal area is open 06:00 to 18:00 only. In autumn it starts getting dark soon after 17:00.
- **Cost**: free.
- **Bring**: the canal is wide and the shark is often far off, so a camera with a zoom helps. Keep the flash off.
- **Check first**: whether the shark has left and whether the closure hours have changed.

## Wrapping up

A live three-metre blacktip within walking distance of a KTX station does not happen often. Bukangi is not an exhibit, though: it is a wild shark that took a wrong turn. Watch quietly by day, stay away at night, and the visit is safe for everyone involved. The park is worth a walk even after the shark is gone.
