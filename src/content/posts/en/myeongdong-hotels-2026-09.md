Myeongdong has more hotel rooms per block than anywhere else in Seoul, which is why it is the default for a first trip. The choice is less about which hotel is best and more about **which side of the neighborhood you book** and **how small a room you can live with**. Here is how to decide.

Prices are per-night bands for a double room in low-to-mid season, from booking-site averages as of September 2026. Add 20–40% for April–May and September–October.

## Pick your side first

Myeongdong is small enough to walk end to end in twelve minutes, but the two halves feel different.

- **Namdaemun-ro side (west)**: the big properties. Wider rooms, quieter at night, a short walk to Namdaemun Market and Seoul Station. Best if you have luggage or want space.
- **Myeongdong-gil side (centre)**: you step out of the lobby into the shopping street. Convenient, louder, and rooms are generally smaller.
- **Cathedral side (east, toward Chungmuro)**: cheaper, calmer, more Korean restaurants nearby, five minutes further from the subway.

## Budget: ₩60,000–110,000

Business hotels, not hostels. Expect a clean 13–16㎡ room, a compact bathroom, a kettle, and no view. Wifi is fast everywhere in Korea, and even budget properties usually have a laundry room.

- **Hotel Skypark chain** — several branches within Myeongdong, all reliable and central. The branches differ mainly in age; the newer ones cost a little more.
- **Sotetsu Hotels The Splaisir Seoul Myeongdong** — Japanese-run, four-star rating at close to budget prices, consistently well reviewed for cleanliness.
- **Ibis Ambassador Myeongdong** — small rooms, good location, dependable.

**Guesthouses and hostels** in the ₩30,000–50,000 range exist here, but Hongdae has more of them and better ones. If a dorm bed is the plan, consider staying there instead and compare with our [neighborhood guide](/en/posts/seoul-where-to-stay-2026-09/).

## Mid-range: ₩150,000–250,000

This is the sweet spot in Myeongdong. Rooms grow to 18–24㎡, breakfast becomes worth eating, and most properties have a lounge or a rooftop.

- **L7 Myeongdong by Lotte** — design-led, rooftop bar with a Namsan view, right on the shopping street. The most recommended mid-range option for a reason.
- **Lotte City Hotel Myeongdong** — larger rooms than most, run by the same group, calmer feel.
- **Nine Tree Premier Hotel Myeongdong** — two properties in the area, both modern and popular with regional travellers.
- **Aloft Seoul Myeongdong** — bright rooms, Marriott points, on the Namdaemun-ro corner.
- **Loisir Hotel Seoul Myeongdong** — bigger rooms and a pool, on the quieter Chungmuro edge.

## Luxury: ₩350,000 and up

- **Lotte Hotel Seoul** — the grande dame, connected to the Lotte department store and duty-free, two towers, several restaurants. The Executive Tower is the newer half.
- **The Westin Josun Seoul** — a short walk west, Seoul's oldest hotel site, recently renovated, quiet grounds.
- **Fairmont, Four Seasons and Josun Palace** sit elsewhere in the city; in Myeongdong the two above are the flagship choices.

## Room sizes: set your expectations

A standard double in a mid-range Seoul hotel is often **15–18㎡**, which is small by European or American standards. Two large suitcases will not both open on the floor at once.

If you need space, three options work better than paying up for a bigger room:

1. **Serviced residences** — kitchenette, washing machine, more square metres, often cheaper per night for stays over four nights.
2. **Twin rooms instead of doubles** — usually larger footprints for the same money.
3. **The Namdaemun-ro properties** — older buildings with more generous floor plans.

## What to check before you book

- **The walk to the subway.** "300m from Myeongdong Station" can mean a hill. Look at the map, not the number.
- **Which exit.** Myeongdong Station exits 5–8 lead to the shopping streets; exits 1–4 come out on the far side of a busy road.
- **Whether the room has a window.** Some cheap central rooms genuinely do not, and listings phrase it as "city view: no".
- **Breakfast is often not worth it** in Myeongdong specifically, because you are surrounded by better and cheaper food.
- **City tax and service charge** are frequently added at the end. Compare final totals.
- **Cancellation windows** tighten during cherry blossom season and Chuseok.

## Booking timing

Peak weeks are early April (blossom) and late October (foliage), plus Korean holidays. Both sell out two to three months ahead and cost 20–40% more. December to February is the cheapest window and Seoul is genuinely pleasant if you dress for it.

## Wrapping up

For most first-time visitors the answer is a mid-range hotel on or just off Myeongdong-gil, booked six to eight weeks ahead, in a twin room if two of you are travelling with luggage. If you want space over convenience, move one block west to Namdaemun-ro.
