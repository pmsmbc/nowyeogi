Choosing a place to stay in Jinju is easy, because there is not much to choose between and the options divide cleanly into three. The short answer: **stay near the fortress if you are here as a tourist, and in Innovation City if you are here for work**.

This post is a summary of the lodging lists the city government publishes. There are no rates in it, for a reason explained further down: the official material does not contain a single price.

![Jinju seen from across the Namgang river, with apartment blocks and low city buildings reflected in the water and a ridge of hills behind](/images/jinju-hotels-2026-09/01.webp)

## The map in three lines

- **Around Jinju Fortress** — Bonseong-dong, Dongseong-dong, Insa-dong, Gye-dong, Jangdae-dong, Okbong-dong, Bonggok-dong. Overwhelmingly the largest supply, and right beside the sights.
- **Innovation City (Chungmugong-dong)** — where the business hotels are. Away from the centre.
- **Jinyangho lake (Panmun-dong)** — one property by the water. Quiet, but nothing at all within walking distance.

## Around the fortress: this is where the rooms are

The city maintains a separate list titled "lodgings near Jinju Fortress", and **114 properties** are on it. By legal-dong: 33 in Jangdae-dong, 29 in Bonggok-dong, 14 in Okbong-dong, 13 in Gye-dong, 11 in Bonseong-dong, 10 in Dongseong-dong and 4 in Insa-dong.

Adjust your expectations before reading that number as good news. **The overwhelming majority of those 114 are motels and inns**, and most have between 10 and 30 rooms. A long list is not the same thing as a choice of large hotels.

The larger properties on it are these.

- **Golden Tulip Hotel Namgang** — Dongseong-dong, 128 rooms. The biggest thing the city classifies as a hotel.
- **Hotel Dongbang** — Okbong-dong, 83 rooms.
- **Almond Hotel Jinjuseong** — Dongseong-dong, 77 rooms.

Domestic budget chains such as **Brown Dot, Swijahotel, Aneuk Air Hotel and Hotel Yaam** all have a Jinjuseong branch here too. Across the entire list there is exactly one property calling itself a guesthouse: **Jinju Guesthouse (The Family Hotel)**, at 9-3 Namgang-ro 633beon-gil in Bonseong-dong, with 16 rooms.

The case for this area is simple. **Jinju Fortress, Chokseongnu, the Jinju National Museum and the Namgang are all walkable.** The lantern festival takes place on the river right in front of it. If you are in Jinju as a tourist, there is not much reason to be anywhere else.

## Innovation City: for a work trip

Chungmugong-dong is Jinju's Innovation City. Of the ten properties the city classifies as hotels, **three are clustered here**: Raon Stay (164 Yeongcheongang-ro), New Raon Stay (166 Yeongcheongang-ro) and Golden Business Hotel (101 Ena-ro).

The character follows from the planning. This is a new district built around relocated public agencies, so what grew there is a **business hotel cluster**. The buildings are new and parking is easy, but reaching the fortress means driving.

The same neighbourhood has the Jinju Pterosaur Footprint Exhibition Hall and the National Copyright Museum. The latter is free and open 10:00 to 17:00 on weekdays.

## The other options

**Jinyangho** has one property, Asia Lakeside Hotel, at 133 Namgang-ro 1beon-gil, inside Jinyangho Park. It suits you if the lake and the sunset are the point, but you give up restaurants and any access to town on foot.

**Sinan-dong, Pyeonggeo-dong and Chiram-dong** are where the 58 properties the city files under "motels and inns" are spread. These are residential neighbourhoods, not tourist ones.

**All 25 pensions and minbak are out in the rural townships** — Daepyeong-myeon, Geumsan-myeon, Naedong-myeon and Jinseong-myeon. Without a car they are not an option.

**There is no hanok stay category in the city's official lodging classification at all.** If you are picturing Jeonju or Gyeongju, this is a different city.

**There are no international chain hotels either.** Of the ten properties classified as hotels, only Golden Tulip Hotel Namgang carries an international brand name, and whether it is actually affiliated is not stated on the official page. Marriott, Hilton, IHG and Accor do not appear on the list.

## Before you book

- **No rates are published.** None of the city's four lodging categories lists a price. You get the business name, address, phone number and room count, and nothing more. That is why there are no price bands in this post either — check a booking site directly.
- **Nor is there any note of English-language service.** Jinju does not get the volume of foreign visitors that Seoul or Busan does, so booking through an international platform is the safer route.
- **How rooms hold up during the lantern festival could not be confirmed** from official sources. But given that the city has around ten hotels in total and the festival is known nationally, booking ahead for October is the sensible assumption.
- **There is no separate lodging list for the Jinju Station area**, because the city organises its lists by legal-dong. The station does have a tourist information office (130 Jinjuyeok-ro, Gajwa-dong).

## So where should you stay

- **One night of sightseeing — near the fortress.** Everything is walkable, and the fortress gates open at 05:00, so you can start early.
- **A work trip, or you have a car — Innovation City.** New buildings and easy parking.
- **Quiet above all — Jinyangho**, as long as you accept that there is nowhere to go out for dinner.

## Wrapping up

Jinju is not a city to agonise over accommodation in. There are few options, the sightseeing is concentrated at the fortress, and that more or less settles the answer.

Just do not arrive expecting a big hotel. **In the whole city only one property has more than 100 rooms.** The flip side is that the city is small enough that wherever you end up, nothing takes long to reach.
