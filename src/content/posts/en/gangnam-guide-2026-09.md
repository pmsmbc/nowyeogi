On 3 and 4 October 2026, Dosan-daero in Gangnam was closed in both directions between Hakdong and Dosan Park crossroads. The 15th **Gangnam Festival** (3–5 October) had moved there from its usual home on Yeongdong-daero and turned the road into a parade route and outdoor stage (Yonhap and Herald Economy, September 2026). And as the National Assembly's transport committee prepares to open its annual audit of the transport ministry on 7 October, previews name one of the hottest items as sitting at the other end of Gangnam: **missing reinforcing steel under Samseong Station**, which has stalled the GTX-A express rail line (Daehan Economy and eToday, October 2026). One end of the district closed a road for a party; the other has a construction site that cannot move.

In short: Gangnam is **a stretch of land south of the Han River that was known until the late 1960s as "Yeongdong", the area east of Yeongdeungpo, which was then ruled into a planned grid and only got the name "Gangnam" in 1975**. This guide rebuilds that story, and today's numbers on vacancies, foot traffic and construction schedules, from district records, Korean Wikipedia and dated reporting. Opening hours and prices for the sights are in [Gangnam things to do](/en/posts/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/); restaurants are in [Gangnam food](/en/posts/gangnam-food-2026-09/); hotels are in [Gangnam hotels](/en/posts/gangnam-hotels-2026-09/).

![A glass-fronted shop on Garosu-gil in Sinsa-dong, with two bright green ginkgo trees on the pavement and two people walking past](/images/gangnam-guide-2026-09/01.webp)

This is **Garosu-gil in Sinsa-dong**, in a photo released by the Korea Tourism Organization. *Garosu-gil* simply means "tree-lined street", and the trees are ginkgos; the tourist signposts carry a ginkgo leaf. The same street also had **the highest retail vacancy rate of Seoul's seven main shopping streets (45.2%)** at the end of 2025. More on that below.

## Gangnam at a glance

"Gangnam" means different things to different people. Korean Wikipedia sums it up: in the widest sense it is all of Seoul south of the Han River; most often it means the three wealthy districts of **Gangnam-gu, Seocho-gu and Songpa-gu**; and in the narrowest sense it is Gangnam-gu alone, or just the commercial strip around Gangnam-daero and Gangnam Station. In Korea a *gu* is a city district with its own district office, and a *dong* is a neighbourhood inside it.

## Yeongdong: the land east of Yeongdeungpo

### Joining Seoul in 1963

Most of today's Gangnam-gu was **Eonju-myeon in Gwangju County, Gyeonggi Province**. The Korean Wikipedia entries for Cheongdam-dong, Apgujeong-dong and Sinsa-dong all say they were **absorbed into Seoul's Seongdong-gu on 1 January 1963**. Apgujeong is said to have been covered in pear orchards during the Japanese colonial period, and the name Sinsa-dong was made from two riverside hamlets, **Saemal (新村, "new village") and Sapyeong (沙平, "sandy flat")**.

Nobody called it "Gangnam" then. Maeil Business (October 2023) explains that until the 1960s "gangnam" just meant "south of the river", and people used **Yeongdong, Nam-Seoul (South Seoul) or Sae-Seoul (New Seoul)** instead. The most common, Yeongdong, meant **east of Yeongdeungpo**, which was then the hub of the south bank. The name survives in **Yeongdong-daero, Yeongdong Bridge and Yeongdong High School**.

### 1966–1972: a grid drawn by land readjustment

| When | What happened | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| February 1966 | Seoul applies to approve a "South Seoul" city plan to house 600,000 people in 120,000 households south of the river over ten years | Wikipedia (Gangnam) |
| 1966 | Yeongdong land readjustment area designated | Maeil Business 2023 |
| **1968** | Land readjustment begins in **Yeongdong District 1** (today's Seocho side, 3.65 million *pyeong*) | Maeil Business 2023, Newsmaker |
| 1969 | Third Han River Bridge (today's Hannam Bridge) completed, linked to the Gyeongbu Expressway | Wikipedia (Gangnam, Teheran-ro) |
| 1971–1972 | Land readjustment begins in **Yeongdong District 2** (today's Gangnam-gu side, 4.72 million *pyeong*) | Maeil Business 2023 (1971), Newsmaker (1972) |
| 1 July 1973 | Area run by a Yeongdong branch office of Seongdong-gu | Wikipedia (Sinsa-dong) |
| **1 October 1975** | **Gangnam-gu created; "Gangnam" becomes an official place name** | Wikipedia (Gangnam-gu) |
| 1976 | Elite high schools start moving in, beginning with Kyunggi High School | Wikipedia (Gangnam) |
| August 1976 | Construction ministry announces apartment blocks for the readjusted Apgujeong, Cheongdam and Dogok districts | Newsmaker |

A *pyeong* is a traditional Korean unit of about 3.3 m², so District 1 alone was roughly 12 km². *Land readjustment* is a planning method in which the city lays roads and public facilities across private plots and, in return, keeps a share of the land to sell and cover costs. Maeil Business says the project became urgent when the national expressway survey team told Seoul to carry out large-scale readjustment south of the Third Han River Bridge, where the Gyeongbu Expressway would start; Newsmaker adds that the method also let the city secure land for the expressway itself. That is why a map of Gangnam looks as if it was drawn with a ruler.

The two sources disagree on when District 2 started: Maeil Business says 1971, Newsmaker 1972. We give both.

### It grew, then split

Gangnam-gu was much bigger at first. Following its history on Wikipedia, **the land east of the Tancheon became Gangdong-gu in 1979, and Seocho-dong, Banpo-dong, Bangbae-dong and others became Seocho-gu on 1 January 1988**; Songpa-gu split from Gangdong-gu the same year. Wikipedia's population table puts Gangnam-gu at **770,097** in 1985, but that was before Seocho-gu split off, so it cannot be compared directly with today.

## Teheran-ro: from "Three Tombs Road" to Tehran

![A rounded stone marker on a Teheran-ro pavement, carved with 'Teheran-ro' in Korean on top and Persian below, with the road and office buildings behind](/images/gangnam-guide-2026-09/03.webp)

Teheran-ro runs about 3.2 km and ten lanes wide from the Gangnam Station crossroads to Samseong Bridge. Its first name was **Samneung-ro (三陵路), "Three Tombs Road"**. According to Wikipedia, Seoul gave it that name on **26 November 1972**, after the three burial mounds of the nearby Seonjeongneung royal tombs.

It was renamed in 1977. That June the mayor of Tehran visited Seoul to sign a sister-city agreement and proposed naming a street in each capital after the other. A stone marking the exchange was unveiled on Samneung-ro on **27 June 1977**, Seoul formally renamed the road Teheran-ro on 1 July, and Tehran opened its own **Seoul Street** that November. The marker in the photo is inscribed in Korean and Persian.

| Period | The face of Teheran-ro (Wikipedia, Teheran-ro) |
| --- | --- |
| Early 1980s | Slow to develop: land was expensive and shops clustered on north–south Gangnam-daero |
| Late 1984 | Zoned as a central commercial and business district |
| 1987 | The Korea World Trade Center and other large buildings open at the east end |
| Mid-1990s | Big companies and banks move their head offices here |
| After the 1997 financial crisis | Banks restructure and start-ups move in: "Teheran Valley" |
| Around 2003 | The start-up boom fades; cosmetic surgery and other clinics move in, earning the nickname "Beauty Valley" |
| Around 2015 | Activity shifts to the two ends, Gangnam Station and Samseong Station |

The name Yeoksam-dong also comes from the road network of old. Wikipedia says it joined three Joseon-era post-station villages, **Maljukgeori and Upper and Lower Bangadari**, hence *yeok* (station) and *sam* (three). Today the blocks around Seolleung, Yeoksam and Gangnam stations are Gangnam's central business district.

## Apgujeong, Cheongdam, Sinsa: where the names come from

The name **Apgujeong Rodeo Street**, according to Wikipedia, dates from **1990**, when the Apgujeong fashion street was nicknamed after Rodeo Drive in Beverly Hills. When Korail opened the Bundang Line between Wangsimni and Seolleung on 6 October 2012, it first named the new station "Cheongsu Naru"; after residents objected and the decision was reviewed, it became **Apgujeong Rodeo Station**.

### K-Star Road: frozen in 2015

Outside exit 2 of Apgujeong Rodeo Station is **K-Star Road**. Munhwa Ilbo (August 2023) reported that Gangnam-gu created it in 2015 to draw Hallyu fans, with **17 life-sized "Gangnam Dol" art toys** (a pun on *idol* and *doll*), each carrying the name of a K-pop group. The same report said the figures stopped at second- and third-generation groups, with no fourth-generation acts and several groups that had disbanded, and that foreign visitors it spoke to were disappointed. We could not confirm whether anything has changed since 2023. How to find it is in [Gangnam things to do](/en/posts/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## The retail numbers: Garosu-gil 45.2%, Cheongdam 13.4%, Gangnam 11.3%

Even within Gangnam, each street is in a very different place. Here is Cushman & Wakefield Korea's **Q4 2025 retail report** (reported by Yonhap in February 2026) for Seoul's seven main high streets.

| High street | Vacancy rate (Q4 2025) | What the report said |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seongsu | 2.5% | More local and foreign visitors |
| Myeongdong | 5.6% | Almost full |
| Hannam and Itaewon | 7.9% | Flagships arriving; spreading into side streets |
| Hongdae | 10.4% | New brands opening |
| **Gangnam** | **11.3%** (down 4.1 points on a year earlier) | Large stores opening; **medical tourism demand** |
| **Cheongdam** | **13.4%** (down 4.6 points) | New luxury flagships |
| **Garosu-gil** | **45.2%** | Long slump with no meaningful new demand |
| Average of seven | 13.8% | — |

**Garosu-gil's vacancy rate is three to four times that of the other streets.** The same firm put it at 43.9% in Q2 2025, and Dailypop (September 2026), quoting a presentation by a retail property company, said it was still in the 40% range in Q2 2026. The article added that in street-view images from around 2016 it was hard to spot an empty shop, whereas now "for lease" and "whole building for lease" notices are common. The Korea Tourism Organization describes Garosu-gil as a street once known for galleries and film companies that has been reshaped around fashion, with frequent pop-up stores.

**Gangnam is not one place.** The Gangnam Station area is filling up, Cheongdam is gaining luxury stores, and Garosu-gil has many empty units. Knowing that before you go keeps expectations in line, and it is better to combine two or three areas than to build a day around a single street.

## Samseong-dong: a tourist zone, a stalled station, a shorter GBC

![The Samseong Station crossroads seen from high above. The Trade Tower and Parnas Tower stand on the right, and construction hoardings and an excavation strip run along Yeongdong-daero in the foreground as buses and cars pass](/images/gangnam-guide-2026-09/02.webp)

This is the Samseong Station crossroads from above in December 2022. The tall building on the right is the World Trade Center's Trade Tower, with Parnas Tower beside it, and construction hoardings line Yeongdong-daero in the foreground. What is happening under that road is now Samseong-dong's biggest story.

### 18 December 2014: the Gangnam MICE Special Tourist Zone

On **18 December 2014** the Seoul Metropolitan Government designated **190,386 m²** around the World Trade Center in Samseong-dong as the **Gangnam MICE Special Tourist Zone** (Newsis). MICE is industry shorthand for meetings, incentives, conventions and exhibitions. The zone is centred on the COEX convention centre, with a department store, three hotels, a duty-free shop, a mall, the trade centre and the city air terminal. At the time Seoul already had five special tourist zones: Itaewon (1997), Myeongdong–Namdaemun–Bukchang-dong–Da-dong–Mugyo-dong (2000), Dongdaemun Fashion Town (2002), Jongno–Cheonggye (2006) and Jamsil (2012); Gangnam became the sixth. A designated zone, the article explains, can use its public open spaces for up to 60 days a year, including for performances and food.

The same article reports that Seoul planned to turn **about 720,000 m² from COEX to the Jamsil sports complex** into an **International Exchange Complex** combining business, MICE, sport and entertainment. The two pillars of that plan are the underground development beneath Yeongdong-daero and the GBC, below.

COEX itself opened as the KOEX exhibition centre in March 1979, and the COEX Mall followed in May 2000. The 2000 Asia–Europe Meeting (ASEM) and the 2010 Seoul G20 summit were both held here (Wikipedia).

### GTX-A at Samseong: missing steel and the "pass-through"

GTX-A is a new deep-level express rail line across the capital region, from Paju in the north-west to Dongtan in the south.

### GBC: from 105 floors to three 49-storey towers

Hyundai Motor Group's **GBC**, on the former Korea Electric Power Corporation site in Samseong-dong, was originally planned as a single 105-storey tower. Energy Economy (September 2026) reports that Seoul and Hyundai agreed in January 2026 to change it to **three 49-storey towers**; the planning change was published on 30 June and passed the building committee in early September. Hyundai Engineering & Construction extended its contract end date **from December 2026 to June 2032**, and the project is **8%** complete, with excavation under way.

### Bongeunsa: a temple among the towers

![A painted wooden hall at Bongeunsa temple seen across the courtyard, with high-rise buildings rising behind the tiled roof on the right](/images/gangnam-guide-2026-09/04.webp)

Across the road north of COEX, **Bongeunsa** was, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, **founded in 794 by the monk Yeonhoe as Gyeonseongsa, and in 1498 Queen Jeonghyeon, a consort of King Seongjong, rebuilt it on a grand scale east of his tomb, Seolleung, and renamed it Bongeunsa.** So Teheran-ro's old name, "Three Tombs Road", and this temple belong to the same story. The view in the photo, a tiled roof with towers behind it, sums up the district. Opening hours and the temple stay are in [Gangnam things to do](/en/posts/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/).

The Gangnam Station crossroads is **vulnerable to heavy rain**. Wikipedia notes that the station sits lower than Yeoksam Station to the east and also flooded in the 2011 downpours.

The Climate Card is Seoul's unlimited travel pass; when you change to the Sinbundang Line at Gangnam, Sinnonhyeon or Sinsa, expect to pay separately with a regular transit card. Both are explained in our [Seoul subway and T-money guide](/en/posts/seoul-subway-tmoney-2026-09/).

**From the airport**: on the airport railroad, change at Gimpo Airport to [[line:9]], which runs straight to Sinnonhyeon, Seonjeongneung and Bongeunsa. Airport limousine [[bus:6103]] serves the COEX side, but as noted above the terminal services ended in 2023. Fares and times by route are in [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/en/posts/incheon-airport-to-seoul-2026-09/).

## How to do it

Gangnam's blocks are huge and the sights are spread out, so **move station by station**. This route runs from the river in the north down to Samseong-dong in the south-east.

1. **Morning**: [[line:3]] to Sinsa for Garosu-gil. Walk it as the shops open, and pass quickly through the stretches with empty units.
2. **Late morning**: [[line:suinbundang]] Apgujeong Rodeo, exit 2, for K-Star Road and Rodeo Street, then the Cheongdam flagship strip.
3. **Lunch**: Apgujeong, Sinsa or Sinnonhyeon. How to choose is in [Gangnam food](/en/posts/gangnam-food-2026-09/).
4. **Afternoon**: [[line:9]] to Bongeunsa. The temple, COEX and the Starfield Library; details in [Gangnam things to do](/en/posts/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/).
5. **Late afternoon**: Seonjeongneung, then west along Teheran-ro, the road renamed in 1977.
6. **Evening**: the Gangnam Station crossroads. It is rush hour, so if you are heading straight to your hotel, go earlier or later.

If cosmetics shopping is on your list, how to choose a store and claim a tax refund is in our [Olive Young guide](/en/posts/olive-young-shopping-2026-09/).

## Crowds and etiquette

- **At Gangnam Station, the exit number is the district line.** Arrange to meet at a specific exit, not just "Gangnam Station"; exits 1–12 are scattered in every direction.
- **The Samseong crossroads and Yeongdong-daero are a building site.** Hoardings like those in the photo can force detours on foot. Use the underground connections to reach COEX.
- **In a heavy rain warning, keep away from low-lying Gangnam Station.** The 2022 flooding was between Gangnam and Sinnonhyeon stations, and the rain tunnel is due in 2030.
- **Festival days close roads.** The 2026 Gangnam Festival shut Dosan-daero between Hakdong and Dosan Park crossroads for two days. Check Gangnam-gu's announcements for next year's dates and venue.
- **The streets behind Cheongdam and Apgujeong are residential.** Do not block the pavement waiting or filming outside entertainment agency buildings.

## Who it suits

**It suits you if:**

- you are in Seoul for a COEX trade fair, a conference or business
- you want to know how a planned city was made, and **the stories behind street names and district lines**
- you plan to group flagship stores, department stores and cosmetics shopping by station
- you want to see **Seoul under construction**, with GTX and the GBC

**It does not suit you if:**

- old alleys and hanok are the point; try the [Bukchon guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/) or the [Myeongdong guide](/en/posts/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/)
- you want to explore one neighbourhood slowly on foot; Gangnam's big blocks can wear you out
- you are after the newest cafés and pop-ups; [Seongsu](/en/posts/seongsu-guide-2026-09/), with its 2.5% vacancy rate, is closer to that

## In short

- **Boundary**: the northern half of Gangnam-gu (Gangnam Station, Sinnonhyeon, Garosu-gil, Apgujeong, Cheongdam, Samseong-dong, Teheran-ro). Gangnam, Sinsa and Sinnonhyeon stations reach into Seocho-gu.
- **Name**: "Yeongdong", east of Yeongdeungpo, until the 1960s. Gangnam-gu created on 1 October 1975.
- **Development**: Yeongdong District 1 readjustment in 1968, District 2 in 1971–1972. Seocho-gu split off in 1988.
- **Teheran-ro**: Samneung-ro in 1972, **marker unveiled on 27 June 1977**, renamed on 1 July.
- **Tourist zone**: Gangnam MICE Special Tourist Zone, 18 December 2014, 190,386 m².
- **Now**: GTX-A's Samseong pass-through on hold over missing rebar; the GBC is three 49-storey towers, 8% built.
- **Numbers**: Samseong and COEX third in Seoul for foreign foot traffic, Gangnam Station fourth. Vacancy: Garosu-gil 45.2%, Cheongdam 13.4%, Gangnam 11.3%.
- **Watch out**: 116 mm of rain an hour flooded Gangnam-gu in August 2022; the rain tunnel is due in 2030.

In one line: **Gangnam is a city ruled out with a straight edge east of Yeongdeungpo, and it is still drawing new lines beneath the old ones.**
