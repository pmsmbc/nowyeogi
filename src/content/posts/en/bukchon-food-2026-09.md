Bukchon and Samcheong-dong do not have many restaurants. A Seoul city notice keeps restaurants and franchises out of the hanok lanes, so the places to eat cluster along **the main streets, Samcheong-ro and Bukchon-ro 5-gil (the road past Jeongdok Library)**. Those few places, though, carry a surprising number of official badges.

The short version: **for lunch, pick one of the three MICHELIN Bib Gourmands (Samcheongdong Sujebi, Hwangsaengga Kalguksu or Anam); for an afternoon snack, sweet red bean porridge at Seoul's Second Best or royal rice cakes from Biwon Tteokjip; for the cheapest meal, ₩7,000 (about $5.25) kimchi noodles at good-price-listed Nunnamujip.** Every place below was checked against MICHELIN, Seoul city records (Seoul Future Heritage and Oraegage), the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) or the Ministry of the Interior and Safety, or against press reports. Any description of taste is quoted from those sources.

## What this guide covers

The Seoul Future Heritage entry for the Bukchon hanok area defines Bukchon as **Gahoe-dong, Gye-dong, Wonseo-dong, Anguk-dong, Jae-dong, Hwa-dong, Sagan-dong, Sogyeok-dong, Songhyeon-dong, Palpan-dong and Samcheong-dong**. This guide uses the same area. The main streets are **Bukchon-ro**, running north from Anguk Station, **Samcheong-ro**, along the east wall of Gyeongbokgung Palace, and **Bukchon-ro 5-gil**, the road past Jeongdok Library between them.

The neighbours have their own guides. Insa-dong and Nagwon-dong south of Anguk Station, plus **The Green Table** in Wonseo-dong (MICHELIN-listed), are in [Where to eat in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/); the hanok café alleys are in [Ikseon-dong food](/en/posts/ikseon-food-2026-09/); the west side of the palace is in [Seochon food](/en/posts/seochon-food-2026-09/); and the office-worker lunch spots are in [Gwanghwamun and City Hall food](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-food-2026-09/). Sights and the red zone map are in [Things to do in Bukchon](/en/posts/bukchon-things-to-do-2026-09/) and the [Bukchon travel guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/).

## Why Bukchon has so few restaurants

### Zoning keeps restaurants out, zone by zone

The rules in force come from **Seoul Metropolitan Government Notice No. 2020-589** (Seoul Gazette No. 3637, 24 December 2020), which revised the Bukchon district unit plan. Pulling out only the restaurant rules:

| Zone | Cafés, bakeries, rice cake shops | Restaurants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bukchon Zone 1** (core hanok housing) | **Only traditional food makers and sellers (drinks, tea, food, bread, rice cakes, sweets) under 100㎡** | Not among permitted uses |
| Zones 2, 3 and 5 | Alongside an exhibition space (up to 20% of its floor area and 100㎡), or only in buildings allowed by the **Bukchon residents' agreement** | **Hanjeongsik (Korean set-meal) restaurants only**, in buildings the agreement allows |
| Gyedong-gil and Changdeokgung-gil zones | 300㎡ and up banned | **Banned on every floor** (Gyedong-gil exempts hanjeongsik restaurants in agreement-approved buildings) |
| Zone 7 | Only alongside cultural facilities, up to 20% and 100㎡ | Banned |

The notice defines a **hanjeongsik restaurant** as one that sells a full traditional Korean set meal of a grain-based main dish with side dishes and dessert. This is why you rarely see a restaurant inside the hanok lanes, and why almost every place in this guide sits on Samcheong-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil or Bukchon-ro.

### Franchises are banned in principle

The same notice bans, on every floor, franchise and chain-store outlets among cafés, bakeries, shops and restaurants in **Zones 4, 6 and 7, Gyedong-gil, Changdeokgung-gil, Gahoe-ro, and the Bukchon-gil and entrance-street zones**. The permitted-use tables for Zones 1, 2, 3 and 5 carry the same wording. There are two exceptions:

- A business that preserves a listed architectural asset such as a hanok and is a **traditional manufacturing or retail trade in keeping with Bukchon** can be allowed after review by Seoul's architectural assets committee.
- Small franchises excluded under **Article 3** of the Fair Transactions in Franchise Business Act are exempt.

Jongno-gu relaxed this a little in **February 2025**. According to Yonhap (21 March 2025), the district had been blocking new franchise outlets since 2016 in five district-plan areas (Bukchon, Ikseon-dong, Hyehwa/Myeongnyun-dong, Buam-dong and west of Gyeongbokgung). It now lets **convenience stores and coffee shops that were licensed before the ban** keep operating under a renewed franchise contract, within the same use and floor area, so head offices cannot squeeze them at renewal. **It did not open the door to new franchises.**

Separately, the Korea Economic Daily (22 May 2026) reported a wave of fashion and beauty flagship stores near Anguk Station at the entrance to Bukchon, and said **Bukchon's vacancy rate fell to 1.9% in the first quarter of 2026, from 4.4% a year earlier**, citing the Korea Real Estate Board. The same article mentioned a KFC pop-up. We could not work out which zone or exception those outlets fall under.

### Samcheongdong Sujebi: since 1982, served in a crock

![A white bowl of hand-torn dough soup in clear broth beside three side dishes of stir-fried anchovies, kimchi and yellow pickled radish](/images/bukchon-food-2026-09/02.webp)

This is a generic photo of sujebi (hand-torn dough soup) from Wikimedia Commons. At this restaurant the soup comes in an earthenware crock, not a bowl.

**Samcheongdong Sujebi** (101-1 Samcheong-ro) carries two official labels. It is a **MICHELIN Bib Gourmand**, and one of the **24 Oraegage** that Seoul named on **22 August 2024**, the first year the scheme covered restaurants.

The Oraegage site tells the story like this: **owner Han Ki-young started with a small snack bar in Samcheong-dong in the 1980s**, and made his name with hand-torn sujebi made at customers' request. He **opened it as a sujebi specialist in 1982**, and unlike other sujebi houses it serves the soup **in a crock**, topped with potato, zucchini and seaweed flakes.

MICHELIN singles out **thin sujebi in anchovy broth** and a **potato pancake made from potato alone**. KTO lists the same two dishes (sujebi with zucchini, clam meat and potato, and a 100% potato pancake) and also has glutinous rice ball soup (chapssal saeal ongsimi) on the menu. MICHELIN advises avoiding **noon to 2pm, the busiest period**, while adding that it is always full anyway.

- DiningCode listing: **sujebi ₩10,000, potato pancake ₩12,000**, green onion or mung bean pancake ₩17,000 each, rice ball soup ₩14,000

### Anam: pork and rice soup in Bukchon

## Seoul Future Heritage: a red bean porridge tea house and a royal rice cake shop

Two places to eat in this area are on Seoul Future Heritage's Jongno-gu list. The register is voluntary, so unlike a cultural heritage designation it does not oblige owners to preserve anything.

### Seoul's Second Best (2014-028)

![Dark red bean porridge with white rice balls in a flower-patterned ceramic bowl on a woven mat](/images/bukchon-food-2026-09/03.webp)

This is a generic photo of patjuk (red bean porridge) from Wikimedia Commons. According to the Seoul Future Heritage entry, the version here is topped with chestnut, ginkgo nuts, beans and sticky rice cake and dusted with cinnamon.

**Seoul's Second Best** ("Seoul-seo Dulijjaero Jalhaneun Jip", 122-1 Samcheong-ro) is Seoul Future Heritage **2014-028**. According to the entry, founder **Kim Eun-suk opened it on this spot in April 1976** as a **traditional tea house**. It was first called **"Ssanghwatang"** and added "Seoul's second best" as a subtitle within a year. The building is a single-storey block of just **22.21㎡**, and the chairs, tables and dishes are said to be unchanged since opening.

There are two signature items:

- **Sipjeondaebotang**: a herbal tea made from a ssanghwa base of seven ingredients (rehmannia, angelica, cnidium, white peony, cinnamon, astragalus and liquorice) plus atractylodes, poria and ginseng. The herbs come from Gyeongdong Market and are brewed in a back room using skills the founder learned from an oriental medicine doctor.
- **Danpatjuk (sweet red bean porridge)**: finely ground red beans cooked into porridge, topped with **boiled chestnut, ginkgo, beans and chapssal-tteok**, and dusted with **cinnamon**. The entry says the monk Beopjeong liked it during his illness.

DiningCode lists **red bean porridge at ₩9,000 and ssanghwa tea at ₩9,000**, sujeonggwa (cinnamon punch) at ₩8,000, and sikhye or ginger-jujube tea at ₩7,000, with hours of 11:00–20:00. Seoul Future Heritage gives the nearest stop as **Financial Training Institute** on village bus Jongno [[bus:village:11]].

### Biwon Tteokjip (2020-006)

**Biwon Tteokjip** (33-1 Bukchon-ro) is Seoul Future Heritage **2020-006** and a Seoul **Oraegage (2017)**. According to the heritage entry, founder **Hong Gan-nan** (1925–1999) learned royal rice cake recipes from **Court Lady Han Hui-sun**, the last holder of the Joseon royal cuisine title, and opened in **1949**. At first she took orders from her home near Changdeokgung Palace without a sign, and the shop was known as "Grandma Hong's rice cake house". It still has no sign.

The heritage entry's timeline:

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1949 | Hong Gan-nan opens in front of Changdeokgung |
| 1984 | Her nephew Ahn In-cheol and his wife Choi Jeong-ok take over |
| 2012 | Their eldest son Ahn Sang-min takes over and moves to Susong-dong |
| 2017 | Named a Seoul Oraegage |
| 2021 | Moves to its current Bukchon-ro address |

The entry says **five kinds** of rice cake are sold in the shop, with more available by phone order. DiningCode lists **duteop-tteok at ₩4,000 a piece**, pine nut, milk, mugwort and black sesame rice cakes at ₩2,500 each, and yaksik at ₩3,000. The Oraegage site gives **10:00–17:00 daily**, and DiningCode notes it **closes early when the rice cakes sell out**. **This is a takeaway shop, not somewhere to sit down.**

The address field on the heritage page still shows the pre-move address (20 Yulgok-ro), and its timeline says the shop moved to "Bukchon-ro 331". The Oraegage site and DiningCode both give **33-1 Bukchon-ro**, which we follow.

## The red zone and dinner

Jongno-gu designated Bukchon a special management area on **1 July 2024**. About **34,000㎡ around Bukchon-ro 11-gil** is the red zone, where **visits for sightseeing are banned from 17:00 to 10:00 the next day**. Since 1 March 2025 a violation costs a **₩100,000 (about $75) fine** (Jongno-gu culture platform).

What matters for meals are the exemptions. Jongno-gu lists **residents, their family and friends, shop customers, merchants and people simply passing through**. Even exempt visitors can be fined for sightseeing behaviour, though: taking photos or video, lingering to look around, or wandering without using a shop. Every place in this guide is on **Samcheong-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil, Bukchon-ro or Yulgok-ro 1-gil**, outside the red zone.

Reports on how merchants are affected keep coming:

- Asia Economy (11 June 2025): a convenience store owner near the Gye-dong side of the hanok village said **sales fell by more than ₩10 million** after enforcement began in March, and a souvenir shop at the entrance said monthly sales were down 30–50%. A samgyetang restaurant owner in the same article suggested seasonal hours.
- Munhwa Ilbo (13 October 2025): local merchants said **sales were down 20–40%** from before the limits, and the Bukchon merchants' association sued to overturn the charter bus ban. Jongno-gu said it chose uniform hours after public hearings and would keep the current rules for now.
- BizHankook (7 August 2026): seventeen months in, residents say life is quieter and merchants report lower sales. **Shop and museum visitors and hotel guests can enter after 5pm, but on-site signage barely says so**, and Jongno-gu said it may consider longer summer hours once monitoring is complete.

**Charter buses** are banned too. From **1 January 2026** about 2.3km of Bukchon-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil, Bukchon-ro 4-gil and Changdeokgung 1-gil is closed to charter buses, with fines of ₩300,000 (₩400,000 and ₩500,000 for repeat offences) (Jongno-gu). Bukchon-ro 5-gil, home to Hwangsaengga Kalguksu, Anam and Seoul Punggyeong, is part of it, so **a group tour cannot simply pull up a coach for lunch.**

## Samcheong-dong's high street, briefly

The Samcheong-dong strip went through a rough patch. Ttangjibgo (21 April 2022) reported that **trade collapsed after Chinese tour groups stopped coming in the 2017 THAAD dispute**, then stalled again in the pandemic, before vacancies on Samcheong-dong-gil dropped to about ten after the opening of Cheong Wa Dae was announced. A food-service professor quoted in the article credited the area's resilience to **long-running "steady sellers"**, which is exactly what the 1970s and 1980s shops in this guide are.

In 2026 Bukchon's vacancy rate is down to 1.9% (Korea Real Estate Board, Q1), with newcomers mostly fashion and beauty stores (Korea Economic Daily). We found no official figures showing more restaurants.

## Getting there

Most Bukchon restaurants are a walk from Anguk Station [[line:3]]. Heading north on Bukchon-ro you reach Biwon Tteokjip; turning onto Bukchon-ro 5-gil towards Jeongdok Library you pass Anam, Seoul Punggyeong and Hwangsaengga Kalguksu in turn. Up Samcheong-ro to the north come Smith Loves Hanok, Soseonjae, Samcheongdong Sujebi, Seoul's Second Best, Darakjeong and Nunnamujip.

To save some climbing, village bus Jongno [[bus:village:11]] stops at the Jeongdok Library entrance (Jeongdok Library directions). For transport cards, see [Seoul subway and T-money](/en/posts/seoul-subway-tmoney-2026-09/).

## Good to know

**Card payments cannot be refused.** Article 19 of the Specialized Credit Financial Business Act bars card merchants from refusing card payment or treating cardholders less favourably. KTO lists cards as accepted at Nunnamujip, Darakjeong, Soseonjae, Smith Loves Hanok, Dugahun and Rossini. The law does not guarantee that every terminal accepts foreign-issued cards, though, so a little cash helps.

**Don't count on parking.** KTO lists no parking at Darakjeong, Soseonjae and Hwangsaengga Kalguksu. DiningCode shows valet parking at some places, but we could not confirm that officially.

**Queues are mostly at lunch.** MICHELIN says Hwangsaengga Kalguksu has a line all day, Samcheongdong Sujebi from noon to 2pm, and Muguok from before opening.

**No tipping.** The menu price is the final price. If the dishes are new to you, start with [Korean food for beginners](/en/posts/korean-food-for-beginners-2026-09/).

## Wrapping up

- **Bukchon's restaurants sit on the main streets.** Notice 2020-589 allows only traditional food shops under 100㎡ in Zone 1 and bans restaurants on Gyedong-gil and Changdeokgung-gil. Franchises are banned in principle.
- **Three MICHELIN 2026 Bib Gourmands**: **Samcheongdong Sujebi** (1982, Oraegage 2024), **Hwangsaengga Kalguksu** (2001) and **Anam** for pork and rice soup. **Muguok**, North Korean-style samgyetang, is new for 2026.
- **Two Seoul Future Heritage places**: **Seoul's Second Best** (1976, red bean porridge and herbal tea) and **Biwon Tteokjip** (1949, royal rice cakes to take away).
- **The cheapest meal**: ₩7,000 kimchi mari guksu at Nunnamujip (good-price listing).
- **Shop customers are exempt from the red zone's 5pm limit**, but every place in this guide is outside the red zone anyway.
