Bukchon is **a neighbourhood deliberately made hard for restaurants to move into**. That is not an impression. It is written into a Seoul city notice.

So before deciding where to eat, it helps to know **which parts are closed off and which are open**. After that come the handful of places with an official certification.

![A Samcheong-dong street with hanok and terrace cafe buildings standing on a stone embankment, ginkgo trees and a no-parking enforcement sign on the right](/images/bukchon-food-2026-09/04.webp)

## The notice restricts restaurants zone by zone

The rules in force are those of the Bukchon district unit plan as revised by **Seoul Metropolitan Government Notice 2020-589** (24 December 2020, Seoul Gazette 3637). It was amended again in 2024 by **Notice 2024-298**, but that change turned the former US embassy staff housing site in Songhyeon-dong into a cultural park.

Here is the land use table from the original, summarised by zone.

| Zone | Restaurant rules (Notice 2020-589) |
| --- | --- |
| **Bukchon Zone 1** (the residential hanok core) | Cafes and bakeries **only as "traditional food shops making and selling traditional drinks, tea, food, bread, rice cakes, confectionery and the like, under 100㎡"** |
| Bukchon Zones 2, 3 and 5 | Cafes and bakeries only alongside an exhibition space, **no more than 20% of its floor area and no more than 100㎡**, or in buildings approved under the **Bukchon residents' agreement**. Full restaurants **only if Korean set-meal (hanjeongsik) houses**, in agreement-approved buildings |
| **Gyedong-gil and Changdeokgung-gil zones** | **Full restaurants barred on every floor** (Gyedong-gil excepts hanjeongsik houses in agreement-approved buildings); cafes of 300㎡ or more barred |
| Bukchon Zones 4 and 6 | Restaurants and cafes of **300㎡ or more** barred |
| Bukchon Zone 7 | Full restaurants barred; cafes only alongside cultural facilities, 20% or less and 100㎡ or less |

So **it is no accident that restaurants are rare deep in the residential lanes.** In Bukchon Zone 1 there is no entry for full restaurants at all.

The "traditional" wording is worth noticing. Under the first plan in 2010 (Notice 2010-11), a cafe in Zone 1 was possible only as part of an exhibition space. The 2020 revision opened the door to **traditional food shops, rice cakes, tea and traditional sweets, under 100㎡**. This is what the city meant when it announced "traditional casual eateries" would be allowed after the joint urban planning and architecture committee passed the plan with amendments on 9 September 2020.

## Franchises are barred in principle

The same sentence recurs zone after zone in the notice.

> Franchise businesses opened through a "franchise business" as defined in the Fair Transactions in Franchise Business Act and the Franchise Business Promotion Act, and businesses opened and run (directly managed) through a "chain business" as defined in the Distribution Industry Development Act, **may not locate on any floor**.

It covers **cafes, bakeries, shops and full restaurants**. The one exception: where a building recognised by the Seoul architectural assets committee is being preserved and used, a **"traditional manufacturing and retail business in keeping with Bukchon's character"** may be approved after review.

Yet you have probably seen a chain coffee shop on the main Samcheong-dong road. There are two reasons.

First, **it was there before the rules**. Jongno-gu has restricted franchises since 2016 in five district unit plan areas, Bukchon, Ikseon-dong, Hyehwa and Myeongnyun-dong, Buam-dong and west of Gyeongbokgung. Franchisees already trading complained that head offices were penalising them at renewal, so **from February 2025, for convenience stores and coffee shops only**, a change of franchise has been allowed within the same use and the same floor area (reported in March 2025).

Second, the notice's franchise ban table names **Bukchon Zones 4, 6 and 7, Gyedong-gil, Changdeokgung-gil, Gahoe-ro, and Bukchon-gil and its approach roads** (Zones 1, 2, 3 and 5 carry their own ban in the permitted use table). **Samcheong-dong-gil Zones 1 and 2 and the Yulgok-ro zone do not appear in that table.** The zone boundaries are drawn on the plan map, so this post does not say which zone any particular shop falls in.

## The certified old shops

Few eateries in Bukchon and Samcheong-dong appear on an official list. These are the ones found.

![Sujebi in clear broth in a white bowl, with three side dishes of anchovies and peppers, kimchi and pickled radish](/images/bukchon-food-2026-09/05.webp)

### Samcheong-dong Sujebi - Oraegage (2024)

One of the **nine Jongno-gu restaurants** among the 24 Oraegage announced by the city on **22 August 2024**. Oraegage is the name Seoul gives to shops that have kept going for 30 years or more.

- 101-1 Samcheong-ro
- Opened as a sujebi (hand-torn dough soup) specialist in **1982**
- Serves it **in an earthenware pot**
- Daily 11:00-20:00 (per the Oraegage website)

The photo above is generic sujebi, not this shop. Here it comes in a pot.

### Seoul's Second Best - Seoul Future Heritage 2014-028

A **red bean porridge house** (danpatjuk), listed as Seoul Future Heritage **2014-028, in the everyday life category**.

- 122-1 Samcheong-ro
- Opened in **1976**
- According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the owner **studied herbal tea and opened a tea house**, then added red bean porridge for younger customers

Its name says second best, but it was certified first.

### Biwon Tteokjip - Oraegage

Strictly speaking this is at the entrance to Bukchon, **33-1 Bukchon-ro**. The Oraegage website lists it as **opened in 1949, selected in 2017**.

The website says it began when **Court Lady Han Hui-sun, the Joseon dynasty's last master of royal cuisine, passed on her royal rice cake recipes**. Daily 10:00-17:00. **It is a rice cake shop, not a place to sit down and eat.**

## What it costs

These are prices published under the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's good price scheme, as checked on 28 September 2026.

**Of Jongno-gu's 86 good price shops, four have addresses in the Bukchon neighbourhoods** (Gahoe-dong, Samcheong-dong, Gye-dong, Hwa-dong and so on), and two of those are a hair salon and a photo studio. **Only two are places to eat.**

| Shop | Location | Published price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nunnamujip | 136-1 Samcheong-ro | **Kimchi mari guksu 7,000 won** · kimchi fried rice 7,000 won |
| Seoul Punggyeong | 39 Bukchon-ro 5-gil, Hwa-dong | Seoul Punggyeong set (lunch, two or more) 16,000 won · large pork cutlet 11,000 won |

Seoul Punggyeong is open **10:00-19:00**, every day from April to November and **closed on Sundays from December to March**. Nunnamujip has no published hours.

**The cheap meals cluster south of Anguk Station, not up the Bukchon hill.** Given the notice above, that is no surprise.

The usual caveat applies. The scheme designates **shops cheaper than the local average**, so these are a floor rather than an average, and **franchises cannot be designated**.

## The 5pm red zone and dinner

![At night a person walks down lamplit steps in a hanok alley, city lights in the distance](/images/bukchon-food-2026-09/01.webp)

In the **red zone**, about 34,000㎡ around Bukchon-ro 11-gil, **tourists may not enter from 17:00 to 10:00 the next morning**, with a **fine of 100,000 won**.

For dinner, what matters is the list of exceptions. Jongno-gu names **shop customers, shopkeepers and staff, residents and their family and friends, and people simply passing through**. **Going to eat at a shop inside the red zone is not restricted.**

That said, the red zone is residential, so there are few restaurants there to begin with. Every place in this post is on **Samcheong-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil or Bukchon-ro**, outside it. The zone and its hours are set out in [the Bukchon overview](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/).

## Things worth knowing

**Every restaurant and cafe is entirely non-smoking.**

**There is no tipping, and the price on the menu is the final amount.**

**Refusing a card payment is illegal**, but older shops may not take cards issued abroad.

**Bukchon is a place where people live.** The quiet of its inner lanes is partly the notice at work.

## Wrapping up

- **The Bukchon district unit plan (Notice 2020-589) restricts restaurants zone by zone.** Zone 1 allows only traditional food shops under 100㎡; Gyedong-gil and Changdeokgung-gil bar full restaurants.
- **Franchises are barred on every floor in principle.** Only existing convenience stores and coffee shops got some relief from February 2025.
- **Samcheong-dong Sujebi is a 2024 Oraegage**, and **Seoul's Second Best is Seoul Future Heritage 2014-028**.
- **Bukchon has two good price restaurants**: kimchi mari guksu at 7,000 won and a lunch set at 16,000 won.
- **The cheap meals are south of Anguk Station.** There is a 3,000 won hangover soup.
- **Customers of shops inside the red zone are exempt after 5pm.**
