Most of what there is to see in Itaewon is **free or simply walkable**. The War Memorial of Korea charges nothing, the Seoul Central Mosque welcomes visitors who dress appropriately, and the World Food Street, Quy Nhon Street, Gyeongridan-gil and Haebangchon's 108 steps are streets you just walk. What matters is **the clock and the calendar**. The War Memorial **stops admitting visitors at 17:00**, and late October in Itaewon brings both **the fourth anniversary of the 29 October 2022 crowd crush and a Saturday Halloween**.

This page covers only **what to see, when and how**. The neighbourhood's history and boundaries are in [the Itaewon guide](/en/posts/itaewon-guide-2026-09/), and Leeum and the Hangangjin side are in [things to do in Hannam-dong](/en/posts/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/). All hours and prices are **as of 6 October 2026, from each venue's official information**.

## War Memorial of Korea: free, last entry at 17:00

![The War Memorial of Korea's main building on a clear day, broad steps and a plaza lined on both sides with national flags](/images/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

29 Itaewon-ro. According to the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), the War Memorial Service Korea opened it on **10 June 1994**; about **10,000 of its roughly 33,000 artefacts** are on display across three indoor floors, with large weapons outdoors.

### Hours and admission

From the museum's visitor and booking pages:

- **Open 09:30-18:00**, **last entry 17:00**, **closing begins 17:40**
- **Free**, and the permanent galleries need **no booking**. Rented-out exhibitions are separate.
- **Closed Mondays, 1 January, and Seollal and Chuseok days.** After a holiday run that includes a Monday, it closes **the next day**.
- **No food** in the galleries, no pets except guide dogs, **no flash, tripods or selfie sticks**, no wheeled shoes. No bikes, scooters or skates in the outdoor exhibition area.

### What's inside

The permanent exhibition list names the **Memorial Hall, War History Room, Korean War Rooms I and II, Korean War Room III (United Nations), North Korean Provocations Room, Donations Room, Overseas Deployment Room, Armed Forces Development Room, Large Weapons Room and Korean War Leaders Room**, plus memorial sculptures and the outdoor exhibition. The Memorial Hall, reached from the second-floor central hall, is where the route begins.

The current special exhibition:

- **'That Day, My Everyday Life Became War'**, **3 September-29 November 2026**, Special Exhibition Room I on the second floor, **free**. The museum describes it as telling the war through everyday objects that soldiers and refugees left behind: tableware, letters and personal belongings. Tel. 02-709-3065.

### Scheduled tours: no sign-up, meet at the door

For visitors from about age 10 (Korean grade 4) and up; just turn up at each gallery entrance.

| Gallery | Weekday (Tue-Fri) Korean | Weekday English | Weekend/holiday Korean | Weekend/holiday English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| War History Room I (1F) | 10:00, 14:00, 15:00 | None | 10:00, 14:00 | None |
| War History Room II (1F) | 10:30, 14:30, 15:30 | None | 10:30, 14:30 | None |
| Korean War Room II (2F) | 10:30, 11:30, 14:30, 15:30 | 10:30, 14:30 | 10:30, 14:30 | 10:30, 14:30 |

- **Korean War Room I is not being toured** while its displays are upgraded.
- **English tours are for foreign nationals and up to two Korean companions per foreigner**; bring a passport or residence card.
- Private study groups and tour companies with their own guide cannot join the scheduled tours.
- Booked tours must be requested on the website **at least three days ahead** and are cancelled if you are five minutes late.

### Children's Museum

The Children's Museum on the same grounds takes **advance bookings only, no walk-ins**. It is aimed at **ages 4-10**, and **adults without children, or children without an adult, are not admitted.** One booking per person per day, up to five people. We could not confirm the admission fee. Tel. 02-709-3200.

## Itaewon-ro and the World Food Street

The main road above [[line:6]] Itaewon Station is **Itaewon-ro**, and the alleys behind it are the heart of the area. Food itself is in [what to eat in Itaewon](/en/posts/itaewon-food-2026-09/); here we look at **the streets**.

### World Food Street (Itaewon-ro 27ga-gil)

Yongsan-gu created the **World Food Street in 2013** inside the Itaewon Special Tourist Zone; its official address is **Itaewon-ro 27ga-gil** (Munhwa Ilbo, 2020). A 2020 upgrade replaced the asphalt walkway with **stone paving** and refitted the old busking stage with an LED screen so anyone could perform.

This is also the alley Yongsan-gu names first in Halloween crowd planning. Its 2023 plan listed **the World Food Street, Itaewon-ro and Quy Nhon Street** as priority areas (Kyunghyang Shinmun).

### Quy Nhon Street (Bogwang-ro 59-gil)

According to News1 (October 2016), this is **Korea's first Vietnam-themed street**, built for 20 years of friendship between Yongsan-gu and **Quy Nhon** in Vietnam.

- **Bogwang-ro 59-gil, 330m long and 8m wide**, opened on 15 October 2016
- A Yongsan delegation first visited Quy Nhon in 1996, and the two became **sister cities the following year**.
- **Lotus patterns** on the road surface, Vietnamese motifs on the lamp posts, and in the central **Quy Nhon Garden** a sculpture of the **nón** conical hat
- The side alleys leading to Itaewon-ro are named Xin chào (hello), Hòa bình (peace), Hy vọng (hope) and Đoàn kết (unity).
- A **golden turtle sculpture** drawn from a Quy Nhon legend was added in January 2022 (Herald Economy).

### Itaewon Antique Furniture Street

A 2016 Edaily report on Yongsan-gu's street upgrade gives the extent:

- **Bogwang-ro** from Itaewon Station to Cheonghwa Apartments, about 450m
- **Noksapyeong-daero 26-gil** from Cheonghwa Apartments to the Saudi Arabian Embassy, about 310m
- The street grew from the 1970s as departing US officers and embassy staff sold off their furniture.
- **85 shops** at the time (2016 figure)

Opening days vary by shop, so no hours are given. We could **not find autumn 2026 dates** for the antique festival that used to run in spring and autumn.

## October events on Itaewon-ro: no word yet on the Global Village Festival

![Itaewon-ro during the 2019 Itaewon Global Village Festival, a long row of white tent stalls and crowds filling the roadway](/images/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/13.webp)

The photo shows Itaewon-ro during the **2019 Itaewon Global Village Festival**. The Seoul Economic Daily (November 2023) described it as the district's flagship street festival, with **over a million visitors in 2022**. Seoul's culture portal still lists **2022 (15-16 October)** as the latest edition.

**We found no announcement of a 2026 'Itaewon Global Village Festival'.**

A different festival used the same road this year:

- **2026 K-Wave Dance Festival**, **Saturday 3 and Sunday 4 October**, around Itaewon-ro. It has **already finished**.
- Newsis (5 October) reports that the Korea Contemporary Dance Promotion Association hosted it with Ministry of Culture funding, with a world dance parade and food and culture areas; Yongsan-gu's mayor Kim Kyung-dae attended the opening on the 3rd.
- Earlier coverage (Korea Sprint) listed 12:00-21:00 on Itaewon-ro between Itaewon and Noksapyeong stations, free.

Haebangchon's **Sinheung Market Global Local Festival** ran on **1-3 October** and has also ended.

## Seoul Central Mosque and Usadan-ro

![Seoul Central Mosque with its two white minarets rising above a dense hillside of houses](/images/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

About 477m from exit 3 of Itaewon Station, at **39 Usadan-ro 10-gil**. VISIT SEOUL says it opened on **21 May 1976** as Korea's first mosque.

### Etiquette

From the Korea Muslim Federation and public tourism sites:

- The federation calls the mosque **an open space**, but it is a place of worship and dress matters.
- **No sleeveless tops, short skirts or shorts.** If that is what you are wearing, borrow a skirt or headscarf from the **changing room next to the security office**.
- **Do not photograph people praying**, and do not disturb prayers.
- **No smoking** inside.
- VISIT SEOUL notes that prayer times shift with sunrise.
- The KTO lists **no lift, no accessible toilet and no parking**; the approach is a steep hill, hard with a wheelchair or stroller.

Visiting hours are not published officially. Tel. **02-793-6908**.

### Usadan-ro: the halal food street

According to Yonhap (April 2020), Yongsan-gu rebuilt **Usadan-ro and Usadan-ro 10-gil** around the mosque as the 'Itaewon Halal Food Culture Street': **500m** from the Itaewon 119 Safety Center (196 Itaewon-ro) to Jangmi Apartments in Hannam-dong, with pavements widened from 2m to 2.5m. At the time there were **about 50 halal restaurants and grocers** near the mosque. "Halal" means food prepared in line with Islamic dietary law; how to pick a restaurant is covered in [what to eat in Itaewon](/en/posts/itaewon-food-2026-09/).

## Gyeongridan-gil: the original '-ridan-gil'

![A fork on Gyeongridan-gil, a hillside road with a zebra crossing and small buildings holding a pizza place and a convenience store](/images/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/12.webp)

The KTO defines Gyeongridan-gil as **the road from the main gate of the Armed Forces Financial Management Corps towards the Grand Hyatt hotel, plus the alleys around it**. The name comes from the **Army Central Finance Corps** (*Gyeongridan*) that once stood here; the official road name is **Hoenamu-ro**. Its success spawned copycat '-ridan-gil' street names all over Korea. It starts near [[line:6]] Noksapyeong Station and climbs steadily towards Namsan.

- Yonhap (April 2020) reports that Yongsan-gu rebuilt **the whole 900m of Hoenamu-ro**, widening the entrance pavement, adding safety railings and creating a small plaza at the Samgeori Market village-bus stop.
- The same article put **the top end of Gyeongridan-gil at the Namsan Outdoor Botanical Garden**. That garden reopened in June 2026 as the Namsan Korean Forest Garden; hours and directions are on [the Hannam-dong page](/en/posts/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/).
- The article also planned two 3m×6m deck viewpoints on Namsan's Sowol-gil. We could not confirm where they ended up.

The Hankook Ilbo (September 2026) quotes a Haebangchon merchant on how Gyeongridan-gil went from Korea's hottest street a decade ago into decline as rents rose. Shops change often, so treat it as a walk rather than a shopping list. The KTO lists it as always open.

## Haebangchon: the 108 steps and Sinheung Market

![Seoul seen from the Haebangchon hillside, a white building with rooftop parasols in front, low houses and high-rises beyond and sunlight breaking through the clouds](/images/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/11.webp)

According to the KTO, Haebangchon ('liberation village') grew up after **liberation in 1945**, as people displaced from the North, returnees from abroad and war refugees settled on the slope below Namsan. It spans **Yongsan-dong 1-ga and 2-ga**, and a 2021 Yongsan-gu regeneration project made it easier to explore. More of its story is in [the Itaewon guide](/en/posts/itaewon-guide-2026-09/).

### The 108 steps

Combining the Seoul Future Heritage site and Newsis (2017):

- Built in **1943** under Japanese rule as the **approach to the Gyeongseong Hokoku Shrine**; the shrine site is now a cluster of low-rise flats.
- Once a single broad stone staircase, it is now split by a central garden strip: 6m wide, 53m long.
- From the **Huam-dong bus terminus roundabout** you see the whole flight; from the top, central Seoul spreads out below.
- An **inclined lift, begun in 2017 and finished in 2018**, runs along the old garden strip at **60m a minute, about one minute bottom to top**, with a stop halfway.
- A panel at the lift entrance explains the steps' shrine history.
- Sewing shops along both sides recall the 1960s-70s cottage knitwear trade (*yokko*) that once drove Haebangchon's economy.

We again could not find official lift operating hours.

### Sinheung Market

- Seoul city media say it served residents as a daily-goods market from **1953** and declined from the 1990s.
- Rather than one straight aisle, its alleys form **a loop shaped like the Korean letter ㅂ**.
- The dark slate roof was replaced by **Seoul Chaeng, a clear ETFE canopy chosen by competition in 2018**: daylight reaches deep into the alleys, and it is lit at night.
- The Hankook Ilbo (1 September 2026) reports **up to 5,000 visitors a day on public holidays**; at its low point only three shops were left and it was nicknamed the 'ghost market'.
- The same article mentions **an alley exhibition about the market's traders, a collaboration with Coca-Cola, from 1 September**. Other reports describe a two-month run, but we could not confirm the end date.

Shop hours vary, so none are listed here.

### Haebangchon Ogeori and parking

In April 2026 Yongsan-gu rebuilt the **Haebangchon public car park** (56 Sowol-ro 20-gil) as a multi-storey, growing it **from 24 to 49 spaces**, for residents and visitors to Haebangchon Ogeori ('five-way junction') and Sinheung Market (Munhwa Ilbo). We could not confirm the fees. The streets are narrow and steep, so it is easier to ride up by bus or taxi and **walk down**.

## Noksapyeong and Yongsan Park

South of Noksapyeong Station lies land from the former US Army base in Yongsan. The two returned areas open to the public, **Yongsan Children's Garden** and **Officers' Quarters Complex 5**, are entered from the Sinyongsan and Seobinggo-ro side rather than from Noksapyeong. Their no-booking rules, closing days and parking ban are on [the Hannam-dong page](/en/posts/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## The 10·29 Road of Memory and Safety

**About 40m from exit 1 of Itaewon Station**, in the alley beside the Hamilton Hotel. This is where many people lost their lives on the night of 29 October 2022. Bereaved families and a citizens' committee, working with Yongsan-gu and others, made it the **'10·29 Road of Memory and Safety' in October 2023**.

According to Yonhap (26 October 2023) and Nocut News (28 October 2024):

- The pavement at the entrance reads **"We still have names to remember."**
- On the right side of the alley are photographic works and a panel explaining what happened; the works change periodically.
- The 2024 works carried the victims' names alongside messages translated into **14 languages, including English, Japanese and Arabic**.

**It is a place of remembrance, not a photo spot.** Do not stop in the alley for pictures or talk loudly, and if you pass through, slow down.

### Fourth-anniversary events (2026)

According to Money Today (5 October), the Ministry of the Interior and Safety is holding the anniversary events with the bereaved families' association, the citizens' committee and the Seoul city government.

| Date | Event | Attendance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sat 24 Oct, 17:30** | Citizens' memorial, **Seoul Plaza** | Open to all, no registration |
| **Thu 29 Oct, 10:29** | Fourth-anniversary ceremony, **north Gwanghwamun Square** | Register 6-19 Oct; random draw if oversubscribed |

Both take place at City Hall and Gwanghwamun, not in Itaewon. Several outlets report that the families declared October a 'month of remembrance and mourning' on 1 October.

## Halloween 2026: 31 October is a Saturday

This year **31 October falls on a Saturday**. News1 (22 September) reports that Interior Minister Yun Ho-jung told the cabinet that **"more people than usual are expected in busy areas such as Itaewon and Hongdae."**

- The government will run a **special Halloween safety period** with designated priority areas.
- During that period it will **pre-emptively raise the crowd-disaster alert to 'caution'**.
- The minister noted that last year the biggest crowds came **on the Saturday including Halloween, or the Friday and Saturday the week before**.

**As of 6 October we found no published Yongsan-gu plan for 2026** (dates, staffing, closures). For reference, **last year's (2025)** Yongsan-gu plan looked like this (Yonhap, 24 October 2025):

- Special period 24 October-2 November, about **1,300 safety staff**
- A **four-stage congestion scale** (free-flowing, caution, congested, very congested) for the World Food Street, judged by smart CCTV
- At 'caution', **keep-right walking** on some sections; at 'congested', **Itaewon Station's exits split by direction (exits 1-2 in, 3-4 out)**
- At 'very congested', emergency text alerts, dispersal advice and **trains passing Itaewon Station without stopping** were on the table

Similar measures are likely, so if you plan to be in Itaewon on 30-31 October:

- **Itaewon Station may be skipped by trains or have one-way exits.** Know the walk from Noksapyeong or Hangangjin.
- Follow staff directions for **keep-right and one-way walking**.
- Do not stop in narrow alleys (the World Food Street, Quy Nhon Street, the Road of Memory and Safety).
- Once Yongsan-gu announces this year's plan, closures and dates may change. Check its website before you go.

## Quick summary

- **War Memorial: free**, opens 09:30, **last entry 17:00**, closed Mondays and the day after a holiday run including a Monday. Tours need no sign-up; English tours in Korean War Room II at 10:30 and 14:30.
- **'That Day, My Everyday Life Became War'** runs to 29 November, free.
- **Seoul Central Mosque**: cover up, no photos of worshippers, no official visiting hours.
- **World Food Street = Itaewon-ro 27ga-gil**, **Quy Nhon Street = 330m of Bogwang-ro 59-gil**, **Gyeongridan-gil = 900m of Hoenamu-ro**.

- **24 October citizens' memorial (Seoul Plaza)**, **29 October ceremony (Gwanghwamun)**; the Road of Memory and Safety is for remembrance.
- **Saturday 31 October is Halloween**, alert level 'caution'. Expect possible skipped stops and one-way exits at Itaewon Station.
