In Ikseon-dong, the useful question is not "which place is officially recognised" but **what time it is**. This hanok neighbourhood by Jongno 3-ga Station, the lane next door in Donui-dong and Seosulla-gil along the Jongmyo Shrine wall have no restaurants in the MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026, none on the Seoul Future Heritage list and none on the government's good-price register (checked 1–2 October 2026). What they do have is a dense run of hanok cafés and restaurants written up by Visit Seoul (the Seoul Tourism Organization's site) and the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), and at night the crowds move to the Galmaegi alley and the tent-bar street.

The short version: **dessert in an Ikseon-dong hanok café or a café facing the Seosulla-gil stone wall in the morning and afternoon; gukbap, kalguksu or dumplings for lunch; a hanok courtyard barbecue or a Seosulla-gil bar for dinner; the street stalls late at night.** Every place below was checked against Visit Seoul, KTO, Seoul city or Jongno-gu records or press reports, and any description of food or atmosphere is quoted from those sources.

## What this guide covers

Here, "Ikseon-dong" means **Ikseon-dong itself plus Donui-dong, Myo-dong, Bongik-dong, Gwonnong-dong and Waryong-dong, with Seosulla-gil and Donhwamun-ro between them** — roughly everything off exits 4, 6, 7 and 8 of Jongno 3-ga Station.

Some neighbours have their own guides and only get a link here. **Nagwon-dong** across the road (rice cakes, braised monkfish, ₩3,000 soup and Euljimyeonok's 2024 move) is in [where to eat in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/), and the **tent-bar street** on Donhwamun-ro 11-gil is in [Jongno 3-ga pocha street](/en/posts/jongno-pocha-2026-09/). Chanyangjip, a 1965 noodle shop in Donui-dong, and Gyerim Sikdang are also covered in detail in the Jongno guide, so they are only named here.

There are two likely reasons. Visit Seoul's write-ups show that most places here opened in converted hanok from the 2010s onward, while Future Heritage and Oraegage reward **long-running businesses** and the good-price register lists **items priced below the local average**. If you want officially recognised old restaurants, cross the road to Nagwon-dong and Insadong; they are collected in [where to eat in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/).

### Why there are so few chains: the 2018 district plan

On 28 March 2018, Seoul's urban planning and architecture committee passed an amended **district unit plan for 31,121.5 m² around 165 Ikseon-dong** (Seoul Economic Daily and Munhwa Ilbo, 29 March 2018). It made Ikseon-dong a designated hanok cluster, and according to those reports **franchises and chain-type businesses were barred from opening, to curb gentrification**. Buildings facing the main roads, such as Donhwamun-ro, were capped at five storeys (20 m).

Jongno-gu eased the rule a little in March 2025. Yonhap (21 March 2025) reported that in five district-plan areas including Bukchon and Ikseon-dong, **franchisees who already held a permit** may keep trading under a new contract — **only convenience stores and coffee shops**, and only in the same use and floor area — so that head offices cannot squeeze them at renewal. It does not open the door to new chains.

### Songam Onban: gukbap in a former banquet-house hanok

**Songam Onban** (31-3 Donhwamun-ro 11na-gil) was added to Visit Seoul in August 2026. Its write-up says it reopened in **a century-old Ikseon-dong hanok**, carrying on the tradition of **Songam, once counted among Korea's three great yojeong** (high-end banquet houses with entertainment), and serves pork gukbap (rice in broth), boiled pork, savoury pancakes, dumplings and traditional liquor.

DiningCode's menu lists **onban ₩12,000, onban set ₩18,000, soy-glazed boiled pork ₩32,000, Songam dumplings ₩9,500 and samgyetang ₩18,000**. Visit Seoul gives **11:30–22:00, open every day**; DiningCode shows a 15:00–17:00 break and last orders at 21:00. 224 m from exit 6.

Songam is not Ikseon-dong's only banquet-house story. According to the Seoul Future Heritage entry for Mugyewon (2021-008), **Ojinam** in Ikseon-dong became a Korean set-meal restaurant in 1953 and was registered as **Seoul's restaurant No. 1**; it is also known as the place where the 1972 inter-Korean joint statement was discussed in advance. When it faced demolition in 2010, Jongno-gu moved its timbers to Buam-dong to build Mugyewon, and a hotel went up on the old site in 2013. That is why Jongno-gu's Ikseon-dong walking course includes "the site of Ojinam".

## Seosulla-gil: along the Jongmyo wall

![A bowl of hojicha bingsu on a pink tray with three small cups of condensed milk, tapioca pearls and coconut](/images/ikseon-food-2026-09/02.webp)

This photo from Wikimedia Commons shows bingsu at a café in Melbourne, not food from a Seosulla-gil shop.

**Seosulla-gil** means "the western patrol road": it ran west of the post where guards patrolled Jongmyo, the royal ancestral shrine (Visit Seoul). A Visit Seoul article says it runs **about 800 m** from 45-4 Jongno 3-ga to 26 Gwonnong-dong and that nearby buildings are **limited to two storeys** out of respect for Jongmyo. One side of the street is the shrine's stone wall, so the shops all face it. Since 20 December 2025 Jongno-gu has run Seosulla-gil as a **car-free street on Saturdays and Sundays from 10:00 to 22:00**, four hours longer than before (Herald Business, 22 December 2025).

Hankyung Business (16 May 2023) described Seosulla-gil as **a spin-off of the Ikseon-dong boom**: jewellery workshops and artists moved in first to escape Ikseon-dong rents, the first food business opened in 2014, and a craft beer pub in 2017 set off a wave of restaurants and cafés. The same article said average monthly sales on Seosulla-gil **rose 60.4%, from ₩4.16 billion in 2019 to ₩6.68 billion in the first quarter of 2023**.

## After dark: Galmaegi alley and the tent-bar street

When the sun goes down, the **Galmaegi alley** in Ikseon-dong and the tent-bar street on Donhwamun-ro 11-gil fill up. Jongno-gu named **Donhwamun-ro 11-gil a "coexistence street" in November 2024**, and on **1 October 2025** signed a joint operating agreement with the Ikseon-dong Galmaegi Alley Merchants' Association and others, covering more than 70 businesses (Jongno-gu press release, 16 October 2025). Yajang means drinking at outdoor tables, and here it is now formally managed rather than banned. The Kyunghyang Shinmun (29 September 2026) reported that **77 businesses** — 24 street stalls and 53 shops — now operate under the scheme.

The Kyunghyang Shinmun (23 September 2026) wrote that more foreign visitors are eating **Korean barbecue in old-style and hanok-converted restaurants** in the Galmaegi alley, and that enforcement officers, safety staff and the merchants' own safety team are on duty from 18:00 to 04:00 every day. Hours, stall numbers, toilets and smoking rules are covered in [Jongno 3-ga pocha street](/en/posts/jongno-pocha-2026-09/).

The scene has made news abroad too. According to the Korea Economic Daily (17 September 2026), **Time Out ranked Jongno 3-ga first among its 40 "coolest neighbourhoods in the world" for 2026** (it was third in 2021), citing the tent-bar nightlife and **Seosulla-gil's independent workshops, jewellery studios and bars**.

## Across the road in Nagwon-dong: what the monkfish street really is

Cross Samil-daero from Ikseon-dong and you reach Nagwon-dong's braised monkfish (agujjim) alley. The earlier version of this guide got this wrong.

- **The Seoul Future Heritage listing is a restaurant, not the street.** Entry **2019-007 is Yetnaljip Nagwon Agujjim** (436 Samil-daero). The phrase "Jongno 3-ga Nagwon-dong monkfish street" appears only once, in the entry's explanation that it is **"the oldest monkfish restaurant on the Jongno 3-ga Nagwon-dong monkfish street"**. On 2 October 2026 none of the 103 Jongno-gu Future Heritage entries was a "monkfish street".
- According to the entry, a couple who had sold agujjim in Masan in the 1960s came to Seoul and opened this restaurant; other monkfish restaurants clustered around it in the 1990s. The same page gives the founding year as both **1972 (history) and 1977 (preservation note)**.
- It is one of nine Jongno-gu **Oraegage** shops in Seoul's 22 August 2024 press release.
- **Centennial Store** status: Esquire Korea (11 January 2023) wrote that it holds both the Centennial Store and Seoul Future Heritage plaques, but we did not confirm it on the government list.

The menu, prices and hours — plus Nagwon Tteokjip, Yujin Sikdang and the ₩3,000 soup next door — are in [where to eat in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/).

## Good to know

**People live here.** Jongno-gu's walking-course pages, including the Ikseon-dong course, carry four requests: don't startle residents with shouting or careless filming; take your rubbish with you; keep noise down and never use loudspeakers or microphones; and **don't peer into or secretly photograph homes**. In many lanes a café door sits right next to someone's front gate.

**This is not Bukchon's red zone.** The visiting-hours ban (17:00 to 10:00 the next day) and ₩100,000 fine apply to **about 34,000 m² around Bukchon-ro 11-gil in the Bukchon special management area**, designated by Jongno-gu on 1 July 2024 (Jongno-gu culture platform). Ikseon-dong is not part of it. Details are in the [Bukchon guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/).

**Card payment cannot be refused.** Article 19 of Korea's Specialized Credit Finance Business Act bars card merchants from refusing card payment. KTO lists cards as accepted at Ikseondong Geujip and Chanyangjip. The law does not guarantee every terminal will accept a foreign-issued card, so carry a Korean payment option or some cash for outdoor stalls and small shops.

**Some places have conditions.** The Queen's Guard is pay-at-the-end, Daramjwi requires an alcohol order, and Heritage Club seats up to four during café hours (Visit Seoul).

**Parking is hard.** KTO and Visit Seoul list no parking for the Ikseon-dong hanok street, Seosulla-gil, Gamkkotdang and Teumari.

**No tipping.** The menu price is what you pay. If Korean food is new to you, start with [Korean food for beginners](/en/posts/korean-food-for-beginners-2026-09/).
