Ikseon-dong is **a neighbourhood of alleys packed with cafes and restaurants made out of converted hanok**. But the first thing this post has to say is not about prices.

**People live here.** Jongno-gu has set out promises it asks visitors to keep, and those come first.

![Shops with mint-green wooden doors and glass fronts running along a narrow alley beneath tiled hanok eaves](/images/ikseon-guide-2026-09/03.webp)

## The four things Jongno-gu asks

This notice is posted on the Ikseon-dong page of Jongno-gu's official tourism site.

> **Parts of the alley walking course are places where residents actually live.**
> For the walk to be enjoyable for everyone, we need your considerate behaviour.
>
> **Promise 1.** Do not startle or disturb residents by shouting or taking photographs carelessly.
> **Promise 2.** If you make rubbish, you must take it with you.
> **Promise 3.** Mind even small noises, and never use a loudspeaker or a microphone.
> **Promise 4.** Do not peer into residents' hanok or homes, or photograph them without permission.

The alleys are narrow, and in many places you cannot tell a cafe door from somebody's front gate. **That is what the fourth promise is about.**

### Do not confuse this with Bukchon

You may have read online that "Seoul's hanok village now has visiting-hour limits and fines". **That is Bukchon, not Ikseon-dong.**

| | Bukchon red zone | Ikseon-dong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visiting-hour limits | **Yes** (10:00 to 17:00 only) | No |
| Fines | **Yes** | No |
| Request for quiet | Yes | **Yes, as guidance** |

Bukchon is designated **a special management area**, and since 1 November 2024 visiting hours for tourists have been restricted across about 34,000 square metres around Bukchon-ro 11-gil. **Ikseon-dong has no such regulation.** There is guidance, though.

## Cafe prices

The Seoul Tourism Organization publishes prices for Ikseon-dong's cafes. These are not estimates but official listings.

- **Scones 4,700-5,800 won** (about $3.50-4.35)
- **Cake 8,000-8,300 won** (about $6.00-6.25)
- **A meal is in the 10,000s of won** (from about $7.50)

The price includes the cost of the converted hanok space. You may find it expensive, but that is the going rate in this neighbourhood.

## One street over it is different

![An Ikseon-dong alley, a shop sign hung above a hanok gate and a bicycle propped against the wall](/images/ikseon-guide-2026-09/04.webp)

This is probably the most useful part of the post.

These are the published prices under the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's "good price" scheme. **All of them are within a ten-minute walk of Ikseon-dong.** Checked on 22 September 2026.

| Restaurant | Area | Published price | From Ikseon-dong |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eulmildae (Somunnan Gukbap Jeonmun) | Nakwon-dong | **Ugeoji haejangguk 3,000 won** (about $2.25) | about 270m |
| Insadong Kalguksu | Insa-dong | Kalguksu, doenjang jjigae and sundubu, 7,500 won each (about $5.60) | about 510m |
| Suin Insadong Dakhanmari | Gyeongun-dong | Samgyetang 13,000 won (about $9.75) | about 420m |

**A 3,000-won hangover soup and an 8,300-won cake are ten minutes apart from the same subway exit.** A fivefold difference.

Some places open from four in the morning. Eulmildae is listed as 04:00 to 22:00.

### This is the floor, not the average

The usual caveat. The criterion for the "good price" scheme is **"items priced below the average for the surrounding local trade area"**. Only places cheaper than their neighbours are included, so this is **the floor**, not the average.

## There are no certified restaurants inside Ikseon-dong

This was a surprising find.

**Not a single officially certified restaurant sits inside Ikseon-dong as a legal neighbourhood.**

- The "good price" scheme - **0** in Ikseon-dong out of 86 in Jongno-gu
- Model restaurants - **0** in Ikseon-dong out of 74 in Jongno-gu
- Safe restaurants - **0** in Ikseon-dong out of 200 in Jongno-gu

Every certified place is across the road in **Nakwon-dong and Insa-dong**.

This is not a criticism. Most of Ikseon-dong's businesses are recently opened cafes and dining rooms, and these schemes are aimed at **long-established places or cheap ones**. But it is worth knowing before you come that **if you are looking for "a place the government has certified", Ikseon-dong does not have one**.

## The Nakwon-dong braised monkfish street

It is right next to Ikseon-dong and completely different in character.

**"The Jongno 3-ga Nakwon-dong braised monkfish street" is the official name**, written out on the Seoul Future Heritage certificate.

**The Seoul style of braised monkfish** differs from the Masan style. It **uses fresh monkfish and is thickened with starch**, so it keeps a shallow pool of sauce. Midodeok sea squirts and bean sprouts go in, and you finish with fried rice made in the pan.

It is **167m from exit 5 of Jongno 3-ga Station**.

One of the restaurants there holds **all three certifications: Seoul Future Heritage (2019), Oraegage (2024) and Centennial Shop**.

## Things worth knowing

**Every restaurant and cafe is entirely non-smoking**, regardless of floor area, since 2015.

**There is no tipping, and the menu price is the final price.** Whether it is a "good price" listing or a tourism organization listing, all prices are written inclusive of VAT.

**Refusing a card payment is illegal.** That said, very old shops may not accept foreign-issued cards, so it is safer to carry a little cash.

**Ikseon-dong gets crowded in the evening.** The alleys are narrow, so when a queue grows it ends up outside somebody's front gate. That is what promises 1 and 3 are for.

## What could not be verified

Honestly stated.

**No guarantee can be given about whether any individual shop in Ikseon-dong is still trading.** One dumpling restaurant introduced on the Seoul Tourism Organization page was already marked **"Closed"**. Businesses turn over quickly here.

**Jongno-gu's food and drink page has gone.** As of September 2026 that address now serves a page about hanok instead, so this post does not cite it.

**The safe restaurant list stops at the 2020 edition.** It was a temporary Covid-era programme.

**No law or regulation governing noise or trading hours inside Ikseon-dong could be found.** What Jongno-gu has posted is guidance, not regulation.

## Wrapping up

- **Jongno-gu asks visitors to keep four promises.** People live here.
- **The visiting-hour limits and fines belong to Bukchon.** They do not apply in Ikseon-dong.
- **Cafes run to 4,700-5,800 won for a scone and 8,000-something for cake.**
- **Across the road in Nakwon-dong there is a 3,000-won hangover soup.** Ten minutes apart, five times the price.
- **There are 0 officially certified restaurants inside Ikseon-dong.** They are all in Nakwon-dong and Insa-dong.
- **"The Nakwon-dong braised monkfish street" is an official Seoul Future Heritage name.**

In one line: **take your photographs in Ikseon-dong and eat in Nakwon-dong.**
