"Meet me at Dongdaemun" is vaguer than it sounds, because Seoul has about four Dongdaemuns. There is a **gate** (Heunginjimun, "the Great East Gate"), a **district** (Dongdaemun-gu), a **market** (Dongdaemun Fashion Town), and two **stations** (Dongdaemun, and Dongdaemun History & Culture Park).

The short answer: the Dongdaemun travellers go to is not Dongdaemun-gu. The gate is in **Jongno-gu** and DDP and the malls are in **Jung-gu**. The Cheonggyecheon stream runs between the two districts, and two separate tourist zones meet here. This is the deep guide to that Dongdaemun, put back together from public records: a stadium built in 1925, a Joseon city wall found underneath it, Zaha Hadid's DDP opening on top in 2014, and a redevelopment decision Seoul made in September 2026.

Things to do, food and hotels are split out into [things to do in Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/), [where to eat in Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/) and [where to stay in Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-hotels-2026-10/). Gwangjang Market, one stop west at Jongno 5-ga, has its own [Gwangjang Market food guide](/en/posts/gwangjang-market-2026-09/), and the Euljiro side has the [Euljiro guide](/en/posts/euljiro-guide-2026-09/).

## Dongdaemun at a glance

In this guide, Dongdaemun means: **south of Cheonggyecheon (Jung-gu)**, DDP and the Dongdaemun Fashion Town tourist zone; **north of it (Jongno-gu)**, Heunginjimun, Dongdaemun General Market, Changsin-dong and Dongmyo. We include Sindang-dong to the east and leave Gwangjang Market and everything west of Euljiro 4-ga to other guides.

Dongdaemun-gu is missing from the table. Korean Wikipedia notes that the district was created on **10 June 1943**, when Seoul first got wards, and took its name from the gate's nickname, but **the gate itself now belongs to Jongno-gu**. In 1975 Changsin-dong and Sungin-dong were moved from Dongdaemun-gu into Jongno-gu as well. Go to "Dongdaemun-gu" today and you get Cheongnyangni and Gyeongdong Market, not DDP or the gate.

## The gate: Heunginjimun

### 1396 to 1869

According to the Korea Tourism Organization, Heunginjimun is the great east gate of the eight gates in the Hanyang (Joseon Seoul) city wall. It was **first built in 1396, when the wall went up**, repaired in 1453, and **the gate you see today was rebuilt in 1869**. It is a two-storey gate tower five bays wide.

Two things set it apart:

- **The ongseong**: a half-moon wall wraps around the outside of the gate as an extra defence. Among Seoul's gates, only Heunginjimun has one.
- **Four characters, not three**: its name board reads 興仁之門, while Sungnyemun and the others use three. Korean Wikipedia passes on the traditional explanation that a character was added to strengthen the energy of the east. Treat that as lore.

![Heunginjimun lit up at night, its stone arch glowing orange under the two-storey gate tower, with white cars and an orange taxi passing in front](/images/dongdaemun-guide-2026-10/12.webp)

### A gate on a traffic island

Heunginjimun now sits like an island in traffic. Per Korean Wikipedia, **the walls on either side were pulled down in 1907–1908 to make way for tram lines**, which is why the gate stands alone.

The wall picks up again on the hill behind it, in **Heunginjimun Park**. According to the Seoul city government (February 2021), the park was made in **2010 on 11,519 m² of the former Ewha Womans University Dongdaemun Hospital site**, and because it is high it lets you **look down inside the ongseong**. By the entrance are **13 inscribed stones** recording who built each stretch of wall, the biggest group in one place of the roughly 280 found around the whole wall. The Seoul City Wall Museum is at the top, and the Naksan section of the wall walk starts here. The walk itself is covered in [things to do in Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/).

### No longer "Treasure No. 1"

Heunginjimun was **designated a Treasure on 21 January 1963**, and for decades it was called "Treasure No. 1". The Cultural Heritage Administration (now the Korea Heritage Service) decided the numbers implied a ranking, and after a 2021 decree change the official name became simply **"Treasure Heunginjimun"** (YTN, February 2021; Kyunghyang Shinmun, July 2021). You will still see the old number in older guidebooks.

The Dongdaemun Fashion Town tourist zone council says on its website that the colonial government listed the gate under the name "Dongdaemun" in 1934 and that the name Heunginjimun was restored in 1996. Those dates are the council's account; we could not confirm them against Korea Heritage Service records.

## A stadium for 83 years, and a wall underneath

### From Gyeongseong Stadium to Dongdaemun Stadium

The DDP site was a stadium. Combining Seoul city sources and Korean Wikipedia:

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Joseon era | Military sites (Hadogam, Hullyeonwon) |
| **15 October 1925** | Gyeongseong Stadium opens |
| 1945 | Renamed Seoul Stadium after liberation |
| January 1985 | Renamed Dongdaemun Stadium |
| **1 March 2003** | Main (football) stadium closes |
| After the 2003 Cheonggyecheon works – April 2008 | A flea market for displaced Cheonggyecheon street vendors occupies the football pitch |
| 18 December 2007 | Demolition of the baseball stadium begins |
| **14 May 2008** | Farewell event; main stadium demolished |

When the football stadium closed, a market moved in. The **Cheonggyecheon restoration, begun in 2003**, cleared out the Hwanghak-dong "goblin market" and the stream-side street vendors, who were moved to a flea market at the stadium. When the stadium was due to come down they moved again, in **April 2008, to the new Seoul Folk Flea Market in Sinseol-dong** (Hankook Ilbo, April 2009). The same paper reported that weekend footfall there halved in the first year, from 8,000 to 4,000. Opinion News traces today's **Dongmyo flea market** scene partly to the traders who drifted back toward the old spot instead.

### The wall that came out of the ground

When the stadium came down, Joseon came up. According to Seoul, **excavations in 2008–2009** found the city wall and its bastions, the **Igansumun water gate**, the Hadogam site and in total **44 building foundations and around 1,000 artefacts**.

Igansumun is a two-arched sluice that let water out of the walled city. A Seoul city article says **the tops of the arches appeared about 3.7 m down in September 2008, and the whole structure was exposed by November**. It had been assumed lost to the stadium and earlier park works.

So DDP was designed around these remains. The water gate was restored in place, the finds went into the **Dongdaemun History Museum** in the park, and the stadium years into the **Dongdaemun Stadium Memorial**. **Two floodlight towers and the torch cauldron** were kept too. Both halls are **open 10:00–19:00, closed Mondays, free** (Seoul city, July 2025).

The station changed with it. The [[line:2]] [[line:4]] [[line:5]] interchange called Dongdaemun Stadium was renamed **Dongdaemun History & Culture Park from 1 December 2009** (Hankook Ilbo, November 2009), after 24 years.

## The market's timeline

### From shacks to Pyeonghwa Market

Dongdaemun's market story starts beside Cheonggyecheon just after the Korean War. Pulling together Seoul Shinmun (2013) and Seoul city (2019):

- Refugees from the North built shacks along the stream and sewed clothes to sell on one or two machines. **Pyeonghwa** means "peace", a name said to carry the traders' hopes.
- A big fire burned the shanty town in 1958; after the stream was covered over, a market building went up. Seoul Shinmun says a building much like today's was **completed in February 1962**.
- In the 1960s and 70s, Pyeonghwa Market **made and sold clothes in the same building**, in cramped mezzanine workshops of cutters, machinists and teenage assistants.
- On **13 November 1970**, a 22-year-old cutter at the market, **Jeon Tae-il**, set himself on fire outside it, shouting "Obey the Labour Standards Act."
- Seoul city writes that by the 1970s this area supplied **70% of the domestic clothing market**.

In the same year, 1970, **Dongdaemun General Market** opened on the north bank at Jongno 6-ga as "the largest single market building in the East" (Jongno-gu). It is still where fabric, buttons and zips are traded, and Jongno-gu calls it the one market where you can see "materials, finished goods and design in one place."

### Malls that open at night

In the 1990s Dongdaemun became a night market. According to the tourist zone council, modern wholesale buildings such as **Art Plaza, Nuzzon and APM** opened one after another as **night fashion markets**, pushing fast, design-led clothing at home and abroad. In the late 1990s, retail-focused fashion malls followed on the west side.

| When | What | Source |

| 1996 | Freya Town opens (now Hyundai City Outlet) | Asia Economy 2015 |

- **31** mall buildings, of which **22 are night wholesale malls open 9 p.m. to 7 a.m.**, and 6 open 24 hours
- About **30,000** shops and about **150,000** people working in the trade
- A million visitors a day and about ₩50 billion (about $37 million) in daily sales

The first line is the one that matters for visitors. **Many Dongdaemun malls are night-time wholesale markets.** By day some floors are shuttered; at night the aisles are full of retailers and buyers with huge bags. Whether a building serves walk-in retail customers, and when, varies by mall, so check the mall you want before you go. ## Two tourist zones

A tourist zone boundary runs right through Dongdaemun. In Korea, a "special tourist zone" is an area designated under the Tourism Promotion Act to get support for tourism facilities and promotion. Two of them meet across Cheonggyecheon here.

| | Dongdaemun Fashion Town | Jongno-Cheonggye |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Designated | **23 May 2002** (Ministry of Culture and Tourism notice 2002-8) | **22 March 2006** |
| Area | about 586,000 m² | about 540,000 m² |
| Extent | Jung-gu: Gwanghui-dong 1–2-ga, Euljiro 5–7-ga, Sindang-dong | Between Jong-ro and Cheonggyecheon, from the Gwanghwamun Building to Sungin-dong crossroads |
| Dongdaemun parts | DDP, Doota, Migliore, Pyeonghwa Market, Goodmorning City | Dongdaemun General Market (Jongno 6-ga), part of Changsin-dong |

The Fashion Town zone was driven by the traders themselves. The council's history says **13,067 people petitioned the National Assembly for designation in March 2001**; the application went to the city in December 2001 and to the ministry in April 2002, and designation followed that May. The Jongno-Cheonggye zone runs all the way west to Gwanghwamun, so [Gwanghwamun, City Hall and Jonggak](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-guide-2026-09/) and [Gwangjang Market](/en/posts/gwangjang-market-2026-09/) sit in the same zone.

### September 2026: 70% vacancy and a redevelopment plan

The post-boom numbers are stark. On **16 September 2026, at its 15th Urban Planning Committee**, Seoul approved with amendments the designation and plan for the **DDP 1, 2 and 3 urban redevelopment districts** (Financial News and SEN Money, September 2026). As reported:

- Area: around DDP and the History & Culture Park, plus part of Gwanghui-dong, **366,949 m²**
- Why: **over 70% vacancy** in some fashion malls
- Aim: turn the fashion district into a **K-culture and stay-longer tourism hub** with entertainment companies, hotels and venues
- Incentives: up to 200% extra floor-area ratio for entertainment firms, up to 50% more for beauty and fashion, up to 150% for hotels of three stars or more
- Space: a **30 m-wide east–west green corridor** linking the Sewoon area to DDP, and a north–south green axis toward Jongmyo and Namsan

This is a planning decision; actual projects depend on landowners in each block coming forward. Visitors won't notice it yet, but it means **some blocks around DDP may become building sites over the next few years**.

The same month the Seoul Design Foundation launched the **"DDP × Dongdaemun Super Pass"**, grouping about 140 shops in Sindang-dong, Jangchung-dong and Gwanghui-dong into six routes (Money Today, September 2026). The idea is to get DDP visitors to walk into the neighbourhood rather than leave.

## Changsin-dong: sewing on a hillside

### A neighbourhood of sewing machines

The slope north-east of the gate is **Changsin-dong**. According to Weekly Kyunghyang (May 2019), war refugees built shacks on the Naksan hillside, and Pyeonghwa Market's sewing work spread up into these houses: orders from the market, sewing in the alleys, finished garments back down to the market. The result is a neighbourhood where **homes and workshops share the same buildings**.

That is why nobody can count the workshops. The same article put the number at **about 900**, citing the sewing association and estate agents, while noting that no precise statistic exists.

![Old houses stacked up the Naksan hillside on a clear autumn day, with the Seoul City Wall running along the ridge above and red ivy and trees in the foreground](/images/dongdaemun-guide-2026-10/23.webp)

### Shrinking numbers

Dong-A Ilbo (October 2024) analysed Statistics Korea's business census microdata:

| Seoul garment sewing industry | 2020 | 2023 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workers | 70,875 | **60,266** |
| Businesses | 15,571 | **13,769** (down 11.6% in three years) |

The paper blamed cheap clothing ordered direct from AliExpress, Temu and Shein, and described "two or three dark workshops every 30 metres" in Changsin-dong's sewing alley.

The museum built to honour the trade didn't last either. **Ieumpium Sewing History Museum**, opened by Jongno-gu on 11 April 2018 at the top of a Changsin-dong lane below the Naksan wall, **closed on 28 February 2023** after drawing fewer than 10,000 visitors a year, according to reporting cited by Korean Wikipedia. Older blogs still list it, so don't make the climb for it.

### Quarry and redevelopment

Higher up the slope is a **quarry cliff** cut during the Japanese colonial period. According to a Seoul urban planning page (July 2023), a redevelopment plan for **Changsin-dong 23 (64,822 m², about 1,058 homes, up to 28 storeys)** stages building heights so they don't block the Naksan ridge and the city wall, and keeps the quarry face as a landscape feature. Changsin-dong's lanes are where people live and work: if you take photos, don't peer into open workshops.

## Dongmyo: a shrine and a flea market

[[line:1]] [[line:6]] Dongmyo Station is named after **Donggwanwangmyo**, the eastern shrine to Guan Yu, a deified Chinese general of the Three Kingdoms era. According to Korean Wikipedia, it was **built from 1599 to 1601 at the request of Ming China** after the Imjin War, with the Ming emperor sending money and a name board. Its **roof shaped like the character 工** joins two buildings in one. It became a **Treasure on 21 January 1963**.

The lanes outside its wall are the **Dongmyo flea market**. Seoul city (March 2023) dates it to the **late 1980s**, places it in Sungin-dong, Jongno-gu, and says it is 62 m from exit 3 of Dongmyo Station. Some shops and stalls open on weekdays, but weekends are the busy days. Across the stream is the old Hwanghak-dong goblin market, and as above, Opinion News links Dongmyo's scene to the scattering of those traders via the stadium and Sinseol-dong.

![A street by Dongmyo on a cloudy day, clothes and bags laid out under colourful parasols, shoppers browsing, and a red sign for a health-food discount store](/images/dongdaemun-guide-2026-10/19.webp)

## Sindang-dong and Gwanghui-dong: outside the wall

South-east of DDP lies the area outside **Gwanghuimun**, a city gate through which the dead were carried out of the walled city, hence its nickname "the gate of corpses." Korean Wikipedia says shamans settled outside it, so the area was called **Sindang (神堂, "spirit shrine")**, and after the 1894 Gabo Reforms the first character was swapped for one meaning "new" (新堂). Today it is better known for its tteokbokki (spicy rice cake) alley and Jungang Market; see [where to eat in Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/).

South of DDP in **Gwanghui-dong** is **Central Asia Street**, full of Cyrillic signs. Jung-gu says it began forming in the early 1990s after Korea and Russia established diplomatic relations, as Russian traders came to buy from the Dongdaemun clothing malls; people from Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan and Mongolia followed, and when Russia's economy faltered in the late 1990s, Mongolians took the Russians' place. The New Geumho Tower, the hub of Mongolian shops, is usually just called "Mongol Tower." The street signs are a record of Dongdaemun's days as an export window.

## What I had wrong

Four things I believed before researching this turned out to be wrong.

**One: I assumed Dongdaemun was in Dongdaemun-gu.** The gate is in Jongno-gu and DDP in Jung-gu. Dongdaemun-gu only borrowed the name, and even Changsin-dong and Sungin-dong moved to Jongno-gu in 1975.

**Two: Heunginjimun is no longer "Treasure No. 1".** It has been a Treasure since 1963, but since the 2021 change its official name is just "Treasure Heunginjimun".

**Three: I thought DDP simply replaced a stadium.** Under the stadium were the Joseon city wall, the Igansumun water gate and 44 building sites, and DDP was built around them.

**Four: I thought the Ieumpium Sewing History Museum was still open.** It opened in 2018 and closed on 28 February 2023.

## How to do it

North and south, day and night are different places here, so this order keeps backtracking down:

1. **Morning**: Dongmyo Station exit 3 for the shrine and flea market. Busier at weekends.
2. **Around lunch**: walk west to Heunginjimun. Climb Heunginjimun Park behind it to look into the ongseong, and take the Naksan wall walk if you have time.
3. **Afternoon**: cross Cheonggyecheon to DDP. See the water gate, History Museum and Stadium Memorial first, then an exhibition.
4. **Evening**: dinner on Central Asia Street or in Sindang-dong; see [where to eat in Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/).
5. **Night**: the malls and DDP lit up. Wholesale buildings start moving around 9 p.m.

## Summary

- The Dongdaemun visitors go to is **not Dongdaemun-gu but Jongno-gu (the gate, Changsin-dong, Dongmyo) and Jung-gu (DDP, Fashion Town)**, split by Cheonggyecheon.
- **Heunginjimun**: first built 1396, rebuilt 1869, the only Seoul gate with an ongseong. Since 2021 its official name is "Treasure Heunginjimun".
- **The DDP site**: 1925 stadium → 2008 demolition → excavation finds **the Igansumun water gate, the wall and 44 building sites** → **DDP opens 21 March 2014**, clad in 45,133 panels.
