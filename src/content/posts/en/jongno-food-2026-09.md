Jongno's food grew up around a market, a temple and a university. In the 1910s Nagwon Market had **sixteen rice cake shops** (according to the owner of Nagwon Tteokjip, quoted by the Kyunghyang Shinmun). Beside Insadong, the Jogye Order of Korean Buddhism runs its own temple food restaurant. And up in Daehangno, a **1925 Chinese restaurant and a 1956 coffee house** from the days when Seoul National University's liberal arts college was there are still open (Seoul Future Heritage).

The short version: **lunch on temple food or Gaeseong-style dumplings in Insadong, an afternoon of rice cakes and traditional tea, then dinner in Nagwon-dong's monkfish and soup alleys or at an old Daehangno spot.** In the MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026, the only Bib Gourmand in this area is **Gaeseong Mandu Koong**, and **nine** restaurants and tea rooms here are listed as Seoul Future Heritage. Every restaurant below was checked against Seoul city, Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), MICHELIN or Ministry of the Interior records or press reports. Any description of taste is quoted from those sources.

## What this guide covers

Here, Jongno means **the back streets on both sides of Jong-ro from 1-ga to 4-ga, Insadong (Gwanhun-dong and Gyeongun-dong), Gyeonji-dong and Gongpyeong-dong, Nagwon-dong and Donui-dong, Unni-dong, Waryong-dong and Wonseo-dong, and Daehangno** (the university district around Hyehwa Station: Hyehwa-dong, Myeongnyun-dong, Dongsung-dong, Yeongeon-dong and Ihwa-dong).

The neighbors have their own guides. Cheongjin-dong's hangover soup alley, Mijin and Imun Seolnongtang are in [where to eat around Gwanghwamun](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-food-2026-09/). Ikseon-dong's hanok cafés are in the [Ikseon-dong food guide](/en/posts/ikseon-food-2026-09/), and Samcheong-dong sujebi is in the [Bukchon food guide](/en/posts/bukchon-food-2026-09/). **Gwangjang Market** is at Jongno 5-ga and has [its own guide](/en/posts/gwangjang-market-2026-09/). The grilled fish and dakhanmari alleys are in [where to eat in Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/), and the Jongno 3-ga tent bar street is in [Jongno 3-ga pocha street](/en/posts/jongno-pocha-2026-09/). This guide covers the gaps between them.

## Temple food: Balwoo Gongyang and Sanchon

Korean temple food is what Buddhist monastics eat. KTO's description says it uses **no meat, no artificial seasoning and none of the five pungent vegetables** (spring onion, garlic, chives, wild rocambole and asafoetida). You may read that it also avoids onion, but onion is not on the official list. Because it drops meat, fish and their stocks, the Seoul Economic Daily (3 August 2025) noted that temple food **meets vegan standards**.

### Balwoo Gongyang, run by the Jogye Order

**Balwoo Gongyang** is on the **5th floor of the Templestay Information Center** (56 Ujeongguk-ro, Gyeonji-dong), directly opposite Jogyesa temple. Its official site describes it as **the only temple food restaurant run directly by the Cultural Corps of Korean Buddhism**, an arm of the Jogye Order. The name means the formal monastic meal eaten from wooden bowls called *baru*.

Courses and prices from the official site, **including 10% VAT**. Dishes change with the seasons.

| Course | Price | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seon | ₩36,000 (about $27) | **Weekday lunch only** |
| Won | ₩50,000 | |

The autumn Seon course lists sweet pumpkin porridge with water kimchi, root vegetable japchae, lotus root and mushroom in mustard dressing, autumn vegetable pancakes, glazed mushrooms, and sorghum rice with seasonal soup. The site explains that the meal opens with porridge because monastics traditionally **begin the day with porridge**.

- Hours: **Mon–Sat, lunch in two sittings 11:30–13:20 and 13:30–14:50 (last order 14:00), dinner 18:00–20:20 (last order 19:10); closed Sunday** (official site)
- Mechanical parking in the building (cars up to 2,500cc); tables for four and six, and private rooms.
- Getting there: 3 minutes from Anguk Station exit 6, 7 minutes from Jonggak Station exit 3-1 (official site)

Buddhist media reported that Balwoo Gongyang held **one MICHELIN star for three straight editions, 2017 to 2019** (Beopbo Shinmun, Bulgyo Shinmun, BTN). It is **not in the MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026**, and its old MICHELIN page no longer loads. We could not find when it dropped out.

### Sanchon and Osegyehyang

**Sanchon** (30-13 Insadong-gil), in the middle of Insadong, was described by the Seoul Economic Daily as a temple food restaurant where **more than 60% of guests are foreign**; staff said they look for language skills when hiring. DiningCode lists a **set meal at ₩29,000 and bibimbap at ₩18,000**, open 11:30–22:00. The same article said about 70% of guests at **Osegyehyang** (14-5 Insadong 12-gil), a vegan Korean restaurant, are foreign. Its menu on DiningCode **marks each dish as with or without the five pungent vegetables**; soft tofu and doenjang bibimbap is ₩12,000 and vegan jjamppong ₩13,000.

The article also reported that foreign participants in temple food cooking classes at Jongno's **Korean Temple Food Center** rose **28.4% to 627 in January–July 2025**, and that classes can now be booked through online agencies such as Airbnb.

## Dumplings and set meals in Insadong

### Gaeseong Mandu Koong: the area's only Bib Gourmand

**Gaeseong Mandu Koong** (11-3 Insadong 10-gil) is a **Bib Gourmand** in the MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026. Yonhap News already photographed it as a Bib Gourmand pick in November 2017.

KTO says it was **started in 1970 by a grandmother from Gaeseong** (now in North Korea) and is run by the third generation. Gaeseong dumplings are known for being large and full; this kitchen fills them with plenty of napa cabbage and mung bean sprouts. MICHELIN describes a converted hanok, wrappers made from dough **fermented for three hours**, and a broth **simmered for eight hours with beef and ten vegetables**. The house signature is Gaeseong-style dumpling soup with *joraengi-tteok*, little rice cakes pinched in the middle like a gourd.

- Prices (DiningCode): **joraengi rice cake dumpling soup ₩19,000, six steamed dumplings ₩15,000, meat dumpling hot pot ₩21,000 per person**. There is a separate **mushroom dumpling plate for vegetarians** (₩18,000).
- Hours: sources disagree. **MICHELIN gives Mon–Fri 11:30–15:00, Sat 11:30–21:00, Sun 11:30–20:00.** **KTO gives weekdays and Saturday 11:30–21:30 (weekday break 15:00–16:00), Sunday 11:30–20:00, open every day.** If you want a weekday dinner, call first (+82 2-733-9240).
- No parking; cards accepted (KTO).

### Seoncheonjip: North Korean home cooking since 1971

**Seoncheonjip** (5 Insadong 14-gil) became **Seoul Future Heritage in 2020 (2020-009)**. According to the listing, its founder fled Seoncheon in North Pyongan province in 1947, **opened here in 1971**, and learned royal court cooking from Hwang Hye-seong, a designated master of Joseon court cuisine. To keep the taste of Pyongan-style pastes and kimchi she kept **some thirty earthenware jars** at a house near Namhansanseong. The listing says Insadong's 1970s art boom brought restaurants into the hanok side streets, and Seoncheonjip became **a regular haunt of writers**.

KTO names a **17-side-dish set meal** as the signature and gives hours of **11:00–21:00, closed Sunday**. DiningCode lists the Korean set meal at ₩15,000, bulgogi at ₩30,000 and soy-marinated crab at ₩38,000. It is a Centennial Store (*Baengnyeon Gage*), a status given by the Ministry of SMEs and Startups, though KTO and Seoul Future Heritage give different years (2018 and 2019).

## How much of Pimatgol is left?

Pimatgol was the back lane where commoners walked to avoid the horses of officials on Jong-ro in the Joseon era. The **Seoul Future Heritage listing (2013-266)** gives its address as the area around **32 Donhwamun-ro 5-gil**, on the Jongno 3-ga side. Its timeline:

- After Junghakcheon stream was covered in 1965, pancake houses moved in; hangover soup restaurants, inns and tea rooms followed in the 1970s.
- Subway Line 1 construction in 1974 erased **the southern lane; only the northern one remained**.
- About twenty shops closed in 2003 redevelopment, and the 2009 Cheongjin-dong redevelopment drew criticism.
- The city then designated the Jongno 2-ga to 6-ga stretch for restoration, and **finished upgrading the Jongno 3-ga to Jongmyo section (470 m) in 2012**.

The listing says parts of Pimatgol survive **between the back of Kyobo Book Centre at Jongno 1-ga and Jongno 3-ga**. The Cheongjin-dong side, now inside new office towers, is covered in [where to eat around Gwanghwamun](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-food-2026-09/). We found no official records of individual shops on the Jongno 3-ga stretch.

## Nagwon-dong's rice cake row: Nagwon Tteokjip

![A heap of small round pink and white honey rice cakes](/images/jongno-food-2026-09/03.webp)

A generic photo of honey rice cakes from Wikimedia Commons, not from a Nagwon-dong shop.

Several *tteok* (rice cake) shops line Samil-daero in front of Nagwon Arcade. **Nagwon Tteokjip** is **Seoul Future Heritage (2013-043)** and was named a **Seoul Oraegage** in 2017. Oraegage, literally "long-lasting shop", is Seoul city's label for businesses of 30 years or more.

According to the listing, around **1912 the current owner's maternal grandmother learned rice cake making from a court lady at Changdeokgung palace and began selling in Nagwon Market**. Three generations on, it has supplied rice cakes for presidential inaugurations since about 1948, and the range has grown from about twenty kinds to **more than sixty**. The best seller is **mugwort injeolmi, developed around 2003 with fresh mugwort**. Rice cakes are made in a workshop at 1 Nagwon-dong and sold at 10 Nagwon-dong; a second branch opened in Insadong in 2006.

The Kyunghyang Shinmun (29 November 2017) gives the founding year as **1919**. It quotes the owner saying there were **sixteen rice cake shops on the street** when it opened, and that work starts at 2 a.m. with the first batch ready around 11 a.m.

DiningCode lists the shop as **Wonjo Nagwon Tteokjip (438 Samil-daero)**, with **packs of mugwort injeolmi or mixed rice cake at ₩2,500 and black sesame dumpling cakes at ₩4,000**, open 08:00–21:00. **It is a takeaway shop, not a place to sit and eat.**

## Nagwon-dong's monkfish alley

![A white plate piled with bean sprouts and monkfish tossed in red chilli sauce, sprinkled with sesame](/images/jongno-food-2026-09/04.webp)

A generic photo of braised monkfish from Wikimedia Commons, not from a Nagwon-dong restaurant.

**Yetnaljip Nagwon Agujjim** (436 Samil-daero) is **Seoul Future Heritage (2019-007)** and a **2024 Seoul Oraegage**. According to the listing, a couple who sold *agujjim* (braised monkfish) in Masan in the 1960s moved to Seoul and opened here, making it **the first restaurant on Nagwon-dong's monkfish street**. Other monkfish restaurants gathered around it in the **1990s**.

The listing also explains how **Seoul-style agujjim** differs from the Masan original: it uses **fresh rather than dried monkfish**, is **thickened with starch**, adds sea squirt and other seafood, and ends with **fried rice** made in the same pan. The founder recalls that they only made monkfish soup until a friend suggested trying the braise.

- Hours: **11:30–22:00, open every day**, cards accepted (KTO)
- Prices (DiningCode): **braised monkfish ₩50,000 small, ₩60,000 medium, ₩70,000 large**, sized to share. Seafood braise and seafood stew cost the same.
- About three minutes' walk from Jongno 3-ga Station exit 5 (Seoul Future Heritage)

The same listing gives the opening year as both **1972 (history) and 1977 (summary)**. The [Ikseon-dong food guide](/en/posts/ikseon-food-2026-09/) also mentions the street briefly.

### Yujin Sikdang: a refugee's gukbap and Pyongyang noodles

**Yujin Sikdang** (40 Jong-ro 17-gil) is **Seoul Future Heritage (2014-039)**. The listing says **a founder from North Korea opened an *abai sundae* (North Korean-style blood sausage) and seolleongtang restaurant in an alley beside Nagwon Arcade in 1968**. After closing in 1985 the family **reopened as Yujin Sikdang at the current site in 1988**; the second generation runs it now.

The listing names two signatures: **gukbap** in a broth of simmered bones and meat, and **Pyongyang naengmyeon** with noodles made from **70% buckwheat** in beef broth. Early customers were mostly elderly refugees from the North; younger diners came after 2005, and Japanese visitors increased after it appeared in a Japanese magazine. The listing estimates around 300 customers a day.

DiningCode lists **mul-naengmyeon and bibim-naengmyeon at ₩11,000, mung bean pancake ₩10,000, seolleongtang and pork-head gukbap ₩7,000**, open **11:30–21:00 (break 14:30–16:00)**. It is two to four minutes from Jongno 3-ga Station (Seoul Future Heritage).

### Euljimyeonok has moved here

**Euljimyeonok**, a Pyongyang naengmyeon house that spent 37 years in Euljiro before redevelopment closed it in 2022, **reopened in Nagwon-dong on 22 April 2024** (Money Today, JoongAng Ilbo). DiningCode gives the address as **12 Samil-daero 30-gil (55-1 Nagwon-dong)**, with **naengmyeon and bibim-naengmyeon at ₩15,000, beef gukbap ₩13,000 and sliced pork ₩30,000**, open **11:00–20:50 (break 14:50–17:00)**. Its Euljiro years are covered in the [Euljiro food guide](/en/posts/euljiro-food-2026-09/).

### ₩3,000 hangover soup

The streets around Jongno 3-ga exit 5 and the wall of Tapgol Park are so full of cheap restaurants and barbers that the Dailian (19 January 2024) called the area **a haven for older people**. The Ministry of the Interior's good-price registry lists **Eulmildae (Somunnan Gukbap)** at 131 Supyo-ro with **ugeoji haejangguk (cabbage hangover soup) at ₩3,000**, open **04:00–22:00** (checked 2 October 2026).

The Herald Business (26 July 2026) reported that a Nagwon-dong soup house called **Somunnanjip** sells gukbap for ₩3,000 and was a regular haunt of the late TV host Song Hae, who died in 2022. ## Daehangno: a 1925 Chinese restaurant and a 1956 coffee house

Daehangno was home to Seoul National University's liberal arts college until the 1970s, and a few of its old haunts survive.

### Hakrim Dabang

**Hakrim Dabang** (2F, 119 Daehak-ro) is **Seoul Future Heritage (2013-267)** and a **2017 Seoul Oraegage**. The listing says it **opened in 1956** and was so tied to the university that students called it **"Lecture Room 25"**. In 1981 a student democracy group held its first meeting here, which became the starting point of the so-called Hakrim Incident, and writers and artists including Yi Cheong-jun, Kim Seung-ok, Kim Ji-ha and Kim Min-gi spent their youth here. It was also a filming location for the 2013–14 drama *My Love from the Star*, which still brings foreign visitors.

DiningCode lists **Vienna coffee ₩6,500, Americano ₩6,000 and cream cheesecake ₩6,500**, open **10:00–23:00**. It is one minute from Hyehwa Station (Seoul Future Heritage).

### Jinachun

**Jinachun** (18 Daemyeong 1-gil, Myeongnyun 4-ga) is **Seoul Future Heritage (2013-244)** and a **2024 Seoul Oraegage**. A 2021 Seoul city summary lists it as the **second-oldest of the 50 restaurants on the Future Heritage list, opened in 1925** (after Imun Seolnongtang, 1904). The listing says an ethnic Chinese founder opened it **on the second floor of the building next to Hakrim Dabang**; the name means "a garden blooming like spring". After changes of owner and a gap, it reopened in Daehangno in 2001 and moved to the current address in 2010.

The listing names **samseon jjamppong in a white broth flavoured with dried chillies instead of chilli oil** as the signature. DiningCode lists **jajangmyeon ₩8,000, jjamppong ₩9,000 and small sweet-and-sour pork ₩25,000**, open **11:30–21:00**. Two to three minutes from Hyehwa Station.

### Lim's Chicken, Hyehwa branch

**Lim's Chicken** is **Seoul Future Heritage (2017-010)**, listed at **143 Daehak-ro (the Hyehwa branch)**. According to the listing it **opened in 1977 in the Shinsegae department store in Myeongdong** as Korea's first fried chicken franchise; since the Myeongdong store closed, Hyehwa is **the oldest surviving branch in Seoul**. DiningCode lists **original fried chicken ₩21,000 and kimchi jjolmyeon ₩11,000**, usually open 15:00 to 01:00.

## Vegetarian, vegan and halal

- **Vegan**: the temple food restaurants (Balwoo Gongyang, Sanchon) and the vegan Korean restaurant Osegyehyang are the clearest choices. Balwoo Gongyang's official site and Osegyehyang's menu both say whether dishes use the five pungent vegetables.
- **Vegetarian dishes on a regular menu**: Gaeseong Mandu Koong marks its mushroom dumplings, chilli-and-chive pancake and potato pancake as vegetarian (DiningCode).
- Ordinary Korean soups and stews are usually made with anchovy or beef stock. Ask before ordering.
- **Halal**: KTO's description calls **Persian Palace** (9 Seonggyungwan-ro 6-gil, Myeongnyun 2-ga) a "halal food restaurant" serving curries and kebabs. We **could not confirm certification by the Korea Muslim Federation or a rating in KTO's Muslim-friendly restaurant scheme.** For certified places see the [Itaewon food guide](/en/posts/itaewon-food-2026-09/).

## Good to know

**Card refusal is illegal.** Article 19 of the Specialized Credit Finance Business Act says **card merchants may not refuse card payment or treat cardholders less favourably**. Official sources confirm cards at Balwoo Gongyang, Gaeseong Mandu Koong, Yetnaljip Nagwon Agujjim and Chanyangjip. The law does not guarantee that every terminal accepts foreign-issued cards, so carry a little cash or a Korean payment option for rice cake shops and soup houses.

**Watch Sundays and Mondays.** Balwoo Gongyang, Seoncheonjip, Chanyangjip, Gyerim Sikdang, Insadong Kalguksu and Insadong Boribap close on Sunday; Nwijo and Kali on Monday; The Green Table on Monday and Tuesday. Yetnaljip Nagwon Agujjim and Sundae Sillok are open every day (KTO).

**Some prices are lunch only.** Balwoo Gongyang's ₩36,000 Seon course is weekday lunch only, and Yungmi's ₩10,000 rice bowls are registered as lunch prices.

**Sources disagree on hours.** Gaeseong Mandu Koong's weekday dinner (MICHELIN and KTO differ) and Yeolchajip's opening time are examples. Check a map app before you go. If Korean food is new to you, start with [Korean food for beginners](/en/posts/korean-food-for-beginners-2026-09/).

**There is no tipping.** The menu price is the final price.

## Wrapping up

- **Temple food**: **Balwoo Gongyang**, run by the Jogye Order (₩36,000–120,000, closed Sunday, one MICHELIN star 2017–2019); **Sanchon**, popular with foreign visitors; vegan Korean at **Osegyehyang**.
- **Insadong**: MICHELIN 2026 Bib Gourmand **Gaeseong Mandu Koong**, North Korean home cooking since 1971 at **Seoncheonjip**, and the poet's tea room **Gwicheon**.
- **Nagwon-dong**: **Nagwon Tteokjip** (c. 1912), Seoul-style braised monkfish at **Yetnaljip Nagwon Agujjim**, refugee-founded **Yujin Sikdang**, **Euljimyeonok** (moved here in 2024), and **₩3,000 hangover soup**.
- **Daehangno**: **Jinachun** (1925), **Hakrim Dabang** (1956), the Hyehwa branch of **Lim's Chicken** (1977), plus registered ₩3,000 kimchi stew and ₩3,500 bibimbap.
