If Tokyo is the first Japanese city most readers visit, Osaka is usually the second, and often the one they like more: flights from Seoul are under two hours, Kansai Airport has direct services from across East and Southeast Asia, and Kyoto, Nara and Kobe are all within 40 minutes by train, so one base covers four cities. The catch is that the airport sits well outside town and two railway companies, Nankai and JR, compete for you the moment you land. This guide starts with that choice. Every fare and opening time below is as of October 2026, taken from Nankai, JR-West, Osaka Metro, each attraction's own pages and OSAKA-INFO, the Osaka Convention and Tourism Bureau site. Prices are in yen; at roughly ¥150 to the US dollar, ¥1,000 is about $6.70.

## Kansai Airport: know your terminal first

Osaka's international gateway is Kansai International Airport (KIX). From Seoul, both Incheon and Gimpo have direct flights, shown as 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours. On the Gimpo route, Peach went up to four daily round trips in February 2026, which spreads departures across the day. Low-cost carriers such as Peach use **Terminal 2**, while the railway station and the limousine bus stops are at **Terminal 1**, so you take the free shuttle between terminals first. The bus company itself says to allow an extra 15 minutes from Terminal 2.

## Airport to city: fares and times

Adult one-way fares. The rule of thumb: Nankai if you are staying in Namba or Shinsaibashi, JR for Umeda, Shin-Osaka or Tennoji, and the limousine bus if you have heavy luggage and a hotel on its route.

**Rapi:t** is Nankai's airport limited express, all reserved seats. Since the fare revision of April 1, 2026 the express fee is ¥700 (¥910 for a Super Seat), so a ticket from the counter to Namba is ¥1,670. The Rapi:t Digital Ticket sold in Nankai's app is cheaper at ¥1,410 for a regular seat and ¥1,590 for a Super Seat, and buying on board adds ¥300, so sort it out before you get on. If you are not in a hurry, the Airport Express on the same tracks costs ¥970 and takes about 45 minutes, which is the better value.

**HARUKA** is JR's airport limited express and runs through Tennoji, Osaka Station and Shin-Osaka to Kyoto. The HARUKA One-way Ticket for overseas visitors costs ¥1,600 to Tennoji, ¥1,900 to Osaka or Shin-Osaka and ¥2,700 to Kyoto from November 1, 2026 (¥1,300, ¥1,800 and ¥2,200 until October 31). It is not sold at counters in Japan: you book on JR-West's online reservation site and collect it from a machine at Kansai-Airport Station with your passport. Regular fares are much higher, around ¥2,400 to Osaka Station in a non-reserved car according to japan-guide (May 2025), which is why the visitor ticket matters. If an express feels like too much, the Kansai Airport Rapid reaches Osaka Station for ¥1,180 in about 65 minutes.

**Limousine buses** cost ¥1,800 to Osaka Station (Umeda) in about 60 minutes and ¥1,400 to Namba OCAT in about 50, and the Umeda route runs every 10 to 15 minutes through the day. The first bus leaves the airport around 6 am and the last around midnight (0:53 for Umeda). Late-night buses are cashless only.

## Transit cards and passes: one ICOCA, then do the maths

As in Tokyo, one IC card rides everything in Osaka: JR, Nankai, Kintetsu, Hankyu, Hanshin and Osaka Metro. The Kansai card is **ICOCA**, but a Suica from Tokyo or a Welcome Suica Mobile works here too, since the cards are interoperable nationwide. To get a new one, the JR ticket office at Kansai-Airport Station sells a visitor ICOCA (the wind-and-thunder-gods design) for ¥2,000 including a ¥500 deposit, one per person on a temporary-visitor passport. On an iPhone you can add **ICOCA** straight from the Wallet app: it needs an iPhone 8 or later on iOS 16 or later, there is no separate app, and you top up with a Visa, Mastercard, JCB or American Express card registered to Apple Pay. Some cards are not accepted, and the balance cannot be refunded from Wallet.

If you will ride Osaka Metro several times a day, the **Enjoy Eco Card** is the cheap option: ¥820 on weekdays and ¥620 on weekends and holidays (children ¥310) for unlimited rides on all Metro lines and city buses for one day, sold at station machines (Yumeshima Station excluded). It also gives discounts at about 20 attractions. A visitor-only Metro and bus pass sold at the airport information centre costs ¥1,000 for one day and ¥1,700 for two, but the Eco Card bought in town is cheaper.

If you plan two or three paid attractions in a day, run the numbers on a pass. The **Osaka Amazing Pass** is ¥3,500 for one day or ¥5,000 for two consecutive days and covers about 40 attractions free, including Osaka Castle's main tower, Tsutenkaku, the Umeda Sky Building (until 15:00) and the Tombori River Cruise, plus Osaka Metro, city buses and the Hankyu, Hanshin, Keihan, Kintetsu and Nankai lines within the city. From October 2026 there is also a Kansai Airport version, a 28-hour ticket for ¥5,300 that includes the Nankai ride from the airport to Namba. It is sold through the SURUTTO QRtto app. The **Osaka e-Pass** is cheaper at ¥2,400 for one day or ¥3,000 for two, but it includes no transport; it gives one free entry each at more than 25 places such as the Umeda Sky Building (until 15:00), Tsutenkaku, the Tombori River Cruise and the Santa Maria cruise. Osaka Castle's tower and Harukas 300 are not on it (only on the ¥6,500 Premium version), you must leave 30 minutes between facilities, and there is no child price, so everyone aged 3 and over pays the same.

## Where to stay: four bases compared

![Umeda's high-rise skyline and the large roof of JR Osaka Station seen from the Hankyu Grand Building](/images/osaka-guide-2026-10/36.webp)

**Namba and Shinsaibashi** are the Rapi:t terminus, so luggage from the airport is easiest here. The [[line:midosuji]], [[line:yotsubashi]] and [[line:sennichimae]] meet, and Kintetsu trains to Nara and Hanshin trains to Kobe start here. Dotonbori and Kuromon Market are on foot and the streets stay busy late, so if eating and nightlife are the point, this is the base.

**Umeda (Osaka Station)** is the northern hub where JR, Hankyu, Hanshin and the [[line:midosuji]] meet. Day trips to Kyoto and Kobe are a single JR ride, the limousine bus stops at hotel doors, and the department stores, underground malls and the Umeda Sky Building are here along with most of the high-rise hotels. Dotonbori is four stops away on the [[line:midosuji]].

**Shin-Osaka** is the shinkansen station, convenient if you are continuing to Tokyo, Kyoto or Hiroshima, and HARUKA stops here too. There is little to see around the station and it is quiet, which suits a sleep-and-go itinerary. Umeda is three stops away on the [[line:midosuji]].

**Tennoji and Abeno** is HARUKA's first stop, 30 minutes from the airport, with the [[line:osakaloop]], [[line:midosuji]] and [[line:tanimachi]] plus Kintetsu's Osaka-Abenobashi Station. Harukas 300 is on top of the station and Shinsekai and Tsutenkaku are a walk away. It draws fewer tourists than Namba or Umeda, so evenings are calmer.

## A 4-day core itinerary

One district per day keeps the moving around short. Fares and hours below come from each attraction's official information.

### Day 1: Osaka Castle, Kuromon Market and Dotonbori

![Osaka Castle's main tower across the wide lawn of the Nishinomaru Garden, with office towers behind](/images/osaka-guide-2026-10/34.webp)

**Osaka Castle's main tower** costs ¥1,200 for adults, ¥600 for university and high school students and nothing for junior high school and younger. It opens 9:00–18:00 with last entry at 17:30 and closes December 28 to January 1. Inside it is a museum with an observation floor at the top, while the park, moats and walls are free, and plenty of reviews say the view from outside is enough. The closest station is Tanimachi 4-chome, where the [[line:tanimachi]] and [[line:osakachuo]] meet (exits 1-B and 9); from Osakajokoen or Morinomiya on the [[line:osakaloop]] it is a 15–20 minute walk.

Lunch is **Kuromon Market**. Right outside Nippombashi Station on the [[line:sennichimae]], a 580 m covered arcade holds around 150 shops. Hours and closing days vary by shop, so think of it as roughly 9 am to evening. The usual way to eat is to buy grilled seafood at the stall and eat it right there.

![Dotonbori at night near Ebisubashi, with neon signboards on both banks reflected in the canal](/images/osaka-guide-2026-10/04.webp)

Evening is **Dotonbori**. The Glico running man seen from Ebisubashi bridge and the neon along both banks of the canal are the default Osaka photo. There is nothing to pay; walk from Namba Station on the [[line:midosuji]], or straight from Kuromon Market, which keeps Day 1 compact.

### Day 2: Shinsekai, Kaiyukan and the Umeda Sky Building

![Shinsekai at night, with people walking between oversized 3D shop signs toward the illuminated Tsutenkaku tower](/images/osaka-guide-2026-10/07.webp)

**Tsutenkaku** is the tower in the middle of Shinsekai. Adults (15 and over) pay ¥1,500 and children (5–14) ¥800, and it opens 9:00–21:45 with last entry at 21:15; over New Year (December 30 to January 3) the prices are ¥1,800 and ¥900. It is 3 minutes from exit 3 of Ebisucho Station on the [[line:sakaisuji]], 6 minutes from exit 1 of Dobutsuen-mae on the [[line:midosuji]] and 6 minutes from the east exit of Shin-Imamiya on the [[line:osakaloop]]. The alleys under the tower are the kushikatsu street, so have lunch here.

![The whale-shark mural on the outside wall of Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan and the tiled plaza in front](/images/osaka-guide-2026-10/23.webp)

The afternoon is **Kaiyukan**, 5 minutes from exit 1 of Osakako Station on the [[line:osakachuo]]. Admission is date-based and moves between ¥2,700 and ¥3,500 for adults. Standard hours are 10:00–20:00, varying by date, with last entry an hour before closing; pick your date on the official e-ticket site to see that day's price. From Shinsekai, take the [[line:sakaisuji]] to Sakaisuji-Hommachi and change to the [[line:osakachuo]].

![Looking up at the two towers of the Umeda Sky Building and the Floating Garden structure that joins them at the top](/images/osaka-guide-2026-10/11.webp)

Finish at the **Umeda Sky Building's Kuchu Teien Observatory**: ¥2,000 for adults and ¥500 for ages 4–12, open 9:30–22:30 with last entry at 22:00. From the north side of JR Osaka Station walk past Grand Front Osaka and through a short underpass and you are there. The Amazing Pass and e-Pass only cover it until 15:00, so if the night view is the goal, pay the fare and go up around sunset.

### Day 3: a day at USJ, or a day trip to Kyoto, Nara or Kobe

**Universal Studios Japan** (USJ) uses date-based pricing for its 1 Day Studio Pass: from ¥8,400 for ages 12–64, from ¥5,500 for ages 4–11 and from ¥7,600 for 65 and over. Tickets are not sold at the gate, so buy in advance on the official site or from a partner seller. Hours vary by date, typically somewhere in 9:00–20:00. From Osaka Station the [[line:yumesaki]] runs direct to Universal City in about 15 minutes for ¥200, and the gate is a 5-minute walk. Many reviews say the most popular area needs a timed-entry ticket from the official app, so install it before you go.

If a theme park is not your thing, spend the day out of town. Adult one-way fares:

| City | From → to | Train | Fare | Time |

| Kyoto | Osaka Station → Kyoto Station | JR Special Rapid | ¥580 | about 29 min |
| Nara | Osaka-Namba → Kintetsu-Nara | Kintetsu Rapid Express | ¥680 | about 37 min |

For Kyoto the [[line:jrkyoto]] Special Rapid is fastest; Hankyu (Umeda to Kyoto-Kawaramachi) and Keihan (Yodoyabashi to Gion-Shijo) are cheaper but slower. For Nara, Kintetsu is the convenient one because Kintetsu-Nara Station sits right beside Nara Park, and a reserved-seat limited express costs ¥520 more, ¥1,200 in total. Kobe is 22 minutes on the [[line:jrkobe]] Special Rapid, so even a half day works.

### Day 4: Harukas 300, then the airport

![The tall silhouette of Abeno Harukas lit up at dusk behind the five-storey pagoda of Shitennoji](/images/osaka-guide-2026-10/26.webp)

Spend the last morning at **Harukas 300**, the observatory on floors 58–60 of Abeno Harukas above Tennoji Station. Since the price change of July 23, 2026 it costs ¥2,200 for adults (high school and above) and ¥900 for elementary school children, opens 9:00–22:00 with last entry at 21:30, and never closes. Buy tickets on the 16th floor, the art museum level, and take the direct lift to the 60th. The building connects to Kintetsu's Osaka-Abenobashi Station and is 2 minutes from JR Tennoji. Afterwards, HARUKA from Tennoji reaches the airport in about 30 minutes and the Kansai Airport Rapid in about 50; from a Namba hotel, take the Rapi:t back. ## Food: Osaka is eaten standing up

![The Glico running-man sign in Dotonbori by day, surrounded by a dense wall of advertising boards](/images/osaka-guide-2026-10/35.webp)

Choose a neighbourhood rather than a restaurant. **Dotonbori and Namba** have takoyaki and okonomiyaki shops in every alley, and **Shinsekai** is the kushikatsu (deep-fried skewers) street; reviews agree the local rule is no double-dipping in the shared sauce, though more shops now hand out individual sauce. **Kuromon Market** is where you eat grilled seafood and sushi at the stall, and the pork buns (butaman) from 551 Horai, with shops inside Namba and Umeda stations, are the snack reviews mention most. Udon here means Osaka-style kitsune udon, and for a late dinner a convenience store does the job.

## Practical notes

- **Visas**: JNTO's guide says visa exemptions apply to short tourist stays for passports from a number of countries, and the Japanese embassy in Seoul confirms 90 days for ordinary Korean passports. Paid work is not allowed on a visa-free entry. Check your own passport's terms before booking.
- **Visit Japan Web**: an online pre-registration for immigration and customs. It is optional, but it speeds up customs and the tax-free procedure.
- **Tax-free changes on November 1, 2026**: purchases up to October 31 work as now, with tax deducted at the till. From November 1 you pay the tax-inclusive price in the shop, then before departure present your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal in the international departure lobby (before checking bags) and receive the refund. A green result means you are done; a red one sends you and the goods to the customs counter. The limits stay the same: within 90 days of purchase and at least ¥5,000 before tax per shop per day. Sealed packaging for food and cosmetics is no longer required, and anything used in Japan is not refundable.
- **Paying**: ICOCA balance covers convenience stores, vending machines and station shops. Market stalls and small restaurants still take cash only in places, so carry ¥10,000–20,000 in notes.
- **Weather**: Japan Meteorological Agency normals put January at 6.2°C and August at 29.0°C on average. June is the heart of the rainy season with 185 mm, the wettest month of the year, and it lingers into July; September is wet with typhoons. Snow is a few flurries in January and February and rarely settles. Cherry blossoms open in late March and peak in early April; autumn colour runs mid-November to early December at Minoh Falls and mid-to-late November at the Expo '70 park.
- **Basics**: Japan is on the same clock as Korea and one hour ahead of Singapore, Hong Kong and Malaysia. Power is 100 V with type-A plugs. There is no tipping, public bins are rare so carry a small bag for rubbish, people in Osaka stand on the right side of escalators, and phone calls on trains are frowned upon.

## Wrapping up

For a first Osaka trip: a Namba hotel, the Rapi:t in, one ICOCA, and a day each for Osaka Castle and Dotonbori, then Shinsekai and Kaiyukan. Pick one of the two observatories, Umeda Sky Building or Harukas 300, and the only real decision left is whether Day 3 is USJ or Kyoto. If you are combining it with Tokyo, [Tokyo for First-Timers](/en/posts/tokyo-guide-2026-10/) covers that end. Other cities will collect in the [overseas travel list](/en/overseas/).
