Aewol is the stretch of coast on Jeju's north-west shore that has become, over the last decade, the most café-dense place on the island. This guide does not recommend individual cafés. It explains what the road actually is, how to reach it, and when to go.

Times and prices are as of September 2026.

![The coast road curving past Aewol village with turquoise water and a harbour breakwater](/images/jeju-aewol-cafe-2026-09/01.webp)

## The coast road

It begins about **10 km from Jeju Airport** and runs roughly **9 km** along the north-west shoreline. **Free, open at any hour.**

It is not only a driving road. There is a **dedicated cycle lane and a footpath** alongside it, and this stretch forms **part of Jeju Olle Route 16**, so walkers and cyclists use it as much as cars do.

Several places along it are worth stopping for: Gueom Harbour, the Sineom-ri breakwater, Gonae Harbour, the Darak rest area and the rock formation known as the Big Stone Face. Driving it end to end without stopping misses the point.

## Why the cafés are here

Geography. The shoreline curves and the road runs tight against the water, so a great many roadside buildings look straight out to sea. And it is **close to the airport**: twenty minutes from picking up a rental car.

The result is that cafés, restaurants and guesthouses have filled these nine kilometres. **The road itself is the destination** rather than any one business on it.

The honest corollary is that it is busy. Parking is difficult on weekend afternoons and the better-known places build queues. **Come in the morning** and it is a different place.

## The Handam coastal trail

![Sunset over the sea from the Handam coastal path with wind turbines on the horizon](/images/jeju-aewol-cafe-2026-09/05.webp)

If you want to get out of the car and walk, this is the one. The **Handam Coastal Trail (한담해안산책로)** runs **1.2 km from Aewol Harbour to Gwakji Gwamul Beach**, and is also known as the **Gwakgeum Olle path**. **Free and always open.**

The waves break beside you for most of it. The path threads between weathered lava formations along a shoreline of black rock, twisting rather than running straight. In 2009 Jeju City named it one of thirty-one "hidden scenic spots" on the island.

**It faces west, so the sunset is the reason to time it.** The walk takes well under an hour, so start it an hour before the sun goes down.

## Gwakji Beach

The trail ends at **Gwakji Beach (곽지해수욕장)**, free and open at all times.

It has one unusual feature. At the far end is **Gwamul Nocheontang**, an open-air bathing spot fed by spring water that, according to local tradition, soaks into the ground on Hallasan and surfaces here at the sea. It is divided into men's and women's sides and it is meant for rinsing the salt water off after swimming.

Gwakji-ri is an old village where prehistoric shell middens have been found. Local legend says a village once stood where the beach is now and was buried in sand overnight.

## The stone salt field

![The Gueom stone salt field, basalt bedrock divided into shallow compartments holding seawater](/images/jeju-aewol-cafe-2026-09/07.webp)

At 708 Aewolhaean-ro is **Korea's only stone salt field**, known locally as *sogeum billae*. **Free and always open.**

Villagers built low earth walls across a broad shelf of cracked basalt, flooded the compartments with seawater and let the sun do the rest. At its height the salt field covered nearly **5,000 square metres** and was a significant livelihood for Gueom-ri.

There is a detail here that says something about Jeju. The salt makers **passed the salt field only to their eldest daughters**. Jeju's women have historically carried more of the island's working life than on the mainland, and the inheritance custom reflects it.

It operated into the **1950s** before cheap mainland salt ended it, and has since been restored as a heritage site. **When the compartments hold water, the surface reflects like a mirror.**

## A half-day route

Leave the airport around **10:00** and stop first at the Gueom stone salt field. Follow the coast road slowly, pulling over at the harbours and rest areas, then **walk the Handam trail** to Gwakji Beach. Pick a café anywhere along the way. Time the return for sunset.

Twenty minutes further west is **Hyeopjae Beach**, which turns a half day into a full one.

## Practical notes

- **Go in the morning.** Weekend afternoons mean difficult parking and queues.
- **Check café prices before ordering.** The sea view tends to be reflected in them.
- **A bicycle works here**, thanks to the dedicated lane.
- **Sunset is the strength.** This is the island's west-facing coast.
- **Buses reach Aewol**, but stopping at will along the coast road really needs a car.

## Wrapping up

Aewol is not a place you pick one café and drive to. It is nine kilometres of road to use slowly: start in the morning, stop at the salt field and the Handam path, drink your coffee wherever the view suits you, and stay until the sun goes down.
