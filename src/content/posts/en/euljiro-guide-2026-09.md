Until 2022, a beer hall that had grilled dried pollack since 1980 stood in the alley off Exit 4 of Euljiro 3-ga Station. In the early hours of 21 April 2022, after the sixth attempt at a court-ordered eviction, its sign came down. That same month, Seoul's mayor stood in the Sewoon Sangga arcade across the road and announced the arcade would be torn down and replaced with greenery.

Here's the short version. **Euljiro is a working district in the middle of Seoul**, where tool, lighting and printing alleys and a beer-and-pollack street still survive, and where large parts of that fabric are being replaced, one redevelopment block at a time. This guide rebuilds Euljiro from public records and dated news reports. It follows the area from the Guri-gae hill and colonial Kogane-machi to its 1946 renaming, the 1960s Sewoon Sangga and its tool alleys, the 2017 "Dasi Sewoon" revival, and the fight over a high-rise opposite the Jongmyo Shrine that ran right through September 2026.

## A name in three layers: Guri-gae, Kogane-machi, Euljiro

### Joseon's copper hill

According to Korean Wikipedia, a low clay hill once stood between today's Euljiro 1-ga and 2-ga. Its soil was copper-coloured, so it was called **Guri-gae** ("copper hill"). In the Joseon era herbal doctors and medicine shops gathered around it, and in 1887 the Jejungwon, Korea's first Western-style hospital, moved here from Jae-dong. In 1883 **Bakmunguk**, Joseon's first modern government printing office, opened in what is now Euljiro 2-ga. Many accounts trace the printing alleys back to it.

### Kogane-machi, "golden town"

Under Japanese colonial rule the Japanese called Guri-gae **Kogane-machi** (黄金町, "golden town"). Wikipedia says the 1914 administrative overhaul split the area into Kogane-machi 1-chome to 7-chome, and the east-west road through it, Kogane-machi-dori, became today's Eulji-ro. Directly south, Meiji-cho (today's Myeongdong) and Honmachi (Chungmuro) were the centres of Japanese commerce.

### 1946: named after a general

After liberation, in **1946**, Japanese-style place names were replaced and the road took the surname of **Eulji Mundeok**, the Goguryeo general who repelled an invasion from Sui China. The Jung District office's own page says the neighbourhood name comes from "the surname of General Eulji Mundeok, who defeated the Sui invasion". It was part of the same clean-up that gave Myeongdong its name that year.

## How Euljiro became a workshop district: the 1960s to 80s

![A tool and lighting shop on a main Euljiro street. Power tools, pumps, hose reels and purple LED strip lights spill out onto the pavement under a row of red shop signs](/images/euljiro-guide-2026-09/01.webp)

Walk Euljiro today and the trade changes every few blocks: tools, lighting, tiles and sanitaryware, sewing machines, printing, paper, coating, steel furniture. Most of that map was drawn between the 1950s and 1980s.

| Trade | When and how | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tools and metalwork** (Ipjeong-dong, Sallim-dong) | Started along the Cheonggyecheon stream right after the Korean War. After the stream was **covered over in 1961**, small steel shops, precision machinists and mould makers packed in, booming with Vietnam War demand and 1970s-80s heavy industry | Seoul Future Heritage |
| **Sewing machines** | Around 200 businesses, some dating from the 1950s (per a 2022 article) | Seoul Future Heritage feature |
| **Printing** (Euljiro to Chungmuro) | Printers clustered after Bakmunguk in 1883; the feature says **around 500 printers moved to Chungmuro in 1984** when Euljiro was redeveloped, creating today's layout | Seoul Future Heritage feature, Wikipedia |
| **Steel furniture** (Yegwan-dong) | Commercial and office furniture, many shops with their own workshops | Korea Tourism Organization |
| **Electronics** (Sewoon Sangga) | Korea's first all-in-one electronics market, peaking in the late-1980s PC boom | Korea Tourism Organization, Wikipedia |

The Seoul Future Heritage page on the Ipjeong-dong ironworks alley says around **530 foundry shops** once stood there, and the saying "bring a blueprint and they'll build you a tank" came from this alley. In 2012 and 2013 it was used as a film location for its industrial-era look.

Printing carries the most weight in the numbers. Citing 2014 Statistics Korea data, Wikipedia reports that **5,492 of Korea's 18,523 printing-related businesses (29.6%) were in Jung-gu**. The Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) describes the Chungmuro and Euljiro printing alleys as the district that "leads Korea's printing industry". The Future Heritage feature adds, though, that the heyday passed after the 1997 Asian financial crisis, when large printers and publishers moved out to Ilsan and Goyang.

![The glass door of a coating workshop with red lettering listing emboss, velvet, thermal and anti-scratch coating. Inside, paper rolls and a worker seated at a machine are visible](/images/euljiro-guide-2026-09/05.webp)

The printing alley isn't only printers. Coating, binding and paper wholesale each have their own shops, and the job names painted on this door show that division of labour.

## Sewoon Sangga: a 1968 experiment

### From firebreak to arcade

The site began with a war. According to the KTO and Wikipedia, in 1945 the colonial authorities cleared **firebreak strips** across Seoul in case of Allied bombing, and one was a 50-metre-wide band running from Jongmyo south to Pil-dong. After liberation and the Korean War, shacks filled the empty land.

In 1966 Mayor Kim Hyeon-ok cleared the shanty town and broke ground on **8 September** (Wikipedia). The KTO calls it "Korea's first urban redevelopment project" of 1966. The name Sewoon (世運) was meant as "may the world's energy gather here".

| Date | Event | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 September 1966 | Groundbreaking | Wikipedia |
| 26 July 1967 | Hyundai Arcade, the northernmost block, completed first | Wikipedia |
| **1968** | The **year the Seoul government gives for Sewoon Sangga**, "Korea's first mixed-use residential-commercial building" | Seoul city news site |
| 1967-1972 | After Sewoon and Hyundai, the Cheonggye, Daerim, Sampung, Pungjeon Hotel, Shinsung and Jinyang blocks went up | Newsis |

The architect was **Kim Swoo-geun**. Wikipedia says his plan put cars on the ground floor and a pedestrian-only deck on the third, linking about a kilometre from Jongmyo to Pil-dong in the air. But eight different companies built the blocks, the plans changed, and the third-floor walkway broke off in the middle. The flats upstairs, with lifts, were briefly among Seoul's most coveted homes.

### Boom and decline of the electronics arcade

![An electronics shop window in Sewoon Sangga, stacked with vacuum tubes, gauges, wires and a circuit board lit in green. Small signs below read 'equipment A/S' and 'circuit building'](/images/euljiro-guide-2026-09/14.webp)

The KTO calls Sewoon Sangga **Korea's first all-in-one electronics market**, where craftsmen handle electrical parts, audio equipment, CCTV, karaoke machines and lighting. It also notes that companies such as TG Sambo Computer and Hancom are said to have started here. Wikipedia says the late-1980s personal-computer boom was its peak, and that footfall fell after the government built the Yongsan Electronics Market in 1987 and moved dealers there.

## Thirty years between demolition and revival

Sewoon Sangga's fate flipped with each new mayor. Combining Newsis (November 2025) and Wikipedia:

| Date | Direction | Event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1995** | Demolish | The year Jongmyo became a UNESCO World Heritage Site, Seoul announced plans to demolish the arcade for a park (delayed over compensation costs) |
| October 2006 | Demolish | **Sewoon Redevelopment Promotion District** designated |
| December 2008 | Demolish | Hyundai Arcade at the north end demolished, replaced by the Sewoon Chorokddi Park |
| 2014 | Revive | Switch to preservation-style regeneration instead of demolition |
| **19 September 2017** | Revive | Sewoon Sangga reopens, phase one of "Dasi Sewoon" ("Sewoon again") |
| 2022 | Revive | Full opening of the **roughly 1 km elevated walkway** linking Sewoon, Cheonggye, Daerim, Sampung, PJ Hotel, Inhyeon and Jinyang, built for ₩110.9 billion (about $83 million) |
| **April 2022** | Demolish | Mayor Oh Se-hoon announces his **"green ecological downtown" strategy** at Sewoon Sangga |
| **26 December 2024** | Demolish | Urban regeneration committee closes the regeneration project and confirms demolition of the walkway |

![An inner corridor of Sewoon Sangga. Yellow signs for karaoke-machine makers Kumyoung and TJ hang from the ceiling beside blue shop name boards, with boxes and audio gear stacked on both sides](/images/euljiro-guide-2026-09/16.webp)

The city cited a Board of Audit finding to justify removing the walkway. According to the Herald Economy (April 2025), planners had projected 10,000 users a day, but the **actual average in October 2023 was 2,122**, about 20% of the forecast. The city removed the Sampung Arcade-to-PJ Hotel section from the pedestrian road network in February 2025 and, according to the report, planned to **demolish that 287-metre stretch from May 2026** (at a cost of ₩2.3 billion), then take down the rest in step with the park project.

## The Sewoon district today: park plans and redevelopment blocks

### The green corridor

The city's vision is a **north-south green corridor from Jongmyo to Namsan**. Figures vary slightly between reports, but the outline is consistent.

| Item | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal basis | Sewoon Redevelopment Promotion Plan, Seoul notice 2024-316 | Etoday, April 2026 |
| Area | 439,356.4 m² | Same |
| Target | Completion by 2035 | Same |
| Park | About 50,000 m² of park on the demolished arcades plus about 86,000 m² of open space on private plots, **around 136,000 m² in total** (about three times Gwanghwamun Square) | Weekly Chosun, November 2025; Etoday |
| Phase one | A temporary park on **5,670 m² around Sampung Arcade**. Implementation plan published 17 July 2025, construction "planned for the first half of 2026" | BusinessPost, July 2025 |
| East-west link | A **30-metre-wide green strip from Sewoon to the DDP** (included in the 16 September 2026 DDP redevelopment decision) | Global Economic, September 2026 |

That last row is the same decision described in the [Dongdaemun guide](/en/posts/dongdaemun-guide-2026-10/). According to the JoongAng Ilbo (November 2025), the city will buy Sampung Arcade itself but receive the other arcade buildings as contributions from the surrounding redevelopment blocks, in return for raising those blocks' height and floor-area limits. That is why the height fight over Sewoon block 4 is tied to the whole park plan.

### What has already changed

The Sewoon district isn't one project but many small blocks moving separately. Changes a visitor can already see:

- **Ipjeong-dong**: The Seoul Future Heritage site says the ironworks alley (Sewoon block 32) received its redevelopment approval in 2021 and records **"demolition for redevelopment under way in 2024"**.
- **Hillstate Sewoon Central**: A two-tower mixed-use complex at 189-1 Ipjeong-dong, eight floors below ground and up to 27 above, with **1,022 homes** (Maeil Business). The Jung District page for Euljiro-dong already lists a daycare centre inside it.
- **Sewoon block 6-1-1**: On 30 June 2026 the city's redevelopment committee approved, with amendments, an office and officetel plan that keeps at least 47% of the site as open green space and moves Exit 7 of Euljiro 3-ga Station underground (Newsis, July 2026).
- **Eulji Myeonok**: The Pyongyang cold-noodle restaurant that opened in Euljiro in 1985 **closed for redevelopment in July 2022** and moved to Nagwon-dong in Jongno (Newsquest, April 2024).

So a shop you saw in a blog or video may no longer be where it was. It's safer to **plan your walk by alley rather than by a single shop**. Places open now are listed in [Euljiro food](/en/posts/euljiro-food-2026-09/).

## Sewoon block 4 opposite Jongmyo: 2026's biggest fight

At the far north of the Sewoon district, across Jongno from **Jongmyo Shrine**, sits **Sewoon block 4** (in Jongno-gu). It was in the news all through 2026. It lies outside Euljiro proper, but the whole Sewoon green corridor plan is bound up with this block's height, so it belongs here. Jongmyo is the royal ancestral shrine of the Joseon dynasty.

## Nogari Alley and Eulji OB Bear

### 1980, a ₩380 beer

Following the Seoul Future Heritage description, here's how the alley began. **Eulji OB Bear** opened in 1980 as a draft-beer chain pub (the KTO gives November 1980), and its founder, originally from Hwanghae Province, served grilled **nogari** (small dried pollack) as the side dish. A 500 cc draft beer cost **₩380** in the early days. The KTO says the alley grew because print workers coming off night shifts stopped for a beer on the way home.

| Date | Event | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1980 | Eulji OB Bear opens | Seoul Future Heritage, KTO |
| 1980s | Nogari Alley forms; customers surge during the 1997 financial crisis | Seoul Future Heritage |
| 1989 | Second bar, Munich Hof, opens | Seoul Future Heritage |
| 2012 | Euljiro Nogari Hof merchants' association formed; annual May festival | Seoul Future Heritage |
| **2015** | **Designated Seoul Future Heritage** (2015-029) | Seoul Future Heritage |
| 2018 | Eulji OB Bear named a "Hundred-Year Shop" by the SME ministry | JoongAng Ilbo |
| **21 April 2022** | Having lost its eviction case, Eulji OB Bear is removed **on the sixth enforcement attempt** | Yonhap, Asia Economy |
| March 2023 | Eulji OB Bear reopens near the Gyeongui Line Book Street in Mapo-gu | JoongAng Ilbo |
| February 2024 | Eulji OB Bear soft-opens a branch near Euljiro 3-ga Station **Exit 10**, across the main road from the alley | JoongAng Ilbo |

The eviction was drawn out. Yonhap reported that in the early hours of 21 April 2022 around 100 people working for the court took down the sign and removed the fittings. The JoongAng Ilbo wrote that the landlord's case went all the way to the Supreme Court, and that the owner of another pub in the same alley had bought a stake in the building. That even the founding shop of a Seoul Future Heritage alley had no protection in a lease dispute was the heart of the debate at the time.

### The alley now

According to a Seoul press release (14 August 2026), the city's G3 Seoul planning committee **walked Euljiro on the evening of 13 August 2026**, through the printing and ironworks alleys, Golbaengi Alley and the outdoor-table beer street. The city wrote that Euljiro has become a landmark known as **"Hipjiro"** (a play on "hip" and "Euljiro"), and that food streets around Golbaengi and Nogari alleys draw young people and foreign visitors. It plans better night-time pedestrian safety and more multilingual signage.

"Hipjiro" appears in city press releases, but it's a nickname, not an official name. The evening atmosphere and menus are in [Euljiro food](/en/posts/euljiro-food-2026-09/).

## The printing alley: still a workplace

![Inside a print shop in the Euljiro printing alley. A large multi-colour offset press stands beside bundles of printed paper and a red hand pallet truck](/images/euljiro-guide-2026-09/06.webp)

The alleys from south of Euljiro 3-ga Station down to Chungmuro Station still run printing presses. The Future Heritage feature describes narrow lanes busy with three-wheeled motorbikes and cargo bicycles. The KTO separates the two halves: the Chungmuro side leans to digital printing, and the Euljiro side is the traditional core of platemaking and press machinery.

This is a workplace, not a theme park, so a few courtesies:

- **Loaded motorbikes, handcarts and forklifts come through constantly.** Don't stop in the middle of an alley to take photos
- **Ask before photographing anyone at work**
- Most shops are wholesale or made-to-order, so there's rarely a reason to go inside

A walking route through Sewoon Sangga and the printing alley is in [Euljiro things to do](/en/posts/euljiro-things-to-do-2026-09/).

- The same route then loops via Chungmuro to **Myeongdong Station (last bus 20:03)**. Myeongdong stop times are in [Myeongdong hotels](/en/posts/myeongdong-hotels-2026-09/)

Fares, the airport railroad and the Climate Card transit pass are in [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/en/posts/incheon-airport-to-seoul-2026-09/).

## How to do it

Euljiro looks completely different depending on the hour.

1. **Weekday daytime**: the tool, lighting and printing alleys are at work. Walk Sewoon Sangga's third-floor deck to see the arcade chain stretching north and south. Seoul's city news site lists Sewoon Sangga as open 09:00-19:00 and closed Sundays and public holidays, though the KTO notes hours vary by shop.
2. **Lunch**: old restaurants in Euljiro 3-4-ga. See [Euljiro food](/en/posts/euljiro-food-2026-09/).
3. **Afternoon**: head down the printing alley toward Chungmuro, or follow the Cheonggyecheon stream east to [Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-guide-2026-10/).
4. **Evening**: when outdoor tables go up in Nogari and Golbaengi alleys. This is exactly the time slot the city is promoting for "night tourism".

**The sights themselves are mostly free.** There's no charge to walk the alleys and arcade corridors; you spend money on food and drink.

## Where to stay

Euljiro has five subway lines and an airport bus, but **the heart of the alleys has surprisingly few places to stay**. The big hotels cluster around Euljiro 1-2-ga, Jeo-dong, Jang-gyo-dong and Cho-dong, and in Euljiro 5-ga to the east. Many hotels with "Myeongdong" in the name actually have Euljiro-area addresses (for example, Lotte City Hotel Myeongdong is in Jang-gyo-dong, Nine Tree Myeongdong II in Cho-dong, and Four Points Josun Myeongdong in Jeo-dong 2-ga). A list counted from Seoul's licensing data is in [Euljiro hotels](/en/posts/euljiro-hotels-2026-09/); the Myeongdong side is in [Myeongdong hotels](/en/posts/myeongdong-hotels-2026-09/), and a city-wide comparison in [Where to stay in Seoul](/en/posts/seoul-where-to-stay-2026-09/).

## Who it suits

**It suits you if:**

- You like old working cityscapes, where the trade changes block by block
- You want to see a building like Sewoon Sangga, at the centre of a demolition-versus-preservation fight, before it changes
- You want to try Seoul's evening ritual of beer at outdoor tables in an alley
- You want a base near an airport bus stop, within walking distance of Myeongdong, Jongno and Dongdaemun

**It may not if:**

- Palaces and hanok are your goal: the [Jongno guide](/en/posts/jongno-guide-2026-09/) or the [Ikseon-dong guide](/en/posts/ikseon-guide-2026-09/) fit better
- Shopping is the focus: the [Myeongdong guide](/en/posts/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/) is better
- You expect a tidy, polished tourist district. Some stretches are lined with construction hoardings

## In short

- **Euljiro**: a road and the numbered blocks Euljiro 1-7-ga. The Euljiro-dong district covers 0.60 km² with 3,050 residents (June 2026).
- **Name**: Joseon's Guri-gae → colonial Kogane-machi → **Euljiro in 1946** (after General Eulji Mundeok).
- **Trades**: tools and metalwork after the 1961 covering of the stream, printing (29.6% of Korea's printing businesses were in Jung-gu in 2014), lighting, sewing machines, and electronics in Sewoon Sangga.
- **Sewoon Sangga**: designed by Kim Swoo-geun, Korea's first mixed-use residential-commercial building per the city (1968). 1995 demolition plan → 2017 revival → 2024 walkway demolition confirmed → park.
- **Nogari Alley**: started by Eulji OB Bear in 1980; Seoul Future Heritage in 2015. **Eulji OB Bear evicted on 21 April 2022.**
- **Sewoon block 4**: about 180 m from Jongmyo, up to 141.9 m tall. Approved June 2026, remains plan deferred August, under review by the land ministry.
- **Airport bus [[bus:6015]]**: Euljiro 1-ga, Euljiro 3-ga (stop named "Euljiro 2-ga") and Euljiro 4-ga; last buses to the airport 19:42-19:50.

In one line: **Euljiro is where downtown Seoul's last factory alleys are turning into parks and towers, and in 2026 the pace of that change is being set by a fight over building heights in front of Jongmyo.**
