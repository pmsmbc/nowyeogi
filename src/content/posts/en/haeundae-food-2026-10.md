Eating in Haeundae (해운대) comes down to picking one of three zones. There is **Gunam-ro**, the street running from Haeundae Station down to the beach, with **Haeundae Market** beside it. There is **Mipo**, the little harbour at the east end of the sand, and **Cheongsapo**, the fishing village over Dalmaji Hill behind it. And there is **Centum City**, two subway stops away, where a department store's restaurant floor solves a rainy day. Our [Busan food guide](/en/posts/busan-food-2026-09/) covers *what* to eat; this one is about *which door to walk through* in Haeundae. Prices and hours were checked in October 2026.

## The beach road: Haeundae Amso Galbi

At 333 Haeundaehaebyeon-ro, a block in from the west end of the beach. The MICHELIN Guide's Busan page calls it a local landmark that has **held the same spot since 1964**, now run by the third generation after moving from a century-old hanok into a modern building. The menu is hanwoo (Korean beef) short rib and bulgogi, nothing else. Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) data says the founder's **diamond-cut scoring** of the rib is still used, and that the house finale is **potato noodles** simmered in broth around the rim of the domed grill.

Two things to know. The **unmarinated rib (saeng-galbi) is limited** because only the prime "flower" cut is used, and it often sells out after lunch; the MICHELIN page says it can be **reserved up to a week ahead**. Weekends and peak season bring long waits. Hours are 11:30–22:00 with a 15:00–17:00 break, open daily. The official site has no price list and is Korean-only; MICHELIN gives it the ₩₩ band, second of four, and lists cards as accepted.

## Gunam-ro: beef gukbap and pork gukbap

The streets around Gunam-ro, which runs from Haeundae Station on [[line:busan2]] towards the sea, hold most of the area's soup houses. Busan is a pork-soup city, but Haeundae's best-known soup house is actually a **beef** one.

**Haeundae Wonjo Halmae Gukbap** is known for a clear, mildly spicy beef soup cooked with bean sprouts and radish. A review-site menu lists beef gukbap and seonji (ox blood) gukbap at **₩9,500** (about $7), and the *ttaro* version with rice on the side at ₩10,000. Maps show the same name at two addresses, 40 Jungdong 1-ro and a spot on Gunam-ro 21beon-gil; the one with recent reviews and a rebuilt two-storey building is Jungdong 1-ro.

For **dwaeji gukbap**, pork soup with rice, KTO data lists **Halmaejip** on the ground floor of the hot-spring centre opposite the Haeundae-gu district office. The description says the stock is made with beef bone, pine and wood-ear mushrooms, ground perilla seed and herbs; a listing updated in May 2026 gives pork gukbap at **₩9,500**, pork-jowl gukbap at ₩13,000, and hours of 09:00–21:00 daily (last order 20:40). Late at night, **Obok Dwaeji Gukbap** at 15 Gunam-ro, two minutes from exit 3 of the station, is listed on DiningCode as 24 hours, with pork gukbap at ₩11,000 and a boiled-pork set at ₩13,000. How to season a bowl is explained in the [Busan food guide](/en/posts/busan-food-2026-09/).

## Haeundae Market: Sanggukine and the hagfish alley

![A wide iron pan of tteokbokki simmering at a Haeundae Market stall, with skewered fish cake, fried snacks and a red menu board behind](/images/haeundae-food-2026-10/01.webp)

**Haeundae Market** is five minutes from exit 3 of Haeundae Station, on Gunam-ro 41beon-gil. It dates from the 1910s and got its current form in a 2008 rebuild. KTO describes queues outside the tteokbokki, fried-snack, kalguksu and pork-soup shops, plus live-fish and grilled-hagfish restaurants. Hours are 09:00–22:00 and vary by shop.

![Fish tanks outside a restaurant in Haeundae Market's hagfish alley, with rows of photo menu signs in Korean, English and Japanese above them](/images/haeundae-food-2026-10/02.webp)

The tteokbokki stop is **Sanggukine**. KTO data calls it one of "Busan's big three tteokbokki shops" and notes that the rice cake is **garaetteok**, the thick cylinder cut, so each piece is fatter and chewier than usual. Fried snacks and fish cake are sold alongside, with takeaway boxes at the counter. Hours are 11:00–23:30 (last order 23:00), **closed Wednesdays**, no parking. Prices are posted at the stall.

In the hagfish alley, many restaurants hang **photo menus in Korean, English and Japanese** above the tanks, so you can point even without a word of Korean. Fish cake (eomuk) is bought by the skewer and eaten standing, with a cup of the broth.

## Mipo and Cheongsapo: clams with a sea view

![Aerial view of Dalmaji Hill and the Mipo side of Haeundae, with the Marine City towers and Gwangan Bridge in the distance on a clear day](/images/haeundae-food-2026-10/05.webp)

**Mipo** is the small harbour at the east end of the beach. KTO notes it was a location for the disaster film *Haeundae* and is ringed by raw-fish restaurants facing the water. **Mipo Kkeutjip** (77 Dalmaji-gil 62beon-gil) is known for clams grilled over briquette coals with onion and butter, and also does grilled eel; 10:00–01:00 (last order midnight), open daily, close to Jung-dong Station on [[line:busan2]].

![Fishing boats moored in the small harbour at Cheongsapo, with a red lighthouse at the end of the breakwater and restaurants below the hill](/images/haeundae-food-2026-10/13.webp)

Over Dalmaji Hill is **Cheongsapo**, a fishing village whose shoreline restaurants KTO groups as the "Cheongsapo restaurant row": grilled clams, grilled eel, raw fish and snow crab, most with the sea in view. **Suminine** (118 Cheongsapo-ro 58beon-gil) is an eel specialist that also grills clams, scallops, rockfish and prawns over coals, and is open 11:30–05:00 daily. Shellfish are sold at market price, so check the board before you sit. To get there, take village bus **Haeundae 2** from Haeundae Station, or ride the Blue Line Park beach train from Mipo to Cheongsapo Station.

## Milmyeon: Haeundae Gaya Milmyeon

![A steel bowl of Busan milmyeon: wheat noodles in chilled broth topped with red chilli paste, cucumber and half a boiled egg](/images/haeundae-food-2026-10/20.webp)

The city's tourism portal, Visit Busan, lists **Haeundae Gaya Milmyeon** (27 Jwadongsunhwan-ro) as the area's milmyeon house. It is near Jung-dong Station on [[line:busan2]], and the listing says the broth is simmered for 60 hours with brisket, shank, chicken feet and herbs, then aged for more than a year. The menu is milmyeon and bibim (spicy, brothless) milmyeon, 09:00–21:00. Visit Busan warns of a queue at meal times, so late morning or mid-afternoon is easier. What milmyeon is, and why it exists only in Busan, is in the [Busan food guide](/en/posts/busan-food-2026-09/).

## Dalmaji-gil cafes and the Centum City restaurant floor

**Dalmaji-gil** is the hill road from Haeundae over to Songjeong, and KTO describes it as lined with view cafes. Cafes here turn over quickly, so pick a currently open one on a map app rather than from an old list.

When it rains, or when you need options, **Shinsegae Centum City** is the easy answer. It connects directly to Centum City Station on [[line:busan2]]. The official store page gives the **restaurant floor as 11:00–21:00** and the store as 10:30–20:00 (20:30 on extended-hours days), and the **only closing day in October 2026 is Monday the 19th**. Korean, Japanese, Western and dessert counters share one floor, which makes vegetarian dishes or a child-friendly table far easier to find than at the market.

## Hotel breakfast and brunch

Most beachfront hotels run a breakfast buffet. The Westin Josun Busan's official page lists the buffet **Camellia**, the Korean restaurant Sherbul and the bakery Josun Deli; breakfast pricing depends on your room rate. Choosing a hotel is covered in the [Haeundae hotels guide](/en/posts/haeundae-hotels-2026-10/). If you would rather eat out, Halmaejip and Haeundae Gaya Milmyeon open at 09:00, and Obok Dwaeji Gukbap never closes.

## For visitors from abroad

- **English menus**: common in Haeundae Market's hagfish alley, where photo signs carry English and Japanese. Haeundae Amso Galbi's website is Korean-only.
- **Cards**: every restaurant in this guide takes cards (MICHELIN lists cards as accepted at the galbi house). Carry some cash for market stalls.
- **Waiting-list apps**: many Korean restaurants run their queues through **Tabling** or **CatchTable**. Tabling is Korean-only; CatchTable has had an English edition since November 2022, so visitors should install CatchTable Global.
- **Halal and vegetarian**: the halal guide HalalSpy (checked March 2026) lists Busan Al-Noor near Haeundae Beach as KMF-certified; certification changes, so confirm before you go. For vegetarians, tteokbokki sauce usually contains fish cake, and milmyeon and gukbap use meat stock, so the Centum City restaurant floor is the realistic choice.

## Practical notes

- **Getting there**: airport limousine bus 1 from Gimhae Airport to Haeundae is ₩9,500 (about $7). From Busan Station take [[line:busan1]] to Seomyeon and change to [[line:busan2]]; Seomyeon to Haeundae is 30–35 minutes. Gunam-ro, the market and Halmaejip are at Haeundae Station; Mipo and Gaya Milmyeon at Jung-dong Station; the department store at Centum City Station.
- **Budget**: gukbap ₩9,500–11,000, clams at market price, hanwoo galbi several tens of thousands of won per person.
- **Closures**: Sanggukine on Wednesdays, Centum City on 19 October; everything else is open daily.

## Wrapping up

For a first visit: lunch at Sanggukine in the market, dinner at the galbi house or over clams at Cheongsapo, and a bowl of gukbap on Gunam-ro the next morning covers Haeundae without leaving it.
