There are three ways to eat in Seochon, the old neighborhood on the west side of Gyeongbokgung Palace: **filling a lunchbox at Tongin Market with brass coins**, **the old-timers and barbecue joints of the Sejong Village Food Street right outside Gyeongbokgung Station**, and **a handful of MICHELIN-listed restaurants tucked into quiet back alleys**. In between sit officially recognized long-running shops such as Hyoja Bakery, which has been baking since 1987.

The short version: **lunch on the coin lunchbox or at a Bib Gourmand noodle or loach soup house, an afternoon snack of bakery bread and oil-fried tteokbokki, then dinner on the food street or at Tosokchon for samgyetang.** The MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026 lists **one one-star restaurant (Onjium) and five Bib Gourmands (Andeok, Yonggeumok, Horapa, Halmaejip and Daesungjip)** in and around Seochon. Every restaurant below was checked against records from Seoul city, the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), the Seoul Tourism Organization (STO), MICHELIN or the Ministry of the Interior and Safety, or against press reports. Any description of taste is quoted from those sources.

## What this guide covers

Seochon here means **Chebu-dong, Pirun-dong, Nuha-dong, Nusang-dong, Ogin-dong, Tongin-dong, Changseong-dong, Hyoja-dong, Gungjeong-dong, Singyo-dong and Cheongun-dong west of the palace, plus Naeja-dong and Sajik-dong north of Sajik-ro.** Seoul Future Heritage describes the Seochon hanok area as the Chebu-dong and Hyoja-dong area, where in the Joseon era "middle-class" professionals such as interpreters and physicians lived.

The neighbors have their own guides. Office-worker lunch spots in Jeokseon-dong, Naesu-dong and Dangju-dong, and the Cheongjin-dong hangover soup alley, are in [where to eat around Gwanghwamun](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-food-2026-09/). Samcheong-dong on the east side of the palace is in the [Bukchon food guide](/en/posts/bukchon-food-2026-09/), Insadong and Nagwon-dong are in [where to eat in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/), and the hanok café lanes are in the [Ikseon-dong food guide](/en/posts/ikseon-food-2026-09/). For sights and walking routes see [things to do in Seochon](/en/posts/seochon-things-to-do-2026-09/), and for the overview see the [Seochon travel guide](/en/posts/seochon-guide-2026-09/).

### Why there are so few chain bakeries and restaurants

According to Seoul Future Heritage, crowds arrived after the Suseongdong Valley was restored in 2012, bringing **over-commercialization and gentrification**. In **May 2016 the city revised the district plan for the area west of Gyeongbokgung**, capping buildings at four stories and **restricting franchise bakeries and restaurants**. That is part of why you see few chains in Seochon's alleys. Bukchon's similar rules are covered in the [Bukchon food guide](/en/posts/bukchon-food-2026-09/).

## Tongin Market: how the coin lunchbox works

![Thin rice cakes coated in red chili seasoning frying in oil on a black griddle, with a flat spatula behind them](/images/seochon-food-2026-09/02.webp)

This photo of oil-fried tteokbokki was taken at Tongin Market in February 2014 and posted to Wikimedia Commons. It is 12 years old, and the caption does not say which stall it shows.

**Tongin Market** (18 Jahamun-ro 15-gil) is Seoul Future Heritage 2015-040. According to its entry, it began in **1941 as a public market for Japanese residents near Hyoja-dong**; after the Korean War, Seochon's population grew and stalls and shops gathered around it. It was **formally registered as a recognized traditional market in 2005** and named a Seoul cultural market in 2010. KTO says the market has run the **Dosirak Café "Tong"** (lunchbox café) since January 2012.

### Step by step

The Seoul Tourism Organization's feature (updated 17 July 2026) sets it out like this:

1. About 100 m into the market is the **Customer Center**. Buy coins at the **lunchbox café on the second floor**.
2. **One brass coin (yeopjeon) costs ₩500 (about $0.40)**, and the STO suggests **about ₩5,000 (10 coins) per person**. You get a disposable lunchbox tray with your coins.
3. Walk the market and pay coins at **participating stalls** to fill the tray. The STO lists oil tteokbokki, tteok-galbi (grilled patties), gimbap with mustard sauce and sweet fried chicken as examples.
4. Go back to the café to eat. Rice, a microwave and cutlery are provided, and café drinks can also be bought with coins.

Coins are accepted **only at participating stalls** (STO listing). A government policy briefing report from 6 April 2026 also found one coin worth ₩500 at Tongin and price tags posted at stalls. Press reports since 2020 all give ₩500, so the price appears to have held for years.

### Opening hours differ by source

| Source | Lunchbox café hours | Closed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STO feature (updated 2026-07-17) | Weekdays 11:00–15:00, Sat 11:00–16:00 | Monday (whole market: third Sunday of the month) |
| STO listing (updated 2026-09-10) | "Wed–Mon" 11:00–15:00, Sat–Sun 11:00–16:00 | Every Monday, third Sunday of the month |
| KTO (updated 2026-03-09) | 11:00–15:00 | Varies by shop |
| Seoul Future Heritage | Tue–Sun 11:00–17:00 (coins sold until 16:00) | Monday, third Sunday of the month |

The STO listing says "Wednesday to Monday" yet lists Monday as closed, so it contradicts itself. What the sources agree on: **avoid Mondays and the third Sunday of the month, and on weekdays arrive before 3 p.m.** The market's shops themselves keep roughly 07:00–21:00, varying by shop (KTO and STO).

The site often given as the market's official homepage, tonginmarket.co.kr, did not load on 2 October 2026 (HTTP 410). The STO points to the market's Instagram (@tongin_official) instead.

KTO puts the number of shops at **around 70**, while Seoul Future Heritage and the STO say **about 80**. Seoul Future Heritage says the market draws over 1,500 visitors a day on average.

### Oil tteokbokki, since 1956

![A vendor uses tongs to add oil-fried tteokbokki to a white plate held by a customer, next to a perilla-leaf pancake and fritters](/images/seochon-food-2026-09/03.webp)

This photo was also taken at Tongin Market in February 2014.

**Wonjo Halmeoni Gireum Tteokbokki** (13 Jahamun-ro 15-gil), inside the market, is **Seoul Future Heritage 2014-036**. Its entry says a woman from the North, known as Grandma Maeng, **started it as a street stall on Jahamun-ro in 1956**; in 1986 the current owner's mother learned the recipe and took over, and it is now in its third generation.

Gireum tteokbokki is **stir-fried in oil, not simmered in sauce**. According to the entry, rice cakes are tossed in **a soy sauce and chili powder seasoning aged for a day** and fried in oil on a cauldron lid, with no water, so they do not go soggy when cool. The shop prepares 40–60 kg of rice cakes on weekdays and 240–260 kg on weekends and **closes when they sell out**.

DiningCode lists **oil tteokbokki and soy sauce tteokbokki at ₩5,000 each** and a mixed pancake set at ₩19,000, with hours of 10:00–19:00.

### Sonmat Gimbap: gimbap bought with coins

**Sonmat Gimbap**, inside the market, was named a **Seoul Oraegage in 2017**. ## Sejong Village Food Street (the old Geumcheongyo Market)

This eating alley starts right at Gyeongbokgung Station exit 2. KTO says it used to be called **Geumcheongyo Market** and received the **official name "Sejong Village Food Street" in 2011**. The neighborhood is called Sejong Village because King Sejong was born here.

- **Car-free**: Jongno-gu has run the street as a **car-free street since 1 February 2023**, covering a **4 m wide, 160 m long** stretch from Jahamun-ro 5 to Jahamun-ro 1-gil 32 (News1, 2 February 2023).
- **Size**: in 2015 the Asia Economy reported **112 shops employing 241 people**. The same report said landlords and tenants had signed an agreement to keep rents stable so that existing shopkeepers would not be pushed out. A current shop count could not be found in official sources.
- **Multilingual QR menus**: since November 2025 Jongno-gu has put multilingual QR menus in **38 restaurants on this street**. Scanning shows **photos, prices, spice level, main ingredients and allergy information** in **Korean, English, Chinese (simplified and traditional) and Japanese** (News1 and Seoul Shinmun, 31 December 2025). Not every restaurant has one.

## Tosokchon Samgyetang

**Tosokchon Samgyetang** (5 Jahamun-ro 5-gil, Chebu-dong) is described by the STO as a traditional samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup) restaurant **open since 1983**. KTO says it became even better known as a favorite of the late President Roh Moo-hyun, and that it grows its own ingredients, including native chickens, **four-year-old ginseng**, chestnuts, jujubes, garlic, Job's tears, perilla, pumpkin and sunflower seeds, walnuts, pine nuts and black sesame. KTO adds that there is a queue at mealtimes, but the dining room is large enough that waits are not long.

- Hours: **10:00–22:00 (last order 21:00), open every day**; all cards accepted (KTO)
- Menus in **English, Japanese and Chinese** (STO)
- Prices (DiningCode): **Tosokchon samgyetang ₩20,000 (about $15), lacquer-tree chicken soup ₩20,000, black-chicken samgyetang ₩25,000, cultured wild-ginseng samgyetang ₩26,000**, rotisserie chicken ₩19,000
- Directions: 203 m from Gyeongbokgung Station exit 2 (STO)

According to the Korea Consumer Agency's price portal, **the average price of samgyetang in Seoul in July 2026 was ₩18,192** (Jeonnam Ilbo, 25 August 2026). In the same report Tosokchon's owner, Jeong Seong-hun, said sharply higher ginseng prices were adding pressure to raise prices. **Tosokchon is not in the MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026.**

### Onjium: one star across from the palace wall

**Onjium** is on the fourth floor of 49 Hyoja-ro. MICHELIN says **Cho Eun-hee, a certified practitioner of Joseon royal court cuisine, and chef Park Seong-bae** lead a young team reinterpreting traditional Korean food for today's ingredients and techniques. There is counter seating facing the kitchen and a drinks pairing. DiningCode lists **lunch at ₩200,000 and dinner at ₩300,000** (menu changes regularly).

### Andeok: cold beef noodles and dumpling soup

**Andeok** sits deep in a Seochon alley. MICHELIN says its **cold beef noodles and dumpling soup** are the most popular dishes; the noodle soup resembles Pyongyang naengmyeon but uses **noodles with a higher buckwheat content**. MICHELIN also mentions **stuffed fried chili peppers** filled with meat and vegetables, and says **lunch queues are long, so arrive at opening time**.

DiningCode lists **dumpling soup ₩16,000, cold beef noodles (April–September) ₩16,000, biji soybean stew (October–March) ₩16,000, and fried stuffed peppers ₩19,000**. From October the cold noodles give way to the winter stew.

### Yonggeumok: loach soup since 1932

![Thick brown loach soup in a black earthenware bowl, topped with garlic chives](/images/seochon-food-2026-09/04.webp)

This is generic chueo-tang from Wikimedia Commons, not from Yonggeumok.

**Yonggeumok** (41-2 Jahamun-ro) is described by MICHELIN as a **chueo-tang (loach soup) specialist in business since 1932**. MICHELIN notes that this Yonggeumok, run by Han Jeong-ja in Jongno-gu, **shares its roots with Yonggeumok in Jung-gu but now goes its own way**. It serves both **Seoul-style soup with whole loaches** and **southern (Namdo) style soup with ground loach**. DiningCode lists **both soups at ₩15,000** and fried loach at ₩28,000.

### Horapa: Thai food in Seochon

**Horapa** means "Thai basil" in Thai (MICHELIN). MICHELIN describes a short menu and singles out **namtok het**, a salad of charcoal-grilled mushrooms, herbs and toasted rice powder, and a cinnamon-scented **massaman** curry. There are tables for solo diners, but seats are few and **reservations are essential**. DiningCode lists namtok het at ₩16,900 and tofu massaman at ₩28,900.

### Halmaejip: a jokbal house from Naeja-dong

According to MICHELIN, **Halmaejip** (1-5 Sajik-ro 12-gil) **opened in Naeja-dong in 1975 and moved to its current spot in 2006**. It is at the end of a quiet alley reached by a narrow path, so it is not easy to find, and it is a small **shoes-off** room. Its gamjatang (pork bone stew) uses only **bean sprouts and garlic chives** rather than perilla leaves or perilla powder, and the jokbal (braised pig's trotters) comes as fattier hind leg or tender foreleg. Menu prices could not be found in official sources.

### Daesungjip: beef knee soup near Dongnimmun

![Clear broth with sliced green onion, and a soft translucent piece of beef knee cartilage on a spoon](/images/seochon-food-2026-09/05.webp)

This is generic dogani-tang from Wikimedia Commons, not from Daesungjip.

**Daesungjip** (5 Sajik-ro) is at the far west end of Sajik-ro toward Dongnimmun Station, a bit apart from central Seochon. MICHELIN describes a milky broth generously filled with **knee cartilage (dogani), tendon and shank**, served with garlic pickled in gochujang, and says owner **Lee Chun-hee** still works the ladle in the kitchen. DiningCode lists **dogani-tang ₩13,000, large ₩17,000, and boiled beef ₩30,000**.

### Moulin: French tasting menus

**Moulin** (8 Jahamun-ro 16-gil) is listed by MICHELIN without a star or Bib. MICHELIN says **chef Yoon Ye-rang** combines classical French technique with Korean seasonal ingredients, numbering each course menu since opening. Its price symbol is ₩₩₩; menu prices are not in official sources.

### Hyoja Bakery

**Hyoja Bakery** is on Pirun-daero next to Tongin Market. The STO calls it **Seochon's first bakery, running since 1987**, with old-style sweet buns, doughnuts and croquettes as well as sourdough-style breads and bagels. ### Naeja Ttangkong

**Naeja Ttangkong** (111 Sajik-ro) bakes peanut crackers. The Oraegage site says the current owner's father learned the craft from a relative at 26; apart from switching from coal briquettes to gas, the method is unchanged, and the shop makes **only about 130 bags a day**. DiningCode lists peanut senbei at ₩9,000 and seaweed or ginger crackers at ₩5,000. It **closes at 4 p.m.**, so go early.

### Sinanchon

**Sinanchon** (8 Sajik-ro 12-gil, Naeja-dong) is a **2024 Seoul Oraegage**, and KTO also tags it as a "Hundred-Year Store." KTO says it is a **hanok restaurant opened in 1986** serving dishes from South Jeolla such as fermented skate with pork and kimchi, skate soybean soup, octopus soup and braised butterfish. The STO names **nakji-kkuri** (octopus wrapped around a wooden skewer and grilled), created by founder Lee Geum-sim from her hometown's ingredients, and octopus soup as its signatures. KTO notes set meals for two or more, lunch specials for three or more and courses for four or more.

DiningCode lists **octopus soup ₩26,000, the Yeongnan set (from three people) ₩35,000 per person, and nakji-kkuri ₩66,000**. It is 130 m from Gyeongbokgung Station exit 7 (STO) and **closed on Sundays**. Weekday closing time differs: 20:20 (Oraegage site), 21:30 (STO) or 21:40 with last order 20:30 (KTO).

## Good to know

**Shops cannot refuse cards.** Article 19 of Korea's Specialized Credit Finance Business Act bars card merchants from refusing card payments or treating cardholders less favorably. KTO says Tosokchon accepts all cards. However, **coins for the lunchbox must be bought at the café**, and the law does not guarantee that every terminal will accept a foreign-issued card, so a little cash helps.

**Watch out for Mondays.** The Tongin lunchbox café, Hyoja Bakery, Andeok (Mon–Tue), Halmaejip, Tori Bakery, Yeogi Inga and Maji (Mon–Tue) are closed on Mondays. On **Sundays**, Yonggeumok, Daesungjip, Sinanchon, the Cheongun-dong Jungguk and Seochon Daeha Sikdang close. Tosokchon and Chebudong Janchijip are open every day (KTO).

**MICHELIN places here are mostly lunch places.** On the MICHELIN pages, Andeok, Yonggeumok (weekdays), Horapa, Daesungjip and Halmaejip (except Tuesday) close by 2 or 3 p.m.

**Many places close when they sell out.** Official sources say this of the oil tteokbokki stall, Hyoja Bakery, the Cheongun-dong Jungguk, Byeolmi Gopchang and Seochon Daeha Sikdang.

**There is no tipping in Korea.** The menu price is what you pay. If Korean food is new to you, start with [Korean food for first-timers](/en/posts/korean-food-for-beginners-2026-09/).

## Wrapping up

- **Tongin Market**: ₩500 per coin, about ₩5,000 per person. **Avoid Mondays and the third Sunday, and on weekdays go before 3 p.m.** Try the **oil tteokbokki** that began in 1956 (Seoul Future Heritage) and **Sonmat Gimbap** from 1980 (Oraegage).
- **Sejong Village Food Street**: the old Geumcheongyo Market, a 4 m by 160 m car-free lane, with four-language QR menus at 38 restaurants and **₩5,000 janchi guksu** (good-price registry).
- **Tosokchon Samgyetang**: since 1983, open 10:00–22:00 every day, ₩20,000 on DiningCode. Not in the MICHELIN guide.
- **MICHELIN 2026**: one-star **Onjium**, Bib Gourmands **Andeok, Yonggeumok, Horapa, Halmaejip and Daesungjip**, and selected **Moulin**. Mostly lunch places.
- **Oraegage**: **Hyoja Bakery** from 1987, **Naeja Ttangkong** with about 130 bags of crackers a day, and **Sinanchon**, serving Sinan-style food since 1986.
