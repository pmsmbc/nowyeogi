Most of Seoul's "former factory" neighbourhoods are former in the full sense: the machines are gone and the buildings sell coffee. Mullae-dong (문래동) is different. Metal workshops still cut and weld steel on the ground floor while artists rent the rooms upstairs, and the cafés sit wedged between the two. If Seongsu-dong feels too polished, this is the rougher version, and it fits comfortably into half a day.

![A narrow uphill alley in Mullae Art Village, both walls covered in murals](/images/mullae-guide-2026-11/04.webp)

## From spinning mills to steel shops to studios

According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the neighbourhood grew up around textile mills built in the 1930s. The most widely cited story is that the name comes from *mullae*, the Korean word for a spinning wheel. Seoul's city news site notes that large spinning companies such as Dongyang and Jongyeon (Kanebo) operated here at the time.

By the 1970s Mullae had turned into a dense steel district of small machine shops and metal suppliers, and business boomed. Cheaper imported parts hit the area hard from the late 1990s. In the 2000s, artists began moving into empty upper floors and warehouses, drawn by low rents, and the cluster became known as **Mullae Art Village** (문래창작촌, Mullae Changjakchon).

![A corner steel supply shop with graffiti-painted shutters](/images/mullae-guide-2026-11/01.webp)

## Walking the art village

Start at Exit 7 of [[line:2]] Mullae Station. City guidance puts the workshop lanes about 200 m away; the official address for the village is 13-8 Dorim-ro 128ga-gil. The streets are public, open around the clock, and free.

What to look for:

- **Steel sculptures.** Pieces made from scrap parts and welding masks stand around the neighbourhood. Seoul's city site describes them as something you are unlikely to see anywhere else.
- **Murals and graffiti.** When a workshop pulls its shutter down, the shutter becomes a canvas. Painted walls continue down the narrower back lanes.
- **Converted workshops.** Visitor reviews consistently mention cafés and restaurants that kept the original steel frames and rough walls rather than tearing them out.

![A giant welding-mask sculpture on a tree-lined pavement](/images/mullae-guide-2026-11/05.webp)

On the main road you will find an oversized welding mask; at a nearby corner stands a giant claw hammer with a weathered wooden handle. Both make easy landmarks for getting your bearings in the lanes.

![A huge claw-hammer sculpture on a round plinth at a street corner](/images/mullae-guide-2026-11/06.webp)

## Seoul Art Space Mullae

At 5-4 Gyeongin-ro 88-gil, **Seoul Art Space Mullae** (문래예술공장) is a creative centre run by the Seoul Foundation for Arts and Culture. The Korea Tourism Organization lists its opening date as 28 January 2010, on the site of a former steel-materials arcade, with shared studios, a multi-purpose performance hall, a recording studio and seminar rooms. It is worth a stop if an exhibition or performance is on. Hours vary by facility, so call ahead (+82-2-2676-4300).

![A factory ground floor converted into a café with grid windows](/images/mullae-guide-2026-11/02.webp)

## Mullae Park

Directly beside Exit 1 of Mullae Station, Mullae Neighbourhood Park is where Seoul's city site suggests beginning a walk before heading into the art village. It also makes a quiet place to sit once you have looped back.

![A narrow lane with ivy walls and mint-green bars](/images/mullae-guide-2026-11/03.webp)

## Practical notes

- **Getting there:** [[line:2]] Mullae Station, Exit 7 for the art village and Exit 1 for the park. There is no parking in the village lanes, so the subway is the sensible choice.
- **Timing:** The lanes are always open, but cafés, galleries and restaurants keep their own hours (as of writing). Late afternoon lets you see the shutter art and then stay on as the bars open.
- **Workshop etiquette:** Many metal shops are still working businesses. The city specifically asks visitors not to get in the way on weekdays, when the workshops are busiest. Don't peer into workspaces or stand in forklift and truck paths for photos.
- **Budget:** Walking around is free; most of your spending will be coffee and food.
- **Contact:** Yeongdeungpo-gu Office, +82-2-2670-3755.

## Wrapping up

Mullae-dong rewards slow wandering more than ticking off sights. Begin at Exit 7 of Mullae Station, use the welding mask and hammer as landmarks while you loop the lanes, and add Seoul Art Space Mullae if its programme lines up. Two to three hours is plenty.
