Skiing is one of the main reasons visitors who have never seen snow come to Korea in winter: you can be on a slope an hour or two after leaving Seoul, and equipment and clothing are both rented on arrival. This guide is for **travellers without a car** — where to go, how to get there, and where English lessons genuinely exist.

![Wide snowfields and slopes running down the mountainside at Yongpyong Resort under a clear sky, with resort buildings on the right](/images/ski-korea-2026-09/09.webp)

## This season's information is mostly not out yet

As of 17 September 2026, **nine of the ten resorts surveyed have not announced their 2026-27 opening dates.** The exception is **Oak Valley**, open from Friday 11 December 2026 to 20 February 2027 — with the resort's own notice adding that the dates may change depending on weather and operating conditions.

**Prices are the same story.** Alpensia's fee page reads "26/27 season in preparation" with every number blank; Muju's price page is a single image promising a better season in 26/27.

So **every price below is last season's (25/26)**, with the single exception of Oak Valley's season pass, and each is labelled that way. This season's prices appear shortly before opening, so treat these as a budget guide and check the official site before booking. What does not change is location, access and lessons.

## Without a car, the answer is Elysian Gangchon

This is **the only ski resort in Korea with a train station inside it**: **Baegyangri Station** on the Gyeongchun Line sits on the resort's own grounds. Sangbong Station to Baegyangri takes about 60 minutes, and from the station a **free shuttle loops every 20 minutes** — on the hour, at :20 and at :40.

### There is one trap you need to know about

**The ITX-Cheongchun does not stop at Baegyangri.**

Taking the faster train sounds easier and is the opposite: on the ITX you have to **get off at Gapyeong or Gangchon and transfer to the Gyeongchun Line**, as the resort's official guide states. **Riding the Gyeongchun Line subway straight through** is the simplest option, and as a metro line it needs no reservation.

The consolation: **ITX-Cheongchun passengers get 40 percent off lift tickets and 50 percent off rental** (shoulder season, ticket holder only).

### It also suits beginners

**Eight of its ten slopes are beginner or intermediate.** The beginner runs, Panda (255 m) and Rabbit (342 m), are short and gentle. Last season a four-hour lift ticket was **₩72,000 (about $54)** on weekdays and ₩85,000 at weekends, with a full equipment set at ₩33,000 and clothing at ₩25,000, both for four hours.

## Where English lessons actually exist

**Foreign-language instruction is officially confirmed at only two resorts.**

**Elysian Gangchon** — the Premium Ski School offers an option to assign a **native-speaker instructor in English or Chinese**, at ₩50,000 (about $38) per student.

**Mona Yongpyong** — publishes a separate fee table for foreigners, the **clearest of any resort surveyed**. Last season a beginner group lesson (1:7, no lift, age 8 and over) was **₩70,000 (about $53) per person** for a half day, minimum four people for the class to run; a private lesson (1:1 to 1:3) was **₩300,000 (about $225) per team** for a half day. Half-days run 10:00-12:00, 14:00-16:00 or 19:00-21:00.

**Alpensia** has a foreigner lesson menu too, but its price data stops in November 2023, so current rates could not be confirmed. Phone **033-339-0337**.

### Konjiam's "foreigner lesson" is easy to misread

Konjiam Resort sells a product called a **"foreigner lesson", and it is not a class taught by a foreign instructor.** The official text says so plainly: **"Foreigner lessons can only be booked by phone or in person, and are not a lesson by a foreign instructor, but a product for foreigner applicants only."** Book it on the name alone and you will sit through a lesson in Korean. High1, Muju, Jisan, Welli Hilli and Vivaldi Park make no mention of foreign-language instruction at all.

## The two foreigner-only shuttles

Two of the direct shuttles from Seoul are **run for foreign visitors and bookable in English**.

**Vivaldi Park** — ₩10,000 (about $7.50) one way, ₩20,000 return; children ₩8,000 and ₩16,000. Last season's route left Hongik Univ. Station 06:30, Dongdaemun History and Culture Park 07:00, Konkuk Univ. and Sports Complex 07:20, returning from the resort at 13:00 and 17:00. This season's routes still say "Coming Soon".

**Welli Hilli Park** — ₩20,000 one way, ₩40,000 return; children ₩18,000 and ₩36,000. Unusually, **the shuttle season alone is already published for this year, 27 November 2026 to 1 March 2027.** Departures are Hongik Univ. Station 05:40 and Myeongdong 05:45, returning at 13:00 and 17:00, with enquiries handled in English, WhatsApp and Line.

Both require **advance booking**, and neither sells seats on the day.

## If you want a real mountain

![Floodlit night slopes at Alpensia, several runs and a lift under orange light](/images/ski-korea-2026-09/05.webp)

Resorts near Seoul are easy to reach but short. For a proper mountain, go to Pyeongchang or Jeongseon.

**Mona Yongpyong (Pyeongchang)** — 28 slopes, 14 lifts, 702 m of vertical. Rainbow Paradise, at 5,600 m, is the longest run in the country. It opened in 1975 as Korea's first ski resort, and **the alpine events of the 2018 Pyeongchang Olympics were held here.**

**High1 Resort (Jeongseon)** — 19 slopes and **three eight-person cable cars**, the only resort in Korea running three. Mountain Top down to the Valley Condo is 4.2 km with 680 m of vertical. **Helmets are lent free to everyone.**

**Muju Deogyusan Resort (Jeonbuk)** — the gondola climbs to Seolcheonbong at 1,520 m in 15 minutes, and the linked Silk Road run is 6.1 km with 810 m of vertical, the longest in the country.

**Alpensia (Pyeongchang)** — small, with six slopes, but it has a scheduled shuttle from Seoul.

### Getting there without a car

- **Yongpyong and Alpensia** — KTX Gangneung Line to **Jinbu (Odaesan) Station**, then a **free resort shuttle** twice a day: Yongpyong at 11:00 and 14:30, Alpensia at 11:00 and 14:10. Resort guests only; about 20 minutes by taxi.
- **Alpensia also runs a Seoul shuttle** — Myeongdong 09:30, Sports Complex 10:00, returning 13:10, **₩34,000 return (about $26)**, advance booking for everyone. An Incheon Airport bus costs ₩34,500 one way.
- **High1 is not on the KTX Gangneung Line.** It is **Cheongnyangni to Gohan on the Taebaek Line, about 3 hours 30 minutes**, or about 2 hours 40 minutes by intercity bus from Seoul to the Gohan-Sabuk terminal.
- **Muju** — Nambu Terminal to Muju terminal is about 2 hours 30 minutes, then a **free shuttle** into the resort four times a day. It is a 15-seat van, so seats can run out.

## Two things that define Korean ski resorts

![The snow park at Phoenix Park, with a halfpipe, a jump and a snowboarder coming down](/images/ski-korea-2026-09/14.webp)

**First, people ski at night.** Most resorts open in the evening, and Konjiam, Jisan and Vivaldi Park run **until two or three in the morning** — so sightseeing in Seoul by day and skiing at night is a workable plan.

**Second, tickets are sold by the hour**, in blocks of two, four or six rather than only full days. Beginners tire quickly, so a short block is the better call.

## Practical notes

- **Wear a helmet.** Elysian Gangchon made them mandatory from 1 January 2026 and accepts only certified ones; Vivaldi Park restricts lift boarding without one; High1 lends them free.
- **Rent the clothing.** Most resorts rent jacket and trousers, last season in the low ₩20,000s for four hours. Gloves and goggles are usually extra.
- **Book online.** Konjiam runs **no on-site ticket window at all**: book online, collect from a kiosk. Jisan is 25 percent cheaper online.
- **The X6+ season pass** covers Yongpyong, High1, Jisan, Welli Hilli, Elysian and Alpensia, on sale since 9 September 2026 — worth it only if you ski all season.
- **Most resort shuttles require advance booking**, and turning up to a full bus does happen.
- **If you do not ski**, the Pyeongchang Olympic and Paralympic Memorial Museum is **free**, open 10:00-18:00, closed Mondays, in Hoenggye near Alpensia.

## Where to go, in one line each

- **A day trip without a car** — Elysian Gangchon, station inside the ski area.
- **English lessons** — Yongpyong or Elysian.
- **An easy shuttle from Seoul** — Vivaldi Park or Welli Hilli Park.
- **A real mountain** — Yongpyong, High1, Muju.
- **Night skiing** — Konjiam, Jisan, Vivaldi Park.

## Wrapping up

The strength of skiing in Korea is not the size of the mountains but the **access**: out of Seoul in the morning, back by evening, with equipment, clothing and lessons all sorted on site. For a first-timer that beats Europe or Japan.

The caveat is that in mid-September **most opening dates and prices are still unannounced**. They come out through November, so check again then — and book the shuttle early.
