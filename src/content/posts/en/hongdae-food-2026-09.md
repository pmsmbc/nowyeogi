Hongdae eats late, cheap and well. It is a student neighborhood that grew up, so the food splits into two halves: the loud, generous, alcohol-adjacent dishes on the main streets, and the careful café-and-brunch culture of Yeonnam-dong ten minutes north. Do both.

Prices below are typical ranges as of September 2026, not fixed quotes.

## Chimaek: fried chicken and beer

![Korean fried chicken in soy, plain and spicy versions, served with beer](/images/hongdae-food-2026-09/01.webp)

**Chimaek** (chicken + maekju, beer) is the local institution and Hongdae is the right place for it. A whole chicken runs roughly **₩20,000–28,000** and feeds two, with beer at ₩5,000–7,000.

The three styles you will see on every menu:

- **Fried (후라이드)** — plain, crisp, the benchmark.
- **Yangnyeom (양념)** — coated in a sweet-spicy red sauce.
- **Ganjang (간장)** — soy-garlic, sticky and savoury.

Ask for **반반** (banban, half and half) to get two styles on one plate. Chains and independents sit side by side; the independents around Sangsu tend to be better and quieter than the chains on the main drag.

## Korean barbecue

![Samgyeopsal, sliced pork belly grilling at the table](/images/hongdae-food-2026-09/02.webp)

**Samgyeopsal**, thick-cut pork belly grilled at your table, is the default. Expect **₩15,000–20,000 per portion**, with a two-portion minimum for a table. Beef (소고기) costs considerably more.

How it works if it is your first time: the staff bring raw meat and you cook it; scissors are for cutting it once it browns; you eat it wrapped in lettuce with garlic, ssamjang paste and whatever side dishes arrive. Side dishes and lettuce are refilled free. Finish with **kimchi fried rice** cooked in the same pan, usually ₩3,000–5,000 extra.

Barbecue restaurants cluster on the streets south of the station toward Sangsu.

## Late-night food

Hongdae is one of the few parts of Seoul where dinner at 01:00 is normal.

- **Pojangmacha** — orange-tented street bars serving fish cake soup, spicy chicken feet and soju.
- **Tteokbokki bars** — rice cakes in chilli sauce with fried extras, often served with a bowl of stock.
- **Gukbap and haejangguk** — soup with rice, the traditional cure for a heavy night. Some places never close.
- **24-hour chains** — reliable if unexciting, and useful when you land late.

## Yeonnam-dong: the café half

![The craft workshop street in Yeonnam-dong](/images/hongdae-food-2026-09/03.webp)

Ten minutes north-east, along the **Gyeongui Line Forest Park**, Yeonnam-dong is where Hongdae grew up. The streets off the park hold small roasters, brunch places, bakeries and craft workshops, in converted houses rather than shopfronts.

What to expect: a flat white or hand-drip for **₩5,000–7,000**, a brunch plate for ₩12,000–18,000, and queues at weekends. Coffee here is taken seriously — several roasters in the area supply cafés across the city.

It is also the best area in Hongdae for a quiet meal if the main street feels like too much.

## Mangwon Market

Two subway stops west, or a 20-minute walk, **Mangwon Market** is a traditional market that locals actually shop in. Prices are roughly half what you would pay in Myeongdong, and the food alley does fried snacks, dumplings, tteokbokki and grilled skewers.

Most stalls run daytime hours and it is busiest in the early evening. Bring some cash even though cards mostly work.

## Vegetarian, vegan and halal

- Hongdae has **more vegetarian and vegan restaurants than most of Seoul**, thanks to the student population — look around Sangsu and Yeonnam.
- Standard Korean side dishes and soups usually contain anchovy or beef stock even when they look plant-based. Ask, do not assume.
- For **halal**, Itaewon has far more choice; from Hongdae it is about 30 minutes on [[line:2]] then [[line:6]].

## Practical notes

- **Two-person minimums** are common at barbecue and chicken places. Solo travellers do better at noodle shops, gimbap places and cafés.
- **Table service is by button or by calling out** "저기요" (jeogiyo). Waiting quietly will not get you served.
- **Cards work almost everywhere**, including street tents.
- **Weekend queues** at popular Yeonnam cafés run 20–40 minutes. Go on a weekday or before 11:00.
- **Alcohol is sold in convenience stores at all hours**, and drinking in the park is legal and normal.

## Wrapping up

Eat chimaek once because it is what this neighborhood does best, book a barbecue table with at least one other person, and spend a morning in Yeonnam-dong to see the quieter half of Hongdae. If you want a market meal rather than a restaurant one, walk west to Mangwon.
