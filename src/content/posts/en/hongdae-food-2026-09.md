Look at the official lists before picking where to eat in Hongdae and one thing jumps out: **of the 51 MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026 Bib Gourmand restaurants, eight are in Seogyo-dong and Hapjeong-dong**, the streets between Hongik University Station and Hapjeong Station. Three are ramen or udon shops, and the rest serve pork soup, beef bone soup, dumpling soup, noodles and Chinese food. The neighborhood is known for clubs and bars, but **almost everything on the official lists is a lunch bowl**.

The short version: **lunch at a Bib Gourmand soup or noodle shop near Hapjeong, dinner at a barbecue place in Seogyo-dong or Sangsu-dong, and late food inside the tourism zone where opening hours are not restricted.** Every restaurant here appears on a MICHELIN, Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) or Ministry of the Interior list, and anything about taste comes from those descriptions.

A **Bib Gourmand** marks a place where you can eat for **₩45,000 (about $34) or less per person**. Seoul has 51 in 2026. The five Seoul restaurants added that year (3rd Samgyetang, Gosari Express, Sobakeeri Suzu, Andeok and Oilje) are all outside this area, so **the eight here are carried over from earlier editions**, not newcomers.

Two more Bib Gourmand ramen shops, Sarukame and Nishimuramen, are a few minutes north in Yeonnam-dong. They are covered in [Where to Eat in Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong](/en/posts/yeonnam-food-2026-09/).

## Lunch: the eight Bib Gourmand spots

![A black earthenware bowl of milky pork and rice soup topped with a heap of chives, surrounded by kkakdugi, cabbage kimchi, green chili, onion and salted shrimp](/images/hongdae-food-2026-09/02.webp)

The photo shows dwaeji-gukbap (pork and rice soup) in general, not a dish from a specific restaurant.

Five of the eight (Damtaek, Seokyonanmyunbang, Jin Jin, Kyodaiya, and Okdongsik on weekdays) have **lunch-only hours ending at 15:00** on their MICHELIN pages. If you only think of Hongdae as a night-out district, you may arrive at dinner time to a locked door. All hours below are as of October 1, 2026.

### Soup: Okdongsik and Hapjeongok

**Okdongsik** (44-10 Yanghwa-ro 7-gil) made its name, according to MICHELIN, with **pork soup that looks like a clear beef gomtang**, made only from the fore and hind leg meat of Jirisan black pigs. Rice is warmed by pouring hot broth over it and draining it several times (a method called **toryeom**), and the bowl comes with paper-thin pork and kimchi dumplings. MICHELIN's English page tags it **"worth queueing for"** and notes counter seating. KTO says it is **5 minutes on foot from Hapjeong Station Exit 2**, and takeout is available.

DiningCode lists **₩14,000 for a regular bowl, ₩19,000 for a large one and ₩7,000 for kimchi dumplings**. Hours differ by source: MICHELIN gives weekdays 11:00-15:00 and weekends 11:00-20:30, while KTO gives weekdays 11:00-22:00 with a 15:00-17:00 break, weekends 11:00-21:00, and no closing day.

**Hapjeongok** (2F, 21 Yanghwa-ro 1-gil) serves gomtang, a long-simmered beef bone soup. MICHELIN says it began with the soup and boiled beef the owner's father-in-law used to make at home, and that generosity is the house style: rich broth, rice warmed in it, tender beef and boiled offal. It also mentions **sokdaetguk**, a doenjang soup with sweet inner cabbage leaves. KTO adds that the broth uses brisket and leg bones from Korean cows.

Quantity is the catch. **About 100 portions of soup a day, and the shop closes when they are gone**, MICHELIN says. KTO lists 11:00-22:00 (last order 20:30), **closed Sunday**, parking available. DiningCode lists gomtang at ₩12,000, a large bowl at ₩14,000 and a medium plate of boiled beef at ₩40,000.

### Japanese noodles: Oreno Ramen, Damtaek, Kyodaiya

**Three of the eight are ramen or udon shops.** KTO's list adds more Japanese places nearby, including Udon Kaden, Ramen Truck, Crazy Katsu (tonkatsu) and Sushi Gen, so even the official records show that **Hapjeong is strong on Japanese noodles**.

- **Oreno Ramen main branch**: MICHELIN describes **tori paitan**, a milky broth from whole chickens, blended once more just before serving to make it frothy. **Noodles, rice and broth are refilled for free**, and the line is usually long but moves fast. KTO lists **11:00-22:00 daily, 10 minutes from Hapjeong Station Exit 7**. DiningCode lists the regular and spicy paitan at ₩13,000 each. MICHELIN gives the address as 16 Dongmak-ro 8-gil, KTO as 14 Dongmak-ro 6-gil.
- **Damtaek** (51 Donggyo-ro 12an-gil): MICHELIN calls it **a modest ramen shop in a quiet lane near Hapjeong Station**, decorated on purpose with vintage objects by Chef Jo Won-hyeon, who focuses on **shio (salt) ramen** and variations on it. MICHELIN lists **Mon-Sat 11:30-15:00, closed Sunday**.
- **Kyodaiya** (39 Seongji-gil): the name means "brothers," and **two brothers run this Sanuki udon shop**. MICHELIN says the thick noodles are cut to order, served as kake udon in anchovy broth, tempura udon or chilled bukkake udon. Both sources agree it is **closed Monday**, but MICHELIN lists 11:00-15:00 and KTO 11:00-20:30 with a 15:00-17:00 break. KTO puts it 5 minutes from Hapjeong Station Exit 7.

Not a Bib Gourmand but on KTO's list, **Udon Kaden** (2-1 Yanghwa-ro 7an-gil) is Chef Jeong Ho-young's udon shop, 5 minutes from Hapjeong Station Exit 2. KTO lists weekdays 11:30-21:00 (break 14:40-17:30), Saturday 11:30-20:30, **closed Sunday**.

### Dumplings, noodles and Chinese: Mipildam, Seokyonanmyunbang, Jin Jin

- **Mipildam** (22 Seongji 3-gil): MICHELIN describes a small **North Korean-style handmade dumpling soup** shop in a Hapjeong-dong alley, run by a young couple using **the recipe of a grandmother from Hwanghae Province**. Seasonal dishes rotate alongside the soup. No official hours were found.
- **Seokyonanmyunbang** (16 Donggyo-ro 12-gil): **Chef Kim Nak-young** came from Italian cooking. MICHELIN picks out **Gueom chicken nanmyeon**, made with Jeju chicken, and **Seokyo nanmyeon** in a mixed chicken and Hanwoo beef broth, plus seasonal noodle dishes. MICHELIN lists 11:00-15:00, **closed Tuesday**.
- **Jin Jin** (60 World Cup buk-ro 1-gil): **Chef Wang Yuk-sung** has cooked Chinese food in Korea for more than four decades. According to MICHELIN, he **keeps the menu to about ten dishes** to hold quality steady, including shrimp toast, stir-fried crab with egg white, mapo tofu and beef with gai lan. MICHELIN lists only Mon-Sat 11:30-15:00 and Sun 12:00-15:00.

## Dinner: barbecue in Seogyo-dong and Sangsu-dong

![Two thick strips of pork belly browning on a round griddle, with lettuce, scallion salad, white kimchi, pickled radish and small sauce cups around it](/images/hongdae-food-2026-09/03.webp)

KTO took this photo of a pork belly table at 401 Jeongyuk Sikdang.

After dark the official lists swing toward grills. Two KTO-listed barbecue places and three government-listed budget ones together cover a wide price range. At Korean barbecue you cook the meat at your table, cut it with scissors and wrap it in lettuce with garlic and sauce.

**Joseon Hwaro Gui** (5 World Cup buk-ro) has held the same spot for **more than 20 years**, according to KTO. It uses only 1++ grade Hanwoo beef and fresh Boseong green-tea pork, and the signature is **fresh Hanwoo rib finger meat**. There are side dishes such as beef tartare, cold noodles, doenjang stew and galbitang, and everything can be taken out. KTO also mentions that singers from a nearby entertainment agency come often, and the walls are covered with autographs. Hours are **11:30-24:00** (break 15:00-17:00, last order 23:30), **closed Sunday**, with parking.

**401 Jeongyuk Sikdang** (395-17 Seogyo-dong) is run by TV entertainers Haha and Kim Jong-kook. KTO says the signature is a **mixed platter of pork belly, neck, jowl and shoulder**, served with white kimchi, pickled radish, scallion salad and anchovy sauce, and **staff grill the meat for you**, which helps if you have never done it. Hours are **16:00-02:00, every day**. No parking.

## Late night: why the hours are so long

There is a legal reason so many places in Hongdae stay open into the small hours.

On **December 2, 2021, the Seoul government designated 1.13 km² of Seogyo-dong, Donggyo-dong, Sangsu-dong and Hapjeong-dong as the Hongdae Culture and Arts Tourism Special Zone** (Seoul Shinmun). It was **Seoul's seventh tourism special zone**, after Itaewon, Myeong-dong/Namdaemun/Bukchang-dong, Dongdaemun, Jongno/Cheonggye, Jamsil and Gangnam.

Article 74 of the Tourism Promotion Act sets special rules for these zones. Its first paragraph says the business restriction rules of **Article 43 of the Food Sanitation Act do not apply inside a tourism zone**. Article 43 is what lets a district mayor **limit the opening hours and activities of restaurants and bars** for public order, so inside the zone that limit cannot be imposed. The second paragraph lets designated tourism businesses use the open space in front of buildings for up to 180 days a year to offer performances and food to foreign visitors.

## Why Hongdae restaurants change so often

Old restaurants are hard to find here for a reason. Kyunghyang Shinmun reported in February 2012 that **Richmond Bakery's Hongdae branch, open for 30 years, closed on January 31, 2012 when its lease ended**, and a coffee chain owned by a large conglomerate moved in. The old deposit was ₩500 million; the new tenant agreed to ₩1 billion for about half the space. The same article counted more than 20 big coffee-chain branches within 100 m of Hongik University Station.

In 2025 the Seoul government named five Mapo-gu restaurants to its **Oraegage** list of places running for more than 30 years (Guldari Sikdang, Mapo Ok, Yeokjeon Hoegwan, Wonjo Jobakjip and Ildeung Sikdang). The three whose addresses could be confirmed (Mapo Ok at 312 Tojeong-ro per MICHELIN, Wonjo Jobakjip at 313 Tojeong-ro in Yonggang-dong and Yeokjeon Hoegwan in Yeomni-dong per KTO) are **all outside the area this post covers**. How the neighborhood changed is covered in the [Hongdae travel guide](/en/posts/hongdae-guide-2026-09/).

## Good to know

**Many of the Bib Gourmand spots are mainly lunch places.** On their MICHELIN pages, Damtaek, Seokyonanmyunbang and Jin Jin close around 15:00. For soup or noodles in the evening, Hapjeongok (last order 20:30) and Oreno Ramen (last order 21:30) stay open latest according to official sources.

**Closing days vary.** Closed Sunday: Hapjeongok, Damtaek, Joseon Hwaro Gui, Udon Kaden, Cheoni Ogyeopsal, Oeyanggan, Bonjour, Wellbeing Ttukbaegi. Kyodaiya closes Monday and Seokyonanmyunbang Tuesday. Open every day: Oreno Ramen, 401 Jeongyuk Sikdang, 1984, Jebi Dabang.

**Some places close when they sell out.** Hapjeongok (about 100 portions a day) and Oeyanggan say so in official sources.

**Card payment cannot be refused.** Article 19 of the Specialized Credit Finance Business Act says a **card merchant may not refuse a credit card or treat cardholders less favorably**. MICHELIN also marks Jin Jin, Hapjeongok and Mipildam as accepting credit cards. The law does not guarantee that every terminal will approve a card issued abroad, though, so a little cash or a Korean payment method is a useful backup. Transit cards are covered in [Seoul Subway and T-money](/en/posts/seoul-subway-tmoney-2026-09/).

**There is no tipping.** The menu price is what you pay.
