Personal color analysis has become one of the most-booked beauty experiences for visitors to Seoul. According to Allure Korea, bookings for it on one travel platform in the first half of 2024 were 130 times higher than a year earlier, and a popular studio in Hongdae was seeing 30 to 40 foreign customers a day, close to 1,000 a month. The short version: choose a studio that works in your language, book two or three days ahead, and go early in your trip so you can shop with the results.

![An eyeshadow palette and makeup brushes on a pink, green and beige background](/images/seoul-personal-color-2026-11/01.webp)

## What personal color analysis is

A consultant holds coloured fabric swatches under your face one at a time, a technique called draping, to see which colour families suit your skin, eyes and hair. Results usually come as one of four seasonal types: spring warm, summer cool, autumn warm or winter cool. Some studios break these down further into 12 or more sub-types.

![An eyeshadow palette with cool shades on the top row and warm shades below](/images/seoul-personal-color-2026-11/02.webp)

Korean outlet Wikitree has noted a shift among foreign tourists: rather than buying famous products straight away, more of them want their skin tone and face shape analysed first, then shop for lipstick, blush and foundation shades to match. Many studios send you home with a personal palette and a list of recommended products as a card or image.

## Prices and session length

Two studios that publish their prices on their official sites give a good sense of the market (as of writing):

- **Color Lume (near City Hall):** Makeup Color Analysis, 40 minutes, ₩80,000 (about $60); Fashion Color Analysis, 60 minutes, ₩100,000; Complete Color Analysis combining both, 80 minutes, ₩150,000. Groups get ₩10,000 off per person.
- **Hybrid Rainbow Studio (Songridan-gil, Jamsil):** a 45-minute basic analysis from ₩60,000, with tax and deposit included in the listed price. Longer packages that add body-shape analysis run two to three hours.

In general, expect ₩60,000–150,000 per person for a standard session, and more for packages that bundle a makeup demonstration or hair and body-shape analysis. Some studios charge extra for an interpreter, so ask whether the quoted total includes interpretation and tax.

## Studios that work in English or Japanese

For **English**, the two studios above are the clearest options. Color Lume says every session is run in English by its founder, so no translator is needed. Hybrid Rainbow Studio is run by a US-born consultant and charges no English surcharge. Hybrid Rainbow is at 24 Baekjegobun-ro 45-gil in Songpa-gu, close to Jamsil Station on [[line:2]] and [[line:8]] and Songpanaru Station on [[line:9]]. Color Lume is at 111 Seosomun-ro (Room 501) in Jung-gu.

For **Japanese**, Myeongdong is the easiest base. Japanese travel media list several Japanese-friendly studios there, among them The Bom (7 Toegye-ro 18-gil), which is registered with the Korea Tourism Organization with hours of 09:30–19:00. Some studios only offer Japanese on certain days, so confirm when booking.

![Myeongdong at night with street-food carts under the trees](/images/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/15.webp)

Studios cluster in Myeongdong, Hongdae and Gangnam's Garosu-gil. If you want to go straight from the session to a cosmetics shop, Myeongdong and Hongdae make the shortest route.

## Try it free at Beauty Play

**Beauty Play** is a K-beauty showcase supported by Korea's Ministry of Health and Welfare and run by the Korea Cosmetic Industry Institute, and anyone can drop in for free. The Korea Tourism Organization lists the Myeongdong branch (73 Myeongdong-gil) as open 10:00–19:00, with beauty experiences until 18:00, and closed on Sundays and 1 January. You can choose either a partial makeup session or a skin check with a specialist, and a personal color experience is offered too. There is also a Hongdae branch, where personal color classes are run by advance registration and many sessions fill up quickly. It is not as detailed as a private one-to-one analysis, but it is a good taster.

## How to prepare

These are the tips studios and travel guides most often repeat:

- **Light makeup, no coloured contacts.** Consultants need to see your natural skin and eye colour, so many recommend coming bare-faced or lightly made up. Studios offering makeup analysis may remove it on site.
- **Wear white or neutral colours.** A strongly coloured top can reflect onto your face and skew the result.
- **Ask about photos.** Snapping each drape helps when you compare shades later while shopping. Whether photos are allowed varies by studio.
- **Book early in your trip.** Day one or two leaves the rest of your stay for shopping with the results.

## Practical notes

- **Booking:** Many studios take bookings by KakaoTalk (Korea's main messaging app) or Instagram message, and you can also book through studio websites or travel platforms such as Klook, KKday and Trazy. Popular studios fill weekends fast, so book two or three days ahead.
- **Changes:** Color Lume asks for at least two days' notice to reschedule. Deposits are common, so check the cancellation policy.
- **Questions to ask:** whether the consultant speaks your language directly or uses an interpreter, whether the total includes tax and interpretation, and what format your results come in.
- **Getting around:** Myeongdong sits between Myeongdong Station on [[line:4]] and Euljiro 1-ga Station on [[line:2]]; the subway is the easiest way to combine a session with shopping.

## Wrapping up

For a first try, book a one-hour English session early in your trip, then take your results to the shops in Myeongdong or Hongdae. If the price puts you off, start with the free experience at Beauty Play Myeongdong.
