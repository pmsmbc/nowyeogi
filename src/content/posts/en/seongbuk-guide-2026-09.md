Of all the Seoul neighbourhoods this blog has covered, Seongbuk-dong is **the only one where a decision to keep it was reversed**.

In 2019 the city of Seoul decided to **keep 279 of the 371 houses** in Bukjeong Village. Then **a notice dated 26 February 2026 scrapped that decision**. The 279 buildings to be retained became **zero**.

![A steep flight of steps hung with a banner pointing the way to Simujang, houses packed tightly onto the hillside beyond](/images/seongbuk-things-to-do-2026-09/02.webp)

## Two notices

The most accurate way to explain Seongbuk-dong is to **set two notices issued for the same neighbourhood side by side**.

> **Seoul Metropolitan Government Notice 2022-366** (25 August 2022) - the permitted height of boundary walls rises from 1.5m to **2.0m**. The stated reason is to reflect the situation in Seongbuk-dong, where **a large number of ambassadorial residences are located**.

> **Seoul Metropolitan Government Notice 2026-96** (26 February 2026) - **all 371 buildings** outside the wall are to be demolished and 474 units of no more than four storeys built in their place.

The same authority made both decisions, about **two slopes of the same neighbourhood**. On one side it raises the walls; on the other it pulls the houses down.

Seongbuk-gu has **40 ambassadorial residences**, and one of its roads is actually called **Daesagwan-ro, Embassy Road**.

## What I had wrong

Two of my assumptions broke while I was preparing this post. I will set them out as they are.

**One: Bukjeong Village is not a shanty town.** A Seoul city document puts it like this.

> **Around 500 tiled-roof houses built in the 1960s and 1970s**

371 buildings, 509 households, and tiled-roof houses. The village that did begin as a shanty town is **Jangsu Village**, and that is in Samseon-dong, not Seongbuk-dong.

**Two: it was the residents who wanted redevelopment.** This is from the Seoul city press release itself.

> an area where housing redevelopment has been pursued **since 2009 as the long-standing wish of the great majority of residents**

So this was not a case of residents wanting preservation while the city pushed development through. **It was the residents who wanted redevelopment, and the city of Seoul that inserted the preservation mechanism.**

## Timeline

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 2009 | Residents begin pushing for redevelopment |
| 18 August 2011 | Designated an improvement zone |
| June 2015 | Switched to the rehabilitation model |
| **14 March 2019** | **Decision to retain 279 of the 371 buildings** |
| 5 September 2024 | Project implementation approved |
| **26 February 2026** | **Individual improvement district abolished, changed to demolition of all 371 buildings** |

The 2019 approach was an unusual one. It was a combined improvement project that **transferred 80% of floor area ratio to the Sinwolgok 1 zone 3km away** and used the profit from that development to improve Seongbuk 2 at low rise. Here is the city's own account.

> In order to save Bukjeong Village, one of the city wall villages, Seoul is **trying something new, away from the wholesale-demolition style of redevelopment** that erases the history and culture of an area.

That attempt was folded seven years later. **The only building that will remain is Simujang**, and in its place go 474 units, a history park of 5,571.1 square metres and a buffer green belt of 5,487.7 square metres. **No public rental housing will be built.**

### It is Seoul Future Heritage, and it is being demolished

**Bukjeong Village is Seoul Future Heritage 2015-016**, and it was still on the current listing as of 1 January 2026. A month later its demolition was formally notified.

It looks like a contradiction, but it is the nature of the scheme. The city of Seoul says so directly.

> The Seoul Future Heritage conservation programme **is not a matter of putting regulatory mechanisms in place for the uniform preservation** of cultural heritage.

The Ikseon-dong and Seochon posts made the same point, but **this is where the limits of it show most clearly.**

### The UNESCO submission and the demolition notice

The timing stands out.

- **16 January 2026** - Seoul submits the World Heritage nomination for the Capital City Walls of Hanyang
- **26 February 2026** - the demolition of Bukjeong Village is notified

And the reason given in that notice for creating the buffer green belt is **the protection of the Hanyang City Wall heritage**. The two decisions are not a contradiction; they are **a single policy**.

## The wealthy quarter and the slope came out of the same development

This was the most unexpected part of the research.

The Seoul Future Heritage citations put it like this.

- Yi Tae-jun's story Moonlit Night - Seongbuk-dong, outside Dongsomun Gate, **was brought into Gyeongseong late, to relieve the housing shortage**
- Kim Kwang-sup's poem The Doves of Seongbuk-dong - set against **the residential land development of the Seongbuk-dong area in the 1960s**

And the 500-odd tiled-roof houses of Bukjeong Village were also built in **the 1960s and 1970s**.

**The upmarket housing of Seongbuk-dong and Bukjeong Village are two outcomes of the same development, at the same time.** They did not arise separately.

## Set beside the other four neighbourhoods

| Neighbourhood | The prescription |
| --- | --- |
| Euljiro | Demolition decided |
| Ikseon-dong | Lifted in 2018, with conservation measures |
| Seochon | Height limits from 1977, conservation from 2016 |
| Seongsu-dong | A 2015 ordinance restraining rents |
| **Seongbuk-dong** | **Preservation in 2019, reversed to demolition in 2026** |

**Seongbuk-dong is the only case where the decision was overturned.**

## Getting there

![A timber hall at Gilsangsa Temple set above a stream, a stone bridge and tiled roofs surrounded by trees](/images/seongbuk-guide-2026-09/11.webp)

[[line:4]] **Hansung University Station**. It helps to know the exits and the buses in advance.

| Where you are going | How to get there |
| --- | --- |
| **Restaurants** | **Exit 5** |
| **Gilsangsa Temple** | **Exit 6**, then village bus **Seongbuk 02** |
| **Bukjeong Village and Simujang** | **Exit 6**, then village bus **Seongbuk 03** |

Both leave from the stop named Hansung University Station Exit 6, Samseongyo. A village bus is 1,200 won.

**Seongbuk-dong is steep.** It may look close on the map, but walking up is hard work. The village bus is the answer.

**Food at the bottom of the hill, sights at the top** - remember that and the route plans itself.

## About Bukjeong Village

**It is not a tourist attraction.** It is a residential area where 509 households live, and it is now facing demolition.

Seongbuk-dong has **no official request for quiet** of the kind Ikseon-dong posts as its four promises. Nearby Jangsu Village has one; Bukjeong Village does not. The absence of a rule does not mean it is fine to peer in.

**If you are going to Simujang you will pass through the village.** Better to know that in advance and go through quietly.

## Who this suits

**It suits you if:**

- You are interested in what a city keeps and what it erases
- You want to walk the Hanyang City Wall
- You want to see a temple and old houses quietly
- You want to see **something you will not be able to see later**

**It does not suit you if:**

- Hills are hard for you - in which case take the village bus
- You are expecting shopping or a cafe strip, in which case Ikseon-dong or Seongsu-dong is the better fit
- **Monday closing** applies to a good many of the sights

## Wrapping up

- **279 buildings were to be kept in 2019, and the notice of 26 February 2026 changed that to demolishing all of them.**
- **The only thing left standing will be Simujang.**
- **Bukjeong Village is Seoul Future Heritage and is being demolished.** Future Heritage is not a regulatory mechanism.
- **The residents had wanted redevelopment since 2009.** It was the city that added the preservation.
- **The 40 ambassadorial residences and Bukjeong Village are in the same neighbourhood**, two outcomes of the same period of development.
- **Exit 5 at Hansung University Station for food, exit 6 for the buses.** Seongbuk 02 for Gilsangsa Temple, Seongbuk 03 for Bukjeong Village.

In one line: **Seongbuk-dong is the neighbourhood where Seoul decided what to keep, and then decided again.**
