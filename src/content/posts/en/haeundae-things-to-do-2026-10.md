Haeundae (해운대) is Busan's most famous beach, and the usual complaint is that once you have seen the sand there is nothing else to do. There is, but it only works if you walk it in order. Start at Dongbaekseom on the western tip, cross the beach, board the Blue Line Park rides at Mipo on the eastern tip, and carry on to Cheongsapo and the Dalmaji hill. That single line fills anywhere from half a day to a full one. The [Busan overview of things to do](/en/posts/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/) mentions the Sky Capsule and X the SKY briefly; this guide goes into the detail.

Hours and prices are as of October 2026, taken from each operator's official listing and Korea Tourism Organization data.

## Dongbaekseom and Nurimaru: free, but closed on the first Monday

The pine-covered headland behind the Westin Josun hotel at the west end of the beach is **Dongbaekseom** (동백섬). The name means Camellia Island; sand long ago joined it to the mainland, and a wooden **coastal boardwalk** now runs around its edge. It is always open and free.

![Wooden deck stairs on the Dongbaekseom coastal trail, with the sea visible between pine trees](/images/haeundae-things-to-do-2026-10/05.webp)

According to the Korea Tourism Organization, the trail passes a rock inscription of the name "Haeundae" attributed to the Silla-era scholar Choe Chi-won, a **mermaid statue** from a local legend, and **Nurimaru APEC House**, the venue of the 2005 APEC leaders' summit. The south side of the island is where Gwangan Bridge and the Oryukdo islets line up in one view.

![Nurimaru APEC House, a round three-storey building standing among the pines of Dongbaekseom](/images/haeundae-things-to-do-2026-10/15.webp)

**Nurimaru** is run by the city and is **free**. The official listing gives **09:00 to 18:00**, last entry an hour before closing, and it shuts on the **first Monday of every month**. Twenty to thirty minutes covers the round summit table and the commemorative exhibits. The building itself, a traditional Korean pavilion reworked in concrete and glass, is worth the walk even from outside.

From [[line:busan2]] Dongbaek Station exit 1 it is about 15 minutes along Dongbaek-ro to the entrance; from the beach you simply keep walking west. Allow **an hour** for the loop plus Nurimaru.

## Haeundae Beach: season over, sand still open

In 2026 Haeundae was the first Busan beach to open, on **26 June**, and the city kept it running until **15 September** after the others closed on 31 August. Swimming hours were **09:00 to 18:00**, and summer 2026 reports put parasol and tube rental at **₩10,000 (about $7.50)** each and the shower and changing rooms at **₩1,000** per use. The city counted more than **10 million** visitors this summer, the first Korean beach to reach that figure.

In October all of that is shut. What remains is the sand itself, which is open around the clock and works for an **early walk and sunrise** or, after dark, the lights of Marine City. Walking the full beach from the Dongbaekseom end to the Mipo end takes **20 to 25 minutes**.

From [[line:busan2]] Haeundae Station take exit 3 or 5 and follow Gunam-ro downhill for about 10 minutes. Gunam-ro is Haeundae's main commercial street, and the market is one block to the side.

## Haeundae Market: five minutes from the station

**Haeundae Market** (해운대시장), in a lane off Gunam-ro, has been trading since the 1910s and took its current form after a 2008 refit. Stalls sell tteokbokki, fried snacks, twisted doughnuts and pork rice soup, and there is a hagfish alley. Hours are nominally **09:00 to 22:00** but each stall sets its own. It is five minutes from Haeundae Station exit 3, so it fits on the way to the beach. What to order is in the [Haeundae food guide](/en/posts/haeundae-food-2026-10/).

## BUSAN X the SKY: right beside Mipo

The observation deck on **floors 98 to 100** of the LCT Landmark Tower at the beach's east end, **411.6 m** up and the second-tallest in Korea. Open **10:00 to 21:00**, ticketing closes 20:30, every day.

Adult admission is quoted as **₩27,000** on some listings and **₩29,000 (about $22)** on others, so budget the higher figure; online resellers often have it cheaper. Sunset is the busiest slot, and going up **an hour before sunset** gets you daylight and night views on one ticket. It is close to [[line:busan2]] Jung-dong Station and sits beside the Mipo Station of the Blue Line Park, so it pairs naturally with the rides. Parking is free for two hours.

## Blue Line Park from Mipo: Sky Capsule sales open Tuesday mornings, four weeks out

This is where Haeundae costs money and where booking trips people up. The park reuses **4.8 km** of the old Donghae Nambu railway between Mipo, Cheongsapo and Songjeong, with two rides on the same corridor.

![Two Sky Capsules, one purple and one yellow, on the elevated rail with the open sea behind](/images/haeundae-things-to-do-2026-10/08.webp)

The **Sky Capsule** is a four-seat pod on a rail 7 to 10 m above the ground between Mipo and Cheongsapo, 2 km in about **30 minutes**. It is priced **per capsule, not per person**: officially **₩50,000 (about $37.50) for two**, ₩55,000 for three and ₩60,000 for four, and one child under seven may ride free as a fifth passenger in a four-seat capsule.

The **Beach Train** runs the whole line from Mipo to Songjeong in about 25 minutes. A single ride costs **₩10,000 from Mipo or Songjeong** and ₩8,000 from Cheongsapo; two rides are ₩14,000 and an all-station hop-on pass is **₩16,000**. The official site lists a package of the train plus a one-way capsule at **₩73,000 to ₩82,000 for two**.

![A red Beach Train pulling into Mipo at sunset, with the Sky Capsule rail and the LCT tower above](/images/haeundae-things-to-do-2026-10/09.webp)

**October hours** are Beach Train **09:00 to 19:30** and Sky Capsule **08:30 to 18:30**; from November to February both finish 30 minutes earlier. The rides run in rain but stop for heavy downpours and typhoons.

Booking is the whole game. Online Sky Capsule sales open **every Tuesday at 07:00 Korean time**, releasing the entire week (Monday to Sunday) that begins **four weeks later**. Reviews consistently say weekend capsules are gone within the morning. A small same-day allocation is sold at the Mipo ticket office, but you need to be there early. The official refund scale is 100% three or more days before, 70% the day before and 50% on the day.

The money-saving pattern is **capsule out from Mipo to Cheongsapo, train back**. Mipo Station is beside X the SKY, about 10 minutes on foot from [[line:busan2]] Jung-dong Station. The official site has an English booking page and the ticket office takes cards.

## Cheongsapo: walk to the Daritdol skywalk

About 10 minutes on foot from Cheongsapo Station, towards the shore, is the **Daritdol Observatory**. Haeundae-gu widened it in August 2024 into a **191 m** U-shaped skywalk with its deck **20 m** above the water and a glass floor at the tip. The five reefs lined up between the platform and the lighthouse are the "daritdol", or stepping stones. It is **free**, open **09:00 to 21:00** from September to November (to 20:00 in winter, to 22:00 in summer), every day. Parking is ₩300 per 10 minutes.

Cheongsapo itself is a small fishing village inside the city, with raw-fish and clam-grill restaurants along the water. From the observatory the old-railway path (the Green Railway) continues towards Songjeong, a **40 to 50 minute walk** if you have the legs, and you can ride the Beach Train back from Songjeong Station.

## Dalmaji-gil, Haewoljeong and Moontan Road: the forest way back

![Aerial view of the wooded Dalmaji hill and Mipo village, with the LCT towers, Haeundae high-rises and Gwangan Bridge in the distance](/images/haeundae-things-to-do-2026-10/11.webp)

The road over the hill between Mipo and Cheongsapo is **Dalmaji-gil** (달맞이길), the "moon-viewing road". Haeundae-gu describes it as an **8 km** drive under cherry and pine trees with more than fifteen bends. At the top stands **Haewoljeong**, a pavilion built in 1997, with a millennium clock tower from 2000, and below the pavilion the **Haewol Observatory**, a 137 m U-shaped skywalk off the cliff, opened in July 2024. All free.

The forest path below the road is **Moontan Road**, literally "get a moon tan", a five-section trail that the Korea Tourism Organization rates at **one to two hours**. It is nearly flat pine forest with sea views at the lookouts, which is why the common combination is **capsule out to Cheongsapo, Moontan Road back on foot**. You can also join it directly from the Blue Line Park's Dalmaji Station.

## Centum City: the Cinema Center and Spa Land

Three stops from Haeundae on [[line:busan2]] is Centum City. The **Busan Cinema Center** hosts the opening and closing ceremonies of the Busan International Film Festival, which runs **6 to 15 October** this year. Outside the festival its cinematheque screens classics and retrospectives; the city's 2026 event listings put special-programme tickets at **₩7,000 to ₩8,000**. The building and its outdoor roof are free to wander. Dates are in the [Busan autumn festivals guide](/en/posts/busan-festivals-2026-09/).

**Spa Land** inside Shinsegae Centum City is a hot-spring complex in a department store, a Korean institution worth one evening. The Korea Tourism Organization listing gives two natural spring sources, 18 baths and 13 themed sauna rooms, **09:00 to 22:00** with last entry at 21:00. Tickets are sold on a **four-hour basis** at roughly **₩20,000 (about $15)** for adults, with different weekday and weekend rates and a surcharge if you overstay. Children must be of primary-school age or older. The station connects straight into the store, which makes it an easy end to a long walking day.

## Night views, briefly

Haeundae's night views come down to two: **Marine City and the LCT towers from the sand**, and **Gwangan Bridge from the south side of Dongbaekseom**. Both are free and take twenty minutes. The Saturday **drone show** is at Gwangalli, not Haeundae; see the [Busan things-to-do overview](/en/posts/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Half-day and full-day routes

**Half day (4–5 hours)**: Dongbaek Station → Dongbaekseom loop and Nurimaru (1 hour) → walk the beach to Mipo (25 minutes) → Sky Capsule to Cheongsapo (30 minutes) → Daritdol Observatory → Beach Train back to Mipo → sunset at X the SKY.

**Full day**: add lunch at Haeundae Market before the beach, walk from Cheongsapo on to Songjeong and ride the train back, then finish at Spa Land or the Cinema Center in Centum City.

If you could not get a capsule, the fallback is **train from Mipo to Cheongsapo, then Moontan Road back on foot**.

## Practical notes

- **From Gimhae Airport**, airport limousine route 1 (Haeundae and Gijang) stops at Haeundae Beach; the adult fare is **₩9,500** after the January 2026 reorganisation. By rail, take the [[line:gimhaelrt]] light rail to Sasang and change to [[line:busan2]] for Haeundae, a little over an hour.
- **From Busan Station**, take [[line:busan1]] to Seomyeon and change to [[line:busan2]] for Haeundae Station, **45 to 50 minutes**.
- **Only three things cost money**: the Sky Capsule, X the SKY and Spa Land. Everything else here is free.
- **First Mondays** close Nurimaru. October's has passed, so the next is 2 November.
- Cards are accepted at the Blue Line Park ticket office, X the SKY and Spa Land, and the Blue Line Park site has English pages.
- The Mipo–Cheongsapo–Songjeong stretch has little shade and a stiff sea breeze. Even in October bring a **hat and a layer**.

## Wrapping up

Seen as a beach, Haeundae is two hours. Walked as a line from Dongbaekseom to Dalmaji, it is a full day. Lock in the Sky Capsule on a Tuesday morning four weeks ahead and the rest of the order can bend to the weather.
