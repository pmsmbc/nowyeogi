Accommodation in Bukchon also starts with two numbers. It is the same shape as Seochon, only bigger.

- **Operating hotels and inns in Bukchon's 11 legal neighbourhoods - none.** Not even a hostel
- **Operating hanok-stay businesses in the same 11 neighbourhoods - 182.** That is **42%** of Seoul's 438

And there is a third number. **138 of those 182 were registered in 2021 or later.** They have grown so fast that Jongno-gu is considering **blocking new hanok stays around Gahoe-dong**.

![Hanok tiled roofs seen from a Bukchon hillside at dusk, with the five-storey hall of the National Folk Museum and the ridge of Bugaksan in the distance](/images/bukchon-hotels-2026-09/04.webp)

## First, where I was wrong

In an earlier version of [the Seochon accommodation piece](/en/posts/seochon-hotels-2026-09/) (since recounted from the licensing data: 121 hanok stays in Seochon's 14 neighbourhoods, 438 in Seoul) I wrote that **I could not get a complete dataset of hanok-stay businesses**, so I used the Seoul Stay list (133 places) promoted by the Seoul Tourism Organization instead.

**The complete dataset does exist.** The Seoul Open Data Plaza publishes **hanok-stay business licensing data for Seoul (OA-16047)**. This piece counts from it, using the update of 23 September 2026.

The two sources differ a lot.

| Source | All of Seoul | Bukchon's 11 neighbourhoods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Licensed hanok-stay businesses (operating)** | **438** | **182** |
| Seoul Stay hanok (promoted by the city) | 133 | 50 |

**Seoul Stay is only part of the registered total** - just the places the city helps promote and run. The Seochon figure of "33 out of 133" came from that list.

On the same Seoul Stay basis, it is **Bukchon 50, Seochon 33**. In the Seochon piece I called Seochon the neighbourhood with the densest concentration of hanok stays in Seoul. **By neighbourhood, Bukchon has more.** Seochon is a third of Bukchon's area, so it is still dense, but I let it read as number one by count, and that needed correcting.

## Where Bukchon begins and ends

Bukchon is not the name of a legal neighbourhood. Like [the overview](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/), this piece counts **the 11 legal neighbourhoods of the Bukchon district unit plan area as set out in a 2020 Seoul city notice**.

> Gahoe-dong, Gye-dong, Wonseo-dong, Anguk-dong, Jae-dong, Hwa-dong, Sagan-dong, Sogyeok-dong, Songhyeon-dong, Palpan-dong, Samcheong-dong

## There are no hotels

I counted the 11 neighbourhoods using Seoul's licensing data.

| Business type (governing law) | Operating | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lodging - yeogwan inns and general lodging (Public Health Control Act) | **0** | 2 closed records (licensed 1976 and 1977) |
| Tourist accommodation - tourist hotels, hostels etc. (Tourism Promotion Act) | **0** | |
| **Hanok-stay business** (Tourism Promotion Act) | **182** | |
| Urban homestay for foreign tourists (Tourism Promotion Act) | 28 | Wonseo-dong has the most, 10 |

Seochon at least had two inns. **Bukchon does not have even that.** The only records left are Hongseongjang in Gahoe-dong (licensed 1977, 12 ondol rooms) and Samhwa in Hwa-dong (licensed 1976), and **both are closed**.

**In practice, the only places to sleep in Bukchon are hanok stays and homestays.**

**Gye-dong and Gahoe-dong together hold 114, or 63%.** In Gye-dong they line Gyedong-gil; in Gahoe-dong they sit around Bukchon-ro 11-gil (the red zone) and Bukchon-ro 12-gil.

They are small, too. **The median declared floor area is 57㎡**, and 156 of the 182 are under 100㎡. Most are **a whole house let to one party** or a few rooms at most.

## 138 of them since 2021

Here are the 182 currently operating, by year of licence.

| Year licensed | Number |
| --- | --- |
| 2010-2020 | 44 |
| 2021 | 16 |
| 2022 | 15 |
| 2023 | 20 |
| 2024 | 21 |
| **2025** | **44** |
| **2026 (to September)** | **22** |

**44 in 2025 alone** - as many as the eleven years from 2010 to 2020 put together. The most recent licence was issued on 22 September 2026.

Jongno-gu has announced the same trend. According to reports of its 1 June 2026 announcement, **hanok-stay businesses in Bukchon's hanok cluster rose from 47 in 2020 to 168**. (Jongno-gu's figure covers the designated hanok cluster, a narrower area than the 11 neighbourhoods counted here.)

### Gahoe-dong may be closed to new ones

Jongno-gu is pursuing a plan to **remove the hanok-stay business from the permitted uses** in the district unit plan for **"Bukchon Zone 1", the area around 31 and 11 Gahoe-dong**. Sijung Ilbo described it as **"restricting new registrations at source"**.

The licensing data show **18 operating at 31 Gahoe-dong and 10 at 11 Gahoe-dong**.

However, **as of 28 September 2026 I could find no final decision notice.** All that has been published is the schedule: hearing residents' views in June, then review by the district urban planning and architecture committee, then a notice. **Nothing has been announced that affects staying at places already operating.**

## Staying inside the red zone

The **red zone** described in [the overview](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/) - **about 34,000㎡ around Bukchon-ro 11-gil** - is **closed to tourists from 5pm to 10am**, with a fine of 100,000 won.

So can guests at a guesthouse inside it get back in the evening? **Yes.** Jongno-gu's official FAQ says:

> To confirm whether someone is exempt, we check ID, **accommodation booking confirmations** and the purpose of visiting shops … **guests are allowed in on showing their booking confirmation**

With a condition attached.

> Long-stay guests are exempt from the fine, but if they **engage in tourist activity** inside the red zone during the restricted hours, they **may be treated as tourists and fined**.

The FAQ defines **tourist activity** too: **taking photos or video, lingering to look around, and wandering the streets without a purpose**.

In practice that means:

- **Checking in after 5pm** - fine. **Keep the booking confirmation on your phone**
- **Checking out before 10am** - fine. Leaving counts as simply passing through
- **Photographing the lane outside your guesthouse in the evening** - **can be fined**
- **A month-long stay** - does not make you a resident. Residents are only people whose resident or foreign-resident registration card shows an address inside the red zone

**12 of the 50 Seoul Stay hanok have an address on Bukchon-ro 11-gil or its branch lanes (11ga, 11na, 11da-gil).** Jongno-gu only publishes the boundary as "around Bukchon-ro 11-gil", so I have not declared which individual places fall inside it. **Ask the guesthouse when you book.**

## Homestays are for foreign visitors

Bukchon's 28 homestays are licensed as **"urban homestays for foreign tourists"**. Article 2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Tourism Promotion Act defines them like this:

> a business in which **a resident** of an urban area uses **the house they live in** … to provide lodging and meals so that **foreign tourists** can experience Korean home culture

For overseas visitors that is good news: **these are aimed at you**. It also means the host must actually live there. **Korean nationals cannot, in principle, stay in them**, while **hanok-stay businesses have no such restriction**.

## Choosing a hanok stay

I set out the legal standards in detail in [the Ikseon-dong accommodation piece](/en/posts/ikseon-hotels-2026-09/). The essentials:

- **An en-suite bathroom is not required.** A shared toilet is legal
- **Sleeping on the floor is not required either.** Some hanok have beds
- **A fire extinguisher and a standalone smoke alarm in every room are compulsory**, plus a **carbon monoxide alarm** where heating is individual
- **There is no 24-hour front desk.** If you arrive late, let them know

**One more thing.** By law a hanok-stay business must have facilities for **either "lodging" or "traditional culture experiences"**. So **not all 182 necessarily take overnight guests** - the licensing data have no field that separates the two.

## Airport bus

**Airport limousine [[bus:6011]] stops at Anguk Station** - the same route mentioned in the Seochon piece.

**Going to the airport:**

| Stop | Where | First bus | Last bus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Changdeokgung (Biwon) | Opposite Garden Tower, next to the Hyundai building (01781) | 04:25 | **20:42** |
| **Anguk Station** | **In front of Hana Bank, exit 1 (01779)** | 04:28 | **20:45** |
| Gyeongbokgung Station | Opposite the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency, exit 1 (01777) | 04:31 | 20:48 |

**If you are in Gye-dong or Wonseo-dong, the Changdeokgung (Biwon) stop** is closer, and its last bus leaves three minutes earlier.

**Coming from the airport**, you get off **between exits 5 and 6 of Anguk Station, in front of SK Hub Plaza next to Jongno Police Station** (01780).

| Departing Incheon Airport | Boarding point | First bus | Last bus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Terminal 1 | 1st floor, platform 5B-3 | 05:44 | 23:14 |
| Terminal 2 | Basement 1, bay 31 | 05:24 | 22:54 |

Fares are 17,000 won for adults and 12,000 won for children. At the airport you need to **buy a ticket at the counter or a ticket machine**.

**If you arrive on a late bus, it will be inside the red zone's restricted hours.** If your guesthouse is inside the zone, **have your booking confirmation ready**.

By rail, take [[line:arex]] to Seoul Station, [[line:1]] to Jongno 3-ga, then [[line:3]] one stop to Anguk.

## Is it worth staying in Bukchon?

**The advantages:**

- **More hanok stays than anywhere else in Seoul** - 182 operating
- **You can walk Bukchon before 10am.** Just do not take photos in the red zone
- Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung are within walking distance either side
- **The airport bus stops at Anguk and Changdeokgung**

**The drawbacks:**

- **No hotels.** For a front desk, breakfast and 24-hour check-in you need to go towards Insa-dong
- **Steep hills.** The upper parts of Gahoe-dong and Samcheong-dong are hard work with a suitcase
- **No evening photos if you are inside the red zone**
- **Hanok are not soundproof**, and your neighbours really do live there. Late-night noise turns into complaints quickly here

## Where to book

- **A quiet whole-house hanok** - Gye-dong and Wonseo-dong are outside the red zone and have plenty of choice
- **The heart of Bukchon** - Gahoe-dong. Know the red-zone rules before you go
- **You need a hotel** - Gwanhun-dong, Insa-dong or Nagwon-dong, south of Anguk Station
- **Travelling with a Korean national** - homestays may be foreigners-only, so check the licence type

## What I could not confirm

**There are no official figures for prices**, so none are given.

**I could not separate the 182 hanok-stay businesses into those offering lodging and those offering only culture experiences.** The licensing data have no such field.

**I have not determined whether individual hanok stays lie inside the red-zone boundary.** Jongno-gu only publishes it as "around Bukchon-ro 11-gil".

**No final decision notice on restricting hanok stays in Bukchon Zone 1** could be found as of 28 September.

**Arrival times at Anguk for the city-bound 6011** are not published on the Airport Limousine website.

## Summary

- **Bukchon's 11 legal neighbourhoods have no operating hotels, inns or hostels.**
- **There are 182 hanok-stay businesses**, 42% of Seoul's total, 63% of them in Gye-dong and Gahoe-dong.
- **138 have been registered since 2021**, 44 in 2025 alone.
- Jongno-gu is pursuing **a ban on new hanok stays around 31 and 11 Gahoe-dong**, but no notice has been issued yet.
- **Red-zone guests can get in during the evening with a booking confirmation**, but photography and other tourist activity can be fined.
- **The 28 homestays are for foreign tourists.**
- **Airport bus 6011 leaves from exit 1 of Anguk Station, last bus 20:45.** The Changdeokgung stop is 20:42.
- **If you need a hotel, go south of Anguk to Insa-dong and Gwanhun-dong.**
