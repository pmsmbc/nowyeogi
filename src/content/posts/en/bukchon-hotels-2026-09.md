If you search for a hotel in Bukchon and find none, the booking site isn't broken. Counting Bukchon's 11 legal neighbourhoods one by one in Seoul's licensing data gives this:

- **Tourist hotels and hostels: 0. Inns and motels: 0**
- **Hanok stays (registered "hanok experience businesses"): 182** — 42% of Seoul's 438 and 51% of Jongno-gu's 358
- **Foreign-tourist urban homestays: 28**

In short, **staying in Bukchon means renting a whole hanok, or a few rooms in one.** The two neighbourhoods with the most hanok stays in all of Seoul are **Gye-dong (58) and Gahoe-dong (56)**, and 138 of the 182 opened in 2021 or later. That growth is why Jongno-gu is considering a block on new hanok stays in part of Gahoe-dong, and why the "red zone" on Bukchon-ro 11-gil closes to tourists in the evening. This post covers which lanes the 182 are on, what changes if you sleep inside the red zone, and where to catch the airport bus.

## How these were counted

From four licensing datasets on the Seoul Open Data Plaza, I took only businesses marked "operating" whose **address (legal dong) is one of the 11 neighbourhoods below**. Tourist accommodation and lodging data were refreshed on 24 September 2026, homestays on 27 September and hanok stays on 23 September. Same data and method as the [Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-hotels-2026-09/), [Ikseon-dong](/en/posts/ikseon-hotels-2026-09/) and [Seochon](/en/posts/seochon-hotels-2026-09/) posts.

| Dataset | Law | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tourist accommodation | Tourism Promotion Act (registration) | Tourist hotels, hostels, family hotels, small hotels |
| Lodging | Public Health Control Act (notification) | Inns (yeogwan), guesthouses (yeoinsuk), general hotels, serviced residences |
| Foreign-tourist urban homestay | Tourism Promotion Act (designation) | Residents hosting foreign tourists in their own home |
| Hanok experience business | Tourism Promotion Act (registration) | A hanok offering stays or traditional-culture experiences |

The four are **separate registers that don't overlap**. A hanok stay is legally a "tourist-use facility", not a hotel, so it never shows up in lodging statistics. That is how a neighbourhood can have zero lodging businesses and 182 hanok stays at once.

Bukchon isn't an official place name. This post uses the **11 legal dong** that the Jongno-gu table in the [Jongno post](/en/posts/jongno-hotels-2026-09/) and the [Bukchon travel guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/) assign to Bukchon, the neighbourhoods covered by the Bukchon district-unit plan.

**358 of Seoul's 438 hanok stays (82%) are in Jongno-gu**: 182 in Bukchon's 11 neighbourhoods and 121 in [Seochon's 14](/en/posts/seochon-hotels-2026-09/). Together the two areas hold 85% of Jongno-gu's total.

Neighbourhood by neighbourhood, Seoul's top hanok areas are **Gye-dong (58), Gahoe-dong (56)**, Chebu-dong (36), Jingwan-dong in Eunpyeong-gu (31), Nuha-dong (30) and **Samcheong-dong (24)**. Bukchon takes first, second and sixth.

## A neighbourhood without hotels

There are **no operating tourist-accommodation or lodging businesses** in the 11 neighbourhoods. The tourist-accommodation register has no record at all for these addresses, past or present.

The lodging register holds just two old records, **both closed**:

- "Hongseongjang" in Gahoe-dong — licensed in 1977 as a **yeoinsuk** (basic guesthouse), 12 Korean-style rooms
- "Samhwa" in Hwa-dong — licensed in 1976 as a **yeogwan** (inn)

[Seochon](/en/posts/seochon-hotels-2026-09/) at least still has two inns; Bukchon has none. **If you need a hotel with a front desk, head south of Anguk Station** (see "Just outside the boundary").

## 182 hanok stays: which lanes

**114 of the 182 (63%) are in Gye-dong and Gahoe-dong.** By street, **Gyedong-gil (29)** leads, followed in Gahoe-dong by **Bukchon-ro (13), Bukchon-ro 11ga-gil (10) and Bukchon-ro 11-gil (10)**, then Gyedong 6-gil (9) and Bukchon-ro 12-gil (9).

The Seoul Hanok Portal explains that the dense hanok clusters around **Gahoe-dong 11 and 31, Samcheong-dong 35 and Gye-dong 135** date from the 1930s, when housing developers bought large plots and built rows of mid-sized hanok. Today's hanok stays largely follow those same lanes.

### Gye-dong (58): Gyedong-gil and its side lanes

Gye-dong is **the legal dong with the most hanok stays in Seoul**, spread along both sides of Gyedong-gil, Gyedong 2-, 4- and 6-gil and toward Changdeokgung-gil.

- **Bukchon's earliest registrations** came on the same day, 18 January 2010: "Siin Tongsin" (Gyedong 6-gil) and "Gahoe Hanok Experience Hall" (Gyedong-gil 85-13). "Hanok Jaeno" (Gyedong-gil 85-7) followed that month
- **Rakkojae Seoul** is registered twice, at Gyedong-gil 49-23 (2010) and 49-21 (2018). According to KTO it is a 130-year-old hanok, converted, with jade-floored ondol rooms and a wood-fired sauna
- **Inwoo House** (Gyedong 6-gil 9, 2011) has, per KTO, a private toilet for each room and runs hanji paper craft, knot-bracelet and fan-decorating sessions. The same address carries a 2010 registration for "Inu House" that closed in 2011
- Some hanok carry hotel brands: **"The Prima Hotel Hanok Dambakjae, Gye-dong"** (Gyedong 6-gil 8-3, 2020, four rooms), with a Gahoe-dong sister (2024)
- **21 registrations from 2025–2026** make up 36% of Gye-dong. The most recent is "Jjokkomi-ok" (Gyedong 2-gil 11), registered 6 August 2026

### Gahoe-dong (56): Bukchon-ro 11-gil, 12-gil and Bukchon-ro

Gahoe-dong has two fewer than Gye-dong but **bigger houses**: its median recorded floor area is about 68 m², against about 56 m² for Bukchon as a whole.

- **25 are on Bukchon-ro 11-gil and its branches (11ga, 11na, 11da, 11ra-gil)**, the red-zone streets. What that means for guests is under "Staying inside the red zone"
- **All six "Nostalgia" stays** (Bluejae, Hiddenjae, Hillojae, Slowjae, Nookjae, Doublejae) are in Gahoe-dong, registered between November 2022 and April 2025
- **Two more Rakkojae** properties are here: "Rakkojae Seoul Chwiunjeong Spa Suites" (Bukchon-ro 11ga-gil 39, 2011) and "Rakkojae Bukchon Bingwan" (Bukchon-ro 11ga-gil 10, 2022)
- Bukchon's largest by recorded area is "Hwigyeomjae" in Gahoe-dong (Bukchon-ro 46-3, 2021) at 368 m², followed by Rakkojae Bukchon Bingwan (212 m²) and "Classic Gotaek Anguk" (185 m²)
- **18 are at lot 31 and 10 at lot 11 of Gahoe-dong**, the area where Jongno-gu is considering limiting new registrations (see "Gahoe-dong: a plan to stop new hanok stays")

### Samcheong-dong (24): 22 since 2021

**22 of Samcheong-dong's 24 were registered in 2021 or later.** Even the oldest, "Bukchon Heritage" (Bukchon-ro 11-gil 73-4, three rooms), dates only from 2018.

- Bukchon-ro 11da-gil (5) and Samcheong-ro (5) have the most, then Bukchon-ro 11-gil (3) and Bukchon-ro 5na-gil (3). **11 addresses are on the Bukchon-ro 11-gil family of streets**
- Three "by Butlery" stays (Gureumjeong, Gouheon, Nuriheon) and "The Chae: Haudo" are here
- The newest is "Sosodang Samcheong" (Bukchon-ro 11da-gil 33), registered 14 September 2026; "Sosodang Bukchon" (2025) is in Gahoe-dong

### Size: mostly one house

The **median recorded facility area is about 56 m²**, close to Jongno-gu's (about 54 m²) and a little larger than Seochon's (about 50 m²). **156 of the 182 are under 100 m²**; the smallest is 16 m² and the largest 368 m².

Only **54 of the 182** record a room count: 12 with one room, 24 with two, 13 with three, 3 with four and one each with five and six. Most are **one group taking a whole house, or a few rooms**.

## Gahoe-dong: a plan to stop new hanok stays

Reports of a Jongno-gu announcement on 1 June 2026 said that **hanok stays in Bukchon's hanok-dense area rose from 47 in 2020 to 168**. Jongno-gu's figure covers a narrower area than this post's 11 neighbourhoods (182).

Jongno-gu said it is examining **"Bukchon Zone 1", around lots 31 and 11 of Gahoe-dong**, with a view to **adjusting the uses permitted in the district-unit plan so that new hanok-stay registrations are restricted**. The timetable in the reports: resident consultation, interdepartmental talks and expert advice in June, then review by the district's urban planning and architecture committee and a formal decision notice.

- In the licensing data, **18 hanok stays operate at Gahoe-dong lot 31 and 10 at lot 11**
- **As of 6 October 2026, a search for "Bukchon" on Jongno-gu's official notice board returns only the December 2020 decision on the Bukchon district-unit plan.** I found no new decision notice
- Registrations have continued: "Bukchon Byeolsol" (5 June) and "Gahoe Roun" (6 July) opened in Gahoe-dong after the announcement, and the 2 October refresh adds two more there
- **Nothing announced affects staying at places already operating.** The proposal targets new registrations

## Staying inside the red zone

Jongno-gu's official **Bukchon Special Management Area** page sets out the red-zone rules:

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Area | **Around Bukchon-ro 11-gil, about 34,000 m²** |
| Restricted hours | **5 p.m. to 10 a.m. the next day**: no tourist visits |
| In force | Warnings from 1 November 2024, **fines from 1 March 2025** |
| Fine | **₩100,000 (about $75)**, reducible by up to half under the district's tourism ordinance |
| Exempt | Residents, their family and friends, shop customers, shopkeepers, people simply passing through (but not if they engage in "tourist activity") |

So can guests get back to a red-zone guesthouse in the evening? **Yes.** The page's FAQ says that to check exemptions, staff look at ID, booking confirmations or the purpose of a shop visit, and that **"guests are admitted on showing a booking confirmation"**.

Being a guest isn't a blanket pass, though. The same FAQ says long-stay guests are exempt from fines, but **if they engage in tourist activity in the red zone during restricted hours, they are treated as tourists and may be fined**.

**"Tourist activity"** is defined as taking photos or video, lingering to look around, and wandering the streets with no connection to using a shop. **The rules apply to foreign visitors in the same way**, and vehicles belonging to exempt people may drive through, but getting out to sightsee can still bring a fine.

In practice:

- **Checking in after 5 p.m.** — fine. **Keep your booking confirmation open on your phone.** Enforcement is on the spot by Bukchon wardens, who may ask questions to check
- **Checking out before 10 a.m.** — leaving counts as simply passing through
- **Photographing the lane outside your guesthouse in the evening or early morning** — can be fined, even as a guest
- **Month-long stays** — you are not a resident. Residents are only people whose national ID or foreign resident card shows an address inside the red zone
- **Arriving by taxi with luggage** — vehicles of exempt people can pass

In the licensing data, **36 hanok stays (25 in Gahoe-dong, 11 in Samcheong-dong) have addresses on Bukchon-ro 11-gil or its branches** (11ga, 11na, 11da, 11ra-gil). Jongno-gu describes the zone only as "around Bukchon-ro 11-gil" and points to the Smart Seoul Map for the exact boundary, so I haven't assumed all 36 are inside it. **Ask your host whether the house is in the red zone when you book.**

**The Bukchon-ro 12-gil area is a yellow zone**: a monitored residential area with no fines, where visitors are encouraged to come between 10 a.m. and 5 p.m. Nine hanok stays (eight in Gahoe-dong, one in Gye-dong) have addresses on that street.

The same page says that **since 1 January 2026, chartered buses with 16 or more seats are banned at all times from about 2.3 km of streets including Bukchon-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil, Bukchon-ro 4-gil and Changdeokgung 1-gil** (first-offence fine ₩300,000). Coaches drop off at Samcheong-ro (in front of the National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art), Samil-daero (west of Tapgol Park) or Donhwamun-ro (opposite Changdeokgung). If a group is renting a whole hanok and moving by coach, check this in advance.

## Hanok stays: what the rules require and what they don't

The full registration standard is set out item by item in the [Ikseon-dong post](/en/posts/ikseon-hotels-2026-09/). The source is **Annex 1 of the Enforcement Decree of the Tourism Promotion Act** (the version in force from 4 August 2026). Here is what matters before a Bukchon stay.

### Required

- A **hanok** meeting the Land Ministry's standard, with the space used for stays **under 230 m² of floor area** (heritage hanok and some others exempt)
- If stays are offered, **convenience facilities such as a bathroom or shower**
- **At least one fire extinguisher**, a **stand-alone smoke alarm in every room**, and a **carbon monoxide alarm** where heating is individual
- **Disinfection at least once a month**, cleaning of rooms and bathrooms and **regular washing of bedding**
- **A manager on site during business hours**
- If stays are offered, **a rate card posted at the reception and on any online interface** used for business, **and those rates must be honoured**

That last item comes from the **amendment in force from 4 August 2026**. Its transitional provision gave hanok businesses registered before then **one month** to comply. Since almost every Bukchon hanok sells online, the price on the booking page is the posted rate the host must keep to.

### Not required

- **A private bathroom for each room** — the text only says "a bathroom or shower, etc." KTO says Inwoo House has one per room, but that isn't mandatory. Ask whether it's en suite or shared
- **Beds, ondol floor heating or floor mattresses** — none is mandatory either way
- **A 24-hour front desk** — a manager is required only "during business hours". If you'll arrive late, tell the host in advance
- **Foreigners-only status** — the definition says "tourists", so **Korean nationals can stay too**

One more point: the licence covers businesses offering **either stays or traditional-culture experiences**, so not all 182 necessarily take overnight guests. The data has no field that separates the two.

## 28 homestays: for foreign visitors only

Bukchon has **28 foreign-tourist urban homestays**. **Wonseo-dong has the most (10)**, then Gye-dong (6), Samcheong-dong (4) and Sagan-dong (3).

- **18 of the 28 (64%) were designated in 2024 or later** (4 in 2024, 7 in 2025, 7 in 2026). The oldest, on Samcheong-ro 4-gil in Hwa-dong, dates from January 2014
- Most of Wonseo-dong's 10 are **flats in multi-unit buildings on Changdeokgung 3-gil and 7-gil**. Hanok stays cluster in Gye-dong and Gahoe-dong; homestays lean toward Wonseo-dong along the Changdeokgung wall
- By building type: 10 detached houses, 8 multi-household houses, 8 multi-unit houses

Under the Tourism Promotion Act's enforcement decree, a foreign-tourist urban homestay is **a resident offering their own home to foreign tourists**, so **Korean nationals generally cannot book them**; as a foreign visitor you can. The detail is in the [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong post](/en/posts/yeonnam-hotels-2026-09/).

**Two of the 28 have "hanok" in their name** ("Hanok Salon 115" in Wonseo-dong and "Bonum 1957 Hanok & Boutique" in Gahoe-dong) but are **designated as homestays, not hanok experience businesses**. If anyone in your party holds Korean nationality, check the licence type rather than the name. **For Korean nationals, the realistic options in Bukchon are the 182 hanok stays.**

## What I got wrong

The previous version of this post (28 September 2026) contained errors and weakly sourced claims.

**One: it mixed in Seoul Stay figures.** Alongside the licensing data (182), it set the Seoul Tourism Organization's Seoul Stay list ("50 of Seoul's 133 hanok stays are in Bukchon") and compared "Bukchon 50, Seochon 33" on that basis. Seoul Stay lists only places the city promotes and supports, a subset of those licensed. The [Seochon post](/en/posts/seochon-hotels-2026-09/) was also recounted from licensing data in October (121), so this version drops Seoul Stay numbers entirely and **compares only licensing data**. The red-zone street count changes likewise, from "12 of Seoul Stay's 50" to **36 registered hanok stays**.

**Two: the opening section about "the old Seochon post" has gone.** It began by saying the Seochon post had claimed full hanok data was unavailable; that post has since been rewritten from the same register.

**Three: the hotel count overlapped other posts.** The old version separately counted "28 tourist-accommodation businesses with 2,324 rooms in 12 neighbourhoods south and east of Anguk (Gwanhun, Insa, Nagwon, Ikseon, Unni, Waryong, Gwonnong, Gyeongun, Gyeonji, Gongpyeong, Susong, Junghak)". Those neighbourhoods are covered by the [Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-hotels-2026-09/), [Ikseon-dong](/en/posts/ikseon-hotels-2026-09/) and [Gwanghwamun](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-hotels-2026-09/) posts, so this version links to them instead of recounting.

**Four: parts of the red-zone advice were inaccurate.** It said that staying in Bukchon lets you "walk Bukchon before 10 a.m." In the red zone, lingering to look around or wandering during restricted hours is itself tourist activity, and guests can be fined. It also stated flatly that Gye-dong and Wonseo-dong are outside the red zone; Jongno-gu publishes only "around Bukchon-ro 11-gil", so this version gives street names only.

**Five: unsourced claims were removed** — that hanok are poorly soundproofed and that the upper parts of Gahoe-dong and Samcheong-dong are hard to climb with luggage. On the slope, only the Seoul Hanok Portal's description of the terrain remains.

**Six: the median area was corrected.** The old "57 m²" recalculates from the same data as **about 56 m²** (56.4).

## What could not be confirmed

**Prices** — there is no official source, so none are given. Hanok and homestay prices vary by site and date. Since the August 2026 amendment, a hanok stay's rate card must also be posted on its booking page, so check there.

**Which of the 182 take overnight guests and which offer only cultural experiences** — the register has no field for it.

**Whether each hanok stay is inside the red-zone boundary** — Jongno-gu says only "around Bukchon-ro 11-gil" and refers to the Smart Seoul Map. I also found **no specific rule on check-in times for red-zone guesthouses**; the official text says only that guests are admitted with a booking confirmation.

**The outcome of the Gahoe-dong Zone 1 restriction** — I found no official record of the June consultation results, the committee review date or a decision notice (as of 6 October).

**Room counts and real size** — only 54 of the 182 list rooms, and I couldn't confirm whether the recorded area is the whole house or part of it.

**Whether stays sharing a name (by Butlery, Nostalgia, Rakkojae and others) share an operator** — the licensing data can't show this.

**Bathroom, bedding and breakfast arrangements** — confirmed only for the few stays KTO describes.

**Journey time from the airport to Anguk on 6011**, inbound arrival times at each stop, and **last subway times from Seoul Station or Gongdeok to Anguk** — not in the official information I found.

## Summary

- Bukchon's 11 neighbourhoods have **no tourist accommodation or lodging businesses**; the two old inn records are closed.
- Instead there are **182 hanok stays**, 42% of Seoul's 438 and 51% of Jongno-gu's 358.
- **Gye-dong (58) and Gahoe-dong (56)** rank first and second in Seoul and hold 63% of the total.
- **138 of the 182 opened in 2021 or later**, 44 in 2025 alone. Most are around 56 m².
- Jongno-gu is considering **restricting new hanok stays around Gahoe-dong lots 31 and 11**; as of 6 October no decision notice has been found.
- **Red-zone guests can come and go in the evening with a booking confirmation**, but photography and other tourist activity can be fined.
- **The 28 homestays are for foreign visitors only**; 10 are in Wonseo-dong.
- Airport bus **6011: last from Anguk 20:45, from Changdeokgung (Biwon) 20:42**. For a hotel, go south of Anguk to Insadong or Ikseon-dong.
