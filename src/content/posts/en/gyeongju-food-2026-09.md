Gyeongju's food is less famous than its ruins, which is a pity, because the city has one of the more interesting local canons in Korea. Two of its specialities are protected by cultural designation and one of them is a pastry.

Prices below are from official or maker sources where a figure exists. Restaurant meal prices in Korea are rarely published, so where a number comes from listings rather than a menu, this guide says so.

## Hwangnam bread

![Three Hwangnam bread pastries on paper, one whole with a comb pattern on top and two cut open to show dense red bean filling](/images/gyeongju-food-2026-09/01.webp)

**Hwangnambbang (황남빵)** is a small round pastry with a very thin skin packed almost solid with red bean paste, stamped on top with a comb pattern borrowed from Silla design. It is closer to a confection than to bread.

It was created in **1939** by **Choi Yeong-hwa**, then twenty-one years old, in **Hwangnam-dong**, adapting his family's red bean rice cake. The city designated it a traditional local food in **1994**.

There is **one shop in Gyeongju trading under the name**, the original at **783 Taejong-ro**, open **08:00 to 22:00 every day**, about five minutes' walk from Daereungwon. It is now run by the third and fourth generations of the same family. A box of twenty is **₩28,000** and a box of thirty is **₩42,000**, so roughly ₩1,400 a piece.

You will also see other shops selling the same style of pastry. That is not counterfeiting. The founder's business split among descendants and an apprentice who trained with him from the age of fourteen, and several operations now work from the same technique under different names.

## Gyeongju bread and barley bread

These two get confused constantly, and they are not the same thing.

**Gyeongju bread (경주빵)** is the 1939 pastry sold under another name: unleavened dough, thin skin, domestic red bean. Ten pieces run about **₩12,000**, with cinnamon and green tea versions a little more.

**Chalbori bread (찰보리빵)** is completely different. It is made from **local glutinous barley** with whole oats, and the texture is chewy and sponge-like rather than thin-skinned, with a lightly sweetened bean filling. The city lists eleven different makers. Twenty pieces run about **₩20,000**.

If you only buy one, buy the Hwangnam bread. If you want something less sweet, buy the barley bread.

Two newer novelties have become local fixtures too: **daereung bread**, a cheese soufflé shaped like a tomb mound, and **juryeonggu bread**, shaped like the fourteen-sided Silla drinking die found at Wolji.

## Ssambap

![A platter of lettuce and other wrap vegetables served with a bowl of ssamjang paste](/images/gyeongju-food-2026-09/05.webp)

**Ssambap (쌈밥)** is the standard Gyeongju set meal, and it is the thing to eat if you want one proper sit-down lunch.

A set is built around grilled meat, usually **Korean beef short rib patty, stir-fried pork, duck or freshwater snail in soybean paste**, served with a tray of organic leaves for wrapping, a soybean stew, and somewhere between ten and twenty side dishes. You wrap the rice, meat and paste in a leaf and eat it in one go.

The city's own restaurant listing names houses including **Neulbom** near Bulguksa, open 09:00 to 21:00 and closed Thursdays. Expect roughly **₩16,000 to ₩21,000** a head, though that band comes from restaurant listings rather than published menus.

## The Silla soup

Gyeongju's designated local dish is not what most visitors order, and it is worth knowing about.

**Yukgaejang from the six villages (6부촌육개장)** is a spicy beef soup built from six ingredients, one for each of the six villages that founded the Silla kingdom: Gyeongju beef brisket, tripe, intestine, dried bracken, a mountain green called *gondalbi*, and leek. It belongs to the city's **Byeolchaeban** brand of revived Silla-era dishes, alongside a *gondalbi* bibimbap.

Two restaurants carry the official designation, one in Hwangnam-dong on Cheomseong-ro and one out near the KTX station.

## Korean beef

The city describes itself as Korea's largest beef-producing area and runs its own brand, **Gyeongju Cheonnyeon Hanwoo**. It took the country's first HACCP certification for cattle rearing in 2007.

The format to look for is the self-service butcher restaurant, where you buy the cut at the counter and pay a small table charge to grill it yourself. There are branches in Bomun, Angang, Oedong and Yonghwang.

## Gyodong Beopju

The one to bring home. **Gyodong Beopju (교동법주)** is an unpasteurised rice wine first brewed roughly **350 years ago** by Choi Guk-seon, a court official who retired to Gyochon, and kept as the Choi family's house liquor ever since.

It was designated a **national intangible cultural property in 1986**. The first holder of the tradition was Bae Yeong-sin; her son now holds it. It is made from glutinous rice only and it is not heat-treated, which is why it is sold locally rather than nationally.

Buy it at the brewery at **19-21 Gyochon An-gil** in Gyochon Village, **09:30 to 18:00, closed Sunday**. A 900 ml bottle runs between **₩35,000 and ₩43,500** depending on the packaging.

Do not confuse it with **Gyeongju Beopju**, a commercial rice wine launched in 1972 that you will find in supermarkets. Different product, different thing entirely.

## Where to eat, by area

**Hwangnidan-gil (황리단길)** is the young street: cafés, desserts, small izakaya, craft shops. Coffee runs about ₩5,000 to ₩7,000 and snacks ₩3,000 to ₩8,000. It is where you go in the late afternoon, not for a serious meal.

**Around Daereungwon** is the bakery cluster. Hwangnam bread, the barley bread shops and the juryeonggu bread shop are all within a few minutes of each other, along with one of the two official Silla soup restaurants.

**Gyochon Village** has the brewery, Choi family formal dining and a traditional distillery, all in a small walkable block.

**Bomun Lake Resort** is resort dining: the Gyeongju beef branch, a brewpub and hotel restaurants. The most expensive zone and the least distinctive.

## Practical notes

- **Buy bread early.** The main Hwangnam shop queues from mid-morning at weekends.
- **Ssambap houses close midweek.** Several take Thursday or Monday off.
- **The bread travels; the wine does not.** Gyodong Beopju is unpasteurised and wants refrigeration.
- **Hwangnidan-gil is not a dinner district.** Eat properly elsewhere and come here after.
- **Vegetarians** will find ssambap adaptable in principle, but the soybean stew and side dishes usually contain anchovy stock. Ask.

## Wrapping up

Eat one ssambap set, buy one box of Hwangnam bread, and if you drink, take a bottle of Gyodong Beopju home. That is the whole Gyeongju food canon in a day, and two thirds of it is protected by the state.
