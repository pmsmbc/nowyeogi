Gangneung is easier to choose a base in than most Korean coastal cities, for one reason: **the KTX station is in the middle of town**, not on the edge of it. That removes the usual trade-off between transport and location.

The city's own accommodation register lists **205 properties** across five zones. It does not publish prices, and neither does this guide, because there is no reliable source for them.

![The Gyeongpodae pavilion on a wooded rise above Gyeongpo lagoon](/images/gangneung-hotels-2026-09/01.webp)

## The zones, as the city divides them

The official register splits the city into five areas, and they are a genuinely useful frame.

- **Downtown (시내권)** — around the station and Jungang Market
- **Gyeongpo (경포권)** — the main beach and lagoon
- **Daegwallyeong (대관령권)** — the mountain pass and ski country inland
- **Jumunjin (주문진권)** — the fishing port up the coast
- **Jeongdongjin and Okgye (정동진·옥계권)** — the sunrise coast to the south

Most visitors want the first two.

## Gyeongpo

The beach option, and the one most people should take for a summer trip. Gyeongpo is the city's main beach, with the **lagoon and Gyeongpodae pavilion** behind it and a flat, cycleable park around the water.

Accommodation here runs from resort hotels to pensions and guesthouses. Properties on the city register include the **Gangneung St. John's Hotel** on Changhae-ro and the **Siesta Guesthouse Gyeongpo** on Haean-ro.

**Transport**: buses 202, 202-1 and 202-2 into town, about 20 minutes.

**Suits**: summer trips, families, anyone who wants to walk out onto sand.

**Downsides**: it is a resort strip, so it is busy and loud in July and August and quiet to the point of empty in winter. You bus into town for dinner.

## Anmok

The coffee street. A shorter beach with about thirty cafés along the road behind it, and a smaller, more scattered supply of rooms.

**Suits**: anyone whose ideal morning is a drip coffee facing the sea, and shoulder-season visitors who want the coast without the Gyeongpo crowd.

**Downsides**: fewer rooms, so book earlier. Little happens here at night.

The **SEA TEA coastal tour bus starts at Anmok**, which makes it a convenient base for a day up the coast.

## Downtown, near the station

![Gyeongpo lagoon and the surrounding parkland seen from the pavilion](/images/gangneung-hotels-2026-09/02.webp)

The practical option, and better than it sounds. Gangneung Station sits in the city centre, the **Wolhwa-gil street park** runs 2.6 km from it through town on an old railway alignment, and Jungang Market is a walk away.

Verified properties on the city register include **Hong C Hotel Gangneung Station** and **Hotel East9**, both in the downtown zone.

**Suits**: short trips, off-season visits, anyone arriving late or leaving early, travellers without a car.

**Downsides**: no sea view, and you bus out to the beaches.

## Jeongdongjin

The sunrise village twenty minutes south, reached by **bus 112** or a **17-minute local train**. Accommodation here exists almost entirely to put people in front of the sunrise.

**Suits**: one specific purpose. If watching the sun come out of the sea is the point of your trip, sleep here.

**Downsides**: it is a small village, the food options are limited, and you are away from everything else.

**The New Year is the exception that proves it.** The sunrise festival runs every 31 December to 1 January and the whole coast fills. The city's own advice for the 1 January sunrise is to book well in advance, and that is not a formality.

## Seongyojang

Worth its own mention. **Seongyojang (선교장)** is a three-hundred-year-old aristocratic estate that operates a hanok stay in its guest quarters.

Staying here also gets you **free daytime admission**, which otherwise costs ₩5,000. If the appeal of Gangneung is its history rather than its beaches, this is the distinctive choice.

## What to check before you book

- **Which beach.** Gyeongpo and Anmok are different places with different characters.
- **Season.** A Gyeongpo resort in February is a very quiet building.
- **Whether it is a pension.** Much of the coastal stock is self-catering rather than hotel service.
- **New Year dates.** Anything on this coast for 31 December books out months ahead.
- **Bus access.** Check the route number and the last service; coastal buses stop earlier than city ones.

## Who should stay where

**Summer beach trip** — Gyeongpo.

**Two days, no car, any season** — downtown near the station.

**Coffee is the point** — Anmok.

**Here for the sunrise** — Jeongdongjin.

**Here for the history** — Seongyojang.

## Wrapping up

For most two-day trips, book near the station. It is central, it is cheaper than the beach, and the SEA TEA bus will take you up the coast for the price of a normal fare. Save Gyeongpo for the summer, when the beach is the reason you came.
