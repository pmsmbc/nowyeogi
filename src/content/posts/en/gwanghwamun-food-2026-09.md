Gwanghwamun, City Hall and Jonggak are Seoul's government and office district, and **a lot of the city's oldest restaurants are still here**. Of the 50 restaurants the Seoul government listed as Seoul Future Heritage in 2021, **at least eight sit inside the area this post covers**. Lay the MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026, the city's Oraegage (long-standing shop) list and the government's good price restaurant list on top of that, and the choices get fairly clear.

The short version: **hangover soup in Cheongjin-dong, ox bone soup near Jonggak, octopus, pollack soup, yukgaejang and cold noodles in Mugyo-dong and Da-dong, and soybean or buckwheat noodles in Seosomun-dong.** Every restaurant here appears on at least one official list, and anything about taste comes from the MICHELIN Guide or Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) descriptions.

A **MICHELIN Bib Gourmand** marks a place where you can eat for **₩45,000 (about $34) or less per person**. Seoul has 51 of them in 2026, and **only two, Mijin and Yurimmyeon, are in this area**. Gwanghwamun Gukbap, Nampo Myeonok and Imun Seolnongtang are **Selected** (listed in the guide without a star or Bib) on their 2026 pages, even if older articles and blogs still call them Bib Gourmand.

**Seoul Future Heritage** is the city's list of modern-era places worth passing on that are not official cultural properties. **Oraegage** is a city certification for long-running shops that was extended to restaurants in 2024. Neither is a taste rating. Both simply record that a place has lasted.

## Gwanghwamun and Cheongjin-dong: what is left of the hangover soup alley

![A black earthenware bowl of milky seolleongtang, a spoon lifting thin slices of beef, wheat noodles and sliced scallion, with kkakdugi in the background](/images/gwanghwamun-food-2026-09/02.webp)

The photo shows seolleongtang in general, not a dish from a specific restaurant.

### Pimatgol now lives inside office towers

**Pimatgol** was the narrow lane behind Jong-ro, the main road, where commoners walked to avoid the processions of high officials. The Cheongjin-dong stretch was known for mung bean pancake houses and hangover soup, but it was **torn down for redevelopment in 2009**. Parts were rebuilt as passages inside new buildings such as Le Meilleur Jongno Town (Korean Wikipedia, "피마길").

According to **Cheongjinok**'s official site, it began in 1937 selling beef-broth rice soup where the Cheongjin-dong firewood market stood, and **in the 1970s about ten hangover soup houses clustered here, giving the lane the name Cheongjin-dong Haejangguk Alley**. The restaurant moved in 2008 because of redevelopment and has been at **32 Jong-ro 3-gil since 2016**. The official menu lists **hanwoo haejangguk at ₩12,000 (about $9) and a large bowl at ₩15,000**. The KTO describes a bone broth with ox blood curd, beef tripe, dried cabbage leaves and bean sprouts. KTO hours are **06:00-22:00, open every day**, so you can eat it for breakfast. Haejangguk means "soup to chase a hangover", and eating it in the morning is the traditional idea.

In the same neighborhood, the government-listed good price restaurant **Heungjinok** (19-12 Jong-ro 5-gil) lists pork bone soup at ₩10,000.

### Mijin: two trays of buckwheat per person

**Mijin** is on the ground floor of Le Meilleur Jongno Town. The MICHELIN Guide says it makes its broth and buckwheat noodles fresh every day in a basement kitchen, and that **one portion comes as two stacked trays of noodles with a kettle of chilled dipping broth**. Grated radish, wasabi, seasoned laver and scallions are already on the table for you to add. The MICHELIN page lists **10:30-21:00 daily**. DiningCode lists cold buckwheat noodles at ₩12,000, with spicy and hot versions, bossam (boiled pork) and pork cutlet also on the menu.

MICHELIN says Mijin dates from 1952, while the Seoul Future Heritage list says 1954.

### Gwanghwamun Gukbap: the lunch queue is in the official write-up

**Gwanghwamun Gukbap** (53 Sejong-daero 21-gil) has a Jung-gu address but is about 5 minutes on foot from Gwanghwamun Station Exit 6 (KTO). According to the MICHELIN Guide, it serves a clear, mild pork broth made with black pork and topped with plenty of chives, and offers a smaller bowl for children. The same write-up says **weekday lunch queues are long because it sits in an office district**. The KTO lists a **break from 14:00 to 17:00 on weekdays (14:30-17:00 on Saturdays), and it is closed on Sundays**. DiningCode lists pork gukbap at ₩12,000 and Pyongyang naengmyeon at ₩15,000.

## Jonggak: Imun Seolnongtang

**Imun Seolnongtang** is at 38-13 Ujeongguk-ro, on the way from Jonggak Station toward Jogyesa Temple. The Seoul Future Heritage list gives its opening year as **1904**, the KTO says 1907, and the MICHELIN Guide calls it **the first restaurant officially registered in Seoul**. The MICHELIN write-up describes beef leg bones simmered for 17 hours in a cast-iron pot and skimmed of fat, and names the boiled beef slices and seasoned boiled beef as specialties.

KTO hours are **Mon-Sat 08:00-21:00 and Sun 08:00-20:00, with a break from 15:00 to 16:30 every day**. **It may close early when the soup runs out, and there is no parking.** DiningCode lists seolleongtang at ₩15,000.

In Gwancheol-dong near Jonggak, the good price restaurant **Bulbaekdang** (34 Ujeongguk-ro 2-gil) lists spicy grilled pork and galbi-style grilled pork at ₩5,500 each and a squid-and-pork version at ₩6,500. That is among the cheapest meals in this post.

## City Hall, Mugyo-dong and Da-dong: octopus, pollack soup, yukgaejang and cold noodles

![A clear dried-pollack hangover soup in a black earthenware bowl on a wooden table, with swirls of egg, scallion and bean sprouts](/images/gwanghwamun-food-2026-09/03.webp)

The photo shows dried-pollack soup in general, not a dish from a specific restaurant.

### "Mugyo-dong octopus" actually began in Seorin-dong

A 1992 Hankook Ilbo article says the first octopus restaurant opened **in 1963 in Seorin-dong, by Jongno 1-ga**, and that by **the early 1970s more than 50 octopus houses** each claimed to be the original. They took the name of Mugyo-dong, the busier district across the road, and **redevelopment starting with road widening in 1975** scattered them. The city's monthly magazine Seoul Sarang (September 2020) traces the same path from Seorin-dong to Mugyo-dong, Pimatgol and Jongno 1-ga.

That article features **Mugyodong Yujeong Nakji's main branch** (22 Sejong-daero 21-gil). DiningCode says it has been running since 1966 and lists stir-fried octopus at ₩18,000 per serving, with **rice ordered separately**.

### The old houses of Da-dong and Mugyo-dong

The Seoul Foundation for Arts and Culture magazine Culture+Seoul (January 2023) calls Da-dong and Mugyo-dong **the oldest eating alley in Seoul**. Da-dong is named after "dabang", the Joseon-era office that served tea to the royal court.

- **Mugyodong Bugeoguk-jip** (38 Eulji-ro 1-gil) opened in 1968 and is on the Seoul Future Heritage list as a place that **serves only dried pollack soup**. DiningCode lists it at ₩11,000, with hours of **07:00-20:00 on weekdays and 07:00-15:00 on weekends**. It is one of the few places here open for breakfast.
- **Bumin-ok** (24-12 Dadong-gil) **opened in 1956** and is both Seoul Future Heritage and an Oraegage. The KTO says it is known for a yukgaejang that is not spicy but clean-tasting, and for seasoned beef tripe, and that office workers nearby have come for lunch for decades. **Break 15:00-17:00, closed Sundays.** DiningCode lists yukgaejang at ₩11,000.
- **Nampo Myeonok** (24 Eulji-ro 3-gil) serves Pyongyang-style cold buckwheat noodles and eobok jaengban, a beef hot pot shared at the table. It is MICHELIN Selected for 2026 and an Oraegage. The MICHELIN write-up notes a major renovation of the main building and both chair and floor seating. The KTO says the dongchimi (radish water kimchi) for the broth is kept in earthenware jars. DiningCode lists naengmyeon at ₩16,000 and a large eobok jaengban at ₩98,000.

In Bukchang-dong, the good price restaurant **Donugasikdang** (25-17 Namdaemun-ro) lists kimchi stew at ₩7,000 and beef head soup at ₩8,000.

## Seosomun-dong and Jeong-dong: soybean and buckwheat noodles

![A stainless steel bowl of white kongguksu, thin noodles in cold soybean broth topped with julienned cucumber, carrot and black sesame](/images/gwanghwamun-food-2026-09/04.webp)

The photo shows kongguksu in general, not a dish from a specific restaurant.

West of City Hall Station, behind Deoksugung Palace, three Seoul Future Heritage restaurants are a few minutes' walk from each other in Seosomun-dong.

- **Jinju Hoegwan** (26 Sejong-daero 11-gil) is **a kongguksu specialist that opened in 1962**. Kongguksu is thin noodles in a cold, thick soybean broth. The KTO says the beans come from Gangwon Province and that **kongguksu is served only from March to November**. DiningCode lists it at ₩16,000, with kimchi stew and grilled pork also on the menu. Closed Sundays.
- **Yurimmyeon** (139-1 Seosomun-ro) is **a MICHELIN 2026 Bib Gourmand** as well as Seoul Future Heritage and an Oraegage. The MICHELIN Guide says the family has run it for over 50 years and makes buckwheat noodles daily with salt from Bigeumdo Island in Sinan and buckwheat flour from Bongpyeong. DiningCode lists buckwheat noodles at ₩11,000 and spicy buckwheat noodles at ₩12,000.
- **Goryeo Samgyetang** (1 Seosomun-ro 11-gil) is a ginseng chicken soup specialist that opened in Seosomun-dong in 1960. The KTO lists it as **open every day**, 10:30-21:00 on weekdays and 10:30-20:30 on weekends. DiningCode lists samgyetang at ₩20,000.

## Good to know

**Weekday lunch overlaps with office workers.** Even the MICHELIN write-up for Gwanghwamun Gukbap warns of long weekday lunch queues. If your schedule is flexible, going a little before or after noon is the easier option.

**Many places close for a break in the afternoon.** Imun Seolnongtang (15:00-16:30), Bumin-ok (15:00-17:00) and Gwanghwamun Gukbap (14:00-17:00 on weekdays) all stop serving for a while.

**Many places close on Sundays.** Bumin-ok, Gwanghwamun Gukbap, Jinju Hoegwan and Heungjinok are closed. Cheongjinok, Mijin, Imun Seolnongtang, Goryeo Samgyetang and Nampo Myeonok open on Sundays.

**For breakfast, try Cheongjinok (from 06:00), Mugyodong Bugeoguk-jip (07:00) or Imun Seolnongtang (08:00).**

**Smoking is banned inside all restaurants, and there is no tipping.**

## What we could not confirm

**Menu prices for most restaurants** come from **DiningCode**, a Korean restaurant listing site, not from the restaurants themselves. Only Cheongjinok's prices were checked against an official menu. Prices may have changed.

**Yurimmyeon's closing day** differs between MICHELIN (Sunday) and DiningCode (Tuesday).

**Gabongru**, a Chinese restaurant near Gwanghwamun on the Oraegage list, appears to have moved recently, and sources give different addresses, so it is left out.

**Baeknyeon Gage** (the SME ministry's "100-year shop" designation) could not be checked against an official list.

**Individual restaurants still left in the old Pimatgol lanes** could not be confirmed from official sources. For the Jongno 3-ga lanes and street stalls, see [what to eat in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/) and [the Jongno pocha street](/en/posts/jongno-pocha-2026-09/).
