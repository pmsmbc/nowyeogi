Of all the Seoul neighbourhoods this blog has covered, Itaewon and Hannam-dong are **the place where a district that had its rules relaxed sits right next to a district being rebuilt from scratch**.

The Itaewon-ro strip became **Seoul's first Special Tourist Zone** in 1997, under a scheme originally designed to **let businesses stay open at night**. The slopes to the south and east, in Hannam-dong, Bogwang-dong and Dongbinggo-dong, were declared a **"New Town"** redevelopment area in 2003. The city notice gives a completion target of **2025**. That year has passed, and the demolition crews are busy.

The basics (location, getting in from the airport, what the area is like) are in our earlier [Itaewon and Hannam guide](/en/posts/itaewon-guide-2026-09/). This guide adds a layer of **official records** on top.

![Bogwang-dong hillside seen from the Hannam Bridge side, red-brick low-rise housing packed densely up the slope with a church and its pointed spire at the top](/images/hannam-guide-2026-09/14.webp)

That hillside is **Bogwang-dong**. Despite the name "Hannam New Town", much of the redevelopment land is on slopes like this one. I could not confirm when the photo was taken, but as residents move out and buildings come down, this view is disappearing.

## Itaewon: Seoul's first Special Tourist Zone

### The name already meant "travellers' inn"

According to the Itaewon 2-dong community center, **Itaewon (梨泰院) was the name of a Joseon-era *won*, a government inn** where officials on the road stayed overnight. The same page notes the area was known for its pear trees.

Its administrative history, from the same source:

| Year | Name |
| --- | --- |
| 1751 | Itaewon-gye, Dunji-bang, southern Hanseong |
| 1914 | Itaewon-ri, Hanji-myeon, Goyang County, Gyeonggi |
| 1936 | Absorbed into Gyeongseong (Seoul) as Itaewon-jeong |
| 1943 | Placed in Yongsan-gu |
| 1946 | Renamed Itaewon-dong |

The community center says the area became **an international shopping street because of the US military presence after the Korean War and the embassies that clustered here**. The Yongsan History Museum adds that the US Army XXIV Corps set up headquarters at the Yongsan Garrison after 1945, that a permanent US presence was decided during the war, and that **a camp town grew up around Itaewon**.

### 25 September 1997: Seoul's first Special Tourist Zone

The National Archives of Korea explains the scheme like this:

- **1993** - Special Tourist Zones are written into the Tourism Promotion Act. The aim: **relax business-hour restrictions to encourage night-time tourism**
- **31 August 1994** - the first five zones: Jeju Island, Gyeongju, Seorak, Yuseong and Haeundae
- **25 September 1997** - Itaewon is designated

All five of the first zones were resorts outside Seoul, and **Itaewon was the first in the capital**. Knowing that the scheme was built to let places stay open late explains, from the paperwork side, why Itaewon has always been an evening neighbourhood.

![A stone-paved lane in the Itaewon Special Tourist Zone, a restaurant decorated with Mexican-style neon signs and paper bunting on the right, clothing shops and signs further down the lane](/images/hannam-guide-2026-09/08.webp)

### December 2025: the zone reaches Hangangjin

Two reports published on 8 January 2026 (Sijung Ilbo and Munhwa News) say the zone **was expanded by a Seoul city notice dated 26 December 2025**.

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Original extent | About 1.4km from the start of Itaewon-ro to the Hannam-dong community center |
| Area after expansion | About **390,000㎡** (about 380,000㎡ before, according to one report) |
| Newly included | **The Hangangjin Station area**, Leeum Museum of Art, Blue Square |
| Yongsan-gu's stated goal | From a place people "visit" to a place people "stay" |

In other words, **the Hannam-dong gallery district is now inside the tourist zone**. The two articles describe the original 1.4km stretch slightly differently, so I have not tried to draw the exact new boundary here.

## After 29 October 2022

No guide to Itaewon can leave out the night of 29 October 2022, when 159 people died in a crowd crush in the alley beside the Hamilton Hotel. Here are only the official changes that followed.

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 26 October 2023 | **10.29 Memorial Alley** created in the alley, led by the bereaved families' council |
| 21 May 2024 | **Special Act on the 10.29 Itaewon Disaster** (full title: on guaranteeing victims' rights, finding the truth and preventing recurrence) promulgated and in force, Act No. 20427 |
| 15 September 2026 | Amendment in force: the special investigation committee's term extended by a year to **16 September 2027**, and the deadline for victim recognition applications to 16 March 2028 |

Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the memorial alley is **about 40m from exit 1 of Itaewon Station**. The words "We still have names we must remember" are set into the ground at the entrance, and the Hamilton Hotel wall carries photographic works and a panel explaining what happened. The artworks and sticky notes are changed every two months.

The alley opens right off Itaewon-ro as you leave the station. If you pass through, **slow down and do not photograph people paying their respects**. This guide deliberately includes no photo of it.

## Hannam New Town: a redevelopment past its target year

### What the city notice says

The formal name is the **Hannam Redevelopment Promotion District** (한남재정비촉진지구). Seoul Notice 2023-154, published in the city gazette on 27 April 2023, opens by listing every notice before it.

- **18 November 2003** - designated a New Town district (Notice 2003-372)
- **19 October 2006** - designated a redevelopment promotion district (2006-357)
- **1 October 2009** - redevelopment plan adopted (2009-387)
- **30 March 2017** - Hannam Zone 1 **released** from redevelopment (2017-112)
- More than ten further amendments follow up to 2023

The overview from the same notice:

| Item | From the notice |
| --- | --- |
| Location | **Bogwang, Hannam, Itaewon and Dongbinggo-dong**, Yongsan-gu |
| Area | **948,390.3㎡** |
| Purpose | Improve living conditions in **the Bogwang-dong area**, dense with old, substandard housing and poor infrastructure |
| Base year / target year | 2007 / **2025** |
| Planned population and homes | 33,012 people / 12,775 households |
| Height | No building anywhere in the district above **90m above sea level** |

The plan's basic direction says it must **not damage the hillside landscape at the foot of Namsan**. That is why the cap is set in metres above sea level rather than in floors.

### Zone by zone (as reported in May 2026)

| Zone | Location and area (notice) | Status in 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 1 | 151 Bogwang-dong area, 58,795㎡ | Released in 2017, now a "retained management" area; a fast-track planning process is under way (Seoul Shinmun) |
| **Zone 2** | **Bogwang-dong** 272-3 area, 114,580.6㎡ | Revised plan approved on 13 May 2026: **1,311 homes** (197 public). Residents moving out in 2026, construction targeted for late 2027 |
| **Zone 3** | **Hannam-dong** 686 area, 386,395.5㎡ | Residents gone, demolition nearly finished. **5,988 homes** in 127 buildings up to 22 floors; sales possibly from late 2026 |
| **Zone 4** | **Bogwang-dong** 360 area, 160,156㎡ | Project implementation approved November 2025: **2,331 homes** in 51 buildings up to 22 floors |
| **Zone 5** | **Dongbinggo-dong** 60 area, 183,707㎡ | Implementation plan recently approved: **2,592 homes** |

The four zones add up to **roughly 12,200 homes**. Seoul Shinmun described the project as **finally picking up speed after 23 years**: it stalled in the 2008 financial crisis and restarted when Zone 3 won approval in 2019.

What this means for a visitor: **south of Itaewon-ro, on the slopes of Hannam-dong and Bogwang-dong, you will see hoardings and construction trucks for years to come.** These are not areas to seek out for old-alley atmosphere, and many blocks already stand empty.

## What I had wrong

Four things I believed before researching this turned out to be wrong.

**One: Hannam New Town is not a Hannam-dong project.** By the notice, **only Zone 3 is in Hannam-dong**. Zones 2 and 4 are in Bogwang-dong and Zone 5 in Dongbinggo-dong. Even the statement of purpose begins with "the Bogwang-dong area".

**Two: there are four zones, not five.** Zone 1 was **released on 30 March 2017** and survives in the notice only as a retained area.

**Three: a Special Tourist Zone was never a "tourist attraction" badge.** As the National Archives explains, **the 1993 purpose was to relax business hours**. I also had not realised Itaewon was not among the first five, joining only in 1997.

**Four: I thought the Leeum side was outside the zone.** Since December 2025, **the Hangangjin Station area is in**. The "Itaewon Special Tourist Zone" now stretches over Hannam-dong's gallery streets.

## Where Itaewon and Hannam-dong begin and end

In Korea, a *beopjeong-dong* is a legal neighbourhood used in addresses, and a *haengjeong-dong* is an administrative unit with its own community center. They do not always match.

| Name | What it is | For visitors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Itaewon-dong** | One legal neighbourhood, run as two administrative units, Itaewon 1-dong and 2-dong | The Itaewon-ro strip; Gyeongnidan-gil (its KTO address is also Itaewon-dong) |
| **Hannam-dong** | Legal neighbourhood | Galleries and boutiques near Hangangjin Station; Hannam Zone 3 |
| **Bogwang-dong** | Legal neighbourhood | Hannam Zones 2 and 4, mostly under redevelopment |
| **Haebangchon** | Not an official name; KTO lists it at **Yongsan-dong 2-ga** | Namsan slope north of Noksapyeong Station |
| **Itaewon Special Tourist Zone** | Designated 1997, expanded 2025, about 390,000㎡ | Itaewon-ro to Hangangjin Station |
| **Hannam New Town** | Hannam Redevelopment Promotion District, 948,390.3㎡ | Slopes south of Itaewon-ro |

![A hillside lane in Haebangchon, wooden-railed stairs climbing through greenery toward N Seoul Tower against a blue sky, cars and bicycles parked along the road](/images/hannam-guide-2026-09/03.webp)

**Haebangchon** means "Liberation Village", and it is exactly that. The Yongsan History Museum says people who came south from the North after 1945, or lost their homes in the war, **built a village on the Namsan slopes near the Yongsan Garrison**. As the photo shows, N Seoul Tower feels very close from its stairways.

## The western wall: Yongsan Garrison and Yongsan Park

For decades, the western edge of Itaewon was **the wall of the US Army's Yongsan Garrison**. Part of the returned land has been opened on a temporary basis as "Yongsan Children's Garden", and two things have changed.

- **30 December 2025** - the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport dropped advance booking and ID checks, **opening it to anyone who walks in**. Hours: Tuesday to Sunday 9am-6pm, Saturday until 9pm, **closed Mondays**. More than 1.8 million people had visited since the temporary opening. The ministry also said it would rename the park in the first half of 2026 after public consultation.
- **14 August 2026** - the vice minister said in a radio interview that the government **would discuss using the garden site for housing with the Seoul city government**, which Seoul Shinmun reported the city opposes. The same article puts Yongsan Park as a whole at about 3.3 million㎡ and the garden at about 297,000㎡.

At the time of writing I could not confirm whether a new name has been chosen or where the housing debate stands. Check the ministry's Yongsan Park office notices before you go.

| --- | --- | --- |

If Bukchon is the neighbourhood that **cut the hours tourists may spend there**, Itaewon entered the records first as the one that **extended its hours**. Ikseon-dong lifted its redevelopment zone in 2018 and kept its old lanes; Hannam Zone 1 was lifted too, in 2017, but the other four zones chose **to rebuild entirely**.

![A green street sign with a mosque illustration reading 'CENTRAL MOSQUE' and '200M' in English and Korean, with a smaller sign pointing toward Usadan-gil below](/images/hannam-guide-2026-09/11.webp)

The **Seoul Central Mosque** (39 Usadan-ro 10-gil) is, according to the Korea Muslim Federation, **Korea's first mosque: the government provided about 1,500 pyeong of land in May 1969, construction began in October 1974 and it opened on 21 May 1976**. Everyone is welcome, but visitors in revealing clothes are asked to borrow a skirt and headscarf from the changing room near the security desk. Follow signs like the one above from Itaewon-ro toward Usadan-gil.

## A route that works

West to east, from hill to river, **Haebangchon → Itaewon-ro → Hannam-dong** lines up with the three subway stations.

1. **Morning** - Haebangchon and Gyeongnidan-gil from [[line:6]] Noksapyeong. The slopes are steep, so wear comfortable shoes.
2. **Lunch** - down to Itaewon-ro. How to choose is in [Itaewon and Hannam-dong food](/en/posts/hannam-food-2026-09/).
3. **Afternoon** - the Seoul Central Mosque above Usadan-gil, then the galleries near Hangangjin. Order and opening hours are in [Itaewon and Hannam-dong things to do](/en/posts/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/).
4. **If you have time** - the returned Yongsan Park land. Remember it is closed on Mondays.

## Where to stay

The expanded tourist zone now ties Itaewon and Hangangjin stations together, but **the character of the hotels still changes station by station**. Around Itaewon Station it is busy late into the night; the Hangangjin side is quieter and generally pricier. See [Itaewon and Hannam-dong hotels](/en/posts/hannam-hotels-2026-09/) for a comparison. If a property is close to a redevelopment zone, check the map for possible construction noise.

## Who this suits

**Good fit:**

- You want a neighbourhood built around world food and evenings
- You want museums and galleries at the centre of your day - the Hangangjin side is now inside the zone
- You are curious about **the stories behind official records**: a city relaxing its rules in one place and rebuilding in another

**Not such a good fit:**

- If old lanes and traditional housing are the goal, the Hannam and Bogwang slopes are a building site. Try the [Bukchon guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/) or the [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong guide](/en/posts/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/)
- If hills are hard for you, skip Haebangchon and Gyeongnidan-gil and stick to Itaewon-ro and Hangangjin

## Summary

- **Itaewon is named after a Joseon-era government inn** for travellers.
- It became **Seoul's first Special Tourist Zone on 25 September 1997**, under a scheme built to **relax business hours**.
- On **26 December 2025** the zone grew to include **the Hangangjin area (Leeum, Blue Square)**, about 390,000㎡.
- **Hannam New Town (Hannam Redevelopment Promotion District): designated 2003, 948,390.3㎡, target year 2025.** Only Zone 3 is in Hannam-dong; Zones 2 and 4 are in Bogwang-dong, Zone 5 in Dongbinggo-dong.
- As of May 2026: **Zone 3 demolition nearly done, Zone 4 approved, Zone 2 aiming to start in late 2027, Zone 5 plan approved** - about 12,200 homes in total.
- **The 10.29 Memorial Alley** is beside exit 1 of Itaewon Station; the investigation committee now runs to **16 September 2027**.
- **Noksapyeong (Haebangchon, Gyeongnidan-gil), Itaewon (main strip), Hangangjin (galleries)** run west to east.

In one line: **Itaewon is Seoul's first tourist zone, created to lengthen the night, and the slopes to its south are being rebuilt wholesale, past their own deadline.**
