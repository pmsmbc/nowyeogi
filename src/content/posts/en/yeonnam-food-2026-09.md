Our [Hongdae food guide](/en/posts/hongdae-food-2026-09/) covers Seogyo-dong, Hapjeong and Sangsu. This post looks only at Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon, and sticks to **places that appear in official or published lists** rather than influencer rankings.

The short version: in Yeonnam-dong the documented story is a **Chinese-Korean restaurant street** and **two Michelin Bib Gourmand ramen shops**. In Mangwon-dong it is **the market and very cheap noodle soup in the lane beside it**.

![The green-fronted entrance building of Mangwon Market with its name on top, the covered arcade opening below between a cafe and small shops](/images/yeonnam-food-2026-09/04.webp)

## Why Yeonnam-dong has so many Chinese restaurants

Walk along Yeonnam-dong's main road and the red Chinese restaurant signs stand out. The reason is **a school next door in Yeonhui-dong**.

- **Overseas Chinese High School Seoul** (한국한성화교중고등학교) is listed by Korea's Ministry of Education at **176 Yeonhui-ro, Yeonhui-dong**, founded on **5 May 1948**.
- Several reports say the school **moved from Myeong-dong to Yeonhui-dong in 1969**.
- A 2022 feature in the local magazine Aneun Dongne says that after **Seoul's old Chinatown around Sogong-dong was demolished in 1970**, many ethnic Chinese families (hwagyo, Chinese families long settled in Korea) moved near the school in Yeonhui-dong, or to cheaper Yeonnam-dong next to it.

The numbers depend on when you ask. A **2015 CNB News report** counted about 3,500 ethnic Chinese residents, around 100 Chinese-run businesses along roughly one kilometre, and about 30 Chinese restaurants. The **2022 magazine feature**, citing the Seoul Chinese association, put the population of the two neighbourhoods at about 3,000.

![Jajangmyeon on a white plate, black bean sauce topped with half a boiled egg, cucumber strips and sesame seeds](/images/yeonnam-food-2026-09/05.webp)

The menus are the interesting part. According to the same feature, the early restaurants served **what the Chinese community ate itself**, such as lamb dishes and hot and sour soup, rather than Korean-Chinese staples like jajangmyeon (noodles in black bean sauce, pictured above as a generic example, not from a specific restaurant). Yeonnam-dong kept the small, cheap places, while Yeonhui-dong grew larger restaurants aimed at Korean customers.

The 2015 article names individual restaurants, but we could not confirm which are still open eleven years later, so they are not listed here.

## Two Bib Gourmands, both ramen

The MICHELIN Guide announced **51 Bib Gourmand restaurants in Seoul on 26 February 2026**. Bib Gourmand marks places where a meal costs **45,000 won (about $34) or less per person**.

Checking the 51 against their addresses, **two are in Yeonnam-dong**, and both are Japanese ramen shops.

### Sarukame

- **15 Yeonnam-ro**
- According to the MICHELIN Guide, it is run by **second-generation owner-chef Hiroto Honma**, who keeps the original recipes.
- The signatures are **clam ramen and crab-and-clam ramen on a shoyu (soy sauce) base**. The crab-and-clam bowl is described as limited to ten servings each at lunch and dinner.
- Counter seating makes it suited to solo diners.

### Nishimuramen

- **4th floor, 265 Donggyo-ro**
- The Seoul branch of a ramen shop started in **Fukuoka** by chef Takahito Nishimura.
- MICHELIN describes it as a **marriage of ramen and French technique**, localised with laver oil, Cheongyang chilli and white-kimchi-style pickles.
- It also mentions **pot rice mixed with cheese and ramen broth** as a standout dish.

Both are listed at MICHELIN's lowest price band (₩). No official menu prices were found, so none are given.

### Tuk Tuk Noodle Thai is not on the 2026 list

**Tuk Tuk Noodle Thai**, often described online as Yeonnam's Michelin Thai restaurant, **is not among the 51 Seoul Bib Gourmands for 2026**, and its individual MICHELIN page no longer loads. If you are working from an older list, bear that in mind.

## Mangwon Market

![The green-floored arcade of Mangwon Market, household goods and grocery stalls on both sides, shoppers pulling trolleys](/images/yeonnam-food-2026-09/03.webp)

A traditional market (jeontong sijang) in Korea is a cluster of small independent stalls, often under a covered arcade. The basics for this one:

- **27 Poeun-ro 6-gil** (Korea Tourism Organization address)
- Grew out of small shops in Mangwon-dong's alleys **around 1975**
- A **215-metre covered arcade**, so it works in the rain
- About **five minutes on foot from Exit 2 of Mangwon Station** on [[line:6]] (Seoul city article, March 2024)

**Opening hours vary by stall.** The Korea Tourism Organization only says "differs by shop", and two Seoul city articles give 09:00-21:00 (2022) and 10:00-20:00 (2024). **Between 10am and 8pm** is the safe window.

### Paying and sitting down

A 2023 News Lock report says **every stall takes cards**, along with T-money transit cards, Zero Pay, **Onnuri gift certificates** (a national voucher for traditional markets) and local Mapo vouchers. A 2024 Seoul city article adds that many stalls take the Seoul Love gift certificate.

The market has also moved to **fixed prices**, so there is no haggling. Pay what the label says.

Inside, the co-operative **Cafe M** lets you bring food bought in the market if you order a drink, according to both the 2022 Seoul article and the 2023 report. That is useful if you want somewhere to sit and eat.

![Large trays of sweet fried chicken, fried sausages and fried shrimp on a market counter, a vendor in a red shirt behind](/images/yeonnam-food-2026-09/02.webp)

## Good to know

**Smoking is banned in all restaurants and cafés.**

**No tipping.** The menu price is what you pay.

**Refusing a card is illegal**, and Mangwon Market is reported to take cards at every stall. Some terminals may still reject foreign cards.

**Weekend parking at Mangwon Market is very difficult**, according to the 2023 report. Take the subway.

**Both are residential neighbourhoods.** Keep your voice down in the back lanes at night.

## Summary

- **Yeonnam's Chinese restaurants trace back to the 1969 move of the Chinese school** to Yeonhui-dong and the 1970 demolition of the old Chinatown.
- **The 2026 Bib Gourmands in Yeonnam are Sarukame and Nishimuramen**, both ramen. Tuk Tuk Noodle Thai is not on the 2026 list.
- **Mangwon Market dates from around 1975**, has a 215 m arcade and takes cards, Zero Pay and Onnuri certificates.
- **Kalguksu for 4,000-5,000 won** is in the lane next to the market.
- **Gimbap for 3,000 won in Yeonnam and jajangmyeon for 7,000 won in Mangwon** are the published good price shop figures.
