Search for a hotel in Seochon (서촌), the old neighbourhood west of Gyeongbokgung Palace, and you will not find one — and that is not a gap in the booking sites. Counting the 14 neighbourhoods (*dong*) west of the palace one by one in Seoul's licensing data gives this:

- **Tourist hotels and hostels: 0**
- **Other lodging businesses: 2, with 39 rooms** — both licensed as inns (*yeogwan*)
- **Hanok experience businesses: 121** — 28% of Seoul's 438 and 34% of Jongno-gu's 358
- **Foreign-tourist urban homestays: 116**

In short, **staying in Seochon almost always means renting a hanok, often a whole one.** If you are a foreign visitor, a homestay is the other option; if you need a hotel with a front desk, you will be in Gwanghwamun. This post sets out which lanes the 121 hanok and 116 homestays are on, what the law does and doesn't require of a hanok stay, and where to catch the airport bus.

![A hanok lane in Seochon on a clear day, tiled walls and wooden gates on both sides and a straight stone-paved path running to the end of the alley](/images/seochon-hotels-2026-09/01.webp)

## How these were counted

From four licensing datasets on the Seoul Open Data Plaza, I kept only businesses with **an address (legal dong) in the 14 neighbourhoods below** and a status of "open". Tourist accommodation and lodging data are the 24 September 2026 updates, homestays 27 September, hanok stays 23 September. The source and method are the same as the [Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-hotels-2026-09/), [Ikseon-dong](/en/posts/ikseon-hotels-2026-09/) and [Bukchon](/en/posts/bukchon-hotels-2026-09/) posts.

| Dataset | Law | What's in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tourist accommodation | Tourism Promotion Act (registration) | Tourist hotels, hostels, family hotels, small hotels |
| Lodging business | Public Health Control Act (notification) | Inns (*yeogwan*), general hotels, serviced residences |
| Foreign-tourist urban homestay | Tourism Promotion Act (designation) | Residents hosting foreign tourists in their own home |
| Hanok experience business | Tourism Promotion Act (registration) | Hanok offering stays or traditional-culture experiences |

These are **four separate registers that do not overlap**. A hanok stay is licensed as a "tourist-use facility business", not as a hotel, so it never shows up in lodging statistics. That is why "two lodging businesses" and "121 hanok stays" in the same neighbourhood are not a contradiction.

Seoul's official hanok portal describes the area west of Gyeongbokgung as **"14 legal dong including Hyoja-dong and Ogin-dong"**, between Inwangsan mountain and the palace, roughly a third of Bukchon's size. Because the portal does not list all 14 by name, this post uses the 14 that the Jongno-gu table in the [Jongno post](/en/posts/jongno-hotels-2026-09/) assigns to Seochon.

### Size: mostly one house

The **median registered facility area is about 50 m²**, a little below Jongno-gu as a whole (about 54 m²) and Bukchon (about 56 m²). **117 of the 121 are under 100 m²**; the range is 13 to 145 m².

Only **37 of the 121** list a room count: nine with one room, 14 with two, ten with three and four with four — none above four. Think of most as **one group taking a whole house, or a few rooms in one**.

## Hanok stays: what the rules require and what they don't

The full registration standard is laid out item by item in the [Ikseon-dong post](/en/posts/ikseon-hotels-2026-09/). The source is **Annex 1 of the Enforcement Decree of the Tourism Promotion Act** (the version in force from 4 August 2026). Here is what matters before a Seochon stay.

### Required

- A **hanok** meeting the Land Ministry's standard, with the space used for stays **under 230 m² of floor area** (heritage hanok and some others exempt)
- If stays are offered, **convenience facilities such as a bathroom or shower**
- **At least one fire extinguisher**, a **stand-alone smoke alarm in every room**, and a **carbon monoxide alarm** where heating is individual
- **Disinfection at least once a month**, cleaning of rooms and bathrooms and **regular washing of bedding**
- **A manager on site during business hours**
- If stays are offered, **a rate card posted at the reception and on any online interface** used for business, **and those rates must be honoured**

That last item comes from the **amendment in force from 4 August 2026**. Its transitional provision gave hanok businesses registered before then **one month** to comply. Since almost every Seochon hanok sells rooms online, the price shown on the booking page is now the posted rate the host must keep to.

### Not required

- **A private bathroom for each room** — the text only says "a bathroom or shower, etc." Ask whether it is en suite or shared
- **Beds, ondol floor heating or floor mattresses** — none of these is mandatory either way
- **A 24-hour front desk** — a manager is required only "during business hours". If you'll arrive late, tell the host in advance
- **Foreigners-only status** — the definition says "tourists", so **Korean guests can stay too**

One more point: the licence covers businesses offering **either stays or traditional-culture experiences**, so not all 121 necessarily take overnight guests. The data has no field that separates the two.

## 116 homestays: for foreign visitors only

Seochon has **116 foreign-tourist urban homestays**. **Nusang-dong has the most (23)**, followed by Cheongun-dong, Ogin-dong and Pirun-dong (12 each) and Tongin-dong and Chebu-dong (11 each).

- **80 of the 116 (69%) were designated in 2024 or later** (23 in 2024, 32 in 2025, 25 in 2026). The oldest, in Pirun-dong, dates from March 2014
- By building type: 38 in multi-household houses, 36 in detached houses, 30 in multi-unit houses
- The busiest streets are Ogin 6ga-gil (7) and Ogin 6-gil (6) in Nusang-dong, Jahamun-ro 19-gil in Ogin-dong (7), Jahamun-ro in Cheongun-dong (6) and Jahamun-ro 21-gil in Singyo-dong (5). **Hanok stays cluster low down in Chebu-dong and Nuha-dong; homestays lean toward the upper neighbourhoods by Inwangsan**

Under the Tourism Promotion Act's enforcement decree, a foreign-tourist urban homestay is **a resident offering their own home to foreign tourists**, so **Korean nationals generally cannot book them**. The detail is in the [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong post](/en/posts/yeonnam-hotels-2026-09/).

A trap for Korean travellers: **four of the 116 have "hanok" in their name** ("Hanok Stay Gain" in Ogin-dong, "Hippier Hanokboda" in Chebu-dong, and "Onsenara's Hanok House" and "Seochon Sarangbang Hanok" in Hyoja-dong), yet they are **designated as homestays, not hanok experience businesses**. If you hold a Korean ID, check the licence type rather than the name.

## What I got wrong

The previous version of this post (23 September 2026) contained errors and weakly sourced claims.

**One: the hanok count was wrong.** It used the Seoul Tourism Organization's Seoul Stay list — "33 of Seoul's 133 hanok stays are in Seochon" — and said I hadn't been able to obtain full hanok licensing data. Seoul Stay lists only places the city promotes and supports, a subset of those licensed. Seoul's hanok experience business register shows **438 open in Seoul, 358 in Jongno-gu and 121 in Seochon's 14 neighbourhoods**. This version is recounted from that register.

**Two: "the densest concentration of hanok stays in Seoul" was wrong.** Bukchon's 11 neighbourhoods have more (182). Neighbourhood by neighbourhood, Chebu-dong (36) ranks third in Seoul after Gye-dong and Gahoe-dong. The [Bukchon post](/en/posts/bukchon-hotels-2026-09/) corrected this first.

**Three: only 13 neighbourhoods were counted.** The old version said the hanok portal defines Seochon as 14 legal dong, then counted 13. This version uses the same 14 as the Jongno-gu table in the [Jongno post](/en/posts/jongno-hotels-2026-09/), and the figures (2 lodging businesses, 116 homestays, 121 hanok stays) match that table.

**Four: "two stops from Seoul Station on Line 3" was wrong.** Line 3 does not serve Seoul Station. You change from [[line:1]] at Jongno 3-ga or from [[line:4]] at Chungmuro onto [[line:3]].

**Five: unsourced claims were removed** — that a 15–20 m height limit "from 1977 to 2024, for 47 years" made hotels impossible, that hanok are poorly soundproofed, that nights are quiet because there are few chains or bars, that most Seochon hanok have individual heating and therefore need CO alarms, and a comparison with Seongsu-dong's inns. I couldn't re-verify any of them.

**Six: the rate-card rule was updated.** The old version said only "post and honour a rate card". Since the amendment in force from 4 August 2026, it must be posted **both at the reception and online**.

## What could not be confirmed

**Prices** — there is no official source, so none are given. Hanok and homestay prices vary by site and date. Since the August 2026 amendment, a hanok stay's rate card must also be posted on its booking page, so check there.

**Which of the 121 take overnight guests and which offer only cultural experiences** — the register has no field for it.

**Room counts and real size** — only 37 of the 121 list rooms, and I couldn't confirm whether the recorded facility area is the whole house or only part.

**Whether stays sharing a name (by Butlery, The Chae, Ihwa Hanok) share an operator** — the licensing data can't show this.

**Bathroom, bedding and breakfast arrangements** — confirmed only for the few stays KTO describes.

**Whether the two inns (Su Motel, Hanokhanok) actually operate** — I could only confirm that the data lists them as open.

**Journey time from the airport to Gyeongbokgung Station on 6011**, inbound arrival times at each stop, and **last subway times from Seoul Station or Gongdeok to Gyeongbokgung or Gwanghwamun** — not in the official information I found.

**Whether Seochon has a visiting-hours restriction like Bukchon's red zone on Bukchon-ro 11-gil, or a plan like Gahoe-dong's to block new hanok stays** — I found nothing in Jongno-gu's official material. Bukchon's rules are in the [Bukchon post](/en/posts/bukchon-hotels-2026-09/).

## Summary

- Seochon's 14 neighbourhoods have **no tourist hotels or hostels** and just **two inns with 39 rooms**.
- Instead there are **121 hanok stays**, 28% of Seoul's 438 and 34% of Jongno-gu's 358.
- **Chebu-dong (36) and Nuha-dong (30)** hold more than half; Chebu-dong ranks third in Seoul.
- **85 of the 121 were registered since 2021**, 26 in 2025 alone. Most are around 50 m² with four rooms or fewer.
- The hanok standard has **no rule on private bathrooms, beds or 24-hour desks**; rate cards must be posted at the desk and online.
- **The 116 homestays are for foreign visitors only**, and some have "hanok" in their name.
- Airport buses: **6011 from Gyeongbokgung Station, last 20:48**; **6002 from Gwanghwamun, last 20:52**; late-night N6002 from the Four Seasons.
