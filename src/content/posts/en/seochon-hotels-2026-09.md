Accommodation in Seochon comes down to two numbers. And they point in opposite directions.

- **Registered lodging businesses in Seochon - 2 of them, with 39 rooms.** Both are yeogwan inns, and **there is not one hotel**
- **33 of Seoul's 133 hanok stays are in Seochon.** No neighbourhood in Seoul has more

Here is why the same place produces both.

![A Sejong Village information board, the neighbourhood's old name and history written in Korean and English](/images/seochon-guide-2026-09/02.webp)

## There are no hotels

Seochon was counted from the city's licensing data. **The Seoul Hanok Portal defines Seochon as 14 legal-status neighbourhoods**, and this tally covers the **13 of them that could be pinned down by address**.

| Business type | Businesses | Rooms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Yeogwan** | **2** | **39** |
| Registered tourist hotel | 0 | 0 |
| General hotel | 0 | 0 |
| Lodging business (residence) | 0 | 0 |
| Yeoinsuk | 0 | 0 |

**Two inns, and that is all.** One was licensed in 1985, the other in 1999.

Tourist accommodation businesses were checked separately, and **there are zero in those same 13 neighbourhoods**. The nearest are in Dangju-dong and Sinmunno 1-ga, over towards Gwanghwamun.

The Seongsu-dong post said "no hotels" too, but Seongsu-dong still had 21 yeogwan with 299 rooms. **Seochon has far less than that.**

The reason is in [the overview post](/en/posts/seochon-guide-2026-09/). **From 1977 to 2024, for 47 years, heights were capped at 15 to 20m.** A hotel could not be built.

## And yet it has the most hanok stays in Seoul

By the Seoul Tourism Organization's Seoul Stay listing there are **133 hanok stays** in Seoul. **33 of them are in Seochon.**

| Area | Hanok stays |
| --- | --- |
| **Seochon** | **33** |
| Within 500m of Anguk Station | 21 |
| Within 500m of Jongno 3-ga Station | 6 |
| Jung-gu (Euljiro and Myeong-dong) | **0** |
| Seongdong-gu (Seongsu-dong) | **0** |

**A quarter of Seoul's hanok stays are in Seochon.**

### Why the two figures do not overlap

An important caveat. **The 33 hanok stays are not included in the 2 lodging businesses above.**

The governing laws are different.

- **Yeogwan and hotels** are businesses **notified under the Public Health Control Act**
- **Hanok stays** are **registered under the Tourism Promotion Act** as the hanok-stay business, which is not a hotel business but a tourist-use facility business

So **the two figures cannot be added together.** The Ikseon-dong post made the same point, but in Seochon the gap is at its most extreme. **Look only at the general lodging statistics and this is a neighbourhood with almost nowhere to sleep; in practice it is the best place in Seoul to sleep in a hanok.**

## What a hanok stay actually is

The Ikseon-dong post goes into this in detail; here are the essentials again. **What the law requires, and what it does not**, is different from what most people assume.

### What the law does not require

**A private bathroom is not compulsory.** The provision goes no further than requiring "convenience facilities such as a bath or shower", so **shared toilets are legal**.

**Nor is there any rule that you have to sleep on the floor.** There is no provision at all about beds, ondol or bedding mats. **Some hanok stays do have beds.**

### What the law does require

- **At least one fire extinguisher**, and **a standalone smoke alarm** in every room
- **A carbon monoxide alarm** as well, if heating is individual
- Total floor area of the sleeping space **under 230 square metres**
- **A manager on duty during business hours**
- **Posting the tariff and keeping to it**

**Most hanok in Seochon have individual heating, so in practice a carbon monoxide alarm should be there.** Take a look when you check in.

**There is no 24-hour front desk.** A manager is present only during business hours, so if you are arriving late you need to contact them in advance.

## Is Seochon a good place to stay

**In its favour:**

- **It has more hanok stays than anywhere in Seoul.** There are 33 to choose from
- **Gyeongbokgung Palace is walkable.** It is one wall away
- **The airport bus stops at Gyeongbokgung Station** (see below)
- **There are no franchises, so it is quiet.** Little late-night bar noise

**Against it:**

- **There are no hotels.** If you want a front desk, breakfast and 24-hour check-in, you have to go over towards Gwanghwamun
- **Hanok have poor sound insulation.** Timber and paper screens
- **There are hills.** The closer to Inwangsan, the steeper it gets
- **Little is open late.** Arrive late and there is not much to eat

## There is an airport bus

**The airport limousine [[bus:6011]] stops at Gyeongbokgung Station.**

- Boarding point - **opposite the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency at exit 1**
- **First bus 04:31, last bus 20:48**
- Adult 17,000 won (about $12.75)

**Route 6011 is the only airport route that stops at Gyeongbokgung Station.** The same route also stops at **Changdeokgung Palace and Anguk Station**, so it works for the hanok stays on the Bukchon side too.

The last bus at **20:48** is later than Euljiro's 19:50 and about the same as Jongno 3-ga's 20:45.

By rail, take [[line:arex]] to Seoul Station and change to [[line:3]] for Gyeongbokgung Station. Two stops from Seoul Station.

## Where to book

- **You want to sleep in a hanok** - Seochon offers more choice than anywhere in Seoul
- **You need a hotel** - go out towards Gwanghwamun or Anguk. There are none in Seochon
- **You want a quiet night** - few franchises and few bars, so the nights are quiet
- **You have heavy luggage or are arriving late** - hanok have no 24-hour front desk, so contact them in advance

## What could not be verified

**There is no official source for room rates in Seochon.** That is why no figures appear here.

**Complete data on hanok-stay businesses could not be obtained.** The Seoul Stay listing stands in for it, and since that is what the city promotes, it may not be every registered business.

**How the two yeogwan operate now** could only be checked against the licensing record; their actual current state was not observed.

## Wrapping up

- **Registered lodging in Seochon is 2 yeogwan with 39 rooms**, and **no hotels at all**.
- **Yet 33 of Seoul's 133 hanok stays are here.** It is the densest concentration in the city.
- **The two figures cannot be added, because the governing laws differ.**
- **Neither a private bathroom nor sleeping on the floor is a legal requirement.** Some hanok have beds.
- **A fire extinguisher and standalone smoke alarms are compulsory**, plus a CO alarm where heating is individual.
- **The airport bus is route 6011**, opposite exit 1 of Gyeongbokgung Station, last bus 20:48.
- **If you need a hotel, go to Gwanghwamun.**
