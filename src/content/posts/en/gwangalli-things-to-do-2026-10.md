Gwangalli (광안리) is a smaller beach than Haeundae, but it is the one that faces **Gwangan Bridge** head-on, and that changes everything after dark. Add a **free drone show every Saturday** and Gwangalli becomes the evening slot in almost every Busan itinerary. For autumn, note that from October the **drone show moves to 19:00 and 21:00**, an hour earlier than in summer, and on the first and third Saturdays a laser show now wraps around it. This post covers what to see and do; where to eat is in the [Gwangalli food guide](/en/posts/gwangalli-food-2026-10/). Hours and prices are as of October 2026, from official sites and Korea Tourism Organization listings.

![Gwangalli Beach at dusk, the curved sand and sea on the left and the beachfront road with its shops and traffic lighting up on the right](/images/gwangalli-things-to-do-2026-10/02.webp)

The sand runs about **1.4 km** from the Namcheon-dong end (the SUP Zone) to the Millak-dong end (the waterside park). From Gwangan Station on [[line:busan2]] it is about 13 minutes on foot to the middle of the beach; Geumnyeonsan Station on the same line is closer to the Namcheon-dong end.

## Gwangalli M Drone Light Show: 19:00 and 21:00 from October

Suyeong-gu district office has run this weekly since April 2022. It plays **twice every Saturday**, at **19:00 and 21:00 from October to February** and at 20:00 and 22:00 from March to September, for **about 12 minutes** each time with around 1,100 drones. No tickets, no reserved seats, and it is **free**.

The **October programme** was posted on the official site on 28 September:

- 3 October — Melody of the Forest
- 10 October — October in Busan
- 17 October — Station Between Dimensions
- 24 October — Melody of the Forest
- 31 October — The Legend of Troy

New this autumn: starting with the Chuseok special on 26 September, Suyeong-gu made the **first and third Saturdays of each month a Drone × Laser Show**, with lasers before and after the drones and the bridge lighting matched to that night's music. In October that means 3 and 17 October.

![Gwangalli Beach at night, a purple light installation laid on the sand, crowds along the beach, and the illuminated Gwangan Bridge and Marine City towers across the water](/images/gwangalli-things-to-do-2026-10/01.webp)

**Where to stand.** The official answer is "anywhere on the beach", and it holds: the drones rise over the water in front of the bridge, so every stretch of sand faces them. The middle draws the biggest crowd. A **wheelchair and stroller viewing area** is set up opposite Starbucks Gwangalli (247 Gwanganhaebyeon-ro) from 40 minutes before each show.

**Cancellations.** The official wording is that the show "may be cancelled or delayed, either one day in advance or on the day" for rain, strong wind, typhoons or communication problems. No wind-speed threshold is published, and four Saturdays were cancelled in July and August 2026 alone, so check the notice board on the official site first. Suyeong-gu TV streams each show on YouTube.

## Gwangan Bridge after dark, and where to photograph it

Gwangan Bridge (광안대교) is a **7,420 m** double-deck suspension bridge. It opened temporarily for the 2002 Asian Games and officially in **January 2003**, and after sunset it is lit with colour-changing lighting. It carries cars only, so the whole experience is about where you stand.

![Gwangan Bridge with its two white towers under a pink sunset sky, with the sea in front and tetrapods on a breakwater to the right](/images/gwangalli-things-to-do-2026-10/05.webp)

- **The middle of the beach** gives you the full span head-on. The best light is about 30 minutes after sunset, while the sky is still deep blue.
- **Millak Waterside Park** is the closest point to the bridge's eastern end. The towers fill the frame with the Marine City high-rises stacked behind.
- **Hwangnyeongsan Observatory** (391-39 Hwangnyeongsan-ro, Nam-gu) is always open and free, and looks down on the bridge and Haeundae at once. Public transport up the mountain is poor, so take a taxi, and the car park fills early on weekend evenings.

![Gwangan Bridge towers and cables lit in purple at night, with the high-rise apartments of Marine City glowing behind](/images/gwangalli-things-to-do-2026-10/04.webp)

## Gwangalli by day: SUP and surfing

Suyeong-gu promotes Gwangalli as a **stand-up paddleboard beach**, with a designated **Gwangalli SUP Zone** at the Namcheon-dong end. **Crazy Surfers** (29 Gwangnam-ro 48beon-gil, Namcheon-dong) has operated since 2015; its Korea Tourism Organization listing, updated March 2026, gives **SUP rental at ₩35,000 (about $26)** and a **surf lesson at ₩65,000**. The season is **1 April to 30 November**, 08:30 to 19:00 (to 21:00 in July and August), ages 11 and up, closed December to March.

## Millak Waterside Park: no more drinking on the rocks

This seaside park at the Millak-dong end sits almost under the bridge. For years it was known as the place where people brought raw fish and soju and sat on the steps all night. Suyeong-gu ended that by making it a **no-drinking zone from 1 July 2023**: no alcohol at any hour, with a **₩50,000 fine**, after years of complaints about rubbish and noise.

![Millak Waterside Park by day, a boat-shaped sculpture covered in tile mosaic, painted tetrapods, and Gwangan Bridge right behind](/images/gwangalli-things-to-do-2026-10/07.webp)

What it is now is **the quietest close-up view of the bridge**. People sit on the stepped concrete to watch the lights come on, or photograph the painted tetrapods with the bridge behind. Always open and free.

## Millac The Market

Under ten minutes on foot from the waterside park, at 56 Millaksubyeon-ro 17beon-gil, this red-brick complex has a wide staircase climbing through its middle. Inside are a **busking square**, a stepped **Ocean View Stand** facing the sea, and a food and drink zone. The stand looks straight at the bridge, which is why many people watch the drone show from here.

![The front of Millac The Market, a wide staircase rising under a steel roof between red-brick walls painted with graffiti](/images/gwangalli-things-to-do-2026-10/09.webp)

Open **10:00 to 24:00 daily** per the Korea Tourism Organization listing (September 2026), free entry. Shops keep their own hours and some are limited-run pop-ups. Parking is **₩4,000 for the first hour, then ₩1,000 per 15 minutes**.

## The beachfront cafes and terraces

Cafes facing the sea line Gwanganhaebyeon-ro the length of the beach, and the point is less the coffee than **the seat**: a window on the second floor or higher, or a terrace, puts the bridge directly in front of you. On Saturdays those seats fill from about an hour before the drone show. At the Millak-dong end, cafes on the upper floors of tall buildings get a closer angle. Specific places are in the [Gwangalli food guide](/en/posts/gwangalli-food-2026-10/).

## Seeing the bridge from the water: yacht tours

The operators are not at Gwangalli but across the bridge at **Suyeongman Yacht Marina** (84 Haeundaehaebyeon-ro, Haeundae-gu). A typical course passes Marine City, Dongbaekseom and Haeundae Beach, goes **under Gwangan Bridge** and along the Gwangalli shore, about an hour in all.

- **The Yacht**: public tours at **₩14,900 weekday daytime, ₩24,900 weekday nights, ₩19,900 weekend daytime, ₩29,900 weekend nights** (children ₩9,900 to ₩14,900), 09:00 to 23:00. Figures from its Korea Tourism Organization listing, April 2026.
- **Yacht-ya**: a catamaran on a 50-minute course. Sunday to Friday **₩12,000** until 18:00 and ₩25,000 from 19:00 to 21:00; Saturdays and holidays ₩35,000 for the 18:30 to 21:30 slots (listing updated August 2026).

The marina has been **under redevelopment since November 2025**, with completion targeted for 2027, so departure arrangements can change. **Confirm the departure point and that tours are running** before you pay. Night tours cost more because the bridge lighting is on.

## Seasonal notes: cherry blossoms, fireworks, New Year

**Namcheon-dong cherry street** runs between the blocks of the Samik Beach apartment complex at the southern end of the beach, usually blooming late March to early April, about 800 m from exit 3 of Geumnyeonsan Station on [[line:busan2]]. The complex is heading into redevelopment, so the street may change within a few years.

The **Busan Fireworks Festival** is on **Saturday 7 November 2026** at Gwangalli. Paid seats, the 18:00 entry rule and free viewing spots are in the [Busan Fireworks Festival guide](/en/posts/busan-fireworks-2026-09/). Check the drone show notices for that date too.

At **year end** a countdown drone show has been held at midnight on 31 December (2,500 drones in 2025). The 2026 details will appear on the official site.

## A Saturday evening route

1. **16:30** Millak Waterside Park for close-up bridge photos, then a rest on the Millac The Market stand
2. **17:30** Walk west along the sand toward the middle of the beach for sunset; in October it sets early
3. **18:15** Take an upstairs window seat or a spot on the sand. On the first and third Saturdays the laser show starts first
4. **19:00** First drone show, then dinner
5. **21:00** Second show from the sand, or a night yacht tour from Suyeongman

## Practical notes

- **From Gimhae Airport**: light rail [[line:gimhaelrt]] to Sasang, change to [[line:busan2]], get off at Gwangan. Under an hour.
- **From Busan Station**: [[line:busan1]] to Seomyeon, change to [[line:busan2]] for Gwangan, about 40 minutes.
- **Exits**: from Gwangan Station exits 3 and 5, walk straight toward the sea. Geumnyeonsan suits the Namcheon-dong end; Gwangan suits the middle and the Millak end.
- **The drone show has an English site** (gwangallimdrone.co.kr/en), and cancellation notices are posted in English too.
- **Cards** work at Millac The Market and almost every cafe. Beach stalls are easier with cash.

## Wrapping up

A paddle in the afternoon, the waterside park and Millac The Market at golden hour, and the 19:00 drone show from the sand make a full Gwangalli day, and only the SUP and the yacht cost money. If you are staying for 7 November, read the [Busan Fireworks Festival guide](/en/posts/busan-fireworks-2026-09/); for dinner, the [Gwangalli food guide](/en/posts/gwangalli-food-2026-10/); for the rest of the city, [Things to Do in Busan](/en/posts/busan-things-to-do-2026-09/).
