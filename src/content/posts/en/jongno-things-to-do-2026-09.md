Jongno's palaces reward **picking the day before you pick the palace**. Changdeokgung and Changgyeonggung close on Mondays, Gyeongbokgung and Jongmyo on Tuesdays, and Jongmyo lets you in differently depending on the weekday. This autumn the calendar shifts again: **from 7 October, Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Jongmyo are free every Wednesday**, and **from 29 October to 15 November all four main palaces and Jongmyo are free**. One thing never changes. **The Changdeokgung Secret Garden is never free, and its six-day-ahead booking race still applies.**

History and transport for the area are in our [Jongno guide](/en/posts/jongno-guide-2026-09/); this page is only about **what to see, when, and how**. Hours and prices are **as of 2 October 2026**, from the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center (the government body that runs the palaces) and each venue's own site.

- **When a regular closing day falls on a public holiday, the site opens and closes on the next working day instead.** So everything opens on Monday 5 October (a substitute holiday) and **every palace and Jongmyo is closed on Tuesday 6 October**. Unhyeongung also closes that day.
- **The combined palace pass costs ₩6,000** and gives one entry each to Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Deoksugung, Changgyeonggung and Jongmyo within six months. It is sold at those ticket offices, **does not include the Secret Garden**, and cannot be refunded once any part is used.
- **Foreign visitors aged 18 and under or 65 and over get in free**, hanbok or not, with ID. The age limit for Korean nationals is different (24 and under), so do not go by Korean-language price lists.
- Gyeongbokgung plus Changdeokgung plus any one more site already costs more than the pass. If your dates fall in the free periods below, you may not need it at all.

## October–November: free days and changed rules

This combines the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center notice of 30 September with its May notice on expanding "Culture Day".

| Date | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Sat 3 Oct (National Foundation Day) | Normal opening |
| Mon 5 Oct (substitute holiday) | Changdeokgung and Changgyeonggung **open as normal** |
| Tue 6 Oct | **All palaces, Jongmyo and royal tombs closed** |
| 7–11 Oct (Royal Culture Festival) | Normal paid opening. **Jongmyo switches to free walk-in every day** |
| Every Wednesday from 7 Oct | **Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung, Jongmyo free** (Culture Day; Secret Garden excluded) |
| Fri 9 Oct (Hangeul Day) | Normal opening |
| 29 Oct–15 Nov (Korea Grand Festival) | **Four palaces and Jongmyo free**, Jongmyo walk-in. **Secret Garden and closing days excluded** |
| 10–15 Nov | Changgyeonggung **evening entry suspended** (17:30–21:00); daytime last entry 16:30 |

"Culture Day" (munhwa-ga inneun nal, a government scheme of discounts on the last Wednesday of each month) used to mean free palace entry only on **the last Wednesday of the month**. In May 2026 the center announced a phased move to **every Wednesday**: royal tombs from 27 May, Deoksugung from 5 August, and **Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Jongmyo from 7 October**. **Gyeongbokgung is not included yet**; the notice says only that it will follow "taking visitor safety and crowding into account". The Changdeokgung booking page still showed the old "last Wednesday" wording when we checked, so confirm at the ticket office if you are counting on a free Wednesday.

## Changdeokgung: the entrance has moved

![Injeongjeon, the throne hall of Changdeokgung, backlit by the sun, its double roof showing green and red painted eaves above stone steps and a raised terrace](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/43.webp)

According to the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), Changdeokgung was built in **1405** as a secondary palace to Gyeongbokgung. After the Japanese invasions it was **the first palace rebuilt, in 1610**, and served as the de facto main palace for about 270 years until Gyeongbokgung was restored in 1867. Together with Changgyeonggung, with no wall between them, it was known as **Donggwol, the East Palace**.

### Use Geumhomun, not Donhwamun

**The main gate, Donhwamun, is under repair and closed until July 2027.** Newsis reported in November 2024 that the ₩10.5 billion project partly dismantles both storeys, and that **the entrance moved to Geumhomun on the west side**. A JoongAng Ilbo report from September 2026 also has visitors using Geumhomun. If a map app sends you to Donhwamun, keep walking west along the wall.

## How to book the Secret Garden

![A small pavilion at the edge of a pond in the Secret Garden woods, one stone pillar standing in the water, trees around it starting to turn](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/44.webp)

The Secret Garden (Huwon) can **only be visited on a guided session**; there is no free wandering. The center's own price page warns that **in April–May and October–November, both the online tickets and the same-day tickets sell out very quickly.**

### Two ways to get a ticket

| Method | When | Places |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Online | From **10:00 six days before the visit (not counting the visit day)** until the day before, first come first served | 50 per session |
| On site | From **09:00 on the day**, first come first served | 50 per session |

So for a Saturday 17 October session, sales open at **10:00 on Sunday 11 October** (Korea time). Booking is under "Secret Garden reservation" (후원관람예약) for Changdeokgung on the center's website, where you can also check and cancel.

### Foreign-language sessions

| Language | Days | Times |
| --- | --- | --- |
| English | Tue–Sun | 10:30, 11:30, 14:30, 15:30 (no 15:30 in Dec–Feb) |
| Japanese | **Wed, Fri, Sun** | 13:30 |
| Chinese | **Tue, Thu, Sat** | 12:30 |
| Korean | Tue–Sun | on the hour 10:00–15:00, plus 16:00 in Mar–Oct |

**Korean nationals cannot join foreign-language sessions** (up to two with a foreign companion). Japanese and Chinese run once a day, three days a week, so fix your weekday first. The official page notes that entry is limited by language group for each session.

### Money traps

- **You must also buy a palace ticket (₩3,000).** The official wording: "a palace ticket must also be purchased for the Secret Garden". Combined-pass holders and those entitled to free palace entry show their proof instead.
- Ages 7–18 pay ₩2,500. **Over-65s pay the full ₩5,000** for the garden even though the palace is free for them.
- **Only children aged 6 and under, and Korean national-merit and disability card holders, enter the garden free.** **Hanbok, the combined pass, free Wednesdays and the Korea Grand Festival free period do not apply to it.**
- No group discount.

### On the day

- **You meet at the garden entrance, which is at least 15 minutes' walk from Geumhomun**, according to the official guide. Miss your session time and you get **neither entry nor a refund**.
- The tour takes **about 70 minutes**.
- **The Ongnyucheon stream area is closed for conservation** until further notice, so the deepest part of the garden is off the route this autumn.
- **From 7 to 18 October, 10:30–16:30**, the Seonhyangjae library building east of Yeongyeongdang opens as a reading room. Any Secret Garden visitor can sit and read; children 12 and under get a bookmark kit while supplies last.

When the colours peak and other foliage spots are in our [Seoul autumn foliage](/en/posts/seoul-autumn-foliage-2026-09/) guide.

## Changgyeonggung: open until 9 pm all year

![Myeongjeongjeon hall of Changgyeonggung framed by the red pillars of a gateway, at the far end of a wide stone-paved courtyard](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/12.webp)

KTO says Changgyeonggung grew out of Suganggung, a residence built for the retired King Taejong, which King Seongjong enlarged and renamed in **1483** for three queen dowagers. It was the royal family's living quarters.

It is **the most useful Jongno palace for an evening**. The official timetable lists general admission **09:00–21:00 (last entry 20:00) in every month**. Unlike Changdeokgung and Jongmyo, it **stays open after dark all year**. ₩1,000, closed Mondays.

### Less is open at night

At a set time, visitors in the daytime areas are moved into the night zone.

| Period | Move to night zone | Greenhouse interior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sep–Oct | 18:00 | Open |
| Nov | 17:30 | Open |
| Dec–Jan | 17:30 | Closed from 18:00 |

- **Night zone**: Honghwamun, Myeongjeongjeon, Tongmyeongjeon, Chundangji pond and the greenhouse area.
- **Part of Chundangji has been closed 18:00–21:00 since 10 February 2026 for lighting repairs**, "until repaired". That notice dates from March, so follow the signs on site.
- The **Gwollae-gaksa** area has partial restrictions since 22 September 2025 while a stable building is restored.

![Chundangji pond at Changgyeonggung on a clear day, green trees and willows reflected in still water](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/14.webp)

The **Great Greenhouse**, says KTO, was completed in **1909** as Korea's first Western-style greenhouse, glass over a mixed iron and timber frame, and became a registered heritage site in 2004.

### 10–15 November: "Donggwol Dongnak"

**From 10 to 15 November, 17:30–21:00, regular night entry is suspended** for a pre-booked night programme called Donggwol Dongnak; daytime visits end at 17:30 with last entry 16:30. When it debuted in 2025, Newsis described a guided walk with short dramas around Changgyeonggung's halls, built on the story of court painters drawing the famous Donggwoldo map of the palaces. **Details and booking for 2026 are "to be announced"** (enquiries 1522-2295, in Korean).

### Walking on to Changdeokgung and Jongmyo

- **Changdeokgung ↔ Changgyeonggung**: the two connect through Hamyangmun gate, but the crossing has its own cut-off (17:00 in February–May, per the notice) and you cannot cross after it. We could not find the autumn cut-off.
- **Changgyeonggung → Jongmyo**: **on Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays and Culture-Day Wednesdays**, a gate on the Yulgok-ro side of Changgyeonggung and Jongmyo's north gate (Buksinmun) open, so you can cross over Yulgok-ro straight between the two. Last entry through that gate is 17:30 in September–October and 17:00 in November.

## Jongmyo: entry rules change by weekday

![A long shrine building and stone wall at Jongmyo under a blue sky, with a yellow ginkgo and red maple behind](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/21.webp)

Jongmyo is **the royal ancestral shrine** holding the spirit tablets of Joseon and Korean Empire kings and queens. KTO says it was founded on this site in **1395**, following the rule of "ancestral shrine to the left of the palace, altar of land and grain to the right". It has the main hall, **Jeongjeon**, and a separate shrine, **Yeongnyeongjeon**.

### Jeongjeon reopened in April 2025

According to Nocut News (21 April 2025), **Jeongjeon reopened on 20 April 2025 after about five years and ₩20 billion of repairs** begun in 2020. Roughly **70,000 handmade roof tiles** replaced a mix of factory and handmade ones, and the 49 spirit tablets in its 19 chambers were carried back in procession through the city. Jeongjeon is a National Treasure (1985), and Jongmyo became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995.

### This autumn at Jongmyo

- **Jongmyo guard patrol ceremony**: new for this autumn's festival, **9–11 October from 15:30 for about an hour**, around the main gate and Jongmyo Plaza, with guards, officers, flag bearers and a military band in costume based on King Jeongjo's 18th-century palace guard.
- **No ancestral rites at Jongmyo in October**: the center's October rites schedule lists "none" for Jongmyo.
- The great royal rite, **Jongmyo Jerye, is held in May**, so it is not something you will see on an autumn trip.
- Photography may be restricted because it is a place of rites; the hanbok photo rules are in [free palace entry in hanbok](/en/posts/hanbok-palace-2026-09/).

## Unhyeongung: free, and open until 9 pm this autumn

![Norakdang at Unhyeongung on a clear day, an L-shaped hanok with a name board and hanging couplets under its eaves, a Western-style building and apartments behind](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/53.webp)

Unhyeongung is not a palace but **the home of the regent Heungseon Daewongun's family**, where **King Gojong was born and raised before he took the throne**. KTO says **Noandang and Norakdang were completed in September 1864**, with Irodang added six years later; Norakdang hosted big family events such as 60th birthdays, and Noandang was the regent's own quarters.

- **Free admission** (official site).
- Usual hours are 09:00–19:00 in April–October and 09:00–18:00 in November–March, but **from 1 September to 29 November a trial late opening runs Tue, Wed and Fri–Sun 09:00–21:00 (last entry 20:30)**. Thursdays close at 18:00; **closed Mondays** (open if a holiday).
- Open on 3, 5 and 9 October; **temporarily closed Tuesday 6 October**.
- A 30 September notice says two evening events, "Unhyeon Byeolbam" and the November moonlight tea room, **have been cancelled**.

It is close to [[line:3]] Anguk Station, one street from the north end of Insadong.

## Insadong: Ssamzigil and the Seoul Museum of Craft Art

Insadong-gil runs from Anguk Station south to Jong-ro. Shops and teahouses are in our [Jongno food guide](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/); here are two places worth going inside.

### Ssamzigil

KTO describes **Ssamzigil** (44 Insadong-gil) as a crafts mall of traditional and contemporary work: ceramics, textiles, metal and wood shops, young designers' stores, and **one-day classes** in pottery, mother-of-pearl, leather, glass and soap. **10:30–20:30, open daily**, no parking.

### Seoul Museum of Craft Art

Opened in **July 2021** in five converted buildings of the former Pungmun Girls' High School, it is **Korea's first public craft museum** (KTO). The museum notes that the site was once the Andong Byeolgung royal residence.

- **Free** (some hired-in exhibitions may charge); just walk in.
- **10:00–18:00**, last entry 30 minutes before closing.
- **Late opening from 15 September to 30 November**: some buildings stay open to 21:00 on certain days. Times differ by building, so check the official timetable.
- **Closed Mondays** (open if a public holiday) and 1 January. The children's museum needs online booking.
- **Exhibition Building 3, 3rd floor is closed** per an 18 September notice.
- **60 m from [[line:3]] Anguk Station exit 1.** No visitor car park.

## Jogyesa: a head temple in the middle of downtown

![The courtyard in front of Jogyesa's main hall, rows of colourful lanterns hanging overhead and a smiling stone child-monk figure on the right](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/66.webp)

Jogyesa is **the head temple of the Jogye Order**. KTO says its predecessor, Gakhwangsa, was founded here in **1910**, and the seven-storey stone pagoda in front of the main hall dates from **1930**. It is **always open, every day**, which makes it an easy early-morning or evening stop.

KTO notes that the courtyard fills with lanterns in spring, lotuses in summer and **chrysanthemums in autumn**. Buddhist press reports show a **chrysanthemum exhibition** running from around October into early November in past years; **we could not find an official 2026 opening date**. The lanterns in the photo above are the Buddha's Birthday decorations from spring.

Keep your voice down inside the main hall during services, and do not photograph people bowing from directly in front.

## Tapgol Park: where 1 March 1919 began

![The octagonal pavilion on a stone platform at the centre of Tapgol Park, trees around it and an open paved square in front](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/78.webp)

KTO says Tapgol Park was laid out in **1897** on the site of Wongaksa temple and opened in 1920 as **Seoul's first modern public park**. On **1 March 1919 the Declaration of Independence was first read aloud here**, starting the March First Movement against Japanese rule. It was renamed from "Pagoda Park" in 1992.

- To see: the octagonal pavilion, the **ten-storey Wongaksa pagoda (National Treasure)**, the Wongaksa stele, the March First monument and relief murals, and statues and memorials of independence leaders.
- **Mar–Oct 09:00–18:00, Nov–Feb 09:00–17:00**, open daily, free, no parking.
- On the main road at Jongno 2-ga, a walk from [[line:1]] Jonggak or Jongno 3-ga.

## Daehangno: Seoul's theatre district

### Marronnier Park

KTO says **Marronnier Park** (104 Daehak-ro) sits on the former Seoul National University arts and law campus; its namesake horse-chestnut trees were planted on **5 April 1929**. Its open-air stage hosts frequent performances and events, and it is always open. Nearest is [[line:4]] Hyehwa.

### October festivals

- **Seoul Performing Arts Festival (SPAF)**: **8–25 October**, 20 Korean and international works at the National Theater of Korea, the Arko Arts Theater and Daehangno Arts Theater, Theater Quad and others (Economic Review, 1 October 2026).
- **PAMS (Performing Arts Market in Seoul)**: 13–16 October, mostly showcases and business meetings.
- A government campaign is handing out **performance discount coupons** until 30 November through Naver Reservation, NOL, Yes24, Timeticket and Ticketlink. We could not confirm the discount size or whether foreign visitors can use them.

Most small-theatre plays in Daehangno are **in Korean**. Check whether a show is non-verbal or music-led before you book.

### Walking further

- The **Naksan Park and Ihwa village** hillside east of Hyehwa, and the city-wall walk, are covered from the Heunginjimun end in [Dongdaemun things to do](/en/posts/dongdaemun-things-to-do-2026-10/). Ihwa and Jangsu villages are residential, and visitors are asked to keep noise down.
- The **ginkgo at Myeongnyundang, Sungkyunkwan** (a natural monument) west of Hyehwa is in [Seoul autumn foliage](/en/posts/seoul-autumn-foliage-2026-09/).
