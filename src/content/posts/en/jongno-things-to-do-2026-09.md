Four of Seoul's five royal palaces, a UNESCO shrine and the best-preserved hanok neighborhood in the country are all within a twenty-minute walk of each other in Jongno. Prices and hours below are as of September 2026, from the Korea Heritage Service.

## Tickets: buy the combined pass

| Site | Adult |
|---|---|
| Gyeongbokgung | ₩3,000 |
| Changdeokgung (palace) | ₩3,000 |
| Changdeokgung Secret Garden | ₩5,000 extra |
| Changgyeonggung | ₩1,000 |
| Deoksugung | ₩1,000 |
| Jongmyo Shrine | ₩1,000 |
| **Combined ticket** | **₩6,000, valid 6 months** |

The **combined ticket** covers four palaces plus Jongmyo but **not** the Secret Garden. It pays for itself at three sites.

**Anyone wearing hanbok enters the palaces free**, foreign visitors included. To qualify the outfit needs a proper wrap-front jeogori top and a skirt or trousers. Rental shops cluster around Gyeongbokgung and Anguk Station at roughly ₩15,000–30,000 for four hours, so two palaces in hanbok costs about the same as paying admission — and it is the reason half the visitors in the photos are dressed for 1450.

## Gyeongbokgung

![The main courtyard and throne hall of Gyeongbokgung Palace](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

The largest and most impressive of the palaces, rebuilt on the site of the 1395 original, with Bugaksan mountain behind it.

**Hours** change by season: 09:00–17:00 in January, February, November and December; 09:00–18:00 in March–May and September–October; 09:00–18:30 in June–August. Last entry is an hour before closing. **Closed Tuesdays.**

Inside the grounds are the **National Palace Museum** and the **National Folk Museum**, both free and both good rainy-day options. The **changing of the guard** ceremony runs at Gwanghwamun gate several times a day and costs nothing to watch.

Go at 09:00. The first hour is genuinely quiet; by 11:00 it is not.

## Changdeokgung and the Secret Garden

![A pond reflecting trees in the Secret Garden of Changdeokgung Palace](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/04.webp)

Changdeokgung is the palace Koreans tend to prefer: smaller, built to follow the shape of the hill rather than flatten it, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Its **Huwon, the Secret Garden**, is the highlight, and it is **not open for free wandering**. Entry is by **timed guided tour only**, with a separate ₩5,000 ticket on top of the ₩3,000 palace entry. Slots sell out, especially in autumn.

**Book online in advance** through the Korea Heritage Service site. A limited number of same-day tickets are sold on site, first come first served, and they go early. The tour takes about 90 minutes and runs in Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese at different times.

## Jongmyo Shrine

![The long main hall of Jongmyo Shrine](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

The royal ancestral shrine, UNESCO-listed, and the quietest major site in the city. The main hall is a single unadorned building over a hundred metres long, and the effect is closer to a cathedral than a palace.

**₩1,000**, closed Tuesdays. Most days admission is by **guided tour at set times** rather than free walking; certain days, usually Saturdays, allow self-guided visits. Check the official site the week you go.

## Bukchon Hanok Village

![Gyeongbokgung Palace buildings against the mountains](/images/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/02.webp)

Several hundred traditional hanok houses on the hill between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung, most of them lived in.

**The rule you must know**: since 1 March 2025, the special management zone around **Bukchon-ro 11-gil** admits tourists **only between 10:00 and 17:00**, with a **₩100,000 fine** outside those hours. It applies even to guests of accommodation in the zone if they are sightseeing. Charter buses are banned on the surrounding roads from January 2026.

Beyond the rule, ordinary courtesy: speak quietly, do not enter gateways, do not photograph through windows, and do not sit on doorsteps. Residents have complained for years and the restrictions are the result.

The **Bukchon Culture Center** and the viewpoint on Bukchon-ro 11-gil are the two things most people come for. Samcheong-dong, just west, has the same architecture with no restrictions and better cafés.

## Insadong

One pedestrian street plus a web of alleys, selling ceramics, calligraphy brushes, tea, paper and a lot of souvenirs. **Ssamziegil** is a spiral shopping building worth ten minutes. Traditional tea houses down the side alleys serve omija and citron tea in ceramic cups.

It is touristy and prices reflect that, but the craft shops at the northern end are real and the galleries are free.

## A two-day route

**Day one**: Gyeongbokgung at 09:00 → National Folk Museum → lunch in Samcheong-dong → Bukchon between 10:00 and 17:00 → Insadong tea → dinner at Gwangjang Market.

**Day two**: Changdeokgung with a booked Secret Garden slot → Jongmyo next door → Ikseon-dong cafés → evening in the Jongno 3-ga alleys.

## Practical notes

- **Tuesdays**: Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung and Jongmyo all close. Deoksugung closes Mondays instead, so it is the Tuesday alternative.
- **Book the Secret Garden** before you fly if your dates are fixed.
- **Free English guided tours** run at the main palaces several times daily; check the board inside the gate.
- **Shoes matter.** Gravel courtyards, steep lanes, and you will walk 12,000 steps without noticing.
- **Toilets** inside palace grounds are free and clean.

## Wrapping up

Two palaces, one shrine and one hanok neighborhood is a full and satisfying two days. Buy the ₩6,000 combined ticket, book the Secret Garden ahead, go at opening time, and treat Bukchon as somebody's street rather than a set.
