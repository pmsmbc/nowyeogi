Almost everything worth seeing in Jeonju is inside the Hanok Village or a short walk from it, and almost all of it is free. The exception is the one site that gives the city its historical reason for existing.

Hours and prices are as of September 2026 and come from official listings.

## Gyeonggijeon

**Gyeonggijeon (경기전)** is why Jeonju matters historically. The Jeonju Yi clan founded the Joseon dynasty, and this walled compound holds the official portrait of **King Taejo**, its first king. It is the only surviving shrine of its kind outside Seoul.

**₩3,000** for adults, **₩2,000** for youth and military, **₩1,000** for children. Jeonju residents pay a third of that.

Hours change with the season: **09:00 to 19:00** in spring and autumn, **to 20:00** in June, July and August, and **to 18:00** from November to February.

Two ways in for free. It is **free on the last Wednesday of each month**, and free for visitors 65 and over or under 6. On the **fourth Saturday of the month**, anyone in hanbok pays half price.

Allow an hour. The grounds include a bamboo grove and the annex housing the dynastic annals.

## Jeondong Catholic Cathedral

![The red brick facade and domed bell tower of Jeondong Catholic Cathedral](/images/jeonju-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

**Jeondong Catholic Cathedral (전동성당)** stands directly across the road from Gyeonggijeon, which makes the pair one of the more striking juxtapositions in Korea: a Joseon royal shrine facing a Romanesque red brick church.

It was built in the early twentieth century on the site where Korean Catholics were martyred in the 1790s. **Free**, open **09:00 to 17:00** outside Mass times.

The two sites together take about ninety minutes and they are the core of any Jeonju visit.

## Omokdae

![The Omokdae pavilion on the wooded ridge above Jeonju Hanok Village](/images/jeonju-things-to-do-2026-09/07.webp)

**Omokdae (오목대)** is a pavilion on the wooded ridge at the eastern edge of the village, and **Imokdae (이목대)** sits just beyond it. **Free**, with no set hours.

The reason to climb is the view. From the ridge you see the full sweep of tiled roofs with the modern city rising behind, which is the image that sells Jeonju. Go at golden hour.

The climb is a set of stairs from the village and takes about ten minutes.

## Jeonju Hyanggyo

![The tiled halls and courtyard of Jeonju Hyanggyo, the Confucian school, among old trees](/images/jeonju-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

**Jeonju Hyanggyo (전주향교)** is the old Confucian school, a quiet compound of halls and courtyards at the southern end of the village. **Free**, open **09:00 to 18:00 from March to October** and **10:00 to 17:00 in winter**, closed Mondays.

Official listings disagree slightly on the hours, so leave yourself margin rather than arriving at the last minute.

The specific reason to come is the **ginkgo trees**, which peak in **November** and turn the courtyard completely yellow. It is the most photographed spot in the city that month, and the compound is small, so go early.

## Pungnammun and the murals

**Pungnammun (풍남문)** is the surviving south gate of the old city wall, free and visible at any hour, standing on a traffic island between the village and the market.

**Jaman Mural Village (자만벽화마을)** climbs the hillside near Omokdae, its houses painted with murals. There is no gate, no ticket and no hours, because it is a residential alley. People live there, so keep the noise down.

## Nambu Market

**Nambu Market (전주남부시장)** sits just past Pungnammun, and it is three markets in one.

The **night market runs every Friday and Saturday from 17:00 to 23:00, all year**. Stalls fill the central aisle with street food and it is comfortably the best evening plan in Jeonju. If your dates include a Friday or Saturday, build the trip around it.

A pre-dawn **Dokkaebi market** trades in the early morning, and the regular market runs through the day. The **Cheongnyeonmol**, a youth-run floor of small shops and cafés, is in the same building.

## Hanji

Jeonju is the traditional centre of **hanji**, Korean mulberry paper, and you can either look at it or make it.

**The Jeonju Hanji Museum** in Deokjin-gu is **free**, open **Tuesday to Saturday 09:00 to 17:00** with last entry at 16:30, closed Sundays, Mondays, 1 January and public holidays. It is outside the village, so plan it as its own trip.

Inside the village, several workshops run paper-making sessions. Prices start around **₩7,000** at the traditional hanji centre and the Hanbyeok cultural centre, and from **₩5,000** at a craft studio on Eunhaeng-ro. The Hanbyeok centre closes Mondays.

## Deokjin Park

**Deokjin Park (덕진공원)** in the north of the city is **free and open 24 hours**. Its draw is a large lotus pond crossed by a wooden bridge, at its best in July and August when the lotus flowers open. Out of season it is a pleasant walk rather than a destination.

## What it costs

A full two days of sightseeing in Jeonju costs about **₩3,000 a person** in admission, because Gyeonggijeon is the only site that charges. Add a workshop if you want to make paper, and the night market if your dates allow.

## Practical notes

- **Gyeonggijeon closes earliest in winter**, at 18:00. Do it before the light goes.
- **The Hyanggyo closes Mondays** and so does one of the paper workshops.
- **The night market is Friday and Saturday only.** Nothing replaces it midweek.
- **Hanbok rental** is widely available in the village and halves the Gyeonggijeon ticket on the fourth Saturday.
- **The mural village and the Hanok Village are people's homes.** Treat courtyards as private.

## Wrapping up

Gyeonggijeon and the cathedral in the afternoon, Omokdae at sunset, the night market after dark if it is the right day. That is Jeonju's core, and it costs three thousand won.
