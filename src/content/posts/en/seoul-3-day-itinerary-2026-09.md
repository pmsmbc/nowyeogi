Most three-day Seoul itineraries you find online are written by people who have never tried to walk them. This one is built around three geographic clusters, so you spend your time seeing things rather than sitting on the subway. Each day is one area plus one evening, and none of them requires more than about 25 minutes of travel at a stretch.

Hours and prices are as of September 2026. Sources are linked at the end.

## Before you start

- **Get a transit card on arrival.** A T-money card costs ₩3,000–5,000 at any convenience store, or buy a Climate Card pass if you expect four or more rides a day. See our [subway and T-money guide](/en/posts/seoul-subway-tmoney-2026-09/).
- **Stay in Myeongdong or Jongno** for this itinerary. Both put you within walking distance of Day 1 or Day 2. Our [neighborhood comparison](/en/posts/seoul-where-to-stay-2026-09/) covers the alternatives.
- **Avoid Tuesday for Day 1.** Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung and Jongmyo all close on Tuesdays.
- **Download Naver Map or KakaoMap.** Google Maps does not do walking directions properly in Korea.
- **Budget** roughly ₩70,000–120,000 per person per day for food, entry and transport, excluding accommodation.

## Day 1 — Palaces and old Seoul (Jongno)

**09:00 · Gyeongbokgung Palace** (₩3,000, closed Tuesdays)
Arrive at opening. The first hour is the only quiet one. Watch the changing of the guard at the main gate, then walk the courtyards to the pavilion at the back.

**10:30 · National Folk Museum** (free)
Inside the palace grounds. Thirty to forty minutes is enough, and it is the best short introduction to how Koreans actually lived.

**11:30 · Samcheong-dong for lunch**
Walk out the east gate. Sujebi, dumplings or a café with a hanok courtyard. Expect ₩10,000–15,000.

**13:00 · Bukchon Hanok Village**
Note the rule: the special zone around Bukchon-ro 11-gil admits tourists **only 10:00–17:00**, with a ₩100,000 fine outside those hours. People live here, so keep your voice down and do not photograph into homes.

**15:00 · Insadong**
Craft shops, Ssamziegil, and a traditional tea house down a side alley. Jogyesa temple is two minutes away and free.

**18:00 · Gwangjang Market for dinner**
Bindaetteok mung-bean pancakes, mayak gimbap and a kettle of makgeolli. About ₩15,000–20,000 a head. Full details in the [Jongno food guide](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/).

**Optional evening**: the drinking alleys around Jongno 3-ga, five minutes away.

Everything today is within a 25-minute walk. Full detail in our [Jongno guide](/en/posts/jongno-guide-2026-09/) and [palaces guide](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Day 2 — Myeongdong, shopping and Namsan

![The Seoul skyline at night seen from Namsan mountain](/images/seoul-3-day-itinerary-2026-09/01.webp)

**09:30 · Myeongdong before the crowds**
The main street is quiet in the morning, which is the best time to shop. Olive Young Myeongdong Town gives an instant tax refund on purchases over ₩15,000 with your passport.

**11:00 · Myeongdong Cathedral** (free, closed to sightseers on Mondays)
Ten minutes, and the only quiet corner in the neighborhood.

**12:00 · Lunch at Myeongdong Kyoja**
Kalguksu noodles, ₩11,000, a Michelin Bib Gourmand listing. The queue moves in fifteen minutes.

**13:30 · Namdaemun Market**
Five minutes west. Cheaper and more chaotic than Myeongdong, with better food alleys.

**15:30 · Optional: National Museum of Korea**
Twenty minutes away on [[line:4]] to Ichon. Free, enormous, and the Room of Quiet Contemplation is worth the trip on its own. Skip it if you would rather shop. See our [museum guide](/en/posts/seoul-national-museum-of-korea-2026-09/).

**17:30 · Namsan and N Seoul Tower**
Take the free outdoor lift beside the Pacific Hotel, then the cable car (about ₩15,000 round trip, 10:00–23:00). Go up before sunset and come down after dark to get both views. The base of the tower is free; the observatory is ₩29,000 and optional.

**20:00 · Street food back in Myeongdong**
The carts run from about 16:00 until late.

More in the [Myeongdong guide](/en/posts/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/) and [what to do and buy](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Day 3 — Hongdae, cafés and the river

![A bridge over the Han River in the evening](/images/seoul-3-day-itinerary-2026-09/03.webp)

**10:00 · Yeonnam-dong**
Take [[line:2]] to Hongik Univ. Station, exit 3. Coffee in one of the small roasteries, then walk the **Gyeongui Line Forest Park**, a two-kilometre green strip built on a buried railway.

**12:00 · Mangwon Market**
Twenty minutes on foot at the far end of the park, or two stops on [[line:6]]. Market prices, fried snacks, dumplings.

**13:30 · Han River**
Ten minutes south to Mangwon Hangang Park. Rent a bike, or just sit. This is what Seoul residents actually do at the weekend.

**16:00 · Hongdae shopping and Sangsangmadang**
Back toward the station for fashion, cosmetics and the culture complex.

**18:30 · Korean barbecue or fried chicken**
Samgyeopsal is ₩15,000–20,000 a portion with a two-portion minimum; a whole chicken with beer is ₩20,000–28,000.

**20:00 · Buskers on the walking street**
Peak time on a Friday or Saturday. Free.

Full detail in the [Hongdae guide](/en/posts/hongdae-guide-2026-09/).

## If you have a fourth day

- **DMZ tour** — a half or full day, booked in advance, and the most distinctive thing you can do from Seoul.
- **Gangnam and COEX** — shopping, the Starfield Library, K-pop.
- **Seongsu** — cafés and pop-up stores in converted factories.
- **A second palace** — Changdeokgung with a booked Secret Garden slot.

## What to skip

- **Trying to see all five palaces.** Two is plenty in three days.
- **Nanta or a jump show** unless you specifically want theatre; the money goes further on food.
- **A hop-on hop-off bus.** The subway is faster, cheaper and covers more.
- **Lotte World** on a short trip unless you have children.
- **Itaewon at lunchtime.** It is an evening neighborhood.

## Adjusting the plan

**Raining?** Swap Day 1's Bukchon for the National Museum of Korea, or Day 3's river for Ikseon-dong's alley cafés and a department store food hall.

**Travelling with kids?** Replace Day 3 with Jamsil: Lotte World, the aquarium and Seoul Sky in one place.

**Second visit?** Replace Day 2 with Seongsu and Hannam, and Day 3 with a day trip.

**Arriving late on Day 1?** Move the palace to Day 2 morning and do Myeongdong shopping on the evening you land.

## Practical notes

- **Palace closures**: Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung and Jongmyo close Tuesdays; Deoksugung closes Mondays.
- **Wear proper shoes.** This itinerary is 12,000–18,000 steps a day.
- **Cash**: rarely needed, but ₩30,000 in small notes helps at markets.
- **Tax refund**: carry your passport while shopping, spend ₩15,000 or more per shop.
- **Last trains** run around midnight; buses and taxis run later.

## Wrapping up

Three days in Seoul works if you treat each day as one neighborhood plus one evening. Palaces on day one, shopping and the mountain on day two, cafés and the river on day three. Everything else is a bonus, and the city rewards a slow walk more than a long list.
