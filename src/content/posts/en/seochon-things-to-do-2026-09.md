Seochon ("west village") is the old neighbourhood **between Gyeongbokgung Palace's west wall and the slopes of Inwangsan**. Its sights are scattered between lanes and mountain, so the question is less where to start than **which day to go**. Most district-run venues close on Mondays; Chilgung shrine and Gyeongbokgung close on Tuesdays. And several things changed in 2026: **Chilgung can only be visited on a booked guided tour since February**, and **Cheong Wa Dae (the Blue House) has been closed to visitors since 1 August 2025**. On the other hand, **Suseongdong Valley, Inwangsan, Sajikdan and the Mumudae viewpoint are open every day, all day.**

The neighbourhood's history, hanok and transport are in our [Seochon guide](/en/posts/seochon-guide-2026-09/). This page is about **what to see, when, and in what order**. All hours and prices are **as of 2 October 2026, from each venue's official information**.

## What this page covers

"Seochon" is a nickname, not an administrative name. Here it means the dongs west of Gyeongbokgung (**Chebu, Pirun, Nuha, Nusang, Ogin, Tongin, Changseong, Hyoja, Gungjeong, Singyo, Cheongun and Sajik-dong**) and Inwangsan's eastern slope behind them.

## Suseongdong Valley: a painted valley dug out from under apartments

![Broad granite slabs and a deep crack in the rock at Suseong-dong under a blue sky, with pine woods and Inwangsan's pale rock peak behind](/images/seochon-things-to-do-2026-09/31.webp)

*Suseong* means "the sound of water". According to KTO, the 18th-century painter Jeong Seon (pen name Gyeomjae) included this spot in his album of eight views around Bugaksan and Inwangsan, and old Joseon geographies list it as a scenic site.

### What was here, and when it came back

From a News1 report of 25 September 2026:

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 1971 | **Nine blocks of the Ogin apartments** built on both banks; parts of the rock and stream covered in concrete |
| 2007 | Seoul decides to demolish them and restore green space |
| Feb 2008 | Site designated as green space; compensation and demolition begin |
| 2010 | Valley and Giringyo bridge designated Seoul Monument No. 31 |
| 2012 | Restoration finished; **opened to the public on 11 July** |
| 11 Jul 2024 | Re-designated as **Seoul Natural Heritage No. 6** under the new heritage system |

The same report says the project cost **₩106 billion** in all (₩100.5 billion of it for buying out land and buildings) and the restored valley is **about 190 m** long.

### Giringyo, the one-piece stone bridge

The stone bridge across the lower valley is **Giringyo**. The heritage designation notes that it appears in Jeong Seon's painting and is "**the longest bridge made of a single stone, the only one inside the old city walls preserved in its original form and place**". There is a viewing spot at the entrance roughly where Jeong Seon is thought to have painted, so you can compare painting and view.

### Before you go

- **It is usually a dry stream** (News1). You hear the water mainly just after rain.
- **Free, always open, no parking** (KTO).
- **Getting there**: from [[line:3]] Gyeongbokgung Station **Exit 2**, take village bus Jongno [[bus:village:09]] to **the last stop**, or walk about 20 minutes through the lanes (News1). Village buses are small local buses; they take the same T-money card as the subway.
- Paths on both sides join the **Inwangsan Jarak-gil** (a gentle trail around the lower slopes), which leads to the bookshop café and the Yun Dong-ju museum.

## Inwangsan and the City Wall

![Stone steps running down beside the Seoul City Wall on Inwangsan, with downtown towers and N Seoul Tower beyond the pines](/images/seochon-things-to-do-2026-09/20.webp)

Behind Seochon rises **Inwangsan (339 m)**, with the **Seoul City Wall** (Hanyangdoseong, the 14th-century wall around the old capital) running along its ridge. The official City Wall site describes the Inwangsan section like this:

- **Donuimun site → Changuimun gate, 4.0 km, about 2 hours 30 minutes**, rated five stars for difficulty
- **Open 24 hours**
- Lots of exposed rock, so take care in winter, and **there are no toilets once you are on the mountain**
- Closed to civilians after the 1968 commando raid on the presidential compound, **opened in 1993**; according to KTO and the Hankyoreh, the remaining guard posts were removed and the mountain was **effectively fully opened in 2018**

### Three ways up from Seochon

| Start | How to get there (City Wall site) | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sajik Park | About 15 minutes' walk from Gyeongbokgung Exit 1, or branch bus [[bus:7025]] / trunk buses [[bus:171]], [[bus:606]], [[bus:706]] to "Sajikdan", then 5 minutes | Combine with Sajikdan and Hwanghakjeong |
| Suseongdong Valley | Village bus Jongno [[bus:village:09]] to the last stop | Also suggested as a way down |
| Yun Dong-ju Poet's Hill (Changuimun) | From the stop by Gyeongbokgung Exit 3, branch buses such as [[bus:1020]] or [[bus:7022]] to "Jahamun Gogae / Yun Dong-ju Poet's Hill", then 2 minutes | Right next to the museum |

The City Wall page lists the Changuimun buses two different ways on the same page; we only give the two numbers that appear in both lists.

### Mumudae Viewpoint and the Forest Rest Area

- **Inwangsan Mumudae Viewpoint** (San 3-1, Ogin-dong) sits **midway along the Jarak-gil** between Sajik Park and Poet's Hill, according to KTO, and is one of Seoul's official night-view spots. It is **always open** and reachable in ordinary trainers.
- **Inwangsan Forest Rest Area** (Cheongun-dong) is a former army post rebuilt as a timber shelter. KTO lists it as **free and closed on Mondays**.

### Taking photos towards Cheong Wa Dae

In October 2025, after the president moved back to Cheong Wa Dae, the Hankyoreh reported that the Capital Defense Command had put up four warning signs on the summit and ridge **banning photos and drones in the direction of the compound and requiring hikers to stay on marked trails**. The unit said it would take them down and reissue signs following the presidential office's policy; the office said it planned the same level of access as in 2017–2022 but had not decided. **We could not confirm which signs stand now**, so follow whatever is posted near the summit and do not fly drones.

## Inwangsan Chosochaekbang: a bookshop in a guard post

![The terrace of Inwangsan's Chosochaekbang bookshop café: people sit at the railing under parasols, with fresh green trees, the city and N Seoul Tower beyond](/images/seochon-things-to-do-2026-09/24.webp)

According to KTO, this bookshop café occupies **a police guard post built after the 1968 raid and used for over 50 years to protect the presidential compound**. Part of the brick wall and the steel door were deliberately kept, and the glass walls look out to N Seoul Tower. Seoul's Media Hub (3 April 2026) says it won a public architecture award in 2020.

- **Daily 08:00–22:00, no closing day** (Media Hub, KTO). KTO gives last orders as 21:30.
- 172 Inwangsan-ro. **No parking.**
- Walk up the Jarak-gil from Suseongdong Valley or over from the Yun Dong-ju museum side. We found no official walking time.

## Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum and Poet's Hill

![Inside the old water tank at the Yun Dong-ju Literature Museum: a narrow path between stained concrete walls leads to a black steel door](/images/seochon-things-to-do-2026-09/33.webp)

Yun Dong-ju (1917–1945) is one of Korea's best-loved poets; he died in a Japanese prison months before liberation. According to KTO, while studying at Yonhi College he **lodged with the novelist Kim Song in Nusang-dong** and often climbed Inwangsan to work on his poems. In **2012** the Jongno Cultural Foundation turned an **abandoned water pumping station and water tanks** on the hillside into this museum. It won a Korean public architecture award in 2012 and the Seoul Architecture Award in 2014.

### The rooms

- **Room 1, "Poet's House"**: his life in photos and facsimile manuscripts
- **Room 2, "Open Well"**: a water tank with its roof removed, now an open courtyard
- **Room 3, "Closed Well"**: a water tank left intact, used for a film screening
- Outside: the **Byeolddeurak** space and the **Poet's Hill** trail

### Hours and rules (Jongno Cultural Foundation)

- **10:00–18:00, last entry 17:30**; the last film in Room 3 starts at **17:15**.
- **Every day 13:30–14:00 the rooms close for cleaning.** Arrive straight after lunch and you may wait half an hour.
- **Free.** Closed Mondays, 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok.
- **No photos in Rooms 1 and 3**; Room 2 only.
- **There is no toilet in the museum**; use the one in Cheongun Park about 100 m away.
- No food, no pets except assistance dogs.
- **In peak months (May, June, September, October) booking is required only for groups and for guided commentary.** Individuals just walk in.

**Getting there**: branch bus from the stop by [[line:3]] Gyeongbokgung Exit 3 to **"Jahamun Gogae / Yun Dong-ju Poet's Hill"** (City Wall site). Over the pass is Buam-dong; if you plan to continue to **Seokpajeong**, a former royal villa now run by the Seoul Museum, note that KTO lists it as closed on Mondays and Tuesdays.

## Cheongun Literature Library

![A small hanok pavilion in the yard of Cheongun Literature Library, with an artificial waterfall over rocks on the left and green woods behind](/images/seochon-things-to-do-2026-09/12.webp)

Just below the museum is **Jongno-gu's first hanok public library** (40 Jahamun-ro 36-gil). The Jongno Cultural Foundation says its roof uses **handmade tiles made the same way as those for the restored Sungnyemun gate**, plus **about 3,000 tiles salvaged from hanok demolished** in the Donuimun redevelopment. Upstairs are hanok rooms and a pavilion by a pond; the reading room is underground. The collection is mostly literature.

- **Tue–Fri 09:00–21:00, weekends and holidays 09:00–19:00**
- **Hanok reading room and pavilion: Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00**
- Closed Mondays, 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok; free
- Borrowing needs membership; visitors can simply sit and read.

## Chilgung: booking only since February 2026

Chilgung ("seven shrines") honours **seven royal concubines who bore kings but never became queens**. According to KTO, King Yeongjo first built a shrine for his own mother, Lady Choe, and later shrines for other kings' birth mothers were moved here. It sits right beside Cheong Wa Dae's state guest house in Gungjeong-dong.

### What changed

Every visit is led by a guide with a safety officer. KTO's Korean listing still says you can visit without booking; that is out of date.

## Cheong Wa Dae: closed since August 2025

**Visits to Cheong Wa Dae stopped on 1 August 2025**, when the president's office prepared to move back from Yongsan. Edaily (17 September 2026), citing documents submitted to a lawmaker, reported:

- The presidential secretariat plans to **"push to resume viewing sometime next year"** (that is, 2027) after repairing damaged visitor facilities.
- **No date, scope or format has been set.** A partial opening excluding the offices and residence is being discussed.
- The Cheong Wa Dae Foundation that ran visits is being wound up.

The old booking website did not resolve when we checked. **For autumn 2026, plan on not seeing inside.** Walking along the road in front is the most you can do.

## Yeongchumun: Gyeongbokgung's west gate

Yeongchumun is **Gyeongbokgung's west gate**, the one facing Seochon. Dong-A Ilbo (19 June 2026) says vibration from a tram line outside it brought down its stone base in **1926**; it was rebuilt in 1975 but kept closed to the public for 43 years until **2018**. It is also the gate King Gojong slipped out of in February 1896 when he fled to the Russian legation.

From **22 June to 3 July 2026**, the gate was closed while **its 1975 steel doors (6.05 m × 4.8 m) were replaced with traditional wooden doors** (Newsis). We found no notice of the reopening, though the works were scheduled to finish then. If it is shut, walk round to Gwanghwamun.

Gyeongbokgung basics (details in [things to do in Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/)):

- **October 09:00–18:00 (last entry 17:00), November 09:00–17:00 (last entry 16:00), closed Tuesdays**, adults ₩3,000.
- **Free 29 October–15 November** (except closing days). The new every-Wednesday free entry **does not apply to Gyeongbokgung yet**.
- **The raised platform of Geunjeongjeon throne hall is closed 2 September–31 October.**
- The Center posted a notice titled "Gyeongbokgung safety inspection day (4 October)", but its content is an image we could not read. Check it if you plan to go that Sunday.

## Park No-soo Art Museum

A district-run museum in **a house built around 1937 in a mixed Korean–Western style**, where the painter Park No-soo lived for about 40 years (KTO). The ground floor has ondol (underfloor-heated) rooms; there are three fireplaces. It was designated a Seoul cultural heritage material in 1991; the foundation calls it "No. 1".

- **Exhibition "Sansu · Gyeongmul" (Landscape and the investigation of things): 19 September 2025 – 6 December 2026**, with landscapes, furniture, ceramics and media art.
- **Adults (25–64) ₩3,000**, youth (13–24) ₩1,800, children (7–12) ₩1,200.
- **50% off in hanbok** or for Jongno residents.
- **Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00 (last entry 17:30)**, closed Mondays, 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok.
- **Shoes off, and only 15 visitors inside at a time**, to protect the building. Expect a short queue at weekends.

It is on the Ogin-dong lane up to Suseongdong Valley, so the two go together.

## Sangchonjae and Hwanghakjeong

### Sangchonjae

A hanok culture house in Ogin-dong (12-11 Jahamun-ro 17-gil). The foundation and KTO say **Jongno-gu bought a long-abandoned police-owned hanok in 2013**, rebuilt it in late-19th-century style over about a year, and **opened it in June 2017**: three single-storey buildings.

- **Tue–Sun 09:00–18:00, free**, closed Mondays, 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok
- Displays on ondol heating and programmes on hanbok, crafts and the seasonal calendar; groups book ahead.

### Hwanghakjeong archery museum

An archery ground on the Inwangsan slope in Sajik-dong (15-32 Sajik-ro 9-gil). According to the foundation, it was **built in Gyeonghuigung Palace on 10 August 1899 on Emperor Gojong's order and moved here in 1922**; it was designated a Seoul tangible cultural property in 1974, and the foundation calls it Korea's only listed archery pavilion.

- **Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00** (entry until 30 minutes before), **free**, closed Mondays and major holidays
- **Try traditional archery**: Fridays 10:00–14:00 and the last Saturday of each month 10:00–12:00, **age 8 and up, ₩4,000**. Bow-making is suspended.
- Please don't photograph inside the exhibition room. KTO gives 10:00–17:00; we followed the foundation, which runs the place.

## Sajikdan: an altar that never closes

![A red hongsalmun gate at the end of the lawn at Sajikdan, with another red gate, trees and buildings beyond a low stone wall](/images/seochon-things-to-do-2026-09/10.webp)

Jongmyo shrine honours royal ancestors; **Sajikdan was the altar to the gods of earth (sa) and grain (jik)**. KTO explains that Joseon placed the shrine east and the altar west of the palace, which is why "jongmyo-sajik" came to mean the state itself. The centre is **an empty altar rather than a building**, ringed by red *hongsalmun* gates (spiked gates marking sacred ground).

- **Always open, free** (KTO; Royal Palaces and Tombs Center fee table). Handy on Mondays and Tuesdays.
- Newspim (27 November 2024) reported that the heritage service began **restoring the Anhyangcheong area (six buildings) in November 2024, aiming to finish in 2026**. We could not confirm whether it is finished; parts may be screened off.

Behind it, Sajik Park leads onto the City Wall trail, Hwanghakjeong and Mumudae.

## Tongin Market: coin lunchbox hours

KTO says the market began in **June 1941** as a public market for Japanese residents of Hyoja-dong. Shops open roughly 07:00–21:00, but the famous **coin lunchbox** (you buy brass coins, *yeopjeon*, then pay stalls in coins to fill a tray) only runs while the lunchbox café is open. **One coin is ₩500.**

| Source | Weekdays | Weekends | Closed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seoul Media Hub (May 2025) | 11:00–16:00 (coins sold until 15:00) | 11:00–17:00 (coins until 16:00) | **Tuesdays**, third Sunday |
| Review Times (June 2026) | 11:00–16:00 | 11:00–17:00 | **Mondays**, third Sunday |

**The two sources disagree on the weekday closure.** Both say **the third Sunday of each month** is closed (18 October and 15 November), so avoid those; if you're going on a Monday or Tuesday, check the market's Instagram (@tongin_official). How the lunchbox works is in [where to eat in Seochon](/en/posts/seochon-food-2026-09/).

## Hong Geon-ik House and Yi Sang's House

![Hong Geon-ik House in Pirun-dong seen from above: several tiled roofs and a small gate in the yard, surrounded by brick apartment blocks](/images/seochon-things-to-do-2026-09/16.webp)

### Hong Geon-ik House

One of Seoul City's **public hanok**, open to visitors. KTO says tradition linked the site to the Joseon minister Yi Hang-bok, but the present buildings appear to have been **built by Hong Geon-ik in 1934–1936**: five tiled timber buildings, a small gate in the back garden and a round stone well, stepped down a narrow slope.

- **Tue–Thu and weekends 10:00–18:00, Fri 12:00–20:00, closed Mondays and public holidays** (KTO)
- Yonhap (1 July 2026) says it was the only public hanok offering wedding-photo shoots in the first half of 2026, and runs a craft programme in the second half.
- It is listed among the venues for **Seoul Hanok Week (2–11 October)**, with craft, flower, tea and traditional-liquor sessions (Yonhap).

### Yi Sang's House

At 154-10 Tongin-dong is **part of the plot where the modernist poet Yi Sang (1910–1937) lived for nearly 20 years after being adopted by his uncle at about three** (Seoul Future Heritage; OhmyNews). The original 300-pyeong house is gone; the National Trust for Cultural Heritage **bought this piece in 2009** and runs a small memorial space with a bust, portrait and an archive of his works.

- **Seoul Future Heritage 2013-215.** It was once a registered cultural property but that **listing was lifted on 23 June 2008**.
- We could not confirm its opening hours.

## Tongui-dong: Daelim Museum and Boan1942

- **Daelim Museum** (21 Jahamun-ro 4-gil) is a private museum founded by DL Group (formerly Daelim). KTO lists it as **closed Mondays, Chuseok and 1 January**. Its Petra Collins show (from August 2025) closed in February 2026, and **we could not confirm what, if anything, is on in October.** Check before you go.
- **Boan1942** uses the old Boan Inn building as exhibition space. Its show "Us and Them" ran 18 August – 27 September; we found nothing on the October programme.

Both are on the way from Gyeongbokgung Station to Yeongchumun, so pair them with the palace.
