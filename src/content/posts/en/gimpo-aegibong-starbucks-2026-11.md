There is a corner of Gimpo, just west of Seoul, where you can hold a Starbucks cup and look across the water into North Korea. The Jogang Observatory at **Aegibong Peace Ecopark** (애기봉평화생태공원) stands 1.4 km from North Korea's Kaepung County, and a Starbucks opened in the same building on November 29, 2024. The park lies inside the Civilian Control Zone, a military-managed strip south of the DMZ, so you cannot simply walk in. You need a ticket and photo ID.

![View across the river to North Korea's Kaepung County from behind an observatory viewer](/images/gimpo-aegibong-starbucks-2026-11/05.webp)

## Jogang Observatory: looking into Kaepung County

The Jogang is the stretch of water where the Han and Imjin rivers meet and flow to the Yellow Sea. The observatory sits 154 m above sea level on Hill 154, a battlefield of the Korean War. An older observatory from 1978 was torn down and replaced by the current buildings, designed by the Korean architect Seung H-Sang.

According to press reports, on a clear day you can make out houses in Kaepung and the ridgeline of Songaksan with the naked eye, while a telescope brings low buildings and farmland into sharper focus. A row of viewers lines the railing, and visitor reviews describe using them for free. Haze or cloud cuts visibility sharply, so check the forecast first.

![Row of binocular viewers along the observatory railing](/images/gimpo-aegibong-starbucks-2026-11/06.webp)

Inside the observatory, the 275-seat Peace Education Hall runs a continuous film explaining the view across the river. Photographing military facilities is prohibited under Korea's military installations law, and so are drones.

## Starbucks Gimpo Aegibong Ecopark

The park's official notice lists store hours of **10:00–16:30**, later on special event days, which is a short day by Starbucks standards. Even if all you want is a coffee, you must buy a park ticket to get there.

Reports from 2025 put the store at around 123,000 customers in its first seven months, with about 1,000 visitors a day on weekends. Reviews also mention that the store is small. On a weekend, plan on taking your drink out to the observatory rather than counting on a window seat.

## From the exhibition hall to the observatory

The park runs in a line: ticket office, Peace and Ecology Exhibition Hall, ecological trail, Jogang Observatory. A shuttle covers the 10 minutes from the ticket office to the exhibition hall between 10:00 and 17:00. The official site allows about two hours for the whole visit.

The trail has five themed gardens, a 112 m suspension bridge and the Sky Forest Garden, a gently zigzagging path that climbs to the observatory.

![Aegibong suspension bridge over the forest with a floral arch](/images/gimpo-aegibong-starbucks-2026-11/02.webp)

The exhibition hall includes a 94-seat theater and a 13-seat VR ride on a virtual train to Kaesong (adult ₩3,000). Guided commentary runs four times a day, at 10:30, 11:30, 14:00 and 15:00. The Korean sessions are the regular ones, and English guide times are posted separately on the park's notice board. Near the observatory stands the Peace Bell, cast partly from shell casings recovered at Korean War remains excavation sites.

![Gold bell tower and bronze bell on the plaza by the observatory](/images/gimpo-aegibong-starbucks-2026-11/04.webp)

## Booking and entry

Entry works in one-hour sessions, from Session 1 (09:30–10:30) to Session 7 (15:30–16:30).

1. Book on the official site with each visitor's details. You get a confirmation text.
2. At the ticket office, staff check the text and **ID for every visitor**, then issue tickets. Tour operators tell foreign visitors to bring their passports.
3. Show your ticket at the military checkpoint on the way in and on the way out.

A few rules matter here:

- Arriving more than 30 minutes after your slot counts as a no-show.
- Walk-up tickets are only issued from no-shows and cancellations, so a weekend visit without a booking may not work out.
- Walk-ups also have to fill in an entry form, which takes longer.
- You can cancel up to 30 minutes before entry opens. Partial cancellations are not possible.
- Military training or bad weather can close the park without notice. Tickets are fully refunded in that case.

If Starbucks is part of the plan, book Session 5 (13:30–14:30) at the latest. Allowing for the exhibition hall and the walk, the final session leaves very little time before the store closes.

## Practical notes

- **Prices** (as of writing): adult ₩3,000 (about $2.25), ages 13–18 and military or police ₩2,000, age 12 and under ₩1,000. Cards only, no cash
- **Free entry**: Koreans aged 65 and over (the senior exemption does not apply to foreigners) and children under 6 with a guardian
- **By car**: the official directions take Route 48 toward Gimpo and Ganghwa, then Local Road 56 toward Haseong from Gunha-ri, turning left at Gaegok-ri. Reviews say parking is free, and that on busy days cars could not go up to the observatory and drivers had to take the shuttle
- **By public transport**: take [[line:gimpogold]] to Sau or Unyang Station, then [[bus:green:7]] to the Aegibong stop. On weekends there are also [[bus:green:3-2]] from Unyang and [[bus:green:7-2]] from Gurae. From Seoul, the official site lists [[bus:9501]] from Nonhyeon Station to Sau Station, where you change. Reviews warn that buses are infrequent, so check times first
- **Not allowed**: pets, bicycles and scooters, cooking, smoking
