Leeum is the private museum founded by Samsung, and it shows some of Korea's most important ceramics, paintings and Buddhist art. Since October 2021 its traditional art galleries have been free to everyone. The short version: **if you only want the permanent collection, book a free slot and walk in**; if you also want the autumn exhibitions, grab a ticket when the 14-day booking window opens. Everything below follows the museum's official visitor page as of October 2026.

![Wooden deck walkway leading to the Leeum entrance, with a glass wall covered in white lettering arranged into floral patterns and the Leeum sign beyond](/images/leeum-museum-of-art-2026-10/08.webp)

## What Leeum is

Leeum opened in 2004 on a hillside in Hannam-dong (한남동), a quiet residential neighbourhood next to Itaewon. It has two main buildings: **MUSEUM 1 (M1)** for traditional Korean art and **MUSEUM 2 (M2)** for modern and contemporary art from Korea and abroad. According to the Korea Tourism Organization, three architects designed the complex on one site: Mario Botta, Jean Nouvel and Rem Koolhaas. M1 is Botta's building. Many visitors come for the architecture as much as the art.

## The free traditional art galleries (M1)

The Seoul city government's guide describes the route: take the lift to the fourth floor, then walk down to the first. On the way you pass Goryeo celadon, buncheong ware (a grey-glazed stoneware from early Joseon), Joseon white porcelain, calligraphy and paintings by Jeong Seon, Kim Hong-do and Kim Jeong-hui, and Buddhist sculpture and metalwork.

One thing to know before you go: some famous pieces, such as Jeong Seon's *Inwangjesaekdo*, were donated by the late Samsung chairman Lee Kun-hee to the National Museum of Korea and other public collections, so they are no longer at Leeum.

The outdoor **Gabriel Orozco Garden** (open since 3 April 2026) is free too. The outdoor deck keeps the same hours as the galleries, closing at 18:00.

## Booking and house rules

- Online booking opens **14 days before** your visit, with **up to 4 tickets** per person.
- Walk-in tickets are sold, but the museum warns that waits get long on busy days, so it recommends booking.
- You can enter from **20 minutes before** your slot until the next slot begins.
- After paying, press "confirm reservation" to finish. The website sells tickets for both Leeum and its sister museum, Hoam (in Yongin, south of Seoul), so check you are on the Leeum page.
- Bags the size of a backpack or larger go into the lobby lockers. No food in the galleries.
- Photos without flash are allowed, except in some exhibition spaces and for works marked "no photography". Tripods and selfie sticks are not allowed.

There are no docent tours at the moment. Instead, a **free digital guide** plays commentary automatically when you stand in front of a work. It speaks Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese. Anyone of primary school age or older can borrow one by leaving a passport or ID card. Strollers and wheelchairs are also lent free at the information desk.

## Practical notes

- **Getting there**: From [[line:6]] Hangangjin Station Exit 1, walk about 100 m towards Itaewon, turn right into the first alley, and climb the hill for about five minutes. By bus, get off at Hannam-dong Saemaeul Geumgo stop, served by [[bus:110A]], [[bus:110B]], [[bus:400]] and [[bus:405]].
- **Parking**: The car park is small and entry is refused when it is full. It is for museum visitors only and closes at 18:00. Public transport is the better option.
- **Hours**: Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00–18:00; the ticket desk closes at 17:30. Closed on Mondays, 1 January, and on the day of Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (the autumn harvest holiday).
- **Breaks**: Camel Coffee and the Leeum Store are in the lobby. Drinks and cookies from the café can be eaten only in the lobby and the Black Box lounge.
- **Phone**: +82-2-2014-6900
