Jeju food is not showy. It is mostly pork and seafood, either simmered for a long time or grilled plainly, using what the island produces. Knowing **which dishes are actually local** makes choosing a meal much easier.

Information here is as of September 2026. Prices and hours change, so check before you go.

## Grilled black pork (heukdwaeji)

The dish Jeju is known for. Meat from the island's native black pig is cut thick, grilled over charcoal, and dipped in *meoljeot* — a salty anchovy sauce that is specific to Jeju rather than standard Korean barbecue.

![Two thick cuts of pork on a wire grill over charcoal, with a dipping sauce](/images/jeju-food-2026-09/01.webp)

Belly and shoulder are the usual cuts, and because the slices are thick they take a while to cook. Staff normally grill the meat at your table, so there is no rush. In Jeju City, a cluster of these restaurants sits in the area known as **Black Pork Street** (흑돼지거리) in Ildo 1-dong.

## Gogi-guksu (pork noodle soup)

Wheat noodles in a milky broth from long-simmered pork bones, topped with sliced boiled pork. It started as banquet food and became the island's everyday bowl.

![A bowl of gogi-guksu: wheat noodles in milky pork broth topped with sliced pork, seaweed and spring onion](/images/jeju-food-2026-09/05.webp)

It is cheap, and many shops open early, which makes it a common first lunch or a last meal before a flight.

## Braised and grilled hairtail (galchi)

Hairtail caught in Jeju waters, braised with radish in a sweet-spicy sauce, or simply salted and grilled whole. The braise is the more common restaurant order and goes with rice. The grilled version arrives as a whole silver fish, and the meat is soft enough to lift off the bone with chopsticks.

Prices move with the catch, so this dish swings more by season than anything else on the island. It is also where the distinction between local and imported fish matters most — restaurants that use Jeju-caught hairtail advertise it, and charge for it.

## Seafood stew and abalone

*Haemul ttukbaegi* is a soybean-paste broth simmered in an earthenware pot with abalone, clams and prawns. It arrives still bubbling and is meant to be shared with rice rather than eaten as a single portion.

Abalone also turns up as porridge (*jeonbok-juk*) or cooked into a stone-pot rice. Restaurants run by *haenyeo* — Jeju's women free-divers, whose culture was inscribed on UNESCO's list of intangible cultural heritage in December 2016 — often serve sea urchin soup or raw abalone as well. These places tend to sit right by the water, keep short hours, and close when the day's catch runs out.

## Momguk

Less known to visitors, but a celebration dish for locals. It is the broth left from boiling pork, thickened with *mom* (gulfweed seaweed). It began as a way to waste nothing and became a regional staple. It is unfamiliar on a first taste, but reviews consistently describe it as the dish that best explains Jeju cooking.

## Dongmun Market

The largest permanent market in Jeju City. Raw fish, seafood, black pork, *omegi-tteok* rice cakes and hallabong citrus are all in one place, and night-market stalls set up in the evening.

![The arched entrance sign of Dongmun Market with shoppers walking in](/images/jeju-food-2026-09/02.webp)

Tourism-authority listings give the hours as **8:00am–9:00pm**. A common approach is to buy sliced raw fish and eat it at a nearby table, or take it back to your accommodation.

![Vegetable and fish stalls lining the covered arcade of Dongmun Market](/images/jeju-food-2026-09/03.webp)

## Practical notes

- **Reservations**: well-known black pork restaurants and haenyeo kitchens get long queues. Many take phone bookings — worth asking your accommodation to call.
- **Hours**: restaurants near tourist sites often close for a break in the afternoon (roughly 3–5pm), and noodle and seafood places may shut early once they sell out.
- **Eating alone**: black pork restaurants usually have a two-portion minimum. Solo travellers will have an easier time with noodles or a stew.
- **Budget**: noodles and stews are inexpensive; grilled black pork and hairtail dishes are the pricier end. Prices vary too much between restaurants to quote usefully — check before ordering.
- **Souvenirs**: hallabong citrus, omegi-tteok and tangerine chocolate are the standard picks, available at the market and at the airport.

## Wrapping up

If you have one meal in Jeju, make it gogi-guksu. Two, add grilled black pork. Three days or more, work in braised hairtail and momguk. Chasing famous names matters less here than walking into a busy place near where you are staying.
