Where should you go in mid-November to see Seoul turn gold? On October 30 the Seoul city government published its list of 110 autumn foliage roads (167 km in total) and forecast the downtown peak for **late October to early or mid-November**. Ginkgo makes up 34.7% of Seoul's street trees, so yellow streets are everywhere. These five are the places where the city's list and Korea Heritage Service records name ginkgo as the main attraction. All are free and within walking distance of a subway station.

![Aerial view of yellow ginkgo trees at Seonggyungwan](/images/seoul-ginkgo-spots-2026-11/07.webp)

## 1. Gyeongbokgung's wall roads: Samcheong-dong-gil and Hyoja-ro

The first highlight the city named on its 2026 list was **Samcheong-dong-gil** in Jongno-gu. It follows the east wall of Gyeongbokgung Palace from Dongsipjagak, the old corner watchtower, up to Samcheong Tunnel. The city describes it as a "golden ginkgo road" where you can enjoy palaces and galleries along the way. The National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art and the National Folk Museum of Korea are both on this road, so it pairs well with an exhibition.

![Yellow ginkgo leaves over the tiled eaves of Gyeongbokgung's wall](/images/seoul-ginkgo-spots-2026-11/05.webp)

**Hyoja-ro**, beside the palace's west wall, is another ginkgo road. It runs from Cheong Wa Dae Sarangchae, a visitor center near the former presidential compound, past Yeongchumun Gate to the National Palace Museum. A Seoul city citizen reporter found the trees about 80% turned at the end of October. Walk Samcheong-dong-gil and then Hyoja-ro and you will have covered more than half the palace perimeter. Start at Anguk Station on [[line:3]] and finish at Gyeongbokgung Station for a tidy route.

![Yellow ginkgo rising above Gyeongbokgung's wall](/images/seoul-ginkgo-spots-2026-11/03.webp)

## 2. Deoksugung Stone Wall Road and Jeongdong-gil

Leave City Hall Station ([[line:1]], [[line:2]]) by Exit 1 and follow the stone wall beside Daehanmun, the palace's main gate. This year the city describes **Deoksugung-gil** as colorful foliage set against the palace's old stone wall.

Where the wall ends, **Jeongdong-gil** continues. It runs from Daehanmun past Jeongdong Rotary to the Kyunghyang Shinmun newspaper building. The city put it on its foliage list for its modern-era buildings framed by yellow ginkgo. People often call the whole route the Deoksugung Stone Wall Road, but strictly speaking that name covers only the stretch along the wall.

![Yellow ginkgo trees along Deoksugung's stone wall road](/images/seoul-ginkgo-spots-2026-11/18.webp)

## 3. The Myeongnyundang ginkgo at Seonggyungwan

This stop is about one tree rather than a road. The **Munmyo Ginkgo** in the courtyard of Myeongnyundang, the lecture hall of Seonggyungwan, the Joseon dynasty's royal academy, was designated a Natural Monument in 1962. According to the Korea Heritage Service, it is about 400 years old, 26 m tall and 12.09 m around at chest height. It was probably planted in 1602, when the shrine burned in the Japanese invasions of the 1590s was rebuilt.

![The huge yellow ginkgo in the Myeongnyundang courtyard](/images/seoul-ginkgo-spots-2026-11/08.webp)

It is 749 m from Hyehwa Station ([[line:4]]) Exit 4, and admission is free. Unlike the other four spots it has opening hours: 09:00–17:00 from November to February, 09:00–18:00 from March to October. Come here first, or you may find the gate closed in the late afternoon.

## 4. Seoul Forest Ginkgo Grove

The city's reason for listing Seoul Forest is its "600-tree ginkgo grove". Elsewhere ginkgo stand in single rows along a road, but here some 600 trees grow close together as a real forest.

From Seoul Forest Station on the [[line:suinbundang]], Exits 3, 4 and 5 put you at the park entrance in under five minutes. Enter through **Gate 9** and the grove starts right away. Last year a city article described a "golden carpet" of fallen leaves at the end of November, so even if you miss the leaves on the branches, the leaf-covered paths are worth the trip. The parking lot is nearly full on weekends, so take the subway.

## 5. Wiryeseong-gil at Olympic Park

**Wiryeseong-gil** in Songpa-gu runs through Olympic Park from the Peace Gate to South Gate 2, and the city describes it as a road lined with ginkgo on both sides. It starts right at Mongchontoseong Station ([[line:8]]) Exit 1, and inside the park it joins foliage paths with views of Lotte World Tower.

A citizen reporter who visited on October 30–31 found the trees still a mix of green and yellow. One option is to see the three downtown spots first and come here the following weekend.

## Practical notes

- **Timing**: the city forecasts a late-October to mid-November peak. Colors vary tree by tree even on the same weekend, so check recent photos on the city's foliage site or Smart Seoul Map before you go
- **Cost**: all five are free (as of writing)
- **One-day route**: Myeongnyundang in the morning, then Samcheong-dong-gil, Hyoja-ro and the Deoksugung Stone Wall Road with Jeongdong-gil, all on Lines 3 and 4. Seoul Forest and Wiryeseong-gil are in the east of the city, so give each its own half-day
- **Parking**: hard at the three downtown spots, and Seoul Forest's lot is nearly full on weekends

## Wrapping up

If you only have one day, combine Myeongnyundang with the Gyeongbokgung and Deoksugung wall roads, and add the Seoul Forest grove if you have a spare weekend.
