Dongdaemun is famous for clothes, but its food grew out of the same markets. The dakhanmari alley started as the place where **fabric and Pyeonghwa Market traders ate** (Korea Tourism Organization). The grilled fish alley began around **1979, when restaurants put coal-briquette stoves out on the street** (Seoul Future Heritage). Sindang-dong's tteokbokki alley traces back to **a single shop in 1953**, and the Cyrillic signs in Gwanghui-dong are what remains of **traders who came to buy clothes after Korea and Russia established relations in the early 1990s**.

The short version: **dinner at the dakhanmari and grilled fish alleys east of Jongno 5-ga, snacks and late nights in Sindang-dong, something different in Gwanghui-dong.** Both MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026 Bib Gourmand spots in this area are in Sindang-dong. Every restaurant named here appears in a KTO, Seoul city, MICHELIN or government good-price listing, and anything about flavour is taken from those descriptions.

## What this guide covers

"Dongdaemun" here means **the area around DDP and Dongdaemun History & Culture Park, Gwanghui-dong, Dongdaemun and Pyeonghwa markets, the eastern end of Jongno 5-ga and 6-ga (the dakhanmari and grilled fish alleys), Changsin-dong, Dongmyo (Sungin-dong) and Sindang-dong (the tteokbokki alley and Seoul Jungang Market).** It spans two districts, Jung-gu and Jongno-gu.

The neighbours to the west have their own guides: [Gwangjang Market](/en/posts/gwangjang-market-2026-09/) for bindaetteok and yukhoe, [Euljiro](/en/posts/euljiro-food-2026-09/) for the nogari alley and old naengmyeon houses, and [Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/) for Jongno 3-ga and Insa-dong. This page sticks to what lies east of them.

## The dakhanmari alley: a dish that started as chicken noodles

![A whole chicken with potato and green onion in clear broth in an aluminium pot on a gas burner, with a plate of rice cakes beside it](/images/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/03.webp)

The photo above shows a typical dakhanmari table, not a particular restaurant.

Dakhanmari means "one whole chicken": a young chicken simmered in broth in a wide pot at your table, eaten with a chilli dipping sauce, with noodles added to the broth at the end. According to KTO, the alley was where **traders from the fabric market and Pyeonghwa Market came to eat**. It sits on a one-way lane you reach by turning at the IBK bank building after leaving Dongdaemun Station Exit 8 or 9; the alley's address is listed as 14 Jong-ro 40ga-gil.

KTO says the alley's roots are **dak-kalguksu, chicken noodle soup**. A grandmother once sold noodles with chicken from her own house, and today's restaurants also began with chicken noodles. The habit of **adding kalguksu noodles once the chicken is finished** is the trace of that history. More than half the regulars are still market people, KTO notes, and the alley is popular with Japanese visitors.

### Jinokhwa Halmae Wonjo Dakhanmari

KTO names **Jinokhwa Halmae Wonjo Dakhanmari** (18 Jong-ro 40ga-gil) as **the oldest restaurant still in the alley**. It **opened in 1978** and was **rebuilt in February 2009** after a fire. It serves **only one dish**, dakhanmari, made with 35-day-old young chickens according to KTO, with rice cakes, green onion, potato and noodles as add-ons.

- Hours: **10:30-01:00 (last order 23:30), open every day** (KTO)
- Cards accepted, non-smoking throughout (KTO)
- Price: DiningCode lists **dakhanmari at ₩33,000 (about $25)**, add-ons ₩2,000 each (potato ₩3,000), rice ₩1,000

For comparison, a KTO article written **as of October 2019** gave the price here as **₩20,000**. The two sources are different, but the gap shows how much prices have moved. KTO's Korean listing says parking is available while its English listing says it is not.

### Jinwonjo Dakhanmari

In the same alley, **Jinwonjo Dakhanmari** (252-11 Jong-ro) opened about 20 years ago according to KTO and **registered a patent in 2007** for its method of cooking young chicken with herbal ingredients and vegetables. ## The grilled fish alley: coal briquettes as heritage

![A whole grilled mackerel on a platter surrounded by small dishes of kimchi, vegetables and pickles, with soup and rice](/images/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/04.webp)

The photo above shows a typical grilled mackerel set meal, not a particular restaurant.

Right next to the dakhanmari alley, on **Jong-ro 40ga-gil between Jong-ro and Cheonggyecheon east of the Jongno 5-ga crossroads**, is a cluster of grilled fish restaurants. Seoul lists it as **the Dongdaemun grilled fish alley, one of its Seoul Future Heritage food streets**, and dates it to **1979**. Seoul Future Heritage is the city's register of modern places worth passing on to future generations; a 2021 Seoul Institute summary counted nine food streets on it citywide, two of them in this guide's area.

A May 2020 column in Mediafine, based on a tbs documentary, described **more than ten grilled fish restaurants alongside set-meal places**, serving traders from Pyeonghwa Market and nearby wholesale markets as well as foreign visitors. One owner said she had been selling seolleongtang when customers tasted the fish she grilled on coal for the staff's own meal and urged her to switch. The fish is salted with sea salt, rested for about a day, grilled once in advance and **finished over coal briquettes when ordered**. The menu was mackerel, Spanish mackerel, Atka mackerel, yellow croaker, saury and hairtail, at **around ₩7,000** at the time.

Prices today are higher.

### Gopchang near Jongno 5-ga

The same 2019 KTO article pointed to **a small alley near Jongno 5-ga Station Exit 6** for gopchang (grilled small intestines), naming Uri Gopchang and Dongdaemun Gopchang, with vegetable-fried, salt-grilled and seasoned versions at **₩10,000 a serving** at the time.

**The Uri Gopchang** (9 Jong-ro 36na-gil) is still in KTO's listings, offering salt-grilled, seasoned and vegetable-fried gopchang plus grilled makchang. KTO lists **10:00-22:00, closed on the 2nd, 4th and 5th Sundays of each month**, no parking. Current prices were not found in official sources.

## The Sindang-dong tteokbokki alley: one shop in 1953

![A row of low tteokbokki restaurants under green awnings in Sindang-dong, with signs reading Yaksok Tteokbokki and Sindang-dong Hurricane Park](/images/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/02.webp)

The photo above was taken by KTO in the Sindang-dong tteokbokki alley.

The Sindang-dong tteokbokki alley is **a Seoul Future Heritage food street**, dated by the city to **1953**. Tteokbokki is rice cakes in a sweet-spicy gochujang sauce; here it is cooked in a pan at your table with noodles, eggs and dumplings. A March 2025 Seoul city article summarises the alley's history:

| Year | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 1953 | Ma Bok-rim opens a tteokbokki shop, starting the alley |
| 1975 | About ten tteokbokki shops open after a stream is covered over |
| 1983 | Several more open inside the Donghwa shopping arcade |
| 1993 | The Sindang-dong Tteokbokki Alley Merchants' Association forms |
| 2013 | Designated a "food theme street" by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism |

According to KTO, in the 1980s **every tteokbokki shop had a DJ booth** where a DJ played requests with listeners' messages. When high school baseball games ended at the nearby Dongdaemun Stadium, students poured into the alley; KTO says many of them now come back in middle age. Today's add-ons include eggs, glass noodles, fish cake, jjolmyeon and ramyeon noodles, squid, shrimp and cheese.

### Mabokrim Halmeoni-jip

KTO describes **Mabokrim Halmeoni-jip** (5 Dasan-ro 35-gil) as **the tteokbokki shop Ma Bok-rim founded in 1953**. She was the first to sell gochujang-based tteokbokki commercially and popularised **pan-cooked "jeukseok" tteokbokki and rabokki** (tteokbokki with ramyeon), using gochujang mixed with chunjang (black bean paste). Since her death the shop has been **run by her daughters-in-law and granddaughter**.

KTO also recalls **a 1996 gochujang TV commercial** in which she appeared with the line "Not even my daughters-in-law know the secret", which made her famous nationwide. You can eat in or take home **an uncooked kit to cook yourself**.

- Hours: **09:00-22:50, closed 2nd and 4th Monday of each month** (KTO)
- Price: DiningCode lists **a set for two at ₩17,000 and for three at ₩20,000**, with dumpling, jjolmyeon and vegetable add-ons at ₩2,000 and egg ₩1,000

### Mabokrim Maknae Adeul-ne and others

**Mabokrim Halmeoni Maknae Adeul-ne** ("the youngest son's place", 221 Dasan-ro) is listed by KTO as **open 24 hours, closed on the 1st and 3rd Tuesday of each month**, with fish cake, fried dumplings, jjolmyeon and eggs as add-ons. DiningCode lists tteokbokki at ₩17,000 for two, ₩20,000 for three, ₩27,000 for four and ₩33,000 for five.

**I Love Sindang-dong**, another shop in the alley on DiningCode, lists Sindang-dong tteokbokki for two at ₩17,000 as well. **Many menus here start at two servings**, so solo diners should ask about the minimum order first.

## Seoul Jungang Market: gopchang trimmers and "Shin Jungang Market"

West of the tteokbokki alley, **Seoul Jungang Market** was founded in **1946 as Seongdong Market**, according to a July 2023 Seoul city article by chef Park Chan-il. It was then one of Seoul's three big markets with Namdaemun and Dongdaemun, and its rice trade once supplied **70% of what Seoul consumed**.

The gopchang story is worth knowing. The market is home to **wholesalers who clean and trim pork offal**, and the article says **the well-known pork gopchang restaurants across the road in Hwanghak-dong originally bought their gopchang here**. That alley dates from the 1970s. The kitchen-equipment arcades next door make grill plates, pojangmacha carts and even the iron pans used for tteokbokki.

The original central passage is now branded **"Shin Jungang Market"** and draws a younger crowd, and in **May 2023 Seoul chose it for its design innovation traditional market project**. The same article listed these prices (**as of July 2023**, so they may have changed):

## Gwanghui-dong's Central Asian street: a food scene built on the clothing trade

![A red restaurant sign reading КЫРГЫЗСТАН in Cyrillic and 'Kyrgyzstan' in Korean on a building wall, below a church sign in Korean and Russian](/images/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/05.webp)

The photo above shows a Kyrgyz restaurant and a Russian-language church in Gwanghui-dong.

South of DDP, around 120 Gwanghui-dong 1-ga, Cyrillic signs line the street. According to Jung-gu district, it formed **after Korea and Russia established diplomatic relations in the early 1990s, when Russian traders gathered at Dongdaemun's clothing malls**. People shipping Dongdaemun goods on to Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan and Mongolia settled in nearby Gwanghui-dong. The dishes Jung-gu highlights are **Uzbek lamb skewers, Central Asian noodle soups, Mongolian khorkhog (lamb cooked with hot stones) and suutei tsai (milk tea)**.

## Late nights and early mornings: restaurants on market time

Two things explain why so much of Dongdaemun opens late or very early.

The first is **the markets' timetable**. KTO lists Jeil Pyeonghwa Market as open **weekdays 09:00-17:30 and again 20:00-05:00** (Saturday 09:00-17:00, Sunday 20:00-05:00, no night trading on Fridays), with restaurants on the 4th floor. Jung-gu describes Dongdaemun Fashion Town as **about 30 malls and some 30,000 shops serving customers around the clock**.

The second is **Special Tourist Zone status**. There are two here: **Dongdaemun Fashion Town, designated on 23 May 2002**, and **Jongno-Cheonggye, designated on 22 March 2006**, which runs along the strip **between Jong-ro and Cheonggyecheon from the Gwanghwamun Building to the Sungin-dong crossroads**. Article 74 of the Tourism Promotion Act says that inside a Special Tourist Zone **the business-restriction rule in Article 43 of the Food Sanitation Act does not apply**. Article 43 is the clause that lets local governments **restrict restaurants' opening hours**, so inside a zone those limits cannot be imposed. Which zone each individual restaurant falls in was not checked.

## Good to know

**Card payment cannot be refused.** Article 19 of the Specialized Credit Finance Business Act says **a card merchant may not refuse card payment or treat card users less favourably**. Official listings mark cards as accepted at Jinokhwa, Jinwonjo, Everest Restaurant (KTO), Geumdwaeji Sikdang and Gosari Express (MICHELIN). The law does not guarantee that every terminal will accept a foreign-issued card, though, so carry a little cash or a Korean payment option in the market alleys. Our [Seoul subway and T-money guide](/en/posts/seoul-subway-tmoney-2026-09/) explains how to get a transport card.

**Hot pots are ordered by the table.** Dakhanmari comes as one pot with a whole chicken, and the jeukseok tteokbokki menus on DiningCode in Sindang-dong start at two servings. If the format is new to you, [Korean food for beginners](/en/posts/korean-food-for-beginners-2026-09/) walks through it.

**Closing days vary.** Open every day: Jinokhwa (KTO), Honamjip, Everest Restaurant. Mabokrim Halmeoni-jip closes on the 2nd and 4th Mondays, Maknae Adeul-ne on the 1st and 3rd Tuesdays, Changsindong Maeun Jokbal and Gosari Express on Mondays, and The Uri Gopchang on the 2nd, 4th and 5th Sundays.

**Sources sometimes disagree on hours.** KTO gives Changsindong Maeun Jokbal's closing time as 22:00, DiningCode as 21:30. Check a map app before you go.

**No tipping.** The menu price is what you pay.

**Prices.** DiningCode prices may be posted by restaurants or users and are not clearly dated. No official prices were found for Jinwonjo Dakhanmari, The Uri Gopchang, Geumdwaeji Sikdang or Gosari Express.

**The current gopchang restaurants in Seoul Jungang Market.** Names and prices were not confirmed in official sources; market prices are from the July 2023 Seoul city article.

**Mongolian restaurants in Gwanghui-dong.** Whether Mongol Nomadic and Cafe Baikal (named in 2020) are still open, and what is inside Mongol Tower today, was not confirmed. **Halal-certified restaurants** were not confirmed from official certification lists either.

**Food court hours in the DDP-area malls** were not confirmed mall by mall, so they are left out.

## Wrapping up

- **East of Jongno 5-ga**: Jinokhwa Halmae Wonjo Dakhanmari (since 1978, open until 1 a.m., ₩33,000 on DiningCode) and the Seoul Future Heritage grilled fish alley (since 1979, coal briquettes, around ₩13,000 now).
- **Sindang-dong**: the Future Heritage tteokbokki alley (1953), Mabokrim Halmeoni-jip's ₩17,000 set for two, and the 24-hour Maknae Adeul-ne. MICHELIN Bib Gourmands **Geumdwaeji Sikdang** and, new for 2026, **Gosari Express**.
- **Gwanghui-dong**: a Central Asian street that grew after Korea-Russia ties in the early 1990s, with about 150 businesses. Shurpa and plov cost around ₩10,000-11,000 on DiningCode.
- **Cheapest listed meals**: ₩4,500 whole fried chicken at Dongdaemun Hanguk Tongdak, ₩5,000 bean sprout bibimbap at Dongmun Sikdang and ₩5,000 noodles at Guksu Jeonmunjeom (good price list).
