In the Pokémon games, you can revive fossils into creatures like Tyrantrum and Omanyte. So what did the real animals behind them look like? That is the question behind the Pokémon Fossil Museum, which opens in Gwangju, a city in southwest South Korea, on December 19, 2026. It is the exhibition's second stop outside Japan, after Chicago, and its first in Korea. Everything below comes from the Gwangju National Science Museum's official pages and Pokémon Korea's announcement, as of November 2026.

One thing to get right first: the venue is **Gwangju National Science Museum** (Gungnip Gwangju Gwahakgwan), not Gwangju National Museum. The names are close and easy to mix up, but they are in completely different parts of the city. The science museum sits in Buk-gu, out by the Cheomdan science and industry complex.

## What the exhibition is

![Mounted Tarbosaurus skeleton with its long tail stretched out in a museum gallery](/images/gwangju-pokemon-fossil-museum-2026-12/07.webp)

The Pokémon Fossil Museum was planned jointly by Japan's National Museum of Nature and Science and The Pokémon Company. The idea is simple: put **Fossil Pokémon** from the games next to the real prehistoric life they resemble, and compare them. The official page gives two examples. Dinosaur skeletons, including the Cretaceous predator Tarbosaurus, are shown beside Tyrantrum, the Despot Pokémon, and ammonite fossils sit beside Omanyte, the Spiral Pokémon. The photo above is a Tarbosaurus mount from another museum, included for reference; it is not a specimen from this show.

![Ammonite fossil cut in half to show its spiral inner chambers](/images/gwangju-pokemon-fossil-museum-2026-12/08.webp)

The exhibition toured Japan before moving to the Field Museum in Chicago, where it runs until April 11, 2027. Gwangju is the next overseas venue. The museum lists it as suitable for all ages, designed for children and adults alike, and Pokémon Korea has promoted it with Pikachu in an excavation look. A full specimen list and the gallery layout have not been published yet.

## Dates and booking

- **Dates**: Saturday, December 19, 2026 to Tuesday, August 31, 2027
- **Opening**: an opening ceremony and media day on Friday, December 18; general admission from the 19th
- **Venue**: special exhibition hall, Gwangju National Science Museum
- **Booking**: advance reservations are expected to open in early December, with details and tickets announced on the museum's website and social media
- **Admission**: the official page only says the fee "varies by program"; no price has been announced

The run is more than eight months long, but the first weekend and Korea's school winter break are likely to be the busiest. If you have dates in mind, check the official site when booking opens in early December.

## Exploring the science museum

![Central hall of Gwangju National Science Museum under a glass dome, with character banners and escalators](/images/gwangju-pokemon-fossil-museum-2026-12/03.webp)

The museum itself is big enough to fill more than the exhibition visit. According to its visitor guide, the main building is open Tuesday to Sunday, 9:30–17:30, with ticketing closing at 16:30. It is closed every Monday, on January 1, and on the days of Lunar New Year and Chuseok (Korea's autumn harvest holiday).

![Science museum gallery with a large hanging model of Jupiter and space exhibits](/images/gwangju-pokemon-fossil-museum-2026-12/02.webp)

Main building admission for individuals is ₩3,000 (about $2.10) for adults aged 20–64 and ₩2,000 for ages 7–19; under-7s and over-65s go free. Groups of 20 or more pay ₩2,000 and ₩1,500. A combined Triple Pass that adds the children's science hall and other halls costs ₩5,000 for adults.

![Children playing in a hands-on kids' science area beyond a gear-decorated archway](/images/gwangju-pokemon-fossil-museum-2026-12/04.webp)

Families with young kids often add the **Children's Science Museum** to the day. The main building also has a play area for children 6 and under, run in four 90-minute sessions at 10:00, 12:00, 14:00 and 16:00.

## Practical notes

- **Address**: 235 Cheomdangwagi-ro, Buk-gu (Oryong-dong). The museum's own pages write the city as Jeonnam-Gwangju Integrated Special City.
- **Bus**: per the museum's directions page, buses 09, 16, 23, 30, 34, 51, 82 and 193 stop at the "Gungnip Gwangju Gwahakgwan" (Gwangju National Science Museum) stop. You can also get off at the "Amkor Korea" stop (Express 03, and 03, 46, 51, 91, 193).
- **By car**: southbound on the Honam Expressway, exit at Gwangsan IC toward the Cheomdan complex, go through the tunnel, and continue about 2 km; the museum is on the left.
- **Parking**: open 09:00–18:00. A flat daily rate of ₩2,000 for regular cars (₩4,000 for vehicles with 25 seats or more), paid on exit at card-only machines. The first 30 minutes are free, and compact cars, eco-friendly cars and multi-child households get 50% off.
- **Contact**: information desk +82-62-960-6210, exhibition planning office +82-62-960-6122

## Wrapping up

The Pokémon Fossil Museum uses Fossil Pokémon as a way into real paleontology. With a run through August 2027, it fits both the winter and summer school breaks. The open questions are the ticket price and how booking will work, so wait for the official announcement in early December and plan your date around it.
