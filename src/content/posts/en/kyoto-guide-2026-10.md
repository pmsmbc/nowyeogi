Kyoto has no airport of its own. From Kansai Airport the HARUKA express takes 75–80 minutes, from Osaka Station the JR Special Rapid takes 29, so a Kyoto trip in practice starts as an extension of an Osaka one. What changes is everything after you arrive: there are only two subway lines, so buses carry most of the sightseeing load, every temple sets its own admission, and 2026 brought two price jumps, Ginkakuji doubling to ¥1,000 in April and the city's accommodation tax rising in March. This guide starts with those new numbers. Every fare and opening time below is as of October 2026, taken from the Kyoto City Tourism Association, the Kyoto City Transportation Bureau, JR-West and each temple's or attraction's own pages. Prices are in yen; at roughly ¥150 to the US dollar, ¥1,000 is about $6.70.

## Getting to Kyoto: from the airport and from Osaka

Kansai Airport (KIX) has direct services from across East and Southeast Asia; from Seoul, both Incheon and Gimpo fly there in 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours, and Peach runs the Gimpo route four times a day since February 2026. Low-cost carriers such as Peach use **Terminal 2**, while the railway station and the bus stops are at **Terminal 1**, so take the free shuttle first. The Kyoto limousine bus calls at Terminal 2 before Terminal 1, so allow an extra 15 minutes.

**HARUKA** is the JR limited express that runs from Kansai Airport straight to Kyoto Station. The HARUKA One-way Ticket for overseas visitors costs ¥2,700 to Kyoto from November 1, 2026 (¥2,200 until October 31). It is not sold at counters in Japan: book on JR-West's online reservation site and collect it from a machine at Kansai-Airport Station with your passport. The regular fare is ¥3,060, a ¥1,860 base fare plus a ¥1,200 non-reserved express fee, so the visitor ticket is worth the effort. The ride takes about 1 hour 20 minutes. Without an express, the Kansai Airport Rapid to Osaka Station and a Special Rapid from there costs ¥1,860 in base fare alone.

**Limousine buses** reach the Hachijo exit of Kyoto Station for ¥2,800 (children ¥1,400) in about 1 hour 25–30 minutes. From the airport to Kyoto there are no reservations: you board in the order you line up. In the other direction every seat is assigned, so book on the operator's website or by phone. Airport departures run from about 6:45 am to after 11 pm. If you fly domestic into Osaka's Itami Airport, the limousine bus to Kyoto Station takes about 50 minutes.

**From Osaka**, three railways compete. For a Kyoto Station hotel take the [[line:jrkyoto]] Special Rapid (¥580, 29 min); for Kawaramachi and Shijo the [[line:hankyukyoto]] limited express (¥410, 42 min); for Gion the [[line:keihan]] limited express (¥490, 48 min). Each is a single ride. From Shin-Osaka the shinkansen's non-reserved car is ¥1,450 and under 15 minutes, and from Tokyo the Nozomi takes from 2 hours 7 minutes.

## Getting around: the ¥230 bus, two subway lines, and the day pass

![A Kyoto City Bus route 100 in light and dark green livery stopped at a bus stand in front of Kyoto Station](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/47.webp)

Within the flat-fare zone a Kyoto City Bus ride is **¥230** (children ¥120). You board at the rear door and pay at the front when you get off, and IC cards such as ICOCA or Suica are accepted at the fare box. The subway is just two lines, the north–south [[line:karasuma]] and the east–west [[line:kyototozai]], with fares of ¥220–360 by distance: Kyoto Station to Shijo is ¥220, and Nijo Castle with one change is ¥260.

If you will ride the bus three or more times in a day, buy the **Subway & Bus 1-day pass**: ¥1,100 for adults and ¥550 for children, valid that day on all subway lines, all city buses and parts of the Kyoto Bus, Keihan Bus and JR Bus networks. It is sold at station counters, information desks and on board buses. On a subway-only day the **Subway 1-day pass** is ¥800 (children ¥400). Show either pass at Nijo Castle and the entry ticket drops to ¥700.

On weekends, holidays, Obon and the New Year period the **tourist express buses** EX100 and EX101 run from Kyoto Station. They cost a flat ¥500, accept the Subway & Bus 1-day pass, and leave from stop D1 outside the central exit, boarding at the front door. EX101 goes only to Kiyomizudera (Gojozaka) in about 12 minutes; EX100 continues to Gion, Heian Shrine and Ginkakuji. Ordinary buses between the sights are known for crowding, so the tourism association advises using trains and the subway where possible and leaving big bags in station lockers or with the Hands Free Kyoto delivery service.

For payment, the same **ICOCA** card as in Osaka rides JR, Hankyu, Keihan, Kintetsu, the subway and the buses. The JR ticket office at Kansai-Airport Station sells a visitor ICOCA for ¥2,000 including a ¥500 deposit, and on an iPhone you can add ICOCA from the Wallet app and top up with a card registered to Apple Pay.

## Where to stay: four bases compared

![The vermilion west gate of Yasaka Shrine and its stone steps seen from the end of Shijo-dori](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/54.webp)

**Around Kyoto Station** is where HARUKA, the shinkansen and the bus terminal meet and where the [[line:karasuma]] starts, which makes luggage and day trips easiest. The Kyoto Tower observation deck (¥1,000, 10:00–21:00) and the station's restaurant floors are at hand and hotels cluster here, but every temple is a bus or train ride away.

**Shijo, Kawaramachi and Karasuma** form the downtown. [[line:hankyukyoto]] Kyoto-Kawaramachi and Karasuma stations, [[line:keihan]] Gion-Shijo and the subway's Shijo Station are all within a walk, Nishiki Market is next door, and the department stores and late-opening restaurants mean dinner is never a problem. The Hankyu and Keihan trains back to Osaka start here.

**Gion and Higashiyama** is the district where you can walk from Yasaka Shrine to Kiyomizudera. [[line:keihan]] Gion-Shijo is close, the lanes go quiet once the day visitors leave, and there are many ryokan and small inns.

**Arashiyama** is the western edge, 15 minutes from the city on the [[line:sagano]]. The Bamboo Grove and Togetsukyo Bridge are on foot and mornings and evenings are calm, but every other sight needs a train ride, so it suits a one- or two-night stay.

Wherever you stay, the city's **accommodation tax** is added per person per night. For stays from March 1, 2026 it is ¥200 when the nightly rate per person is under ¥6,000, ¥400 from ¥6,000 to under ¥20,000, ¥1,000 from ¥20,000 to under ¥50,000, ¥4,000 from ¥50,000 to under ¥100,000, and ¥10,000 from ¥100,000.

## A 4-day core itinerary

Grouping Kyoto into east (Higashiyama), north and west (Arashiyama) keeps the bus rides short. Fares and hours below come from each temple's or attraction's official information.

### Day 1: Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizudera and Gion

![Two parallel paths of vermilion torii gates at Fushimi Inari Taisha's Senbon Torii](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/02.webp)

Start at **Fushimi Inari Taisha**. There is no admission and it is always open, so an early morning or evening visit is possible. From Kyoto Station it is two stops and about 5 minutes on the [[line:jrnara]] for ¥150, right outside Inari Station; from the [[line:keihan]] Fushimi-Inari Station it is a 5-minute walk. The Senbon Torii is near the entrance, but the round trip to the summit takes 2–3 hours and you can turn back at any point.

Midday is **Kiyomizudera**: ¥500 for adults, open 6:00–18:00 (until 18:30 in July and August) with no closing days. During the spring (March 27–April 5), summer (August 14–16) and autumn (November 21–30) night viewings it stays open until 21:30 with last entry at 21:00. From Kyoto Station take City Bus 206 or 100 to Gojozaka and walk 10 minutes uphill; on weekends and holidays the tourist express bus from stop D1 takes 12 minutes. The grounds can only be entered from Kiyomizu-zaka by the Nio-mon gate or from Chawan-zaka, so ignore map apps that suggest other routes.

![Yasaka-dori early in the morning, with wooden townhouses and street lanterns leading to the five-storey Yasaka Pagoda of Hokanji](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/50.webp)

On the way down, follow the stone-stepped lanes of Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka past the five-storey Yasaka Pagoda of Hokanji and walk into **Gion**. Hanamikoji itself is a public street, but many of the stone-paved side alleys are private, and no-entry signs have stood there since 2024. Stopping, following or photographing geiko and maiko without permission is also prohibited. Yasaka Shrine is free and always open, and in the evening the hundreds of lanterns on its dance stage are lit.

### Day 2: Ginkakuji, the Philosopher's Path, Nishiki Market and Nijo Castle

Begin at **Ginkakuji**, the Silver Pavilion. Since April 1, 2026 admission is ¥1,000 for adults (high school and above) and ¥500 for elementary and junior high school students, and it opens 8:30–17:00 from March to November and 9:00–16:30 from December to February. From Kyoto Station bus 5 or 17 takes 35–40 minutes to the Ginkakuji-michi stop. From there the **Philosopher's Path** runs about 2 km south to the Nanzenji area; when the cherry trees bloom in early April it is one of the city's most popular hanami walks.

![Inside the Nishiki Market arcade, shoppers choosing snacks at a stall flying a strawberry daifuku banner](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/22.webp)

Lunch is **Nishiki Market**, a covered street of about 390 m with more than a hundred shops. Hours are typically 10:00–18:00, but many shops close on Wednesday or Sunday. Eating while walking is not allowed; you eat in front of or inside the shop you bought from. It is 3 minutes from the subway's Shijo Station and [[line:hankyukyoto]] Karasuma Station, and 4 minutes from Kyoto-Kawaramachi.

![The wide gravel forecourt and pine trees of Nijo Castle's Ninomaru Palace, with visitors at the entrance](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/28.webp)

The afternoon is **Nijo Castle**. The entry ticket is ¥800, or ¥1,300 as a set with the Ninomaru Palace. Entry runs 8:45–16:00, the grounds close at 17:00 and the Ninomaru Palace takes visitors until 16:10. It closes December 29–31, and the Ninomaru Palace also closes December 26 to January 3 and on Tuesdays in January, July, August and December (the following day if the Tuesday is a holiday). The Honmaru Palace is ¥1,000 and reservation-only online from 30 days ahead. The entrance is right outside Nijojo-mae on the [[line:kyototozai]]; from Kyoto Station ride the [[line:karasuma]] to Karasuma-Oike and change, about 15 minutes and ¥260. Show a 1-day pass and the entry ticket is ¥700.

### Day 3: Arashiyama, then Kinkakuji

![The walking path through the Arashiyama Bamboo Grove, lined with bamboo fences on both sides](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/15.webp)

**Arashiyama** is 15 minutes and ¥240 from Kyoto Station on the [[line:sagano]]. From Saga-Arashiyama Station walk the Bamboo Grove (free) first and enter **Tenryuji** by its north gate. The garden is ¥500 (¥300 for elementary and junior high students), the Hojo and other buildings add ¥300, and it opens 8:30–17:00 with reception until 16:50. The Cloud Dragon painting in the Hatto is a separate ¥500 and is shown only on weekends, holidays and special spring and autumn periods. The temple is closed October 29–30, 2026.

![Togetsukyo Bridge over the Katsura River with the Arashiyama hills behind](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/36.webp)

Follow the street in front of Tenryuji down to the Katsura River and **Togetsukyo Bridge**; the riverside walk is free. If there is time, ride the **Sagano Romantic Train**, which covers about 7 km of the Hozugawa gorge from Torokko Saga to Torokko Kameoka in 25 minutes. The fare is the same for any distance, and the official booking site shows it once you pick a date. Same-day tickets go on sale at Saga station from about 8:35 am, first come first served, with a few standing tickets after the reserved seats sell out. To get back to the city, the Randen tram to Shijo-Omiya takes 20 minutes for ¥250.

![A wide view of Kinkakuji's Golden Pavilion reflected in its pond on a sunny day](/images/kyoto-guide-2026-10/10.webp)

Finish at **Kinkakuji**, the Golden Pavilion: ¥500 for adults, ¥300 for elementary and junior high school students, open 9:00–17:00 every day. City Buses 12, 59, 205 and M1 stop at Kinkakuji-michi, and bus 205 from Kyoto Station takes about 40 minutes. The visit centres on the view across the pond, so it does not take long, and cashless payment is accepted alongside cash.

## Food: the city of tofu and matcha

Kyoto's food is eaten sitting down rather than on the street as in Osaka. The dish that food guides and reviews mention most is **yudofu**, tofu simmered in kombu broth, and the specialist restaurants cluster on the approach to Nanzenji. Around Arashiyama and Nanzenji you also find **yuba**, the skin lifted from heated soy milk, while **obanzai** restaurants serving small home-style seasonal dishes are around Nishiki Market. **Nishin soba**, buckwheat noodles topped with sweet soy-simmered herring, was invented in Kyoto in 1882, and Kyoto ramen means a rich chicken and soy broth. For dessert the standards are shaved ice and soft serve made with Uji matcha and the cinnamon rice sweet **yatsuhashi**, and Gion has a kuzukiri (arrowroot noodle) shop that dates from 1716. The tofu dengaku skewers and soy-milk soft serve sold on the approaches to the temples are eaten in front of the shop, not while walking, in the Kyoto manner.

## Practical notes

- **Visas**: JNTO's guide says visa exemptions apply to short tourist stays for passports from a number of countries, and the Japanese embassy in Seoul confirms 90 days for ordinary Korean passports. Paid work is not allowed on a visa-free entry. Check your own passport's terms before booking.
- **Visit Japan Web**: an online pre-registration for immigration and customs. It is optional, but it speeds up customs and the tax-free procedure.
- **Tax-free changes on November 1, 2026**: purchases up to October 31 have the tax deducted at the till. From November 1 you pay the tax-inclusive price, then before departure present your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal in the airport's international departure lobby (before checking bags) to receive the refund. The limits stay the same, within 90 days of purchase and at least ¥5,000 before tax per shop per day, and sealed packaging for food and cosmetics is no longer required. Anything used in Japan is not refundable.
- **Paying**: ICOCA balance covers convenience stores, vending machines, station shops and the city buses. Many temples still take admission in cash (Kinkakuji is one that also accepts cashless), so carry ¥10,000–20,000 in notes.
- **Weather**: Japan Meteorological Agency normals (1991–2020) put January at 4.8°C and August at 28.5°C on average. July is the wettest month at 223.6 mm, followed by June at 199.7 mm; the tourism association puts the rainy season at early June to mid-July and warns that midsummer days often top 35°C. Winter dips below 5°C on some days but snow is infrequent. Cherry blossom peaks in recent years fell between March 19 and April 10, and most autumn colour peaks between mid-November and early December.
- **Manners and fines**: smoking outside designated areas carries a ¥1,000 fine, and carving the bamboo at Arashiyama or Fushimi Inari can cost up to ¥300,000. Photography is prohibited in Gion's private alleys and parts of the district. Bins are scarce, so carry a bag for rubbish, and phone calls on trains are frowned upon.
- **Basics**: Japan is on the same clock as Korea and one hour ahead of Singapore, Hong Kong and Malaysia. Power is 100 V with type-A plugs and there is no tipping. Leave big luggage in a Kyoto Station locker or with the Hands Free Kyoto delivery service and the buses become much easier.

## Wrapping up

For a first Kyoto trip: a hotel near Kyoto Station or Kawaramachi, one HARUKA ride in, one ICOCA, and a day each for the east (Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizudera, Gion), the north (Ginkakuji, Nijo Castle) and the west (Arashiyama, Kinkakuji). If you are pairing it with Osaka, [Osaka for First-Timers](/en/posts/osaka-guide-2026-10/) covers the airport and the Osaka end, and [Tokyo for First-Timers](/en/posts/tokyo-guide-2026-10/) is there if you are continuing east.
