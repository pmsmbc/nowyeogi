Myeongdong is known for shopping, but its food has a longer history than most visitors expect. Hadongkwan has been serving beef soup since **1939**, Eunho Sikdang in Namdaemun Market has made oxtail soup since **1932**, and Myeongdong Kyoja started in **1966** as a noodle shop in a converted hanok. The lane in front of the Chinese Embassy dates back to **1882**, when Qing merchants opened Chinese restaurants there, and Seoul now lists it as Future Heritage.

The short version: **lunch at Myeongdong Kyoja or Hadongkwan, snacks from the street stalls and Namdaemun's hotteok, dinner in Namdaemun Market's hairtail alley or one of Myeongdong's older side-street restaurants.** The MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026 has no Bib Gourmand in this area; Myeongdong Kyoja and Hadongkwan appear as "Selected" restaurants. Every place named here appears in a MICHELIN, Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), Seoul city, Namdaemun Market or government good-price listing, and anything about flavour is taken from those descriptions.

## What this guide covers

"Myeongdong" here means **Myeongdong 1-ga and 2-ga, Chungmuro 1-ga and 2-ga, Namsan-dong, Hoehyeon-dong, and Namchang-dong where Namdaemun Market is**, all in Jung-gu. Roughly, the area between Euljiro to the north, Samil-daero to the east, the lanes south of Toegye-ro below Namsan, and Sungnyemun Gate to the west. Sogong-dong, home of the Lotte Hotel, sits just outside and only comes up in the MICHELIN section.

The neighbours have their own guides: [Gwanghwamun and City Hall](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-food-2026-09/) for Mugyo-dong octopus and Da-dong's old restaurants, [Euljiro](/en/posts/euljiro-food-2026-09/) for Woo Lae Oak and the nogari alley, [Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-food-2026-09/) for Jongno 3-ga and Insa-dong, and [Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/) for dakhanmari and Sindang-dong tteokbokki. This page stays inside Myeongdong and Namdaemun Market.

## Myeongdong Kyoja: from "Jangsujang" to a new main branch

According to the history page on Myeongdong Kyoja's official website, it began in **1966 as "Jangsujang", a kalguksu shop in a converted hanok in Suha-dong**. When it moved to Myeongdong it took the name **"Myeongdong Kalguksu"**, and tax records give **20 January 1970** as the official opening date under that name. So many copycat "Myeongdong Kalguksu" signs appeared around the country that the shop **renamed itself Myeongdong Kyoja in 1978**, the website says. That is why the sign still reads "Founded 1966" and "(formerly Myeongdong Kalguksu)".

### The main branch moved in 2026

The main branch used to be at **29 Myeongdong 10-gil**. The official website now lists the main branch at **129 Toegye-ro, in front of Myeongdong Station Exit 8**. Its store page says the **"new Myeongdong Station branch" opened in 2024 was merged into the main branch when the main branch moved in 2026**, and that it seats about 420. KTO's listing also gives 129 Toegye-ro (Chungmuro 2-ga).

- The Myeongdong Station branch opened on **23 December 2024**. A notice the same day said the original first branch was under construction.
- The website now lists three locations: **the main branch (129 Toegye-ro), a second branch (8 Myeongdong 10-gil) and an Itaewon branch (136 Noksapyeong-daero)**.
- The MICHELIN page and DiningCode still show the old address, 29 Myeongdong 10-gil. What is at that address now could not be confirmed.

## Hadongkwan: gomtang since 1939

The MICHELIN Guide Seoul 2026 describes **Hadongkwan** (12 Myeongdong 9-gil) as a Myeongdong institution that has kept its reputation since **1939**. Gomtang is a clear beef soup; MICHELIN's English text says Hadongkwan's version is generously filled with brisket, offal and tripe. MICHELIN also notes that it closes when the day's soup runs out, that you pay first, and that takeaway is available.

## Myeongdong Chinese Street: Chinese restaurants since 1882

The lanes around the Chinese Embassy (Myeongdong 2-gil) are **Myeongdong Chinese Street**, designated **Seoul Future Heritage in 2017 (No. 2017-013)**. According to Seoul's Future Heritage page, it formed when **merchants who arrived with Qing troops during the 1882 Imo Incident opened fabric shops and Chinese restaurants** here, making it **the oldest Chinese street in Korea**.

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1882 | Qing merchants open fabric shops and Chinese restaurants around Myeongdong |
| 1886 | Yuan Shikai builds the Qing legation |
| 1909 | The Hanseong Chinese Primary School settles on the street |
| 1949 | The Republic of China (Taiwan) embassy replaces the Qing legation |
| 1992 | After Korea-China relations are established, the site becomes the Chinese Embassy |
| 2002 | The embassy moves to Hyoja-dong; the street's trade shrinks sharply |
| 2013 | The embassy returns to Myeongdong in a new 24-storey building |

Redevelopment and restrictions on foreigners in the 1970s led many ethnic Chinese residents to emigrate or move to Taiwan, the page adds. Today a few Chinese restaurants, importers of Chinese books and goods, and money changers remain.

The Chinese restaurant here on KTO's list is **Gaehwa** (52-5 Namdaemun-ro, Myeongdong 2-ga). KTO describes it as a long-popular spot near the Chinese Embassy and names **yuni-jjajang** (black bean noodles with minced meat), seafood jjamppong, and its **gogi-twigim**, fried pork with a thinner batter than tangsuyuk, eaten with salt and pepper. KTO lists **11:30-22:30 (last order 20:50), closed Saturdays**; DiningCode lists jjajangmyeon at ₩7,000, fried rice ₩8,000 and mapo tofu rice ₩9,500. The Saturday closure catches people out.

## Myeongdong street stalls: from name registration to card readers

![A snack cart on a Myeongdong street piled with orange fried crisps in paper cones, with shops hanging SALE banners and shoppers behind](/images/myeongdong-food-2026-09/02.webp)

The photo above is a Myeongdong snack cart from Wikimedia Commons. It is not recent, so today's stalls look different.

Officially, Myeongdong's street vendors are **"georigage" (street shops)**. Jung-gu district treats them as licensed businesses rather than something to clear away, and the rules have been reported in some detail.

### 2016: the real-name system

According to the Seoul Shinmun (20 June 2016), Jung-gu introduced a **real-name system for Myeongdong stalls on 27 June 2016**:

- Only people already trading in Myeongdong were eligible, **one stall per person**, run in person
- **No transferring, subletting or delegating**; breaking the rule could cost the road-use permit
- **366 stalls** registered at the time, paying about **₩1.3 million a year** in road-use fees
- Trading could start from **4pm in summer, 3pm in winter, 2pm on weekends and holidays**
- An **alternating-day system**: trade one day, rest the next (replacing an earlier three-shift system)
- Five zones: Myeongdong-gil, Jungang-ro, Chungmuro-gil, 1st Avenue and 3rd Avenue

A Yonhap report from February 2023 (carried on Daum) put the number of permitted stalls at **362**, and quoted a vendor paying **about ₩900,000 a year**, depending on location and stall size.

### 2023: the overcharging debate and price labels

In summer 2023 Myeongdong's stall prices became news. Newsis and the Kyunghyang Shinmun (9 July 2023) cited **four bungeoppang for ₩5,000 and a grilled squid for ₩12,000**. Jung-gu said it would work with the Myeongdong tourist zone council on a **price display system**, and Edaily (19 July 2023) reported plans to **make price labels mandatory across Myeongdong from October**, with fines of up to ₩10 million.

In May 2024 Seoul city, Jung-gu and the Seoul Metropolitan Police ran joint inspections in the Myeongdong tourist zone with about 50 people in 15 teams (Newdaily, 31 May 2024). The same article compared stall prices of **₩5,000 for a Korean hot dog (₩1,800 at a franchise) and ₩5,000 for tanghulu (₩3,000 at a franchise)**. All of these are **examples from the dates of those articles**; today's prices may differ.

### 2024: business registration and card readers

Edaily (5 February 2024) reported that Jung-gu planned to finish **business registration and card terminal installation for the stalls by March 2024**, after talks with the local tax office in November 2023. The same plan included:

- **Monthly price monitoring of ten popular items** such as tteokbokki, fish cakes and grilled squid, shared with vendors
- Vendors **consulting the district before raising prices**, with increases phased in
- Standard caps, masks and gloves, and regular health certificates

On 17 July 2024 Jung-gu launched **special loan guarantees** (up to ₩2 million for existing operators) for stalls that had registered and installed card readers, which it called **the first such scheme for street vendors in Korea**; Woori Bank supplied card terminals and funding. A Seoul Shinmun article from March 2025 said **278 of 350 Myeongdong stalls (79.4%)** had registered as businesses by December 2024, and Jung-gu was named top district in Seoul's street-stall management assessment.

### 2025-2026: crackdowns and hygiene testing

According to Gukje News (1 August 2025), Jung-gu ran a **special crackdown from 18 June to 17 July 2025**, issuing **45 administrative penalties and 136 on-site warnings** for unlicensed trading, breaking the alternating-day rule, not displaying permits, illegal subletting and corporate-style operations. It also announced mystery-shopper checks on prices, hygiene and service.

Sijung Ilbo (31 March 2026) reports **346 stalls** in Myeongdong today, of which **243 (70.2%) sell food**. From **April 2026** the district began **random sampling of stall food** for testing by private laboratories.

### What the rules say

Jung-gu's street stall regulation (last amended 1 January 2025) contains a few points worth knowing:

- Stalls may sell accessories, general goods, fruit, records and **simple heated food**. **They may not sell alcohol.**
- Food must not be handled with bare hands; vendors must use paper, tongs, spoons or disposable gloves, and wear hygiene caps and uniforms.
- The regulation does not set fixed hours; each permit sets them, and trading outside permitted hours is a violation.

Current start times and how the alternating-day rule works in 2026 could not be confirmed from official sources. In 2016 stalls opened in the afternoon (3-4pm on weekdays, 2pm on weekends).

Namdaemun Market is walkable from Myeongdong, and Hoehyeon Station on [[line:4]] is right next to it. ### The braised hairtail alley

![Steam rising from a black pot of galchi-jorim, pieces of hairtail fish with radish, zucchini and green onion in red chilli broth](/images/myeongdong-food-2026-09/04.webp)

The photo above shows a typical galchi-jorim, not a dish from a Namdaemun restaurant.

Galchi-jorim is hairtail (cutlassfish) braised with radish in a spicy red sauce. According to KTO, **Namdaemun's galchi-jorim alley formed around 1988**. Hairtail was cheap at the time, and restaurants began braising it spicy to suit market traders; word of mouth did the rest. The street had simply been a row of restaurants. **The first two to specialise in galchi-jorim were Huirak Sikdang and Wangseong Sikdang**, and neighbours gradually changed their signs. Restaurants use hairtail from Yeosu, Jeju, Mokpo or Busan, KTO says, and if the spice is too much, go to a place that also serves grilled fish.

The Namdaemun Market website says the alley originally sold **kalguksu and dumplings before switching to fish**, that the sauce is built on radish, green onion, garlic and chilli powder, and that some places use rice-washing water for the broth. The website gives the alley's hours as **03:00-21:00** (individual restaurants vary).

### Hotteok and dumplings

![Several golden griddle-fried hotteok pancakes leaning against each other in a row](/images/myeongdong-food-2026-09/03.webp)

The photo above shows typical hotteok, not hotteok from a Namdaemun stand.

Hotteok is a pan-fried filled pancake, usually sweet. The Namdaemun Market website calls **Yachae Hotteok** (in front of 12 Namdaemun-ro) "the most famous hotteok stand in Namdaemun Market". It started with honey hotteok only; when trade slowed, the owner created a **vegetable hotteok filled with onion, garlic chives, carrot and glass noodles, inspired by fried seaweed rolls**. The dough is started at 6am and used the same day, and the hotteok is brushed with soy sauce. DiningCode lists **vegetable, honey-seed and red bean hotteok at ₩2,500 each**, and notes frozen packs for taking abroad.

The same website describes **Kim Jin-ho Hotteok** (21 Namdaemunsijang 4-gil) as hotteok filled with nuts and soybean powder, **griddled with a little oil rather than deep-fried**.

For dumplings, **Gamegol Son Wangmandu** (42 Namdaemunsijang 4-gil) is the standard. The market website says it **started as a dumpling snack bar in Dongdaemun in 1978 and opened its Namdaemun shop in 2008**; dumplings are made on the ground floor and the second floor has seating for noodles and dumplings. DiningCode lists **meat or kimchi wang-mandu (large dumplings) at ₩6,000, hand-cut kalguksu ₩7,000** and ten galbi dumplings for ₩5,000. Hours are 08:00-20:00 on the market website and 08:20-19:30 on DiningCode.

### Oxtail soup: Eunho Sikdang and Jinjujip

**Eunho Sikdang** (28-4 Namdaemunsijang 4-gil) has made oxtail soup **since 1932, now in its fourth generation**, according to KTO, using only Korean beef oxtail simmered for a long time. It is on Seoul's 2021 list of 50 Future Heritage restaurants, also dated 1932. KTO lists **weekdays 06:05-21:00 (last order 20:40), weekends 06:05-16:00 (last order 15:40)**, open every day, cards accepted. DiningCode lists **kkori-gomtang ₩33,000, kkori-tomak (larger oxtail pieces) ₩38,000, seolleongtang ₩14,000, haejangguk ₩12,000**.

**Jinjujip** (22-2 Namdaemunsijang-gil) is described on the market website as a **50-year-old haejangguk and oxtail restaurant**. Its haejangguk comes with congealed blood, dried cabbage leaves and bean sprouts, and the oxtail comes in two large chunks eaten with a chive sauce. DiningCode lists **seolleongtang ₩12,000, haejangguk ₩10,000, oxtail soup ₩29,000**. The market website says **open 24 hours**, but DiningCode says 08:00-21:00, so check before going late.

## Early mornings and late nights

Myeongdong and Namdaemun Market sit inside the **Myeongdong, Namdaemun, Bukchang-dong, Da-dong and Mugyo-dong Special Tourist Zone**. Jung-gu's tourism website says it was designated on **30 March 2000** (Ministry of Culture and Tourism notice 2000-2) and **extended on 27 December 2012** toward Mugyo-dong and Da-dong, covering about **872,809 m²**. Its boundary runs from Samil-daero (beside the Sejong Hotel) along Toegye-ro past Shinsegae, Namdaemun Market, the Plaza Hotel, City Hall, Cheonggye Plaza, Gwangtonggyo and the Janggyo Building, back to the Sejong Hotel. Jung-gu puts daily foot traffic at 1.5 million on weekdays and 2 million at weekends.

Article 74 of the Tourism Promotion Act says that inside a special tourist zone, **the business restrictions in Article 43 of the Food Sanitation Act do not apply**. That article lets local authorities limit restaurant opening hours, so those limits cannot be imposed inside the zone. Parts of Namsan-dong and Hoehyeon-dong south of Toegye-ro are outside the boundary, and whether each individual restaurant is inside the zone was not checked.

## Halal and vegetarian

**No restaurant in this area could be confirmed against an official halal certification list.** KTO's listings do not show halal-certified restaurants in Myeongdong or Namdaemun Market either. Seoul's halal restaurants cluster in Itaewon, covered in the [Itaewon food guide](/en/posts/itaewon-food-2026-09/).

What official sources do say about ingredients:

- **Myeongdong Kyoja**: the website says the kalguksu uses **chicken broth** and the dumplings are filled with **pork**. Not suitable for vegetarian or halal diets.
- **Hadongkwan, Eunho Sikdang, Jinjujip**: all beef-broth soups.
- **The kalguksu alley's** barley bibimbap, sticky rice and hotteok are not built around meat, but what goes into the broths and sauces could not be confirmed from official sources.

The nearest vegan restaurant with a MICHELIN Bib Gourmand is Gosari Express in Sindang-dong, covered in the [Dongdaemun guide](/en/posts/dongdaemun-food-2026-10/).

## Good to know

**Card refusal is not allowed.** Article 19 of the Specialized Credit Finance Business Act says **a card merchant may not refuse card payment or treat card users less favourably**, which also rules out charging more for card payment. Myeongdong's stalls have been installing card readers since 2024. The law does not guarantee that every terminal accepts foreign-issued cards, though, so carry a little cash or a Korean payment method in market lanes.

**Check the price before buying.** Jung-gu has inspected price labelling in Myeongdong since 2023. If nothing is posted, ask first.

**Closing days vary.** Hadongkwan closes Sundays, Gaehwa Saturdays, Huirak Galchi Sundays, and Wonjo Namsan Wangdonkkaseu Tuesdays. Myeongdong Kyoja closes only on Seollal and Chuseok day. Many Namdaemun Market businesses close on Sundays (1414 Born Market, Daedo Sikdang and others).

**Some dishes come in portions for two.** At least one galchi-jorim restaurant notes on DiningCode that it takes orders for two or more. Solo diners can go for grilled fish or the kalguksu alley. If Korean dishes are new to you, [Korean food for beginners](/en/posts/korean-food-for-beginners-2026-09/) is a good start.

**Sources disagree on hours.** Jinjujip (24 hours vs 08:00-21:00), Buwon Myeonok (21:00 vs 20:00) and Gamegol Son Wangmandu (20:00 vs 19:30) differ between the market website and DiningCode. Check a map app before setting out.

**No tipping.** The menu price is what you pay.

## Wrapping up

- **Myeongdong Kyoja**: started in 1966 as "Jangsujang", renamed 1978. In 2026 the main branch moved to **129 Toegye-ro (Myeongdong Station Exit 8)**. Kalguksu and bibim-guksu ₩12,000, dumplings and kong-guksu ₩13,000. Closed only on Seollal and Chuseok day.
- **Hadongkwan**: gomtang since 1939, moved from Suha-dong to Myeongdong, open **Mon-Sat 07:00-16:00**. Regular gomtang ₩18,000 (DiningCode listing).
- **MICHELIN 2026**: no Bib Gourmand in the area. Myeongdong Kyoja and Hadongkwan are Selected; **L'Amant Secret** in Hoehyeon-dong has one star.
- **Street stalls**: real-name system in 2016 (366 stalls), price labelling from 2023, card readers in 2024, 346 stalls in 2026 with 243 selling food.
- **Namdaemun Market**: the galchi-jorim alley from around 1988 (about ₩14,000 per person, DiningCode listings), the kalguksu alley with its free tastes (around ₩9,000), ₩2,500 vegetable hotteok, and oxtail soup at Eunho Sikdang since 1932.
- **Cheapest meals**: Yuga's ₩7,000 jajangmyeon, Urijip Matjarang's ₩7,000 doenjang stew and Bajae Coffee's ₩2,000 Americano (good-price registrations).
