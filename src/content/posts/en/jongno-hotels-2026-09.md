Jongno gives you a choice you cannot make anywhere else in Seoul: a modern hotel next to the palaces, or a room in a 100-year-old courtyard house. They are completely different experiences and both are worth considering.

Prices are per-night bands for a double room in low-to-mid season, from booking-site averages as of September 2026. Add 20–40% in April–May and September–October.

## Pick your side first

![Gwanghwamun Square with the palace gate and mountains behind](/images/jongno-hotels-2026-09/02.webp)

- **Gwanghwamun** ([[line:5]]) — the business end. Big international hotels, the palace across the road, quiet at night, five minutes from Cheonggyecheon.
- **Insadong and Anguk** ([[line:3]]) — mid-range hotels and guesthouses, walkable to two palaces and Bukchon, lively in daytime.
- **Jongno 3-ga** ([[line:1]] [[line:3]] [[line:5]]) — cheapest rooms, three subway lines, the drinking alleys and Gwangjang Market on your doorstep. Noisier and rougher around the edges.
- **Bukchon and Samcheong-dong** — hanok guesthouses. Beautiful, steep, and subject to the rule below.

## The Bukchon rule, before you book

Since **1 March 2025**, the special management zone around **Bukchon-ro 11-gil** allows tourist activity **only between 10:00 and 17:00**, with a **₩100,000 fine** outside those hours. The city has said this applies even to guests of accommodation inside the zone if they are judged to be sightseeing rather than travelling to or from their lodging.

In practice you can still stay there and come and go, but you cannot wander the lanes with a camera at 20:00. If that is what you pictured, book a hanok in **Samcheong-dong** or **Seochon** instead, where there is no restriction.

## Hanok stays: what they are really like

![A traditional hanok house with wooden doors on a sloping street in Bukchon](/images/jongno-hotels-2026-09/01.webp)

A hanok guesthouse is a converted traditional house: rooms open onto a small courtyard, floors are heated from beneath (**ondol**), and you sleep on a padded mattress laid on the floor rather than a bed.

**Typical price**: ₩70,000–150,000 for a double, sometimes less.

**What to expect:**

- **Rooms are small** and often have no wardrobe, just a rail.
- **Bathrooms are sometimes shared**, and even private ones are usually across the courtyard.
- **Soundproofing is poor.** Paper-and-wood walls carry sound; a neighbour's conversation is audible.
- **Ceilings and doorways are low.** Anyone over about 185cm will duck.
- **Breakfast is often included** and is usually toast, eggs and coffee rather than a Korean spread.
- **Winter is warm** — ondol floors are excellent. **Summer is variable**; check for air conditioning.

For one or two nights it is one of the most memorable stays in Korea. For a week with two suitcases, it is a trial.

## Budget: ₩50,000–100,000

- **Guesthouses in Ikseon-dong and Jongno 3-ga** — small private rooms, central, cheap, with three subway lines nearby.
- **Hotel Sunbee (Insadong)** — a long-standing budget favourite in the middle of Insadong.
- **Business hotels around Jongno 3-ga** — plain, functional, and the cheapest beds within walking distance of the palaces.
- **Simple hanok guesthouses** in Seochon, west of Gyeongbokgung, often start in this range.

## Mid-range: ₩120,000–250,000

- **Somerset Palace Seoul** — serviced residences near Anguk with kitchenettes and more space than most Seoul hotels.
- **Fraser Place Central Seoul** — similar, near Jongno 3-ga, good for longer stays.
- **Ibis Ambassador Insadong** — reliable, central, small rooms.
- **Boutique hanok hotels** in Bukchon and Samcheong-dong — hanok character with private bathrooms, at roughly double a guesthouse price.

## Luxury: ₩350,000 and up

- **Four Seasons Hotel Seoul** — at Gwanghwamun, opposite the palace, generally rated the best hotel in the city.
- **JW Marriott Dongdaemun Square** — at the eastern edge of Jongno by the old city gate, with the wall walk outside.
- **Lotte Hotel Seoul** and **The Westin Josun** are twenty minutes' walk south in Myeongdong — see the [Myeongdong hotel guide](/en/posts/myeongdong-hotels-2026-09/).

## Getting there from the airport

- **Airport bus [[bus:6002]]** — ₩17,000, runs through Jongno.
- **AREX to Seoul Station**, then [[line:1]] to Jongno 3-ga or a transfer to [[line:5]] for Gwanghwamun.

Full details in the [Incheon Airport to Seoul guide](/en/posts/incheon-airport-to-seoul-2026-09/).

## What to check before you book

- **Hills.** Bukchon and Samcheong-dong are genuinely steep. "500m from Anguk Station" can be a hard ten minutes with a suitcase.
- **Whether a hanok has private or shared bathrooms**, and whether the bathroom is inside the room or across the yard.
- **Air conditioning** in summer, in older hanok specifically.
- **Luggage storage and stairs.** Many guesthouses have no lift and narrow stairs.
- **Check-in hours.** Small guesthouses often staff the desk only in the afternoon and evening.
- **Whether the address is inside the Bukchon special zone** if you want to be out late.

## Wrapping up

For a first trip focused on history, book a mid-range hotel near Anguk or Gwanghwamun and add **one night in a hanok** for the experience. If you want the hanok without the restrictions, choose Samcheong-dong or Seochon.
