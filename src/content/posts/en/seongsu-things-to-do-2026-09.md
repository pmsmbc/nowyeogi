Seongsu-dong is known abroad for its warehouse cafés, but this autumn the biggest thing to see is a **garden show spread across the whole of Seoul Forest**. The city opened the 2026 Seoul International Garden Expo there on 1 May, and it runs **until Tuesday 27 October**; it passed 10 million visitors on 16 September, according to The Fact. On the same land stands a water treatment plant built in 1908, now a **free museum**, and under the elevated subway on Achasan-ro the district runs shops and displays for the area's handmade shoe trade.

**Days and months matter here.** The Seoul Forest greenhouse and the Water Works Museum both **close on Mondays**, the greenhouse **closes at 16:00 from 1 November**, and the butterfly garden **shuts for the season after October**. Yeonmujang-gil, the busiest shopping street, is closed to most cars **only on weekend afternoons (14:00–18:00)**. This page covers **what to see, when and how**. For the neighbourhood's background and a full day plan, see the [Seongsu-dong travel guide](/en/posts/seongsu-guide-2026-09/). Times and prices are **as of 6 October 2026, from official pages and dated news reports**.

## Seoul Forest: open every day, indoor sites closed Mondays

273 Ttukseom-ro. The city's park site says Seoul Forest opened on **18 June 2005** and covers **480,994 m²**. It was built on land at Ttukseom that had held a racecourse and a water works, with funding and volunteer help from more than 5,000 citizens, the city says. There are four zones: a culture and arts park, an eco forest, an experiential learning area and a wetland.

- The **horse sculpture** near the entrance commemorates the racecourse.
- The **winter pond** is only 3 cm deep; the city describes it as a photo spot for its reflections of metasequoia trees.
- **Wind Hill** is the park's highest point, and the **footbridge** runs from there out to the Han River park.

### Sika deer: you can see them, but not feed them

The city calls the sika deer enclosure **"a symbol of Seoul Forest"**. You can see the deer during eco forest hours (05:30–21:30).

**Sales of deer feed stopped on 1 January 2020**, so there is no feeding.

### The greenhouse is an old water works building

![Inside the Seoul Forest insect and botanical garden, palms and tropical plants growing densely under a glass roof](/images/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

According to the city, the insect and botanical garden is a greenhouse made from the **rapid filtration building of the old water treatment plant**, with about 100 species of tropical plants plus insects, reptiles and amphibians. The **Gallery Garden** (1,600 m², 2005) reuses the plant's **old sedimentation tank structure**. In other words, Seoul Forest and the museum below were once **a single water works site**.

### Park rules worth knowing

From the Seoul Forest FAQ.

- **Motorbikes, Segways and other powered rides are not allowed in.** Riding a wheeled powered device off the roadway inside the park carries a **₩50,000 (about $38) fine**. That covers shared e-scooters too, so leave them outside.
- **There is no bike or skate hire inside the park.** The city points to Ttareungyi, Seoul's public bike-share, or private rental shops outside. Inline skates and skateboards go in the free skate park.
- **No tents, including sunshades.** Picnic mats are fine.
- **No drones**: flying without military approval risks a fine of up to ₩2 million.
- **Dogs on leads of 2 m or less.** No lead or leaving droppings: ₩50,000 each. Keep dogs off lawns and planted gardens.
- Ball games are limited to soft-ball catch for pre-school children with a parent. **No kite flying.**
- Personal, small-scale photography is fine; commercial shoots need advance contact and a fee.

## Water Works Museum: free, in a 1908 treatment plant

![Front of the pump house at the Water Works Museum, a red-brick gable with a stone plaque reading SEOUL WATERWORKS 1907 above a granite arched entrance](/images/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/04.webp)

27 Wangsimni-ro, at the western edge of Seoul Forest. The Seoul Waterworks Authority (Arisu) describes the site as **Korea's first modern water treatment plant, the Ttukdo Water Source First Purification Plant**.

- In December 1903 the Americans Collbran and Bostwick received a waterworks franchise from Emperor Gojong; the Korean Water Works Company **began building in August 1906 and finished in August 1908**.
- From **1 September 1908** the plant sent 12,500 m³ of slow-sand-filtered water a day to central Seoul and Yongsan. Sources disagree on how many people that served: Arisu says **125,000**, the Korea Heritage Service **about 165,000**.
- Part of the site still runs as the **Ttukdo Arisu Water Purification Centre**, producing tap water 24 hours a day for about 1.02 million people in 71 neighbourhoods. As a national security facility it is open only to pre-booked institutional groups.
- It is **Seoul Metropolitan City Tangible Cultural Heritage No. 72**, covering **the pump house, one slow sand filter and 5,575.3 m² of land** (Korea Heritage Service).

### Hours and admission

- **09:00–18:00, the same on weekdays and weekends. Free.**
- **Closed every Monday, 1 January, and on Seollal and Chuseok days** (Lunar New Year and the harvest festival).
- No booking needed to look around. **Only the 40-minute guided tour must be booked.** Self-guided visitors can scan QR codes for a mobile audio guide, with sign-language versions also by QR.
- Hands-on learning programmes are free but need booking.
- Pushchairs, wheelchairs and picnic mats can be borrowed at the information desk of the Water and Environment Hall, which also has lockers. Packed lunches can be eaten in the outdoor rest area.
- No food in exhibition rooms, no pets except guide dogs, no commercial filming, and no scooters, inline skates or roller shoes indoors.

### What is there

From Arisu's exhibition pages.

| Building | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Main hall (pump house)** | The red-brick building that pumped water from 1908, with a granite porch and round-arched windows in a Renaissance-revival style. The ceiling was removed to show the roof trusses, and part of the floor is glass so you can see the old equipment below |
| **Slow sand filter beds** | Reinforced-concrete beds where Han River water passed through sand and gravel. Five were built in 1908 and one added in 1938; all six remain. Used until 1990, they are, the city says, **"the oldest surviving reinforced-concrete structure in Korea"** |
| **Water and Environment Hall (annex)** | Displays on forests and water, pollution and saving water, on the site of the old staff quarters |
| **Outdoor display** | Pipes, pumps and machinery in use from the 1920s on, plus a well with a bucket and a hand pump to try |

## Yeonmujang-gil: cars kept out only on weekend afternoons

Yeonmujang-gil is the lane in Seongsu 2-ga reached from [[line:2]] Seongsu Station exit 3. It is lined with flagship stores and pop-ups, but it has **no separate pavement**, so people and cars share the road.

### Weekend pedestrian safety street

From Seongdong-gu's announcement as reported by the Kyunghyang Shinmun (26 March).

- **28 March–29 November, every Saturday and Sunday 14:00–18:00**, vehicles are kept out. That is an hour later than last year (13:00–17:00).
- Residents, local workers and delivery vehicles can still drive in, so it is not fully car-free.
- Sections: from Seongsu-il-ro 40 (Gyeongdong Elementary School junction) to Yeonmujang-gil 56-1, Seongsui-ro 7-gil 46 to 36, and the Geumho Town 2 apartment access road section.
- Eight volunteer traffic marshals and seven pedestrian safety staff work at seven key points.
- A 15 September report pointed out that the restriction covers **only four hours on weekends**, so on weekday afternoons taxis still weave through the crowds. Expect cars on weekdays.

### E-scooter-free street

The Hankook Ilbo (30 September) reports that **Yeonmujang-gil is one of Seoul's five "e-scooter-free streets"**. The ban covers e-scooters, self-balancing devices such as Segways, and fully motor-powered bikes. Riding in a banned zone means a **₩30,000 fine and 15 penalty points** on ordinary roads, and illegally parked shared devices are towed. We could not find the hours that apply on Yeonmujang-gil.

### Seongsu Station is crowded

- Average daily entries and exits at Seongsu Station rose from 78,014 in 2023 to 102,488 in 2025 and **114,194 in the first half of 2026** (Shinailbo, citing Seoul Metro).
- Seoul Metro puts safety staff at exits 2 and 3 and on the platform during weekday evening rush hour, and is **building a new stair exit, "3-1", in front of exit 3**, due for completion in **November 2026** (Kyunghyang Shinmun, December 2025).
- Seongdong-gu counted about 46 million visitors to Seongsu in 2020 and about 70 million in 2024. Celebrity appearances at pop-ups cause sudden crowd surges, so the district now shares information in advance with local police (News1, January).

## Understand Avenue and the "Seongsu Brooklyn" route

**Understand Avenue** is a complex of shipping-container buildings in front of Seoul Forest Station. In September 2026 Seongdong-gu announced barista classes on the first floor (Korean 2nd) of its Power Stand building, so it is operating. ChosunBiz (23 September) reports that Seoul's building committee approved a 49-storey complex in front of Seoul Forest Station with 383 homes, a 622-room hotel and a 903-seat theatre, and that the theatre will adjoin Understand Avenue.

**Seongsu-dong galbi alley → Atelier-gil (4 min) → Seoul Forest (5 min) → Understand Avenue (5 min) → Water Works Museum (5 min)**

## Quick summary

- **Seoul Forest is free and open every day.** Greenhouse and butterfly garden closed Mondays. **Butterfly garden ends 31 October; greenhouse closes at 16:00 from November.**
- **The Garden Expo runs until 27 October**, with 131 of its 167 gardens inside Seoul Forest. Free outdoor films on 10, 16 and 24 October.
- **The Water Works Museum is free, 09:00–18:00, closed Mondays**: a 1908 pump house and Korea's oldest surviving reinforced-concrete filter beds.
- **The shoe shops, Hope Platform and Shoe Spot Seongsu** are free to browse along Achasan-ro from Ttukseom to Seongsu Station.
- **Yeonmujang-gil keeps most cars out only Sat–Sun 14:00–18:00** (until 29 November), and is an e-scooter-free street.
- **Creative×Seongsu runs 5–11 October**, with Fashion Seongsu on the 10th. Check pop-up dates on official notices.
- For help, the **tourist information centre inside Seongsu Station** or the Seoul Forest visitor centre.
