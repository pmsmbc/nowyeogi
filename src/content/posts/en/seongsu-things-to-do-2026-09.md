You came to Seongsu-dong for the cafes. Understood. But **there is more here than cafes, and a good deal of it is free.**

This post is put together only from material published by Seongdong-gu, the Seoul Metropolitan Government and the Korea Heritage Service.

![Seoul Forest seen from across the Han River, with a line of high-rise residential towers behind it](/images/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

## Seoul Forest - check the day of the week first

**The park itself is free and open 24 hours.** But **the indoor facilities all close on Mondays.**

| Facility | Season | Hours | Closed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The park as a whole | All year | 24 hours | Never |
| Ecological forest (deer enclosure, pedestrian bridge) | All year | 05:30-21:30 | Never |
| Insect and plant garden | March-October | 10:00-17:00 | **Mondays** |
| Insect and plant garden | November-February | 10:00-16:00 | **Mondays** |
| Butterfly garden | **May-October only** | 10:00-17:00 | **Mondays** |
| Visitor centre | All year | 09:00-18:00 | Open year-round |

**The butterfly garden does not open at all from November to April.** Last admission to the insect and plant garden is 30 minutes before closing.

The community centre **opens only irregularly, when there is an event on.** The gallery garden is an outdoor garden rather than an indoor space, so it has no separate opening hours.

### The deer are there. You cannot feed them

![A sika deer in the Seoul Forest enclosure looking out through the wire fence](/images/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/04.webp)

The official Seoul city page calls the sika deer enclosure the symbol of Seoul Forest. You can see them any time within the ecological forest hours of 05:30-21:30.

However, **the sale of deer feed was discontinued on 1 January 2020.** If you came to feed them, you will be disappointed. Plenty of people arrive not knowing this, which is why it is written down here.

### If you were thinking of hiring a bike

**There is no hire point inside the park.** This is the Seoul city government's official answer.

> There is currently no rental point inside the park. For bicycle hire, please use the Seoul Bike (Ttareungi) unmanned rental system outside the park, or a private rental shop.

And this one you really do need to know. **Electric kick scooters and Segways are banned from the park**, and breaking the rule carries a **50,000 won fine** (about $37). A lot of travellers get around on scooters these days, but not in Seoul Forest.

**Tents and sun shelters are also banned**, as are **drones** (a fine of up to 2 million won). Pets are allowed on **a lead of 2m or less**.

Personal photography is unrestricted. Only commercial shoots need advance contact and a fee.

### You can walk to the Han River from here

There is a **pedestrian bridge**. It runs from the **Wind Hill in the ecological forest to Han River Park**, and the bridge deck doubles as a viewing platform.

Note, though, that **the bridge is not open 24 hours.** It keeps the same **05:30-21:30** as the ecological forest. The park stays open all night; this bridge does not.

## The Seoul Waterworks Museum - the oldest red brick in the neighbourhood

Seongsu-dong being a red-brick neighbourhood is not only a cafe-street story. **There is a brick building here that was put up in 1908 and is still standing.**

- **Admission free**
- **09:00-18:00**, the same on weekdays and weekends
- **Closed Mondays, 1 January, and on Seollal and Chuseok day**
- Five minutes on foot from exit 3 of Seoul Forest Station

This is the site of the **Ttukdo Water Source No.1 Filtration Plant**. Construction began in 1906 and finished in 1908, and it produced 12,500 cubic metres a day, supplying about 165,000 people at the time. It is **the starting point of modern waterworks in Korea**.

The heritage designation is worth stating precisely. It is **not national registered cultural heritage** but **Seoul Tangible Cultural Heritage no. 72**. It was designated in 1989, and the designation covers **one water delivery building, one slow filtration bed and 5,575.3 square metres of land** - not the building alone.

The Seoul city description of that slow filtration bed is a nice detail: it calls it the oldest surviving reinforced concrete structure in the country.

**You only need to book ahead if you want a guided commentary.** To simply look round, turn up. **There is an English-language information page as well.**

### Seoul Forest and the museum were one site

This is where it joins up. The **insect and plant garden at Seoul Forest is the old rapid filtration building** of the plant, and the **gallery garden is the old sedimentation basin**, converted.

So **Seoul Forest and the Waterworks Museum are effectively sharing one filtration plant site between them.** Fittingly for a neighbourhood where factories became cafes, a water treatment plant became a park and a museum.

## The handmade shoe street - the workshops are still running

![The street and shopfronts running underneath the elevated structure of Seongsu Station](/images/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/09.webp)

Seongdong-gu explains officially why this became a shoe neighbourhood. When the 1997 financial crisis pushed the Kumkang Shoes production headquarters out to Bupyeong, the shoe workshops scattered - and they converged on **Seongsu-dong, where the Kumkang head office was and which was also close to the Esquire and Elcanto factories in Seongnam.**

Seongdong-gu runs five public facilities here. All of them are free.

- **Shoe Spot Seongsu** - on the second floor of Seongsu Station, towards exits 1 and 4. Shoe displays and a hands-on space
- **Handmade shoe joint sales outlet, building A** - 105 Achasan-ro, between exits 1 and 2 of Seongsu Station
- **Handmade shoe joint sales outlet, building B** - 61 Achasan-ro, between Seongsu and Ttukseom stations
- **The handmade shoe hub centre, Suda** - an exhibition gallery and workshop, with shoemaking and leather keyring sessions
- **Seongsu Handmade Shoe Hope Platform** - 53 Achasan-ro, five minutes from exit 4 of Ttukseom Station. Pop-up stores on the ground floor, a leather craft workshop on the first

There is a line Seongdong-gu has written about this neighbourhood.

> The red-brick factories, and the sight of traders on bicycles along Yeonmujang-gil, are part of Seoul's heritage

### There is something to see inside Seongsu Station too

The **Seongsu industrial culture themed complex** is inside the station building. It is one of the few facilities Seongdong-gu has labelled in both Korean and English.

Within it, **My Store SS** is the information space for visitors to Seongsu-dong. Be aware, though, that **it is an unstaffed touchscreen, and its hours are not in any official source.** There is nobody there to ask.

## Ttukdo Market

There is one traditional market. It is **a registered market in the Seoul city system, of the alley type**. It is 15 minutes on foot from exit 3 of Seongsu Station.

Seongdong-gu notes that **there is a food alley inside the market.**

However, **no official source gives its trading days or hours.** In a traditional market every stall differs. If you need to know, ask the merchants' association directly on 02-464-4426.

One thing to clear up. **Ttukdo Cheongchun Market** comes up online as if it were a permanent market, and **no such market exists.** Cheongchun Market is the name of **a one-day night market event the Ttukdo Market traders' association holds once each autumn**. It was on 25 October in 2024 and 1 November in 2025, and **the 2026 date has not yet been announced.**

## Which exit to take

![The station name plate on the pillar at Seongsu Station exit 1, marked Line 2, number 211](/images/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/06.webp)

Seongsu-dong has three stations and each leads somewhere different.

| Destination | Station and exit |
| --- | --- |
| The cafe street (Yeonmujang-gil) | [[line:2]] Seongsu Station, exit 3 |
| The handmade shoe street | [[line:2]] Seongsu Station, exits 1 and 2 |
| Shoe Theme Park | [[line:2]] Seongsu Station, exit 4 |
| Ttukdo Market | [[line:2]] Seongsu Station, exit 3, then 15 minutes on foot |
| Handmade Shoe Hope Platform | [[line:2]] Ttukseom Station, exit 4 |
| Seoul Forest | [[line:2]] Ttukseom Station, exit 8 |
| Seoul Forest and the Waterworks Museum | [[line:suinbundang]] Seoul Forest Station, exit 3 or exit 4 |

![An exit sign in the underground passage at Seoul Forest Station pointing the way to exit 4](/images/seongsu-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

For Seoul Forest Station, **the official sources contradict each other**. The Seoul Metropolitan Government says exit 4, Seongdong-gu says exit 3, and the Seoul Tourism Organization says exits 3 and 4. Hence exit 3 or exit 4 here.

## Seongdong-gu has laid out a route

This is the official Seongdong-gu themed route called **Seongsu Brooklyn**.

**The galbi alley, then Atelier-gil, Seoul Forest, Understand Avenue and the Seoul Waterworks Museum**

It is telling that **stop number one is not a cafe but the galbi alley** - the grilled rib restaurants. The catch is that **nowhere in Seongdong-gu's material is there a description, an address or a number of businesses for that alley.** Only the name, on the route list.

When Seongdong-gu explains its red-brick policy, it cites **Bushwick in Brooklyn, New York and the Aka Renga warehouses in Yokohama** as overseas comparisons. "The Brooklyn of Korea" is not a phrase bloggers invented; it is the district office's own wording.

**Understand Avenue** is a container complex next to Seoul Forest. It is a social contribution project run by a social cooperative, the Social Innovation Institute, and it opened in April 2016. **Its notices have been updated as far as June 2026, so it is operating.** Its central plaza serves as a passage through to Seoul Forest.

## If you want to be shown around

Stated plainly. **Seongsu-dong is a neighbourhood with weak tourist information.**

- **There is no Seongsu-dong course in the Seoul city walking-tour programme.** Every course was checked
- **My Store SS at Seongsu Station is an unstaffed touchscreen**
- **The English and Japanese on the Seongdong-gu website are a Google auto-translate widget**

In practice there are two places where you can ask a person.

- **The Seoul Forest visitor centre** - 09:00-18:00, open year-round
- **The 1330 tourist interpretation line** - the Korea Tourism Organization's multilingual phone and chat service

## What could not be verified

**The number of stalls at Ttukdo Market differs by more than double between official sources.** Seongdong-gu says 139, the Seoul city dataset says 63. It was not possible to establish which is right, so no figure is given here.

**The designation date of the Waterworks Museum also differs**: Seoul Arisu Headquarters says 11 September, the Korea Heritage Service says 19 September - eight days apart. So this post says only that it was designated in 1989.

**The opening hours of the individual units at Understand Avenue could not be confirmed.** The 10:00-18:00 on the website is the customer support desk, not the units. Repeated calls for new tenants suggest the line-up is in flux, so no unit names are given either.

**The hours of My Store SS** and **the 2026 date for Cheongchun Market** are likewise absent from official sources.

## Wrapping up

- **All the Seoul Forest indoor facilities close on Mondays.** The butterfly garden opens May to October only.
- **The deer are there, but feed sales were discontinued in 2020.**
- **There is no bike hire inside the park, and electric kick scooters are banned** (50,000 won fine).
- **The pedestrian bridge to the Han River is open only 05:30-21:30**, which is not the same as the park's hours.
- **The Waterworks Museum is free, 09:00-18:00, closed Mondays.** It is the site of a 1908 filtration plant.
- **All five of the public handmade shoe facilities are free.**
- **There is no walking-tour course here.** For guidance, use the Seoul Forest visitor centre or 1330.

It would be a shame to see only the cafes. **The Waterworks Museum and Seoul Forest are next door to each other and both are free.** Just avoid Monday.
