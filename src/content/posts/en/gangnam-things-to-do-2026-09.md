The things most worth your time in Gangnam are, oddly, **two old and quiet places**: Bongeunsa, a Buddhist temple first founded in 794, and Seolleung and Jeongneung, where two Joseon kings and a queen are buried. The two are linked. According to the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), Queen Jeonghyeon rebuilt the temple beside her husband King Seongjong's tomb in 1498 and gave it the name Bongeunsa, and in 1562 Queen Munjeong moved King Jungjong's tomb to its present site after consulting Bou, the abbot of Bongeunsa.

Add Starfield Library in the middle of COEX Mall, Dosan Park and Garosu-gil, and that is a full day. **Days and months matter here.** The royal tombs **close on Mondays**, and **from 1 November they close at 17:30**. This page covers only **what to see, when and how**. The district's background is in our [Gangnam travel guide](/en/posts/gangnam-guide-2026-09/). All hours and prices are **as of 6 October 2026, from each site's official information**.

## Bongeunsa Temple: free, founded 794

![The back of Bongeunsa's giant Maitreya Buddha statue, looking down over the temple courtyard towards the high-rises around COEX](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/04.webp)

531 Bongeunsa-ro. KTO's materials say it was founded in **794** by the monk Yeonhoe as Gyeonseongsa, and in **1498** Queen Jeonghyeon, King Seongjong's queen, rebuilt it and renamed it Bongeunsa. It originally stood near King Seongjong's tomb and moved to its present site under King Myeongjong.

- In the Joseon era it was the head temple of the Seon (Zen) order; in modern times the Dongguk Institute of Buddhist Scripture Translation was founded here.
- KTO's English listing says the temple holds **3,479 woodblocks of 13 Buddhist scriptures** and works by the calligrapher **Kim Jeong-hui**.
- As in the photo above, the terrace in front of the giant Maitreya Buddha looks straight across to the towers on Yeongdong-daero.

### Hours and admission

- **Entry to the grounds is free** (KTO English listing).
- **Open all year**, but the hours differ by source. KTO's Korean listing gives **09:00–18:00** ("call for details"), its English listing **05:00–22:00**. If you are going for the dawn or evening service, call **+82-2-3218-4800** first.
- The temple stay office is open **09:00–17:00 and closed on Mondays**.

### Temple stay and the weekly programmes

A temple stay is a Korean programme, started in 2002, in which visitors spend time living by a Buddhist temple's daily routine. Bongeunsa lists its programmes for international visitors on the national Templestay booking site. This is what is on it now:

| Programme | When | Price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Regular temple stay (overnight)** | Saturday 13:50 to Sunday 11:00 | **₩120,000 (about $90) per person**, same for all ages | Runs until 27 December 2026 |
| **BONG-PASS** | Every **Tuesday 10:00–16:00** | ₩5,000, pre-school free | Drop in for tea and meditation, no fixed schedule. Tea room holds 10 at a time |
| **Twilight Resonance** | Every **Sunday 17:00–19:00** | ₩5,000 | Temple tour, Buddhist instruments, evening service. Starts at Suwolgwan hall |
| **Five Elements Yin Yoga Night** | Every **Friday 19:00–21:00** | ₩80,000 | In English, 8–16 people, **until 23 October** |

The overnight stay runs: orientation, temple etiquette, a temple tour, a monastic meal, the evening service and meditation on day one; a 04:00 wake-up, the dawn service, 108 prostrations, breakfast and tea with a monk on day two.

- **Bookings open one month before the date and close the day before.**
- Refunds: **full refund 14 days before, 70% seven days before**, nothing after that.
- **Socks are required**; bare feet are not allowed in the halls. The temple supplies only soap, a hair dryer and tissues, so bring other toiletries, a change of clothes, an umbrella and any medicine. A tumbler is recommended.
- The temple stay website lists **no smoking, no alcohol and no revealing clothes** in the temple.

### 11–13 October: the 1232nd anniversary Traditional Culture Fair

From Bongeunsa's notice on the Templestay site:

- **Sunday 11 to Tuesday 13 October, 10:00–16:00**, in front of Suwolgwan hall, **all free**
- Bookable on the day for international visitors: **tea meditation**, 30 minutes, 10:30 / 11:30 / 13:30 / 14:30 / 15:30, 15 per session; **traditional mask (tal) making**, 30 minutes, 10:00 / 11:00 / 13:00 / 14:00 / 15:00, 20 per session
- Drop-in for everyone: **dasik** (traditional tea sweets) and **paper flower** making

13 October is a Tuesday, so it falls on a BONG-PASS day too.

## Seolleung and Jeongneung: closed Mondays, 17:30 closing from November

![A royal burial mound at Seolleung on a grassy rise, with stone figures and stone horses standing on either side and pine woods around it](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

1 Seolleung-ro 100-gil. A Historic Site, inscribed on the **UNESCO World Heritage List in 2009** as part of the Royal Tombs of the Joseon Dynasty (Seoul Metropolitan Government).

From KTO's materials:

- **Seolleung** is the tomb of **King Seongjong**, ninth king of Joseon, and his third queen, **Queen Jeonghyeon**. The two mounds sit on separate rises sharing one shrine. Facing the shrine, **the left (west) mound is the king's and the right (east) mound the queen's**.
- Seongjong died in 1494 and the tomb was built here in **1495**. The site had been the burial ground of Grand Prince Gwangpyeong, a son of King Sejong, whose grave was moved to Suseo-dong.
- **Jeongneung** is the tomb of **King Jungjong**, 11th king. It was first built near another queen's tomb in Goyang, but in **1562 Queen Munjeong moved it here after consulting Bou, the abbot of Bongeunsa**, intending to be buried beside the king herself. The new site was low and often flooded, so she was eventually buried at Taereung in Nowon-gu instead.
- Both tombs were dug up during the **1592 Japanese invasion** and the coffins burned; the kings were reburied in 1593.

### Hours and admission

From the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center's hours and fees pages:

| Months | Hours | Last entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| March–October | **06:00–21:00** (early-morning and evening opening) | 20:00 |
| **November–January** | **06:30–17:30** | **16:30** |
| February | 06:00–18:00 | 17:00 |

- **Closed every Monday.** If Monday is a public or substitute holiday, it opens and closes on the next working day instead.
- Staff start ushering visitors out 30 minutes before closing.
- **Adults ₩1,000** (foreigners aged 19–64; Koreans 25–64). Groups of ten or more ₩800.
- **Free** for foreigners **aged 18 and under** or 65 and over, and for **anyone wearing hanbok**, among others. The same free list also includes **"Culture Day" every Wednesday** (excluding Gyeongbokgung and the Changdeokgung rear garden).
- A lunchtime pass (11:30–13:30 at Seolleung) costs ₩3,000 for ten visits over three months.
- You can see King Seongjong's mound close up from a **path beside it**, all year.

### Getting there and rules

- The Seoul city government directs visitors to **exit 10 of Seolleung Station** ([[line:2]] [[line:suinbundang]]).
- The whole site is **non-smoking**. Alcohol, picnic mats, tents, sports equipment and pets (except assistance dogs) are not allowed, and packed lunches only in the designated area (visitor rules).
- KTO's English listing mentions a **Seolleung and Jeongneung History Center** on site explaining the tombs.

### This autumn at Seolleung

- The **"Joseon Royal Tombs Golden Bell" quiz**, planned for Sunday 20 September at Seolleung, was **postponed**; no new date yet (notice of 18 September).
- The 2026 ancestral rites are **Queen Jeonghyeon on Friday 2 October** (already past) and **King Jungjong on Wednesday 9 December**.
- **From Sunday 1 November closing moves from 21:00 to 17:30.** An after-dinner walk is possible only until Saturday 31 October.

## COEX: Starfield Library and the aquarium

![Starfield Library in COEX Mall, people walking between towering lit bookshelves](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

Across the road from Bongeunsa, **Starfield COEX Mall** at 513 Yeongdong-daero connects directly to [[line:2]] Samseong Station. The mall is open **10:30–22:00** (Starfield website; individual shops vary).

### Starfield Library

From the Starfield website and KTO:

- **10:30–22:00, open all year, free** for everyone.
- A **2,800 m² two-level space** in the centre of the mall, with **13-metre bookshelves**.
- About **70,000 books** and some **600 magazine titles** including foreign magazines, plus a foreign-language section and shelves curated by well-known people.
- Tables with laptop sockets; the upper level looks down into the sunken basement area.
- Author talks, lectures and book concerts are held regularly; see "this month's talks and performances" on the website.

It is **an open space in a busy mall, with people walking through all day**, not a quiet reading room. Starfield itself pitches it as somewhere to sit, rest or meet someone as much as to read.

### SEA LIFE COEX Aquarium

The old **COEX Aquarium** website (coexaqua.com) now redirects to **SEA LIFE COEX Aquarium**. When the name changed is not stated.

- **10:00–20:00, last entry 19:00**
- Online tickets: **adults (13+) ₩28,000** (list price ₩35,000), **children (36 months–12) ₩25,000** (list price ₩31,000), under 36 months free with proof of age
- Couple ticket (two adults) ₩51,000, family ticket (two adults and one child) ₩74,000; VR and digital photos cost extra.
- KTO's English listing still shows the older **₩26,000** adult price. Go by the official ticket page.
- KTO describes 183 display tanks and about 40,000 animals of 650 species, with more shark species and individuals than anywhere else in Korea.

### COEX events in October and November

Most COEX exhibitions are trade fairs. These are the ones open to the public:

- **Smart Life Week 2026**, **Tuesday 6 to Thursday 8 October**, Halls A and B. A Seoul city AI and robotics expo; **pre-registered visitors get in free**, says Kyeonggi Ilbo (August). Hall B also hosts the "Seoul AI Robot Show" with humanoid robot performances.
- **Local Vibe 2026**, **Friday 20 to Sunday 22 November**, Hall C. A fair of regional Korean brands: local food, traditional liquor, crafts and goods (Edaily). Admission price not found.

### Yeongdong-daero is a building site

Under Yeongdong-daero in front of COEX, a huge underground transport hub is being built. Chosun Ilbo (20 July) reports that **GTX-A Samseong Station will not open in 2026**: missing steel reinforcement was found in columns, and a government inspection team is continuing checks **until 20 November**. The construction is also why this year's **Gangnam Festival moved from Yeongdong-daero to Dosan-daero** (Dong-A Ilbo).

## Dosan Park and the Dosan Ahn Chang-ho Memorial Hall

![The Dosan Ahn Chang-ho Memorial Hall and statue beside a path in Dosan Park, with azaleas in bloom](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/06.webp)

20 Dosan-daero 45-gil, Sinsa-dong. A park honouring **Ahn Chang-ho (pen name Dosan)**, a leader of Korea's independence movement under Japanese rule.

- KTO says it opened on **10 November 1973**, is **open 24 hours and free**.
- Ahn's grave was moved here from Manguri Cemetery, and that of his wife **Lee Hyeryen** from Los Angeles; they are buried together. There is a bronze statue and a stone engraved with his sayings.
- The boulevard **Dosan-daero**, running from Cheongdam-dong to Nonhyeon-dong, is named after him too.
- A memorial ceremony is held every **10 March**, the anniversary of his death.

### The memorial hall

From the hall's visitor information:

- **10:00–18:00, last entry 17:30, free**
- **Closed on New Year's Day and the Seollal and Chuseok holidays.** This year it closed **only on 5 October** (the substitute holiday) and opened as normal on 3 and 9 October.
- **All guided tours are by reservation**: weekends 10:00–17:00, weekdays by phone (+82-2-541-1800).
- No food or pets (except guide dogs) in the galleries, no wheeled shoes, **no flash, tripods or commercial filming**. The whole park is non-smoking.
- KTO says the hall was built in 1998 and displays records of the Korean Provisional Government and Ahn's diaries.
- Parking: the paid **Dosan Park back-street car park** run by Gangnam-gu.

KTO notes that restaurants, cafés, galleries and fashion shops have clustered around the park.

## Garosu-gil, Apgujeong Rodeo and Cheongdam

![A side street off Garosu-gil in Sinsa-dong, people walking between low brick and white-walled shop buildings](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/07.webp)

### Garosu-gil, Sinsa-dong

KTO defines it as the street from **the crossroads in front of Hyundai High School to the junction with Dosan-daero east of Sinsa Station**. "Garosu" means roadside trees.

- It used to be full of galleries and film companies; now it is mainly fashion, with luxury and designer shops, beauty brands and **pop-up stores**.
- Hours vary by shop. **No parking.** Gangnam Tourist Information Center +82-2-3445-0111.
- Walk from [[line:3]] [[line:sinbundang]] Sinsa Station.

For cosmetics and on-the-spot tax refunds, see our [Olive Young shopping guide](/en/posts/olive-young-shopping-2026-09/).

### Apgujeong Rodeo, Cheongdam and K-Star Road

- **K-Star Road**: according to Munhwa Ilbo (March 2019), Gangnam-gu installed **17 "GangnamDol"** bear figures bearing K-pop group names along the roughly **1 km stretch from Galleria department store to Cheongdam crossroads**, in three rounds in 2014–2015. By 2019 several of the groups had already disbanded and the paper said the street needed an overhaul. **How many figures remain today could not be confirmed.**
- **Agency buildings** mostly have no public spaces. SM Entertainment has moved to Seongsu.
- **Dosan-daero** hosted the **2026 Gangnam Festival** on 3–5 October. Chosun Ilbo and Dong-A Ilbo report that the 15th edition was the first on Dosan-daero: a residents' parade on 3 October over about 1 km from Cheongdam crossroads to Dosan Park crossroads, a K-pop concert on the night of the 4th with PSY, ZEROBASEONE and KiiiKiii, and a temple concert at Bongeunsa on the 5th. **It is over.**

## Yangjaecheon: the stream in autumn

![A walking path along Yangjaecheon Stream in late autumn, people strolling under a tunnel of red and yellow leaves](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/08.webp)

Yangjaecheon is a stream running through Seocho-gu and Gangnam-gu. The photo above was taken on the Gangnam section's path on 21 November 2016; its caption describes people enjoying the autumn reds and yellows. KTO's "Yangjaecheon Neighbourhood Park" listing is the **Seocho** stretch near Yangjae Citizen's Forest Station: open 24 hours, with separate walking and cycling paths.

Foliage timing changes each year. KWeather (11 September) expects **peak colour in central Korea around 19 October to 1 November** this year. That forecast is for mountains, so city streams may differ. More autumn walks are in our [Seoul autumn foliage guide](/en/posts/seoul-autumn-foliage-2026-09/).

## Quick summary

- **Bongeunsa is free** and open all year. BONG-PASS on Tuesdays, evening service experience on Sundays at 17:00, overnight temple stay ₩120,000. **Free Traditional Culture Fair 11–13 October.**
- **The royal tombs close on Mondays**, ₩1,000 for adults, free under 19 for foreigners and in hanbok. **From 1 November they close at 17:30.**
- **Starfield Library, 10:30–22:00, free**, about 70,000 books. The aquarium is now **SEA LIFE**, ₩28,000 for adults online.
- **Dosan Park is free and always open**; the memorial hall is free, 10:00–18:00, tours by reservation.
- **Garosu-gil and Apgujeong Rodeo** are for strolling. K-Star Road's current state is unconfirmed; SM is now in Seongsu.
- The Gangnam Festival (3–5 October) is over, and Yeongdong-daero is a GTX construction site.
