Gangnam is not a sightseeing district, but it has four things worth your time, and two of them are much older and quieter than the neighborhood's reputation suggests. Everything here is within twenty minutes of Samseong Station. Hours and prices are as of September 2026.

## Starfield Library at COEX

![The 13-metre bookshelf wall of the Starfield Library inside COEX Mall](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

A **13-metre-high wall of bookshelves** holding around 50,000 books, in the atrium of the COEX shopping mall. It is free, open **10:00–22:00 daily**, and it is one of the most photographed places in Seoul.

Manage your expectations: it is an open space in the middle of a busy mall, not a library in the traditional sense. You can pick up and read books, but people are walking past constantly and the upper shelves are decorative. Come on a **weekday morning** if you want a photo without a queue for the spot.

COEX itself is one of Asia's largest underground malls and connects directly to Samseong Station on [[line:2]]. There is an aquarium, a cinema and a very large food hall down there.

## Bongeunsa Temple

![Daeungjeon hall at Bongeunsa temple with high-rise buildings behind](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

Across the road from COEX, a working Buddhist temple founded in 794, rebuilt many times, with glass towers rising directly behind its wooden halls. The contrast is the point, and it photographs beautifully.

**₩1,000**, open **05:00–21:00 every day**. Allow 45 minutes. The main hall's painted ceiling and hanging paper lanterns are the highlight, and there is a large stone Buddha statue up the steps at the back.

![Paper lanterns on the decorated ceiling of Bongeunsa temple's main hall](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/02.webp)

**Temple stay**: Bongeunsa runs one of the few **English-language temple stay programmes** in Seoul, on the **first weekend of each month**, from Saturday 14:00 to Sunday 11:00, at **₩120,000 per person**. It includes meditation, a tea ceremony, temple meals and the 04:00 morning service. Shorter daytime programmes also run. Book ahead through the temple or the official Templestay site.

It is quiet at 06:00 and full of tour groups by 11:00.

## Seolleung and Jeongneung Royal Tombs

![A grassy burial mound at the Seolleung royal tombs](/images/gangnam-things-to-do-2026-09/04.webp)

The burial mounds of King Seongjong, his queen and King Jungjong, set in a wooded park that survived the entire construction of Gangnam around it. UNESCO World Heritage, and the single most surprising thing in the district.

**₩1,000** for adults, **₩500** for ages 7–18. Open **06:00–21:00** March to October and **06:30–21:00** November to February, last entry 20:00.

Because it opens at dawn, it is the best morning walk in Gangnam: gravel paths, old pines, and almost nobody there before 09:00. Allow an hour. It is five minutes on foot from Seolleung Station on [[line:2]] and a fifteen-minute walk from COEX.

## K-pop and Apgujeong

Gangnam is where the entertainment industry lives, though most of it is behind office doors.

- **K-Star Road** in Apgujeong Rodeo — a street of bear statues representing K-pop groups, with agency buildings nearby. Free, and honestly modest; it is a photo stop rather than an attraction.
- **Agency buildings** — HYBE, SM, JYP and others have offices around Yongsan and Gangnam. Most have no public areas; fans gather outside anyway.
- **Concerts** — the big venues are in Jamsil and at COEX. Tickets sell through Korean sites and usually need a local booking service for foreigners.
- **Pop-up stores and album shops** — the reliable way to actually buy things. Check what is running when you visit; they change monthly.

Set expectations low here. The K-pop "sights" of Gangnam are mostly a street and some buildings.

## Shopping

- **Garosu-gil (Sinsa)** — a tree-lined street of boutiques, flagship stores and cafés. The main street has gone chain-heavy; the side streets, called Serosu-gil, are more interesting.
- **Apgujeong Rodeo** — luxury boutiques and designer resale.
- **COEX Mall** — everything under one roof, useful when it rains.
- **Gangnam Station underground mall** — hundreds of small shops, the cheapest clothes shopping in the district.
- **Express Bus Terminal underground (Goto Mall)** — enormous, very cheap, and where locals actually shop. Fifteen minutes on [[line:3]] or [[line:9]].

For cosmetics and tax refunds, Myeongdong is still better — see our [Myeongdong shopping guide](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Other options

- **Lotte World Tower and Seoul Sky** — technically Jamsil, ten minutes east on [[line:2]], the highest observatory in the country.
- **Seonjeongneung's neighbouring parks** and the **Yangjae Citizen's Forest** for a longer walk.
- **Jjimjilbang** — Gangnam has several large 24-hour bathhouses, a good rainy-day or late-night option.

## A half-day route

Seolleung Royal Tombs at 08:00 → walk or one stop to Bongeunsa → COEX and Starfield Library for lunch → [[line:suinbundang]] to Apgujeong Rodeo → Garosu-gil in the late afternoon → barbecue near Sinsa.

## Practical notes

- **Everything is far apart.** Use the subway between areas.
- **The Sinbundang Line costs extra** and is not covered by the Climate Card.
- **COEX is a maze.** Follow the ceiling signs to lettered exits rather than trying to navigate by memory.
- **Bongeunsa and the tombs open before dawn**, which is a genuine advantage if you have jet lag.
- **Free wifi** across COEX and most cafés.

## Wrapping up

Do the tombs and the temple early, the library and mall at midday, and the shopping streets after. That is a satisfying half to full day, and the two historic sites are the ones you will remember.
