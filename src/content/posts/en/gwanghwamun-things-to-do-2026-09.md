Most visitors pass through Gwanghwamun and City Hall on the way to Gyeongbokgung. Stop here instead and you find **a palace open until 9pm every night**, **two changing-of-the-guard ceremonies that take different days off**, and **a museum that opens on Mondays**. The short version: whichever day the big palaces close, this one-kilometre stretch can fill it.

This post does not repeat Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung and Jongmyo, which are in [our Jongno palaces guide](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/), or the rules for free palace entry in hanbok, which are in [the hanbok palace guide](/en/posts/hanbok-palace-2026-09/). Autumn leaves along the Deoksugung stone wall are covered in [Seoul autumn foliage](/en/posts/seoul-autumn-foliage-2026-09/). For the neighbourhood as a whole, see [the Gwanghwamun and City Hall overview](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-guide-2026-09/). All hours and prices are **as of September 2026**.

## Deoksugung: the palace that stays open until 9pm

![Gwangmyeongmun gate at Deoksugung under a blue sky, its sweeping eaves painted in green and orange dancheong, the name board 光明門 hanging under the roof and visitors walking beyond](/images/gwanghwamun-things-to-do-2026-09/03.webp)

According to the Royal Palaces and Tombs Center, Deoksugung opens **09:00-21:00 all year (last entry 20:00)**. Unlike Gyeongbokgung, whose hours change with the season, **evening opening is permanent** here, so it works after dinner.

- Admission **₩1,000 (about $0.75)**, free if you wear hanbok
- **Closed every Monday**
- The main gate, Daehanmun, faces City Hall Station on [[line:1]] and [[line:2]]

The palace runs free guided walks in foreign languages. The official schedule lists **English at 10:45 and 13:30, Japanese at 09:30 and 16:00, Chinese at 10:00 and 14:30** (Tuesday to Sunday), about 45 minutes each.

![Deoksugung seen from above, Junghwajeon hall and its wide stone courtyard in front, the white-columned Seokjojeon and a round fountain behind to the left, city towers beyond](/images/gwanghwamun-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

### Seokjojeon: book one week ahead at 10am

The Western-style stone building inside the palace is **Seokjojeon**, now the Daehan Empire History Museum. You go in with a guide.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Hours | 09:30-17:30, last tour 16:30 |
| Booking | Online, opens **one week before at 10:00**, first come first served |
| Group size | 15 online places per tour, up to 5 per person |
| Walk-in | 5 places per tour, **only for over-65s and foreign visitors** |
| Length | Standard 40-45 min, in-depth 65-70 min |
| Price | No extra charge beyond the palace ticket |

- Entry opens **3 minutes before** the tour; once it starts, you cannot join.
- The palace says to allow **10-15 minutes** to walk from Daehanmun to Seokjojeon.
- The official tour page notes that **English tours at 11:50 and 14:50 take on-site sign-ups only**.
- Cancel or change until 23:00 the day before.

### Dondeokjeon is closed until 25 October

A notice on the Deoksugung page says **Dondeokjeon is closed to visitors from 1 September to 25 October 2026**, alongside a notice about its exhibition. Check the page for what opens after that.

Jungmyeongjeon, on the Jeongdong-gil side, opens **09:30-17:30 (last entry 17:00)**.

### MMCA Deoksugung: the Lee Dai-won retrospective

The branch of the National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art inside the palace shows **"Lee Dai-won: Nothing Makes You Sad" until 8 November**. The museum describes about 120 works and 100 archival items by Lee (1921-2005), known for bright paintings of orchards, mountains and fields.

- **₩2,000**, plus the ₩1,000 palace ticket
- **Open until 21:00 on Wednesdays and Saturdays**, free from 18:00
- Free for under-25s and over-65s, among others
- Closed Mondays

## Changing of the guard: two gates, opposite days off

There are **two** guard ceremonies in this area, run by different bodies with different closing days.

| | Deoksugung, Daehanmun gate | Gyeongbokgung, Gwanghwamun gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Changing ceremony | **11:00, 14:00** | **10:00, 14:00** |
| Length | About 20 min | 20 min |
| Also | Patrol 15:00-16:00 (location varies) | Gate watch 11:00 and 13:00; open training 09:35 and 13:35 |
| No ceremony | **Mondays** | **Tuesdays and other palace closing days** |
| Run by | Seoul city (Royal Guard Changing Ceremony) | Korea Heritage Agency |

**On Mondays go to Gwanghwamun; on Tuesdays go to Daehanmun**, and you can catch one on any day. Both can be **cancelled in rain or bad weather**.

Gyeongbokgung's **patrol procession** runs on weekends and holidays in March-May and September-November. The Korea Heritage Agency says it stays **inside the palace on Saturdays and walks from Gwanghwamun to Insadong on Sundays**.

### Take part at Daehanmun

The Royal Guard site lists these extras:

- **"I am a royal guard"**: join the 11:00 or 14:00 ceremony as a guard. **One person per session, first come**, primary-school age and up, **arrive an hour early** for costume and practice.
- **Traditional costume try-on**: **Saturdays, Sundays and holidays 10:30-15:30**, free, sign up on site.
- Bookings for participation events open on **the 20th of the previous month**, one per month.

The site warns that **Saturday activities may be cancelled because of rallies in front of Daehanmun**.

## Gwanghwamun Plaza: under the statues

![The statue of Admiral Yi Sun-sin holding a sword on Gwanghwamun Plaza, with a building with large screens and the Kyobo Building with its big text banner behind](/images/gwanghwamun-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

### The Story of King Sejong and Admiral Yi

Below the King Sejong statue is a free exhibition run by the Sejong Center. The Sejong section sits under the plaza and the Admiral Yi section on the Sejong Center's second basement.

- **10:00-18:30 (last entry 18:00)**, free
- **Closed Mondays**; if a Monday is a public holiday, it opens and closes the next weekday instead
- Six zones on Sejong (including the creation of Hangeul, the Korean alphabet, science and the arts) and six on Admiral Yi (his life, warships, naval battles)
- Seoul city media mention a **4D theatre**, **Hangeul calligraphy** and an **audio guide in five languages**
- [[line:5]] Gwanghwamun Station exits 1 and 8

**Hangeul Day, 9 October**, celebrates the alphabet King Sejong created. We found no official notice of special events here that day; it is a Friday, so the exhibition is open as usual.

### Gwanghwamun Woldae: the royal terrace, back since 2023

The broad stone platform in front of Gwanghwamun gate is the **woldae**, a ceremonial terrace. The Kyunghyang Shinmun reported:

- Restoration celebrated on **15 October 2023**
- Excavated size **48.7m north-south by 29.7m east-west**, with a 7m-wide royal path in the middle
- Rebuilt from records saying it was built in March 1866
- A pair of **stone guardian animals**, lost for decades, returned as a donation from the family of the late Samsung chairman Lee Kun-hee
- The **haetae** statues beside the gate moved to the south end of the terrace
- The gate's name board changed to **gold letters on black**

### Garden of Gratitude: opened May 2026

At the plaza's edge near the Sejong Center is the **Garden of Gratitude**, opened on **12 May 2026**. Seoul city presents it as a tribute to the countries that fought in the Korean War.

- Above ground: 23 pillars **6.25m tall**, called "Light of Gratitude 23"
- Evening light: **20:00-23:00** (19:00-22:00 in winter), six times a night at 30-minute intervals
- Below ground: a media space called **Freedom Hall**
- Guided tours: **Tuesday-Sunday 10:00-20:00**, 12 a day, 20 people each, booked on Seoul's public reservation site, **English tours available**; you can also look round on your own

The project was controversial, and press reports after the opening described mixed reactions.

### The Gwanghwamun text board

The huge text banner on the Kyobo Building facing the plaza changed to its autumn edition in late August: lines from poet Lee Moon-jae's "Shangri-La". Herald Business says it stays up **until the end of November**. The design came from a university student competition.

### Autumn market and the outdoor library

- **Small business autumn market**: listed on the plaza's event calendar for **1 September-5 November**.
- **Gwanghwamun Book Yard**: part of the Seoul Outdoor Library, covered below.

## Jeongdong-gil: an art museum beyond the palace wall

Follow Deoksugung's stone wall west and you are on Jeongdong-gil. Foliage timing is in [Seoul autumn foliage](/en/posts/seoul-autumn-foliage-2026-09/); here we list only places to go inside.

### Seoul Museum of Art, Seosomun main building

Halfway along the wall lane is SeMA's main building, which keeps the facade of the **1928 former Supreme Court** and has a new building behind it. Regular galleries are **free**; special exhibitions are charged.

| Day | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| Tue-Thu | 10:00-20:00 |
| Fri | 10:00-**21:00** |
| Sat, Sun, holidays | 10:00-19:00 (Mar-Oct) / 10:00-18:00 (Nov-Feb) |
| Mon | Closed (open if a public holiday) |

SeMA's 2026 plan lists the first Asian museum solo show of American media artist **Lynn Hershman Leeson** here in **October**. **Sources give different opening dates**, so we have not listed one.

## Seoul Museum of History and Gyeonghuigung: open until 9pm through November

Walk west from Gwanghwamun along Saemunan-ro to the **Seoul Museum of History**. It is free and currently in an **extended evening period (15 September-30 November 2026)**.

- **Tue, Wed, Fri, Sat, Sun 09:00-21:00** (last entry 20:30)
- **Thursday only 09:00-18:00**
- Closed Mondays, but **open when Monday is a public holiday**
- Autumn exhibitions: **"Seoul Urban Planning"** (Gallery A, to 8 November) and **"Yeomin Gongsu"** (Gallery B, to 25 October)

Right behind the museum is **Gyeonghuigung**, a palace managed by the museum.

- **09:00-18:00 (last entry 17:30)**, **free**
- Closed 1 January and Mondays
- Visitors are asked not to bring in coffee, drinks or snacks
- Paid guided tours on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays

## National Museum of Korean Contemporary History: open on Mondays

On the east side of Gwanghwamun Plaza at 198 Sejong-daero. Most exhibition spaces here close on Mondays; **this one does not**.

- **Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri, Sun 10:00-18:00**, **Wed and Sat 10:00-21:00**
- Entry until 30 minutes before closing, **free**
- Closed only on **1 January and the days of Seollal and Chuseok** (Lunar New Year and Korean harvest festival)
- No food or pets

We have not listed its current special exhibition; see the museum's website.

## Seoul Plaza and the Seoul Metropolitan Library

### The autumn text board

The facade board on the old City Hall building, now the Seoul Metropolitan Library, changed to its autumn message on **28 September**. The Seoul Shinmun gives the line as, roughly, "A line of wind, a leaf of autumn colour: in every alley, autumn becomes a poem."

The library itself opens **Tuesday-Friday 09:00-21:00, weekends 09:00-18:00**, and **closes on Mondays and public holidays**. By that rule it is **closed on 3, 5 and 9 October**.

### Seoul Outdoor Library: Fridays to Sundays until 1 November

Free reading spaces with beanbags and bookshelves open at three places: Seoul Plaza, Gwanghwamun Plaza (Gwanghwamun Book Yard) and Cheonggyecheon between Mojeongyo and Gwangtonggyo bridges, **4 September-1 November, every Friday, Saturday and Sunday**.

- September: evenings **16:00-22:00**
- **From October, daytime 11:00-18:00**, according to published guides
- Dates can shift around plaza events. Seoul Plaza's schedule for 2-4 October lists the reading plaza.

### The winter ice rink has not been announced

**The 2026-2027 Seoul Plaza ice rink dates had not been announced at the end of September.** Last season ran 19 December 2025-8 February 2026, at ₩1,000 per one-hour session.

## Cheonggye Plaza and Bosingak

![Beyond the stone railing of a Cheonggyecheon bridge, the Spring sculpture rises in purple and blue spirals on Cheonggye Plaza with people walking around it](/images/gwanghwamun-things-to-do-2026-09/09.webp)

### Cheonggye Plaza

This is where the Cheonggyecheon stream begins. The shell-shaped sculpture is **Spring** by Claes Oldenburg and Coosje van Bruggen, 20m tall and 6m wide, **lit from inside at night**.

The Korea Tourism Organization lists a three-colour **candle fountain with a two-tier, roughly 4m waterfall**, the **Palseokdam** pool built with stones from Korea's eight historic provinces, and a **1/100 model** of the whole stream.

A walk along the stream is in [our Myeongdong guide](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/), and the Korea Tourism Organization's free centre near Jonggak is in [the HiKR Ground Pikachu event post](/en/posts/hikr-ground-pikachu-2026-09/).

**The 2026 Seoul Lantern Festival** appears on Seoul's festival site only as **"(planned) December, Cheonggyecheon area"**. Dates circulating online are not official yet. The same site lists **Seoul Light Gwanghwamun, 11 December-3 January 2027, 17:30-22:00**, on Gwanghwamun Plaza.

### Bosingak: the noon bell

In front of exit 4 of Jonggak Station on [[line:1]] stands the **Bosingak** bell pavilion. The Seoul Culture Portal says the bell is rung **at noon every day except Monday**, and residents and tourists can take part.

- No eligibility limits, **first come first served**
- Apply **online** on the Seoul Culture Portal or **on site**
- **Tuesdays have a programme for foreign visitors**
- Today's bell was **cast in 1985**; the original from 1468 is in the National Museum of Korea

## A route by time of day

**Morning: the guards**
10:00 at Gwanghwamun unless it is Tuesday; 11:00 at Daehanmun unless it is Monday. Pick whichever the day allows.

**Around midday: under the plaza**
The King Sejong and Admiral Yi exhibition (10:00-18:30), a free indoor stop for hot or rainy days.

**Afternoon: Jeongdong-gil**
Time it to your Seokjojeon booking (opens one week ahead at 10:00), then follow the wall to SeMA.

**Late afternoon: Seoul Museum of History and Gyeonghuigung**
Gyeonghuigung first, as it closes at 18:00; the museum is open until 21:00 except Thursdays.

**Evening: Deoksugung by night, or the plaza lights**
Last entry to Deoksugung is 20:00. On Wednesdays and Saturdays the art museum inside is open until 21:00. The Light of Gratitude display starts at 20:00.

**If it is Monday**
Deoksugung and most museums are closed. Use the **Gwanghwamun guard ceremony**, the **National Museum of Korean Contemporary History**, and Gyeongbokgung, which opens on Mondays; see [our Jongno palaces guide](/en/posts/jongno-things-to-do-2026-09/).
