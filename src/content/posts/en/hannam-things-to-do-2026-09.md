Much of what Hannam-dong offers **needs a booking or a condition checked first**. Leeum Museum of Art opens bookings 14 days ahead, only members can go upstairs at the Hyundai Card Music Library, and Blue Square needs a show ticket. Step a little away from Hangangjin Station, though, and you find **former US base land you can walk into without booking** and **a garden on Namsan's slope that reopened this June**.

This page covers **Hannam-dong and the area around Hangangjin Station** only. The Seoul Central Mosque, Haebangchon, Gyeongridan-gil, the World Food Street, the War Memorial, the 29 October memorial dates and Halloween crowd rules have moved to [things to do in Itaewon](/en/posts/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/). For the neighbourhood as a whole, see [the Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-guide-2026-09/). All hours and prices are **as of 6 October 2026, from each venue's official information**.

The National Museum of Korea is close by too, but it has [its own guide](/en/posts/seoul-national-museum-of-korea-2026-09/).

## Leeum Museum of Art: autumn shows and booking rules

![A gilt-bronze bodhisattva statue glowing gold under dark gallery lighting, one hand raised and the other holding a small flask](/images/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/02.webp)

60-16 Itaewon-ro 55-gil. According to the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO), Leeum is made up of **MUSEUM 1** for traditional Korean art and **MUSEUM 2** for modern and contemporary art from Korea and abroad, in buildings by three architects on one site: **Mario Botta, Jean Nouvel and Rem Koolhaas**. The photo above shows a gilt-bronze bodhisattva on display at Leeum (per its Wikimedia Commons record).

### Exhibitions, September to November

| Exhibition | Space | Dates | Admission |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Koo Jeong A: OUSSSMOS | M2 | 5 Sep-27 Dec 2026 | Paid (adult **₩18,000**, about $13.50, as reported by Daily Art) |
| Inside Other Spaces: Environments by Women Artists 1956-1976 | Black Box, Ground Gallery | 5 May-**29 Nov** 2026 | Paid |
| Idea Museum, After Tomorrow | Grand Hall lounge | 5 Sep-29 Nov 2026 | Not shown on the site |
| Gabriel Orozco Garden | Outdoors | From 3 Apr 2026 | **Free** |
| Traditional art permanent exhibition | M1 | Permanent | **Free** |

Daily Art reports that the Koo Jeong A show brings together **about 35 works**, from early-1990s pieces to new Leeum commissions.

### Booking and visiting rules (Leeum visitor information)

- **Online booking opens 14 days before** your visit, **up to four people** at a time
- Walk-in tickets are sold, but **waits get long** when the galleries are busy
- Entry from **20 minutes before** your slot until the next slot
- Ticket sales **end at 17:30**
- Backpack-sized bags go in the **lockers**
- **No flash**; tripods and selfie sticks restricted; some special exhibitions ban photography entirely

### Getting there

- From [[line:6]] **Hangangjin Station exit 1**, walk 100m towards Itaewon, turn right into the first alley and climb for about five minutes
- Buses [[bus:110A]] [[bus:110B]] [[bus:400]] [[bus:405]] stop at **Hannam-dong Saemaeul Geumgo** (Leeum FAQ)
- The car park is small: **no entry when full**, open only until 18:00, and for museum visitors only. Public transport is recommended.

## Hyundai Card Music Library: check the entry rules first

246 Itaewon-ro. It is one of the most-mentioned names on Itaewon-ro, but **not everyone can go upstairs.**

| | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| Tue-Sat | 12:00-21:00 |
| Sun & public holidays | 12:00-18:00 |
| Closed | Seollal and Chuseok holidays (Mondays are not in the schedule) |

According to Hyundai Card's DIVE space guide:

- **Ground-floor café: open to everyone**
- **Second-floor library: Hyundai Card members plus two guests**, or DIVE app members on their own
- **Eight visits a month**, **aged 19 and over**, card (or app) and ID checked
- **About 10,000 vinyl records** plus rare overseas pressings, played on turntables
- **Up to 30 people** at once, then a waitlist
- No parking or valet; no pets, outside food or professional cameras; the whole building is non-smoking

The official guide does not make clear whether overseas visitors can get into the library, so plan on the café.

## Namsan Korean Forest Garden: the old botanical garden, reopened in June

![A red wooden footbridge over a pond of water lilies and irises, surrounded by pines and broadleaf trees, at the Namsan Outdoor Botanical Garden](/images/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

The **Namsan Outdoor Botanical Garden** on Namsan's southern slope reopened on **27 June 2026** as the Korean Forest Garden (Hanguk Sup Jeongwon). The photo above is filed with the KTO under the old name, so it may show the garden before the makeover.

- Address: **259-16 Itaewon-dong**, about **30,000㎡**
- The original garden opened in **1997** under a project to restore Namsan's natural landscape (Seoul city media)
- **Three themes and 11 gardens** modelled on Korea's representative forests and gardens

| Theme | Gardens |
| --- | --- |
| Tradition and culture | Jidangwon (pond), Yeongjiwon, Mugunghwa (rose of Sharon) garden |
| Nature and ecology | Azalea hill, plum garden, moss garden, bamboo grove, pine garden |
| Rest and relaxation | Pine plaza, ginkgo lawn, Namsan Maru |

Seoul city media say the design brings in regional forest scenes such as Damyang's bamboo and Jeju's traditional woods. Past the pond, heading for the Subokcheon spring, you join the **pine forest trail**, which needs no booking.

- Getting there: from [[line:6]] Hangangjin Station exit 2, take [[bus:405]] to the **Namsan Arts Center** stop (or the Hyatt hotel), per the city's directions.
- Enquiries: Namsan Park office **02-3783-5900**
- **We could not find opening hours or closing days in the reopening notices.**

The top end of Gyeongridan-gil also arrives at this garden; the street itself is on [the Itaewon page](/en/posts/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Yongsan Park: two former base sites, no booking needed

Of the land returned from the US Army's Yongsan Garrison, **two areas** are currently open to the public. The Yongsan Park website lists both under "temporary opening": **Yongsan Children's Garden** and **Officers' Quarters Complex 5**. Both are free, and both close on Mondays. Neither is near Hangangjin, so **give them a separate half-day**.

### Yongsan Children's Garden: booking scrapped on 30 December 2025

According to the government's Korea.kr briefing, **from 30 December 2025 the advance booking system, ID checks and security screening were all dropped.** You simply walk in during opening hours.

| | Open | Last entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tue-Fri, Sun | 09:00-18:00 | 17:00 |
| Sat | 09:00-**21:00** | 20:00 |
| Closed | **Mondays**, 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok days | |

Last-entry times come from the KTO; indoor facilities close earlier, at 17:30 on weekdays and 20:30 on Saturdays. **When a Monday is a public holiday, it closes the following day instead.**

- Address: **29 Hangang-daero 38-gil** (Yongsan Park website)
- Five minutes' walk from exit 1 of Sinyongsan Station on [[line:4]], ten from exit 1 of Yongsan Station
- **No parking.** The official advice is to use public transport.
- No e-scooters or pets; push strollers are fine (Seoul city media)
- Stroller and wheelchair loans at the information centre; an in-garden shuttle runs on the hour on weekdays and three times an hour at weekends

Seoul city media and the KTO mention the **Yongsan Seoga** library, exhibition spaces, a viewing hill and a café. The garden's homepage lists programmes such as autumn busking (18 Sep-31 Oct), an autumn-leaves walk (2 Oct-29 Nov) and a media-art show (1 Sep-29 Nov); check the site for times.

The government said it would **rename the garden in the first half of 2026** to something not tied to one age group, but in October the official site still uses the name Yongsan Children's Garden.

### Officers' Quarters Complex 5: a red-brick housing estate

![Two-storey red-brick houses with red tiled roofs under a clear autumn sky, a large white Yongsan Park letter sculpture on the lawn in front and apartment towers behind](/images/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

This former US officers' housing in the south-east of the garrison opened earlier, in **August 2020**. The Yongsan Park website explains that the site held **Seobinggo**, the Joseon-era royal ice store; after liberation the US Army called the area "Camp Sobinggo". The Korean government took back about 49,368㎡ in **1986** and built officers' housing there.

- Address: **221 Seobinggo-ro**
- **Tue-Sun 09:00-18:00**, last entry **17:30** (Yongsan Park website)
- Closed: Mondays (or the next day when Monday is a holiday), 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok days
- **Free**; up to **500 people at a time**, so expect a queue when busy
- **Ten of the 18 blocks** remodelled as a children's library, playgrounds, rest areas and exhibition rooms
- **No parking for general visitors.** Only vehicles for disabled drivers and cars carrying two or more children under 13 may park, free

## Quick summary

- **Leeum**: book 14 days ahead, up to four people at a time. **Koo Jeong A: OUSSSMOS** runs 5 Sep-27 Dec; the traditional art galleries and the Orozco garden are free. Five minutes from Hangangjin exit 1.
- **Blue Square**: walk in from the station passage. *Elisabeth* runs to 15 November.
- **Music Library**: upstairs is for Hyundai Card and DIVE members; the café is open to all. Closed Mondays.
- **Namsan Korean Forest Garden**: reopened 27 June 2026 with 11 gardens; [[bus:405]] from Hangangjin.
- **Yongsan Children's Garden**: **free, no booking** since 30 December 2025. Open to 21:00 on Saturdays only, closed Mondays, no parking.
- **Officers' Quarters Complex 5**: Tue-Sun 09:00-18:00, 500 people at a time, no general parking.
