If you have already done Itaewon's big three, the more useful question is what has **newly opened**. Parts of the former US Army base can now be entered without booking, and the botanical garden on Namsan's southern slope reopened under a new name this June.

Our [Itaewon things-to-do guide](/en/posts/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/) covers the War Memorial, Leeum and the Seoul Central Mosque in brief. This post does not repeat them: for **Leeum it adds only the autumn exhibitions and booking rules**, for the mosque **only the visiting etiquette**, and then moves on to other places. For the area as a whole, see [the Itaewon and Hannam-dong overview](/en/posts/hannam-guide-2026-09/). All hours and prices are **as of September 2026**.

The National Museum of Korea is close by too, but it has [its own guide](/en/posts/seoul-national-museum-of-korea-2026-09/).

## Yongsan Park: two former base sites, no booking needed

Of the land returned from the US Army's Yongsan Garrison, **two areas** are currently open to the public. The Yongsan Park website lists both under "temporary opening": **Yongsan Children's Garden** and **Officers' Quarters Complex 5**. Both are free, and both close on Mondays.

### Yongsan Children's Garden: booking scrapped on 30 December 2025

According to the government's Korea.kr briefing, **from 30 December 2025 the advance booking system, ID checks and security screening were all dropped.** You simply walk in during opening hours.

| | Open | Last entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tue-Fri, Sun | 09:00-18:00 | 17:00 |
| Sat | 09:00-**21:00** | 20:00 |
| Closed | **Mondays**, 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok days | |

Last-entry times come from the Korea Tourism Organization (KTO); indoor facilities close earlier, at 17:30 on weekdays and 20:30 on Saturdays. **When a Monday is a public holiday, it closes the following day instead.**

- Address: **29 Hangang-daero 38-gil** (Yongsan Park website)
- Five minutes' walk from exit 1 of Sinyongsan Station on [[line:4]], ten from exit 1 of Yongsan Station
- **No parking.** The official advice is to use public transport.
- No e-scooters or pets; push strollers are fine (Seoul city media)
- Stroller and wheelchair loans at the information centre; an in-garden shuttle runs on the hour on weekdays and three times an hour at weekends

Seoul city media and the KTO mention the **Yongsan Seoga** library, exhibition spaces, a viewing hill and a café. The garden's homepage currently lists programmes such as autumn busking (18 Sep-31 Oct), an autumn-leaves walk (2 Oct-29 Nov) and a media-art show (1 Sep-29 Nov); check the site for times.

The government said it would **rename the garden in the first half of 2026** to something not tied to one age group, but in September the official site still uses the name Yongsan Children's Garden.

### Officers' Quarters Complex 5: a red-brick housing estate

![Two-storey red-brick houses with red tiled roofs under a clear autumn sky, a large white Yongsan Park letter sculpture on the lawn in front and apartment towers behind](/images/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

This former US officers' housing in the south-east of the garrison opened earlier, in **August 2020**. The Yongsan Park website explains that the site held **Seobinggo**, the Joseon-era royal ice store; after liberation the US Army called the area "Camp Sobinggo". The Korean government took back about 49,368㎡ in **1986** and built officers' housing there.

- Address: **221 Seobinggo-ro**
- **Tue-Sun 09:00-18:00**, last entry **17:30** (Yongsan Park website)
- Closed: Mondays (or the next day when Monday is a holiday), 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok days
- **Free**; up to **500 people at a time**, so expect a queue when busy
- **Ten of the 18 blocks** remodelled as a children's library, playgrounds, rest areas and exhibition rooms
- **No parking for general visitors.** Only vehicles for disabled drivers and cars carrying two or more children under 13 may park, free

It is a fair distance from Hannam-dong, so it works better as a separate half-day.

Leeum's visitor information page sets these rules:

- **Online booking opens 14 days before** your visit, **up to four people** per booking
- Walk-in tickets are sold, but **waits can be long** at busy times

- Parking is limited and only until 18:00. From exit 1 of Hangangjin Station on [[line:6]], walk 100m towards Itaewon, turn right into the first alley and climb for about five minutes.

## Hyundai Card Music Library: check the entry rules first

It is one of the most-mentioned names on Itaewon-ro, but **not everyone can go upstairs.**

| | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| Tue-Sat | 12:00-21:00 |
| Sun & public holidays | 12:00-18:00 |
| Closed | Seollal and Chuseok holidays (Mondays are not in the schedule) |

According to Hyundai Card's DIVE space guide:

- **Ground-floor café: open to everyone**
- **Second-floor library: Hyundai Card members plus two guests**, or DIVE app members on their own
- **Eight visits a month**, **aged 19 and over**, card (or app) and ID checked
- **About 10,000 vinyl records** plus rare overseas pressings, played on turntables
- **Up to 30 people** at once, then a waitlist
- No parking or valet; no pets, outside food or professional cameras; the whole building is non-smoking

The official guide does not make clear whether overseas visitors can get into the library, so plan on the café. Address: **246 Itaewon-ro**.

## Seoul Central Mosque: etiquette before you go

The mosque itself is introduced in [our Itaewon guide](/en/posts/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/). Here are only the **visiting rules given by the Korea Muslim Federation and public tourism sites**.

- Korea's first mosque, opened on **21 May 1976** (VISIT SEOUL)
- The Korea Muslim Federation calls it an **open space**, but it is a place of worship and dress matters.
- **No sleeveless tops, short skirts or shorts.** If you are dressed that way, borrow a skirt or headscarf from the **room next to the security office**.
- **Do not photograph people praying** or disturb prayers.
- **No smoking** inside
- VISIT SEOUL notes that prayer times shift with sunrise.
- The KTO lists **no lift, no accessible toilet and no parking**; the approach is a steep hill, hard going with a wheelchair or stroller.

It is about 477m from exit 3 of Itaewon Station on [[line:6]], at **39 Usadan-ro 10-gil**; tel. 02-793-6908.

## Haebangchon: Sinheung Market and the 108 steps

![A sloping street in Haebangchon under cherry blossoms, a sign pointing to Sinheung Market and Haebangchon on the building at right, and N Seoul Tower beyond the trees](/images/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/09.webp)

Haebangchon is the hillside neighbourhood directly below Namsan. Its name, "liberation village", comes from the refugees from the North and war evacuees who settled here after **liberation in 1945**.

### Sinheung Market: a roof you can see the sky through

A column by an urban architect on Seoul's media hub describes it like this:

- A daily-goods market for residents from **1953**, in decline from the 1990s
- Urban regeneration and the merchants' own efforts produced today's mix of old and new shops
- A **looping, ㅂ-shaped layout** rather than one straight aisle
- A transparent ETFE canopy called **Seoul Chaeng**, finished in **2018**, lets daylight deep into the alleys

Shop hours vary, so they are not listed here. The food is in [Itaewon and Hannam-dong food](/en/posts/hannam-food-2026-09/).

### The Haebangchon 108 steps

According to the Seoul Future Heritage site, these steps were built in **1943**, under Japanese rule, as the approach to the Gyeongseong Hokoku Shrine. Once a single wide stone staircase, they are now split by a garden strip.

- Designated **Seoul Future Heritage in 2015**
- An **inclined lift** added in 2017-2018 runs bottom to top in **about a minute**
- 1960s-70s knitting workshops still survive nearby

We could not confirm the lift's operating hours from an official source.

## Namsan Korean Forest Garden: the old botanical garden, reopened in June

![A red wooden footbridge over a pond of water lilies and irises, surrounded by pines and broadleaf trees, at the Namsan Outdoor Botanical Garden](/images/hannam-things-to-do-2026-09/01.webp)

The **Namsan Outdoor Botanical Garden**, on Namsan's slope in Itaewon-dong, reopened on **27 June 2026** as the Korean Forest Garden (Hanguk Sup Jeongwon). The photo above is filed with the KTO under the old name, so it may show the garden before the makeover.

- Address: **259-16 Itaewon-dong**, about **30,000㎡**
- The original garden opened in **1997** under a project to restore Namsan's natural landscape (Seoul city media)
- **Three themes and 11 gardens** modelled on Korea's representative forests and gardens

| Theme | Gardens |
| --- | --- |
| Tradition and culture | Jidangwon (pond), Yeongjiwon, Mugunghwa (rose of Sharon) garden |
| Nature and ecology | Azalea hill, plum garden, moss garden, bamboo grove, pine garden |
| Rest and relaxation | Pine plaza, ginkgo lawn, Namsan Maru |

Seoul city media say the design brings in regional forest scenes such as Damyang's bamboo and Jeju's traditional woods. Past the pond, heading for the Subokcheon spring, you join the **pine forest trail**, which needs no booking.

The city's directions: from exit 2 of [[line:6]] Hangangjin Station, take [[bus:405]] to the **Namsan Arts Center** stop (or the Hyatt hotel). Namsan Park office: **02-3783-5900**.

**We could not find opening hours or closing days in the reopening notices.**

## Itaewon Antique Furniture Street

A 2016 Edaily report on Yongsan-gu's street upgrade gives the extent:

- **Bogwang-ro** from Itaewon Station to Cheonghwa Apartments, about 450m
- **Noksapyeong-daero 26-gil** from Cheonghwa Apartments to the Saudi Arabian Embassy, about 310m
- The street grew from the 1970s as departing US officers and embassy staff sold off their furniture
- **85 shops** at the time (2016 figure)

Opening days differ shop by shop. We could **not confirm autumn 2026 dates** for the antique festival that used to run in spring and autumn.

## 3-4 October: a street festival on Itaewon-ro

**We found no confirmed 2026 dates for the Itaewon Global Village Festival.** Seoul's culture portal still shows only the 2022 edition (15-16 October).

Instead, several outlets report 「**2026 K-Wave Dance Festival**」 on the same stretch of Itaewon-ro:

- **Saturday 3 and Sunday 4 October 2026, 12:00-21:00**
- Itaewon-ro between Itaewon and Noksapyeong stations, about 400-480m
- **Free**
- 30 world-food stalls plus 10 Korean-food stalls
- World dance parade at 17:30 on 3 October; "Street Moon Night" at 20:00 on both days
- Hosted by the Korea Contemporary Dance Promotion Association, with support from the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, Yongsan-gu and the Itaewon Special Tourist Zone association

Because it takes over the road, traffic on this stretch may change; check Yongsan-gu notices for closures.

## 29 October: the Road of Memory and Safety

About 40m from exit 1 of Itaewon Station, the alley beside the Hamilton Hotel became the **10·29 Road of Memory and Safety** in October 2023, remembering the crowd crush of 29 October 2022. According to the Kyunghyang Shinmun, the entrance reads "We still have names to remember", with memorial plaques on the hotel wall and the road's name set into the pavement at the end of the alley.

It is **a place of remembrance, not a photo spot.** If you pass through in late October, slow down here.

**Monday 5 October is a substitute holiday** for National Foundation Day, which falls on a Saturday this year. Under their "closed the day after a Monday holiday" rule, the Children's Garden and Complex 5 should **open on 5 October and close on Tuesday 6 October**. We could not find how Leeum and the Music Library handle substitute holidays.

## A route by time of day

**Morning: Yongsan Park**
[[line:4]] to Sinyongsan and walk to the Children's Garden, open from 09:00. Complex 5 (221 Seobinggo-ro) is better as its own half-day.

**Around noon: Hangangjin**
[[line:6]] to Hangangjin. Leeum at your booked slot (book 14 days ahead), or the Music Library café, open from 12:00.

**Afternoon: Namsan's slope**
[[bus:405]] from exit 2 to the Korean Forest Garden, then on to the pine forest trail.

**Late afternoon: Haebangchon**
Seoul's media hub describes Sinheung Market's canopy as appealing after dark too. Take the lift up the 108 steps.

**Evening: Blue Square**
If you have tickets, walk straight in from the station passage.

**On 3-4 October**
Give the afternoon to the Itaewon-ro festival (12:00-21:00).

## Wrapping up

- **Yongsan Children's Garden**: **free, no booking** since 30 December 2025. Open to 21:00 on Saturdays only, closed Mondays, no parking.
- **Officers' Quarters Complex 5**: Tue-Sun 09:00-18:00, 500 people at a time, no general parking.
- **Leeum**: book 14 days ahead, four per booking. **Koo Jeong A: OUSSSMOS** runs 5 Sep-27 Dec; the traditional art galleries and the Orozco garden are free.
- **Music Library**: upstairs is for Hyundai Card and DIVE members; the café is open to all.
- **Seoul Central Mosque**: no sleeveless tops, short skirts or shorts, and no photos of worshippers.
- **Namsan Korean Forest Garden**: reopened 27 June 2026 with 11 gardens.
- **3-4 October**: K-Wave Dance Festival on Itaewon-ro; **5 October** is a substitute holiday.
