Search for a place to stay in Yeonnam-dong (연남동), the leafy neighbourhood just north of Hongdae, and you get hundreds of guesthouses, Airbnb listings and "Yeonnam stays". Count only the **legal neighbourhoods of Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong (망원동)** in Seoul's licensing data, though, and the picture is lopsided.

- **562 foreign-tourist urban homestays**: 413 in Yeonnam-dong and 149 in Mangwon-dong. **Yeonnam-dong has more than any of Seoul's 329 legal neighbourhoods**; Mangwon-dong ranks 10th
- **One tourist accommodation**: Chris House, a six-room hostel in Yeonnam-dong registered in July 2026
- **One lodging business (hotel or inn)**: a ten-room inn in Mangwon-dong licensed in 1970. **Yeonnam-dong has not a single record in the lodging data**
- **No registered hanok stays**

The short version: **almost every place to stay in Yeonnam and Mangwon is a room in somebody's home, and by law that kind of stay is for foreign visitors only.** This post goes through both neighbourhoods one by one, then covers Seongsan-dong and Yeonhui-dong just outside, Donggyo-dong for anyone who needs a proper hotel, and the airport train and buses with last departures by stop.

## How these were counted

The four licensing datasets on the Seoul Open Data Plaza were filtered to **addresses in Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong, Mapo-gu**. Tourist accommodation, lodging and hanok data are as of 24 September 2026, homestays as of 27 September; only places licensed by that date and trading at that point are counted. This is the same data and method as the [Hongdae](/en/posts/hongdae-hotels-2026-09/), [Seongsu](/en/posts/seongsu-hotels-2026-09/) and [Gangnam](/en/posts/gangnam-hotels-2026-09/) accommodation posts.

| Dataset | Law | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tourist accommodation | Tourism Promotion Act (registration) | Tourist hotels, hostels, family hotels, small hotels |
| Lodging business | Public Health Control Act (notification) | Inns, motels, general hotels, serviced residences — and tourist hotels and hostels again |
| Foreign-tourist urban homestay | Tourism Promotion Act (designation) | A resident's own home taking in foreign visitors |
| Hanok experience | Tourism Promotion Act (registration) | Stays in traditional Korean houses |

| Legal neighbourhood | Tourist accommodation | Lodging | Homestays (rooms) | Hanok |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yeonnam-dong (north of Hongik Univ. Station Exit 3, either side of the forest park) | 1 (hostel, 6 rooms) | **0** | **413 (688)** | 0 |
| Mangwon-dong (Mangwon Station on Line 6, Mangwon Market, the Han River park) | 0 | 1 (inn, 10 rooms) | 149 (214) | 0 |
| **Total** | **1** | **1** | **562 (902)** | **0** |

Three things to know:

- **Homestay room totals add up the room numbers recorded in the data.** Five of Yeonnam-dong's 413 homestays leave that field blank, so they are not in the 688
- **"Yeonnam" is also a neighbourhood brand.** The street name Yeonnam-ro carries on into Seongsan-dong, and places around Hongik University Station in Donggyo-dong and Seogyo-dong often use Yeonnam in their names. These are listed under "Just outside the boundary"
- **Seongsan-dong sits between the two.** It lies west of Yeonnam-dong and north of Mangwon-dong, so a listing that feels like "Yeonnam" or "Mangwon" is often in Seongsan-dong by address

## Yeonnam-dong: 413 homestays and one hostel

### The one tourist accommodation: Chris House

Yeonnam-dong's only tourist accommodation is **Chris House** in the MGI Building at 15 World Cup buk-ro 6-gil. It is registered as a hostel with **six rooms**, from **21 July 2026**. It is also the only record with a Yeonnam-dong address in the tourist accommodation dataset, closed businesses included.

Hostels count as tourist accommodation under the Tourism Promotion Act, so **Korean nationals can stay too**. For a Korean traveller, this is effectively the only registered option inside Yeonnam-dong. Many Korean "hostels" are mostly private rooms, so check the room layout when you book.

### Not one lodging record

The lodging dataset keeps records going back to the 1960s, closed inns and motels included. **There is not one record with a Yeonnam-dong address, open or closed.** On the data, this is a residential area where no inn or hotel has ever traded, and where homestays started appearing in 2012.

## Homestays are for foreign visitors only

Article 2 of the Enforcement Decree of the Tourism Promotion Act defines a foreign-tourist urban homestay as **a city resident offering lodging in the home they live in** to **foreign tourists**, so that they can experience Korean home life. The qualifying housing types are detached houses, multi-household houses, apartments, row houses and multi-family houses.

That has three consequences:

1. **Korean nationals generally cannot stay in them.** If you are travelling with a Korean friend or partner, the options are Chris House in Yeonnam-dong or the hostels and hotels just outside, below
2. **The host has to live there.** Renting out an empty flat as a whole does not become legal under this licence
3. **Officetels (studio-office buildings) are not on the list of qualifying housing**, so a short-term officetel rental cannot be designated as a homestay

The rules are enforced. Mapo-gu said it inspected **252 lodging businesses in 2019 and caught 19**: six registered homestays that had broken the Tourism Promotion Act, for example by taking Korean guests, and 13 unregistered operations (Green Economy Newspaper, 31 December 2019). The same report describes Mapo-gu as the area with the most registered lodging businesses in the country, because homestays flourish around Hongdae and the Gyeongui Line Forest Park.

### If you are booking on Airbnb

Airbnb made business registration details and a registration certificate **mandatory for new Korean listings from 2 October 2024, and for existing listings from 16 October 2025**. Listings without them **cannot take bookings for stays from 1 January 2026** (Airbnb Newsroom, 18 August 2025).

So a Yeonnam or Mangwon listing on Airbnb today has, in principle, been registered in some form. **What the platform may not filter is which licence it holds and whether Korean nationals can stay.** If you are Korean, or travelling with someone who is, ask the host directly before booking.

### How to check a listing is registered

- The Seoul Tourism Organization's **Seoul Stay** site lists homestays and hanok stays that have completed legal registration with their district and that the city helps promote
- **The Seoul Open Data Plaza homestay dataset** is the most reliable check: search the name or address to see its status, designation date and room count

## Staying in a residential street

![A residential lane in Yeonnam-dong, a small shop with a green awning and low multi-family buildings along a narrow street, sandwich boards out front and red-brick houses in the distance](/images/yeonnam-hotels-2026-09/04.webp)

Most Yeonnam and Mangwon homestays are in low-rise houses. Worth knowing before you go:

- **The neighbours really live there**, and so does your host. Late-night conversations in the lane and suitcase wheels carry
- **Many multi-household houses have no lift.** With heavy luggage, ask which floor you are on
- **There is no 24-hour front desk.** Get check-in instructions in advance if you will arrive late
- **Rubbish rules** vary. Korea uses paid rubbish bags and strict recycling separation, so ask your host at check-in
- **For quiet nights**, a block or two back from the Gyeongui Line Forest Park ("Yeontral Park") beats the park edge, which stays busy into the evening. In Mangwon-dong, the lanes between Mangwon Market and the Han River park are a fairly quiet residential area

The neighbourhood story is in the [Yeonnam-dong & Mangwon guide](/en/posts/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/), and places to walk to are in [things to do in Yeonnam & Mangwon](/en/posts/yeonnam-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Getting to the airport

Yeonnam-dong's biggest practical advantage is that **both the airport train and the airport bus leave from Hongik University Station without a change**. Yeonnam-dong is on the Exit 3 side. Mangwon-dong is on [[line:6]] at Mangwon Station, so you need one change.

### Airport Railroad (AREX)

The AREX [[line:arex]] all-stop train calls at Hongik University. **The Express train runs nonstop between Seoul Station and the airport, so it does not stop here.** Fares are as of writing.

| Route | Fare (transit card) | First | Last |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hongik Univ. → Incheon Airport T1 | ₩4,650 (about $3.50) | 05:28 | 23:48 |
| Hongik Univ. → Incheon Airport T2 | ₩5,250 | 05:28 | **23:48** |

Weekday and weekend times are the same. Trains after 23:48 run only as far as Geomam, so do not mistake them for airport trains.

**Coming from the airport**, the last all-stop trains to Seoul Station leave **T1 at 23:38 and T2 at 23:32**. The later 23:56 (T1) and 23:50 (T2) trains **terminate at Digital Media City and do not reach Hongik University**.

### Staying in Mangwon-dong

- **From Digital Media City**, change to [[line:6]]: World Cup Stadium, Mapo-gu Office, then Mangwon — three stops. This also works if you end up on a late train that terminates at Digital Media City
- **From Hongik University**, take [[line:2]] one stop to Hapjeong, then [[line:6]] one stop to Mangwon

## Which to choose

- **Foreign visitor who wants to live in the lanes** — a Yeonnam-dong homestay. With 413 it has the widest choice in Seoul, and AREX and bus 6002 at Hongik University Station are close
- **Foreign visitor who prefers newer places and a quieter area** — Mangwon-dong. 139 of its 149 homestays date from 2024 or later, near Mangwon Market and the Han River park
- **Korean national, or travelling with one** — homestays are generally off-limits. Choose Chris House (six rooms) in Yeonnam-dong, or the hotels and hostels in Donggyo-dong and Seogyo-dong
- **Need a front desk and breakfast** — the Yanghwa-ro hotels in Donggyo-dong: L7 Hongdae (340 rooms), Holiday Inn Express (294) and Mercure (270)
- **Early-morning flight** — southern Yeonnam-dong, close to Hongik University Station, where you can use both N6002 (02:20–04:00) and the first AREX train (05:28)
- **Lots of luggage** — check for a lift first; 83% of Yeonnam-dong homestays are in multi-household or detached houses

## Summary

- Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong have **562 foreign-tourist urban homestays**. **Yeonnam-dong's 413 (688 rooms) are the most of any Seoul neighbourhood**; Mangwon-dong's 149 rank 10th.
- **The only tourist accommodation is Chris House (six rooms) in Yeonnam-dong**, and the only lodging business is a 1970 inn in Mangwon-dong. Yeonnam-dong has no lodging records at all.
- Yeonnam-dong's homestays go back to 2012, with **59% designated since 2024**; in Mangwon-dong it is **93%**.
- Homestays must be **the host's own home** and are **for foreign visitors only**. Airbnb has blocked bookings at unregistered listings from 1 January 2026.
- For a hotel, go to **Donggyo-dong, south of Hongik University Station** (L7, Holiday Inn Express, Mercure).
- Last **AREX from Hongik University to the airport: 23:48**; airport bus **6002 from Hongik Univ. 21:11, Hapjeong 21:17**; **6011 from Mapo-gu Office Station 21:03**; and the late-night **N6002**.
