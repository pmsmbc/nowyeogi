On 17 September 2026, Seoul Administrative Court sided with Jongno district office in a lawsuit brought by Bukchon shopkeepers and tour-bus drivers against the **ban on charter buses** in the neighbourhood (Hankook Ilbo). Two weeks later, on 2 October, the city opened its biggest-ever Seoul Hanok Week across Bukchon and Seochon, and made a point of asking visitors to **keep quiet out of consideration for residents** (Maeil Ilbo). Bukchon is Seoul's most visited hanok neighbourhood, and also **the first place in Korea where the law decides when tourists may come in and how they may arrive**.

The short version: **Bukchon was a Joseon-era district of aristocrats and officials, rebuilt in the 1930s as estates of hanok for sale, saved after 40 years of swinging between protection and deregulation, and then, once crowds descended on those saved lanes, given a curfew for tourists.** This guide rebuilds that story from statutes, city notices, the district office's own FAQ and dated press reports. Museums, workshops and viewpoints are in [Bukchon: things to do](/en/posts/bukchon-things-to-do-2026-09/), restaurants and cafés in [Bukchon food](/en/posts/bukchon-food-2026-09/), and hanok stays plus the red-zone rules for hotel guests in [Bukchon hotels](/en/posts/bukchon-hotels-2026-09/).

This lane is **Bukchon-ro 11-gil**, the most photographed street in Bukchon, and today it is the **red zone**. The photo was taken at sunrise. Since November 2024, **entering this lane as a tourist before 10am is not allowed**. Details below.

## Bukchon at a glance

"Bukchon" (북촌, literally "north village") is not an administrative name. The Seoul Hanok Portal explains it as **"the neighbourhood above Cheonggyecheon stream and Jongno"**. That is why different documents draw it differently.

| Name | What it covers | Size | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bukchon** (Seoul Hanok Portal) | Gahoe-dong, Gye-dong, Samcheong-dong, Wonseo-dong, Jae-dong and Palpan-dong, bounded by the two palaces, Yulgok-ro and Samcheong Park | **1,076,302㎡** | Seoul Hanok Portal |
| **Bukchon district unit plan area** | The planning designation. The 2020 revision notice covers **11 legal neighbourhoods**: Gahoe, Gye, Wonseo, Anguk, Jae, Hwa, Sagan, Sogyeok, Songhyeon, Palpan and Samcheong-dong | **1,128,372.7㎡** | Seoul Notice No. 2020-126, Sijung Ilbo |

### Reading Bukchon by its streets

The Seoul Hanok Portal says the defining features of Bukchon are **its terrain and its watercourses**. The ground is low in the south and steepens to the north, forming four small valleys, and the streets follow the streams north to south. Those streets are today's **Samcheong-dong-gil, Gahoe-dong-gil, Gyedong-gil and Wonseo-dong-gil**, and the portal notes that Gyedong-gil, Gahoe-ro, Samcheong-dong-gil and Changdeokgung-gil keep the same lines from the 1750 capital map to today.

## The red zone and the special management area: rules as of October 2026

For a first visit, this is the part that matters most.

### The law came in 2019; the power to fine came in 2021

| Date | Law or step | What it did | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Promulgated 3 Dec 2019 | Tourism Promotion Act amendment (Act No. 16684) | **Created special management areas** (Article 48-3): districts where crowds beyond capacity threaten residents' peaceful lives, where measures such as limiting tourist visiting hours can be taken | Korea Law Information Center |
| 4 Jun 2020 | In force | The enforcement decree added a mandatory public hearing for residents before designation | Korea Law Information Center |
| Promulgated 13 Apr 2021, **in force 14 Oct 2021** | Amendment (Act No. 18009) | Added **fees, and restrictions on vehicles and tourists**, to what a local ordinance may impose, and allowed **fines** for breaches (Article 48-3, paragraphs 6 and 7); required signboards | Korea Law Information Center |
| Nov 2022 to Dec 2023 | Jongno-gu study | Research on designation and management measures | Sijung Ilbo |
| **1 Jul 2024** | Jongno-gu designation | **Korea's first special management area** | Jongno-gu, Sijung Ilbo |

The 2019 amendment's stated reason is that "in some tourist areas, visitors beyond capacity have been crowding in, and the damage to residents has emerged as a serious problem". But **the legal basis for blocking passage and fining people arrived only with the 2021 amendment**. Jongno-gu cites "Article 48-3(7) of the Tourism Promotion Act" for the charter bus fine.

### Four zones

Putting the July 2024 announcement (as reported by Sijung Ilbo) next to the district office's current page:

| Zone | Location and size | As of October 2026 | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Red zone** | Around Bukchon-ro 11-gil, **about 34,000㎡** | **Tourists barred 17:00 to 10:00 next day**, 100,000 won fine | Jongno-gu |
| Orange zone | Bukchon-ro 5ga-gil (26,400㎡), around Gyedong-gil (34,000㎡) | No time limit; focused guidance. **Missing from the district's web page**, but still shown on the on-site signs in an August 2026 report | Sijung Ilbo 2024, BizHankook 2026 |
| Yellow zone | Around Bukchon-ro 12-gil (11,700㎡) | No fine; visitors asked to come 10:00-17:00; intensive monitoring | Jongno-gu, Sijung Ilbo |
| **Charter bus restriction** | Bukchon-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil, Bukchon-ro 4-gil, Changdeokgung 1-gil, **about 2.3km** | **Banned at all times**, fines since 1 Jan 2026 | Jongno-gu |

**The red zone is the only place where an individual visitor can be fined.** The bus restriction is aimed at bus operators and drivers.

Gates opening straight onto the lane with a few steps in front: this is what Bukchon's residential streets look like. A house with a number plate is, in most cases, someone's home.

### Red zone rules

From Jongno-gu's official page.

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Tourist hours | **10:00 to 17:00** |
| Restricted hours | **17:00 to 10:00 next day** |
| Grace period | 1 Nov 2024 to 28 Feb 2025 |
| Fines from | **1 March 2025** |
| Fine | **100,000 won** (about $75). May be reduced by up to half under the district ordinance |
| Enforcement | On-site checks by **"Bukchon sheriffs"** (dedicated enforcement officials). Fines only after guidance and a warning are ignored |
| Days | Every day (no exceptions listed) |

**Exempt** are residents, their family and friends, shop customers, merchants and people simply passing through. According to the district office, hotel guests are let in on showing a **booking confirmation**, and shop customers can explain their purpose or show a receipt. A BizHankook report in August 2026 added that **museum visitors** can also enter after being checked.

The catch is the definition of "tourism". Jongno-gu's FAQ, translated:

> This includes taking photos or videos, lingering while looking around, and wandering the street for sightseeing unrelated to using a shop. Such acts are regarded as tourism and, **even for exempt persons**, may be judged a violation and fined.

In practice:

- **Even as a hotel guest**, taking photos in the lane after 5pm can get you fined.
- **Long-stay guests (month-long rentals) are not residents.** Residents are only people whose resident card or foreigner registration card shows an address inside the red zone.
- **Foreign visitors are treated the same.** Under the Act on the Regulation of Violations of Public Order, officials may record a passport number and issue a prior notice on the spot; the fine can be paid immediately or before leaving Korea.
- Vehicles of exempt people may drive through, but **getting out to sightsee** is finable.

Jongno-gu also explains why it does not charge an entry fee: it would "cement Bukchon as a tourist attraction". So the structure is simple: **free during the day, closed to tourism at night and in the early morning.**

### How many fines have actually been issued?

| When | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 Mar 2025 | Four days into fines: **zero** paid | The PR |
| Late Sep 2025 | District mayor Jeong Mun-heon: "Over the past year or so, **not a single fine** has been imposed" | Hankook Ilbo, 14 Oct 2025 |
| 6 Aug 2026 | As 5pm approached, Bukchon sheriffs guided tourists out of the red zone and they left without friction | BizHankook |

Fines are rare because **officers explain and warn first, and fine only those who ignore them**. We could not find any official count published after October 2025.

### The 2026 debate: is 5pm too early?

BizHankook (August 2026) found residents and merchants split. A resident of 35 years said it was "definitely quieter than before"; a shopkeeper of seven years asked for "6pm or 6:30pm in summer, when the sun sets late". Jongno-gu says it may consider **flexible hours such as a summer extension** after monitoring visitor and sales data, while the mayor said in October 2025 that the current rule would stay "for the time being". **As of October 2026, it is still 10am to 5pm.**

### The September 2026 ruling

According to Hankook Ilbo, Seoul Administrative Court's 7th Administrative Division ruled on **17 September 2026**:

- **The drivers' claim was dismissed on the merits.** The measure stayed within the purpose and scope of the Tourism Promotion Act and did not breach proportionality.
- **The merchants' claim was thrown out** for lack of standing, since the measure does not restrict their trading hours, place or methods.
- The court cited figures showing **visitors to the hanok village in 2025 were up 16 to 18% on the same period of 2024**, so the measure could not be said to have depressed visits.

This is a first-instance ruling; we could not confirm whether it was appealed.

## What kind of place Bukchon was

### A district of aristocrats and officials

The Seoul Hanok Portal says Bukchon, on the southern slopes of the ridge linking Bugaksan and Eungbong, was long home to powerful families. It gives one telling number: in the **1906 household register, 43.6% of the 10,241 people (1,932 households) lived in households headed by yangban aristocrats or officials**. The area holds five historic sites, four Seoul folklore heritage items, three tangible cultural properties and one cultural heritage material, according to the portal.

### The 1930s: big estates subdivided into hanok for sale

![Two Bukchon hanok on a lane corner in clear morning sunlight, with tiled roofs above walls of red brick and granite, and a rocky ridge rising behind on the right](/images/bukchon-guide-2026-09/02.webp)

Most of the hanok you see are not aristocrats' houses. The Seoul Hanok Portal, translated:

> Housing companies bought Bukchon's large plots and wooded land and built small and medium hanok on them en masse; the hanok clusters of Gahoe-dong 11 and 31, Samcheong-dong 35 and Gye-dong 135 were all formed in this period.

"This period" is around the 1930s. The portal says large plots were split up after 1912 because of a housing shortage, and that these hanok used **new materials such as glass and tile, partly standardised floor plans, glass doors on the main hall and tin awnings under the eaves**, built from standard timber supplied by sawmills. They were a new type of urban house. The best-known developer was **Jeong Se-gwon** of Geonyangsa; his story is told in detail in the [Ikseon-dong guide](/en/posts/ikseon-guide-2026-09/), where he built in the same way.

The portal adds that such housing kept going up until the early 1960s, so that apart from a few school and institutional sites, almost all of Bukchon was hanok.

### 1970s to 1990s: the schools left, and the rules swung

| When | What happened | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1976 | Kyunggi High School moved out; its building became Jeongdok Library. Talk of a "folk landscape area" | Seoul Hanok Portal |
| 1978 | Whimoon High School moved out; a 15-storey Hyundai Engineering & Construction HQ rose in 1983 | Seoul Hanok Portal |
| **1983** | **Designated a "Type 4 aesthetic district"**: hanok protection begins in earnest | Seoul Hanok Portal |
| 1989 | Changdeok Girls' High School left; the Constitutional Court took the site | Seoul Hanok Portal |
| **May 1991** | Height limit for houses eased from one storey to **10m (3 storeys)**; multi-unit villas start going up | Seoul Hanok Portal |
| **1994** | Height limit around Gyeongbokgung raised from 10m to **16m**, up to five storeys; hanok demolished for villas across Bukchon, Wonseo-dong included | Seoul Hanok Portal |

The portal is blunt about the 1983 policy:

> It was carried out by the administration without discussion or agreement with residents, regulated hanok as strictly as cultural heritage, and at the same time demolished many hanok to widen and pave Bukchon-ro: a contradictory administration that bred resentment.

It also says that while all of Bukchon was hanok in the 1960s, **many were lost to villas from the 1990s on**. That is why the lanes mix hanok with three- and four-storey buildings.

### 2001: support instead of restriction

In 1999 a residents' group, the **Jongno Bukchon Gakkugi Association**, pushed the Seoul Institute, residents, experts and the city to draw up a new policy, and **"Bukchon Gakkugi" ("tending Bukchon") started in 2001**. The portal lists three pillars:

- A **voluntary hanok registration system** instead of top-down restriction
- **Encouraging and subsidising repairs** that keep the hanok look while allowing modern living
- Improving the neighbourhood through residents' participation

The next big step came in 2020. Seoul put a revised Bukchon district plan out for public inspection (Seoul Notice No. 2020-126), and on **24 December 2020** designated nine hanok clusters in Jongno and Seongbuk, Bukchon included, as **architectural asset promotion zones** under the Hanok Act (Money S). Bukchon's management plan was to be announced with the district plan revision on the 31st, listing **62 architectural assets** in Bukchon.

### 2026: next in line, guesthouses

According to Sijung Ilbo (1 June 2026), Jongno-gu began revising the **Bukchon district plan** after counting **hanok stay businesses ("hanok experience" lodgings) in the hanok cluster up from 47 in 2020 to 168 in 2026, 3.5 times**. The target is **Gahoe-dong 31 and 11 ("Bukchon Zone 1")**, and the district is considering **banning new hanok stay registrations there altogether**. Until now, the guidelines let a hanok switch to lodging freely as long as its exterior stayed intact. The plan was to hold resident briefings in June and then go to the district planning committee. **As of October 2026 we could not find a final notice.** What this means when booking is in [Bukchon hotels](/en/posts/bukchon-hotels-2026-09/).

## Songhyeon-dong: land closed for 110 years

The large open lot in front of Anguk Station Exit 1, west of Gamgodang-gil, is the **Songhyeon-dong site**, in one of the 11 Bukchon neighbourhoods. Korean Wikipedia says the name (松峴, "pine hill") goes back to a pine-covered rise in early Joseon.

| When | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Japanese colonial period | Housing for Chosen Industrial Bank staff | Wikipedia |
| After 1945 | Owned by the US government; **US embassy staff housing** | Wikipedia |
| 1997 / 2000 | Bought by Samsung Life; museum plan abandoned | Wikipedia |
| 2008 | Bought by **Korean Air** for a hotel; lost in court because of nearby schools | Wikipedia |
| Dec 2021 | Three-way land swap between Seoul, LH and Korean Air | JoongAng Ilbo |

| **Dec 2026** | **Temporary opening due to end** | Newsis, Sep 2026 |
| Early 2027 | Construction starts; **entire site closed** during works; completion targeted for 2029 | Newsis, Sep 2026 |

The plan reported by Korea Economic Daily (March 2026) includes 18,544㎡ of green space, 6,360㎡ of plazas and paths, a **6,200㎡ "Songhyeon Culture Madang"** about the size of Seoul Plaza, 270 car spaces on basement level 1 and **90 tour-bus spaces on levels 2 and 3**. On the east side will stand the **Lee Kun-hee donation museum (working title)**, with an observation deck for the park. Korea Economic Daily said construction would start in the second half of 2026; by September, Newsis reported early 2027.

Two practical takeaways. **The window to walk the Songhyeon green plaza is closing in 2026**, and from 2029 **a 90-space coach park right at Bukchon's doorstep** may change how tour groups arrive again. The plaza also hosts events, such as the sculpture festival in September 2026 (Korea Economic Daily).

## Samcheong-dong-gil, Gamgodang-gil and Gyedong-gil

![Samcheong-dong-gil on a clear day. On the left, shops sit above a stone embankment covered in ivy and a drooping willow; on the right, a pavement lined with fresh green ginkgo trees. The road is empty](/images/bukchon-guide-2026-09/05.webp)

**Samcheong-dong-gil is not in the red zone and has no time limit.** JoongAng Ilbo (August 2025), citing the Jongno Cultural Center, says the name Samcheong (三淸, "three clear things") refers to clear mountains, water and hearts, and that "Samcheong's green shade" was one of King Jeongjo's eight views of Hanyang. The same article says Samcheong-dong appears as a backdrop in Netflix's *KPop Demon Hunters*, which has drawn even more foreign visitors, and that the **Jongno 11 village bus** from the City Hall side is packed with tourists. The Seoul Hanok Portal describes galleries lining Samcheong-dong-gil opposite Gyeongbokgung, with restaurants and cafés along it and Hwadong-gil.

At its southern end, at 30 Samcheong-ro, is **MMCA Seoul**, opened on 13 November 2013 in the converted former Defense Security Command building (Wikipedia). According to the museum, it is open **Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri and Sun 10:00-18:00, Wed and Sat 10:00-21:00**, closing only on 1 January, Seollal and Chuseok. **Wednesday and Saturday evenings 18:00-21:00 are free**, as is entry for under-25s and over-65s. It is one of the few indoor sights in Bukchon **open on Mondays**. Exhibitions are listed in [Bukchon: things to do](/en/posts/bukchon-things-to-do-2026-09/).

**Gamgodang-gil** climbs from near Anguk Exit 1 to Jeongdok Library. According to Korean Wikipedia, Gamgodang was the family home of King Sukjong's queen **Inhyeon**, around 37-1 Anguk-dong, and **Empress Myeongseong**, King Gojong's wife, was born there in 1851. Part of the building was moved to Yeoju when Duksung Girls' High School was built. **Jeongdok Library** at the top opened on 4 January 1977 in the former Kyunggi High School; four of its buildings are registered cultural heritage (Wikipedia).

**Gyedong-gil** runs north from near Anguk Exit 3 to the gate of Joongang High School. It was busy enough to be classed an orange zone in 2024, but there is no time limit. Gye-dong 135 is one of the 1930s hanok estates named by the Seoul Hanok Portal.

![A junction deeper in Samcheong-dong. The road climbs between an ivy-covered brick building and a café with a second-floor balcony, with a forested hill behind](/images/bukchon-guide-2026-09/06.webp)

Keep climbing Samcheong-dong-gil and you reach Samcheong Park and the woods below Bugaksan. The Jongno 11 bus terminates at Samcheong Park.

## Crowds and etiquette

What Jongno-gu counts as "tourism" is set out above. Beyond that, the on-site signs and press reports keep repeating the same things.

- **Speak softly.** Residents quoted by Hankyoreh in 2018 said "when people grab a door knocker for a photo and let go, it is really loud".
- **Do not climb gate steps or leave bags on them.** A 2024 Seoul city report shows volunteers asking a tourist to move a bag off a resident's steps.
- **Do not open gates or peer inside.**
- **Wheel suitcases along main roads.** Hankook Ilbo (January 2025) named the sound of suitcase wheels at dawn as one of residents' biggest complaints, and that applies to guests heading to a red-zone hanok stay too.
- **Hanbok photo shoots belong in 10:00-17:00, and away from residents' gates even outside the red zone.**
- **The Constitutional Court sits on Bukchon-ro by Anguk Exit 2.** During the presidential impeachment hearings in January 2025, Hankook Ilbo reported barricades from Exit 2 and half of Bukchon-ro lined with police buses. On a major hearing or verdict day, use Exit 1 or 3 instead.

## Who it suits

**A good fit if you:**

- Want to walk hanok lanes, **between 10am and 5pm**
- Are curious how a city handled 40 years of conservation policy and then overtourism
- Like galleries and museums (Samcheong-dong-gil, MMCA Seoul)
- Need somewhere for a Monday (MMCA Seoul is open)

**Not a good fit if you:**

- Want **dawn or dusk photos of the hanok lanes**: not in the red zone
- Expect rows of grand aristocratic mansions with big courtyards: most Bukchon hanok are 1930s urban houses built for sale
- Struggle with hills: take the village bus
- Are on a coach tour expecting door-to-door drop-off: you will walk from a drop-off zone

## In short

- **Size**: 1,076,302㎡ by the Seoul Hanok Portal, 1,128,372.7㎡ (11 legal neighbourhoods) by the district plan and special management area; about three times Seochon.
- **History**: in 1906, 43.6% in yangban or official households → **1930s housing-company hanok for sale** → 1983 protection, 1991/1994 easing and villas → 2001 Bukchon Gakkugi.
- **Residents**: Gahoe-dong and Samcheong-dong population **down 27.8% from 2013 to 2023**; 2025 visitors up 16-18% year on year.
- **Red zone**: Korea's first special management area from 1 July 2024 → Bukchon-ro 11-gil, 34,000㎡, **no tourism 17:00-10:00**, 100,000 won fine since 1 March 2025. Photos count as tourism even for hotel guests and foreigners.
- **Charter buses**: 2.3km banned at all times, **fines from 1 January 2026** (300,000-500,000 won). Drop-offs in front of MMCA, opposite Changdeokgung and by Tapgol Park. Upheld in court on 17 September 2026.
- **What's next**: a possible ban on new hanok stays in Gahoe-dong (from June 2026), the Songhyeon plaza's temporary opening ending in December 2026, Songhyeon Cultural Park and the Lee Kun-hee museum targeted for 2029.

In one line: **Bukchon took 40 years to save its hanok, and once crowds poured into the lanes it had saved, it became the place that decides when, and how, visitors may come.**
