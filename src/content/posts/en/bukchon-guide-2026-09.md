Of all the Seoul neighbourhoods this blog has covered, Bukchon is **the first to regulate tourists rather than buildings**.

The others capped heights, lifted redevelopment zones, held down rents or ordered demolition. Bukchon **decided when people may come in.** Between 5pm and 10am the next morning, you may not enter Bukchon-ro 11-gil as a tourist.

![An empty uphill lane on Bukchon-ro 11-gil under a blue sky, lined on both sides by hanok with geometric patterns set into their walls](/images/bukchon-guide-2026-09/04.webp)

The lane in the photo above is the **red zone**.

## Korea's first special management area

The legal basis is the **Tourism Promotion Act**. An amendment of 3 December 2019 (Act No. 16684) created the "special management area", in force from 4 June 2020. The official reason for the amendment reads:

> an area designated for management because **visits by tourists beyond its carrying capacity** risk damaging the natural environment or **disturbing the peaceful living environment of residents**

The law existed from 2020, but **the first place actually designated, four years later, was Bukchon**. Jongno-gu made the designation on **1 July 2024**, and the Seoul Metropolitan Government described it as the first in the country.

### There are four kinds of zone

| Zone | Where | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Red zone** | Around Bukchon-ro 11-gil, about **34,000㎡** | **No tourist visits 17:00-10:00**, 100,000 won fine |
| Orange zone | Bukchon-ro 5-ga-gil (26,400㎡) and around Gyedong-gil (34,000㎡) | Focus of guidance patrols when announced in 2024 |
| Yellow zone | Around Bukchon-ro 12-gil | No fine; visitors asked to come 10:00-17:00 |
| **Charter bus restriction zone** | Bukchon-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil, Bukchon-ro 4-gil, Changdeokgung 1-gil and others, about **2.3km** | Charter buses banned **at all times** |

The orange zone areas come from press coverage of the July 2024 designation. **Jongno-gu's current official guide page lists only the red zone, the yellow zone and the bus zone; the orange zone is not on it.** For visitors, **the red zone is the only place where a fine applies**.

### Red zone rules

Summarised from Jongno-gu's guide:

- **Visiting hours**: 10am to 5pm
- **Restricted hours**: 5pm to 10am the next day
- **Guidance period**: 1 November 2024 to 28 February 2025
- **Fines from**: **1 March 2025**
- **Fine**: 100,000 won (the district ordinance allows a reduction of up to half)

The exemptions are **residents, their family and friends, customers of shops, shopkeepers and people simply passing through**. There is a catch.

> This includes taking photos or video, staying to look around, and wandering the street for tourism regardless of any shopping. Such acts are treated as tourist activity, and **even people in the exempt categories** may be fined for breaking the rules.

In other words, **a guest staying in the red zone who takes photos in the lane after 5pm is covered**. Jongno-gu states explicitly that **long-stay guests, such as people on a month-long stay, are not residents**. A resident is someone whose national ID card or foreign resident card gives an address inside the red zone.

**The rules apply to foreign visitors in the same way.** The guide says a notice is issued on the spot using the passport number, with payment either immediately or before leaving the country.

![Bukchon-ro 11-gil at dusk under a deep blue sky, a street lamp lit, tiled roofs and patterned walls climbing the hill](/images/bukchon-guide-2026-09/01.webp)

**A dusk shot like this one can no longer be taken in the red zone.** In October the sun sets around 6pm, so watch the sunset from somewhere outside the red zone.

### Charter buses are shut out too

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Roads | Bukchon-ro, Bukchon-ro 5-gil, Bukchon-ro 4-gil, Changdeokgung 1-gil and others, about 2.3km |
| When | **At all times** (weekends and public holidays included) |
| Guidance period | 1 July to 31 December 2025 |
| Fines from | **1 January 2026** |
| Fine | 300,000 won first time, 400,000 second, 500,000 third |
| Enforcement | CCTV cameras |

The stretch announced at the 2024 designation was 1.5km; **the route actually enforced grew to 2.3km.** It covers **charter buses with 16 or more seats**. In exchange, Jongno-gu has set up **three drop-off points within 1km**: Samcheong-ro (in front of MMCA Seoul), Samil-daero (west side of Tapgol Park) and Donhwamun-ro (opposite Changdeokgung).

**If you come on a group tour, you will be dropped at one of these three and walk in.**

Shopkeepers and charter bus drivers sued over the ban, and **the Seoul Administrative Court dismissed the case on 17 September 2026**. According to the Hankook Ilbo, the court found the restriction "necessary and reasonable".

## What I had wrong

Four things I thought I knew turned out to be wrong while researching this post.

**First, the special management area was not designated on 1 November 2024.** The designation was on **1 July 2024**; 1 November was the start of the guidance period for the time limit, and **fines began on 1 March 2025**. There are three dates.

**Second, the special management area and the red zone are not the same thing.** The special management area covers **the whole Bukchon district unit plan area (1,128,372.7㎡)**, including parts of Samcheong-dong and Gahoe-dong. **The red zone with the time limit is 34,000㎡ of that, about 3%.** Samcheong-dong-gil, Gyedong-gil and the Jeongdok Library area are not in the red zone.

**Third, the time limit did not appear out of nowhere in 2024.** Jongno-gu explains:

> **Since 2018**, Jongno-gu had set village visiting hours of 10am to 5pm for the Bukchon-ro 11-gil area and run guidance through Bukchon wardens made up of residents. However, **voluntary guidance alone proved hard to make effective**

**The same 10-to-5 rule was a request for six years before it became a fine.**

**Fourth, most of Bukchon's hanok are not Joseon-era aristocrats' houses.** The Seoul Hanok Portal says:

> Housing companies bought up Bukchon's large plots and wooded land and built small and medium-sized hanok on them in groups. **The hanok residential areas where hanok are now concentrated, at 11 and 31 Gahoe-dong, 35 Samcheong-dong and 135 Gye-dong, were all formed in this period.**

"This period" is **around the 1930s**. These were **houses built for sale**, with glass doors and tin awnings. The best-known developer was **Jeong Se-gwon (1888-1965)**, founder of Geonyangsa, and there is a bus stop in Gahoe-dong named after the site of his work.

## Where Bukchon begins and ends

**Bukchon is not an official district name.** It means "north village", the neighbourhood above Cheonggyecheon and Jongno.

According to a 2020 Seoul notice, **the Bukchon district unit plan area spans 11 legal dongs**:

> Gahoe-dong, Gye-dong, Wonseo-dong, Anguk-dong, Jae-dong, Hwa-dong, Sagan-dong, Sogyeok-dong, Songhyeon-dong, Palpan-dong, Samcheong-dong

Here is the distinction a visitor actually needs:

| Name | What it is | Feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bukchon Hanok Village** | Around 11 and 31 Gahoe-dong, Bukchon-ro 11-gil | Dense hanok housing, **red zone** |
| **Samcheong-dong** | A legal dong; visitors usually mean **Samcheong-dong-gil** | Galleries, cafés, restaurants |
| **Gye-dong** | A legal dong; **Gyedong-gil** | Local shops and cafés, by Joongang High School |
| **Gahoe-dong** | A legal dong | Home to most of the hanok clusters |

**Samcheong-dong is one neighbourhood inside Bukchon.** Treat them as separate places and your route will tangle.

The Seochon guide quoted the Seoul Hanok Portal saying that Seochon is **"about a third of the size of Bukchon"**. Bukchon is roughly three times as large.

## Timeline

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| Around 1930 | Housing companies subdivide plots and sell hanok in bulk |
| 1976 | Kyunggi High School moves out (its site becomes Jeongdok Library); **talks on a folk landscape designation** |
| **1983** | **Designated a Type 4 aesthetic district**; hanok preservation begins in earnest |
| **May 1991** | Height limit relaxed from one storey to 10m (three storeys); multi-unit housing starts to go up |
| 1994 | Height limit around Gyeongbokgung raised from 10m to 16m, up to five storeys |
| 1999 | New policy drawn up at the request of the residents' group Jongno Bukchon Gakkugi-hoe |
| **2001** | **Bukchon Gakkugi (Bukchon improvement) begins**: hanok registration and repair grants |
| 2018 | 10am-5pm village visiting hours on Bukchon-ro 11-gil (voluntary) |
| January 2020 | District unit plan revised; architectural asset promotion zone proposed |
| **1 July 2024** | **Special management area designated** |
| **1 March 2025** | **Red zone fines begin** |
| **1 January 2026** | **Charter bus fines begin** |

The Seoul Hanok Portal is frank about the 1983 approach:

> It was carried out by the administration without discussion or agreement with residents, regulated hanok as strictly as cultural properties, and **at the same time demolished many hanok to widen and pave Bukchon-ro, a contradictory way of running things** that angered residents.

That backlash led to the 1991 relaxation, and multi-unit housing filled the gaps left by hanok. **Bukchon Gakkugi in 2001 changed course to "don't force, support the houses that register"**.

For the record, **the Seoul Hanok Portal's Bukchon page does not give a current count of hanok**, so I have not included one. The figures circulating online come from different sources and different years.

### The next target is accommodation

In June 2026 Jongno-gu began revising the Bukchon district unit plan. According to the Sijung Ilbo, **hanok stay businesses in Bukchon's hanok cluster grew 3.5 times, from 47 in 2020 to 168**, and the plan is to restrict new lodging businesses around Gahoe-dong. There is more in the [where to stay in Bukchon](/en/posts/bukchon-hotels-2026-09/) post.

| Neighbourhood | Prescription | What was regulated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Euljiro | Demolition decided | Buildings |
| Ikseon-dong | Redevelopment zone lifted in 2018, with safeguards | Buildings |
| Seochon | Height cap from 1977, preservation plan in 2016 | Buildings and business types |
| Seongsu-dong | Rent-restraint ordinance in 2015 | Rents |
| Seongbuk-dong | Preservation in 2019, reversed to demolition in 2026 | Buildings |
| **Bukchon** | **Preservation in 1983, relaxed in 1991, visiting hours limited in 2024** | **Tourists** |

**The other five all dealt with buildings or money.** Bukchon spent forty years preserving buildings and then ran into **a second problem: too many people coming to the place it had preserved**.

## Getting there

![Gyedong-gil, a brick-paved street running between a hanok café with its windows folded open and buildings covered in climbing plants](/images/bukchon-guide-2026-09/03.webp)

Use [[line:3]] **Anguk Station**. The exit depends on where you are going.

| Destination | How to get there |
| --- | --- |
| **Bukchon Hanok Village (red zone)** | **Exit 2**, then up Bukchon-ro towards Jaedong Elementary School |
| **Jeongdok Library and Gamgodang-gil** | **Exit 1**, about a 10-minute walk |
| **MMCA Seoul and the start of Samcheong-dong-gil** | **Exit 1**, about 750m |
| **Changdeokgung** | **Exit 3**, a 5-minute walk |

**Bukchon gets steeper the further north you go.** If the climb is too much, take a village bus.

- [[bus:village:01]] Jongno 01 and [[bus:village:02]] Jongno 02 run from around Anguk Station up to the **Gahoe-dong Community Centre** and **Bukchon Hanok Village entrance** stops. Jongno 02 continues past the Board of Audit and Inspection to Sungkyunkwan University.
- [[bus:village:11]] Jongno 11 runs from **Samcheong Park** down Samcheong-dong-gil, past Gyeongbokgung and City Hall to **Seoul Station**.

From the airport, **airport limousine [[bus:6011]]** stops at Changdeokgung and near exit 1 of Anguk Station.

## How to do it

The easiest plan is to **start with the red zone, after 10am**.

1. **Morning** - Anguk Station exit 2 to Bukchon-ro 11-gil (red zone). Fewer people, and within the permitted hours.
2. **Lunch** - head down to Gyedong-gil or Samcheong-dong-gil to eat. Suggestions are in the [Bukchon food](/en/posts/bukchon-food-2026-09/) post.
3. **Afternoon** - Jeongdok Library, MMCA Seoul and the galleries of Samcheong-dong-gil. **These are outside the red zone, so there is no time limit.**
4. **Before 5pm** - be out of the red zone.

**If you want to add Gyeongbokgung or Changdeokgung**, palace entry is free if you wear hanbok, but there are specific criteria. See [free palace entry in hanbok](/en/posts/hanbok-palace-2026-09/). Gyeongbokgung closes on Tuesdays and Changdeokgung on Mondays.

The list of sights is in the separate [things to do in Bukchon](/en/posts/bukchon-things-to-do-2026-09/) post.

### Lane etiquette

What Jongno-gu counts as **tourist activity** is set out above. Beyond that, the signs in the lanes keep repeating three things:

- **Speak softly**
- **Don't climb the steps in front of gates or leave bags there**. They are residents' front doors
- **Don't open gates or peer inside**

## Who it suits

**It suits you if:**

- You want to walk hanok lanes. **Come between 10am and 5pm**
- You want to walk between two palaces
- You like galleries and museums (Samcheong-dong-gil, MMCA Seoul)
- You are curious how forty years of preservation policy kept changing

**It may not suit you if:**

- **Early-morning or dusk photos of hanok lanes** are the goal. Not in the red zone
- You want an evening stroll. Skip the red zone and use Samcheong-dong-gil and Gyedong-gil
- Hills are hard for you. Take the village bus
- You come by charter coach. You will walk from the drop-off point

## Summary

- **On 1 July 2024 Bukchon became Korea's first special management area under the Tourism Promotion Act.**
- **In the red zone (about 34,000㎡ around Bukchon-ro 11-gil), tourist visits are banned from 5pm to 10am**, with a **100,000 won fine** from 1 March 2025.
- **Taking photos and lingering count as tourist activity**, even for guests staying there. The same goes for foreign visitors.
- **The red zone is about 3% of the special management area.** Samcheong-dong-gil, Gyedong-gil and Jeongdok Library have no time limit.
- **Charter buses have been banned at all times since 1 January 2026**, with fines of 300,000 to 500,000 won.
- **Most of Bukchon's hanok are houses built for sale around 1930**, not Joseon aristocrats' homes.
- **Anguk exit 2 for the hanok village, exit 1 for Jeongdok Library and Samcheong-dong, exit 3 for Changdeokgung.**

In one line: **Bukchon took forty years to save its hanok, and then had to decide what time people could come.**
