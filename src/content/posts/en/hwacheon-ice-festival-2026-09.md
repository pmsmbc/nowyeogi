Every January, when the river in Hwacheon freezes, a festival opens on top of the ice: you drill a hole through it and fish for trout. Thousands of people sitting on a frozen river is the image it is known for.

For a foreign visitor there is a specific reason to know about this one. **There is a separate fishing area reserved for foreigners, and a passport alone discounts every programme.**

![Crowds of ice anglers filling the frozen Hwacheoncheon, with snow-covered mountains behind and an English 'Sancheoneo Ice Fishing' sign](/images/hwacheon-ice-festival-2026-09/01.webp)

## The 2027 dates are not announced

**As of September 2026, the 2027 dates have not been announced.** Every page of the festival's own site carries a line saying the content belongs to the 2026 festival and that 2027 content is in preparation. The English site says the same, and the newest notice-board post is a closing message dated 2 February 2026.

**The last edition ran from Saturday 10 January to Sunday 1 February 2026, 23 days.** In the official history, **since 2010 it has opened without exception on the first or second Saturday of January and run 23 days** — a pattern, not a promise. If you are planning around the dates, check the site around November.

**Every price below is from the January 2026 edition.** The 2027 prices are not out.

## There is a fishing area just for foreigners

The most useful single thing to know about this festival. Its official name is the **Foreigners & FAM Tourists Ice Fishing Area**, downstream of the festival's suspension bridge, in a zone separate from the general areas.

- **Open 09:00–18:00** (ticketing 09:00–17:00). Unlike the general areas, there is no weekday or weekend split.
- Capacity 1,300 at a time.
- **Individual foreign travellers (FIT) pay ₩10,000 (about $7.50) per person** and get ₩5,000 (about $3.75) back as a Hwacheon local gift voucher. Groups booked through an overseas travel agency (MOU) pay ₩15,000 (about $11) with a ₩10,000 voucher.
- Children under 4 are free.

**The facilities are the real advantage.** The official guide states that the area has a **foreigners' rest area, a Muslim prayer room and a grill zone**, and the English page names the prayer room explicitly. That is not a common thing to find at a Korean festival.

**Booking it is awkward.** There is no online booking link or procedure on the official site — the only channel listed is a phone number, **the Hwacheon County Global Marketing Team on 033-440-2328**. Whether advance booking is required is not stated.

One oddity: **the price table for this area appears only on the English site, not the Korean one.**

## Bring your passport

Even if you skip the dedicated area, bring it. The festival's fee table lists **"foreigners (holders of a foreign passport)"** among its discount categories, covering the general fishing areas, lure fishing, bare-hand catching, night fishing and the indoor ice sculpture hall. **Programmes that cost ₩15,000 drop to ₩10,000.**

One condition: the official text says **proof must be presented for the discount to apply, and discounts cannot be combined**. Bring the physical passport, not a photo.

## What you actually do

**Ice fishing is the centre of it**, across three areas.

- **Walk-up area** — downstream, first come first served, capacity 8,000. The official guide warns that **weekend slots often sell out during the morning**.
- **Reservation area** — upstream, booked online through **NOL Interpark** and collected on site; whether foreigners can book that way, or whether overseas cards work, could not be confirmed.
- **Foreigners' area** — above.

**The equipment situation is easy to misread.** The official packing list is a rod, bait, a chair and an ice auger, and **the auger is the only item provided on site**. There is a tackle shop inside the grounds, but the official material does not say whether rods can be rented. **Only artificial bait is allowed**, and only tackle certified as eco-friendly.

### Bare-hand catching may be the more famous part

Getting into the icy water and catching trout with your hands — the scene most photographs come from.

- **Seven sessions a day**, on the hour from 10:00 to 16:00. Sign-up is same-day and on site, and **closes 20 minutes before each session**.
- **A short-sleeved T-shirt and shorts are issued**, with changing rooms, lockers and a footbath.
- You go in with **bare hands and bare feet**, and have **3 minutes to catch up to 3 fish**.
- **Age 14 and over only.** Pregnant people and anyone with a health condition are excluded.

## You eat what you catch

Beside the fishing areas are a **grill zone and a sashimi counter**. Hand over your trout and they cook it: the grill takes **15 to 20 minutes**, sashimi about **10 minutes**, both **₩3,000 (about $2.25) per fish**.

**There is an important limit.** The official notice states that **they do not sell fish other than the ones you caught**. Catch nothing and you cannot buy one. Taking fish home is capped at 3 per person.

## Getting there from Seoul

**Hwacheon has no railway station**, so a bus is unavoidable.

**Option 1 — direct from Dong Seoul Terminal.** First bus 06:45, last 18:10, **about 2 hours 55 minutes, ₩14,400 (about $11)**.

**Option 2 — via Chuncheon**, the route the festival's own site recommends. ITX-Cheongchun from Cheongnyangni to Chuncheon Station, about 1 hour at ₩9,600 (about $7.20), or Namchuncheon Station, about 55 minutes at ₩8,300 (about $6.25). Then an intercity bus to Hwacheon, **₩5,800 (about $4.35)**, every 30 to 60 minutes.

**An official warning is attached.** Because intercity buses are busy during the festival, **boarding at Chuncheon Station may leave you without a seat**. Getting off at Namchuncheon and boarding at the Chuncheon Intercity Bus Terminal is safer.

### The terminal-to-festival leg is the problem

**The external shuttle to the festival site runs at weekends only**: Saturday and Sunday 09:00–18:00, four buses circulating every 20 minutes, with the intercity bus terminal on the loop.

**No official weekday transport from the terminal to the site could be confirmed** — the official site does not cover it, so if you arrive by bus midweek, sort this leg out in advance. The programme overview does list a **foreigner shuttle bus**, but it is a name with no detail page: no route, no times, nothing anywhere.

## Things people say about this festival

**You will see "CNN named it one of the 7 wonders of winter" almost everywhere**, and the Korea Tourism Organization's own page says it was selected by CNN in 2011.

Checking it produces something odd. **The festival's own site makes the claim without naming CNN.** Its invitation text calls the festival one of the 7 winter wonders of the world and one of the 4 great winter festivals, with no source given, and the word "CNN" appears nowhere on the site. The original article could not be located, so this guide treats it as **the organiser's own marketing phrase**.

**Visitor numbers need the same caution.** The last edition with an official attendance figure is **2019, at 1.8 million**. From 2020 onward there are no figures at all.

**Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism grades**: a Global Growth Festival in 2019, and a Korea Representative Festival from 2014 to 2018. Nothing is recorded after 2020.

### Some years are missing

**Three years are absent from the official history: 2011, 2021 and 2022.** Every other year is listed, which reads as the festival not being held — but the site gives no reason and no word like "cancelled".

**2020 also opened unusually late, on 27 January**, where the years either side all opened between 4 and 14 January. A postponement is implied; no official explanation could be found.

## Fishing area rules

Officially set, and not knowing them can get you turned back at the entrance.

- **No outside food**, checked at the entrance.
- **No ice boxes, coolers or polystyrene boxes.** Bags for your trout are handed out at the operations booth.
- **No tents, windbreaks, heaters or gas stoves** — they block sightlines and pose a fire risk. Which means **your clothing is your only protection from the cold.**
- **No smoking anywhere in the fishing area**, and no pets.
- Take-home limit **3 fish per person**.

## Practical notes

- **Dress for it.** The official guide states that winter in Hwacheon is bitter enough that the ice freezes thick here earlier than anywhere else in the country, and recommends a winter coat, winter boots, gloves and hand warmers. It also suggests bringing a large, padded chair.
- **Staying overnight in Hwacheon can make the fishing free.** Use accommodation within Hwacheon County and keep the receipt: 2 free fishing vouchers for a bill under ₩100,000 (about $75), up to 6 at ₩150,000 (about $113) or more. **A screenshot of a phone booking is not accepted as proof.**

## Wrapping up

This festival suits foreign visitors because **it needs no language**. You drill a hole, you wait, you catch something, and the people next to you cook it.

Three things to remember: **bring your passport** (everything is discounted), **ask about the foreigners' fishing area on 033-440-2328**, and **if you are going on a weekday, work out the terminal-to-site leg before you travel.**

The 2027 dates look likely around November. Worth one more check of the official site then.
