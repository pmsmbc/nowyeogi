2,494 people live in Myeongdong (Jung-gu district, end of June 2026). On an average day, **100,910 foreign visitors** pass through it (Seoul AI Foundation, 2025). Visitors outnumber residents roughly forty to one. That is Myeongdong.

The short version: Myeongdong is **the square kilometre of Seoul that the most foreign visitors walk**, and to cope with those numbers the district office has put name tags on the street stalls, price tags on the shops and LED screens on the lampposts. This is the deep guide to that Myeongdong, put back together from public records: from Joseon-era Myeongnye-bang and colonial Meiji-cho to the 1946 name "bright village", the 1898 cathedral and the old national theatre, the 2000 tourist zone, and the Namsan gondola that a court stopped again in September 2026.

Things to do, food and hotels have their own guides: [things to do in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/), [where to eat in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-food-2026-09/) and [where to stay in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-hotels-2026-09/). Euljiro, just north, is in the [Euljiro guide](/en/posts/euljiro-guide-2026-09/), and City Hall and Bukchang-dong to the west are in the [Gwanghwamun, City Hall and Jonggak guide](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-guide-2026-09/).

![Myeongdong at night, street-food carts lined up under the trees as people browse. A yellow sign on the stall at right says card payments are accepted, and a lamppost on the left carries a Myeongdong 3-gil street sign and a Myeongdong Theater banner](/images/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/15.webp)

Several threads of this guide are in that one photo: the licensed street carts (a real-name permit system since 2016), the "card payments accepted" sign (card readers handed out by the district from 2024), and the banner for Myeongdong Theater (built in 1936, once the national theatre).

## Myeongdong at a glance

"Myeongdong" comes in three sizes, and they don't match.

| Name | Covers | Area | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Myeongdong (legal neighbourhood)** | Myeongdong 1-ga and 2-ga | Smallest | Encyclopedia of Korean Culture |
| **Myeongdong (administrative dong)** | Myeongdong 1–2-ga, Chungmuro 1–2-ga, Namsan-dong 1–3-ga, Euljiro 1–2-ga, Da-dong, Mugyo-dong and more | 0.99 km² | Jung-gu |
| **Myeongdong tourist zone** | Myeongdong, Namdaemun Market, Bukchang-dong, Sogong-dong, Da-dong, Mugyo-dong, part of Cheonggyecheon | 872,809.2 m² | Jung-gu |

In this guide, Myeongdong means **Myeongdong 1–2-ga and Chungmuro 1–2-ga (the shopping streets), Namsan-dong to the south (the foot of Namsan) and Hoehyeon-dong to the west (Namdaemun Market)**. The Sogong-dong and Bukchang-dong hotel blocks and the Da-dong and Mugyo-dong restaurant streets are inside the tourist zone but belong to the City Hall side, so they are in the [Gwanghwamun, City Hall and Jonggak guide](/en/posts/gwanghwamun-guide-2026-09/). The Euljiro alleys north of Euljiro 1-ga Station are in the [Euljiro guide](/en/posts/euljiro-guide-2026-09/).

## A name in three layers: Myeongnye-bang, Meiji-cho, Myeongdong

### Joseon's "south village"

In the Joseon dynasty this area was **Myeongnye-bang** (明禮坊), a ward in the southern district of Hanseong (Joseon Seoul). According to the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture and Jung-gu, today's name takes its "Myeong" from Myeongnye-bang. As a counterpart to Bukchon, the "north village" of the aristocracy, the area under Namsan was called **Namchon**, the "south village", and Korean Wikipedia notes that many poor scholars lived here.

The place left its biggest mark in the records through Catholicism. Per Korean Wikipedia, in **1784** early converts including Yi Seung-hun met at the house of **Kim Beom-u** in Myeongnye-bang and formed a community of believers. The Korea Tourism Organization (KTO) describes the cathedral site as "where the Korean Catholic community was first born". That's why the cathedral stands on this particular hill.

### Meiji-cho and Honmachi

Under Japanese colonial rule the area became **Meiji-cho** (明治町), named after the Japanese Meiji era. Korean Wikipedia records that Meiji-cho 1-chome and 2-chome were placed under Jung-gu on 10 June 1943. Immediately south, today's Chungmuro was **Honmachi** (本町); together the two were the centre of Japanese commerce and settlement. Wikipedia dates Myeongdong's start as a commercial district to this period.

### 1946: the "bright village"

After liberation, in **1946**, Meiji-cho became Myeongdong 1-ga and 2-ga. A Seoul city article says the name was meant as "bright village", and the encyclopedia notes the two were merged into a single Myeongdong in 1955. In other words, the Japanese era name was dropped and the old Joseon "Myeong" brought back.

## Two landmarks: the cathedral and the theatre

### Myeongdong Cathedral, 1898

![Myeongdong Cathedral on a clear autumn day, seen from the foot of the stairs: a red-brick bell tower with a green spire, people climbing the steps past lamps and banners](/images/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/62.webp)

The red-brick church on the hill at the south end of the shopping streets is **Myeongdong Cathedral**. Combining Korean Wikipedia and the national heritage portal:

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 1784 | First community of believers at Kim Beom-u's house in Myeongnye-bang |
| 1887 | Land assembled; site preparation begins |
| 5 August 1892 | Construction starts |
| **29 May 1898** | **Consecrated.** Designed and supervised by the French missionary Father Eugène Coste |
| **22 November 1977** | **Designated a Historic Site** (national heritage portal) |
| June 1987 | Pro-democracy protesters shelter in the cathedral; Cardinal Kim Sou-hwan stands between them and the police |

KTO calls it **the first large Gothic-style Catholic church built on the Korean peninsula** and Korea's first parish, and notes that relics of martyrs rest in the crypt. It is still the working cathedral of the Archdiocese of Seoul, so keep your voice down inside. Visiting routes and Mass times are in [things to do in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/).

### The old national theatre: Myeongdong Theater

Halfway along Myeongdong-gil, the old building with the rounded corner is **Myeongdong Theater**. Korean Wikipedia and the Kyunghyang Shinmun (November 2023) show it has lived through Myeongdong's whole century.

| When | Name and use |
| --- | --- |
| **1936** | **Meiji-za**, a cinema for the Japanese community |
| 1 June 1957 | Myeongdong Arts Hall, serving as the national theatre |
| 1962 | Myeongdong National Theater |
| 1973 | Closes when the National Theater moves to Namsan (Jangchung-dong) |
| 1975 | Sold to a financial company and used as offices |
| December 2003 | Bought back by the government |
| **2009** | **Reopens as Myeongdong Theater** (558 seats, run by the National Theater Company of Korea) |

![Myeongdong Theater lit up at dusk: ornamented columns and oval windows on a rounded corner building, with the words 'Myeongdong Theater' glowing above the entrance and a play banner on the left wall](/images/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/20.webp)

The encyclopedia describes postwar Myeongdong as a **street of culture**: the national theatre and the cathedral were here, and writers and artists gathered in the coffee houses to talk music, art, literature and theatre. The theatre leaving in 1973 marked the end of that era, and the building spent about 30 years as offices before it became a theatre again in 2009.

## From brokerages to shopping, and the most expensive land in Korea

Korean Wikipedia says that **before the 1970s, Myeongdong was where the securities firms clustered**, the centre of the Korean economy. The encyclopedia adds that it was Seoul's signature commercial district through the 1970s, then lost ground as Gangnam was developed and financial institutions moved to Yeouido. Clothing and cosmetics shops, and foreign tourists, filled the gap.

Land prices put numbers on that. Here are the top of the **2026 official standard land prices**, announced by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport in December 2025, as reported by Etoday:

| Rank | Site | Official price per m² (2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Nature Republic Myeongdong World store (Chungmuro 1-ga, 169.3 m²) | **₩188.4 million** (about $141,000) |
| 2 | Woori Bank site (392.4 m²) | ₩187.6 million |
| 3 | Former Uniqlo site (300.1 m²) | ₩171.8 million |

The Nature Republic site has been **the most expensive land in Korea for 23 years running**, up 4.4% from ₩180.5 million in 2025. All of the top eight sites are in the Myeongdong area. The brokerages left, but on the land-price map Myeongdong is still at the top.

## The tourist zone: 2000, expanded in 2012

Korea designates "special tourist zones" (gwangwang teukgu) where shopping and tourism rules are relaxed. Myeongdong got one early. The Hankook Ilbo reported in March 1999 that Seoul was pushing for a zone covering Namdaemun Market, Bukchang, Sogong and Myeongdong, and Jung-gu says it was designated on **30 March 2000** (Ministry of Culture and Tourism notice 2000-2).

| | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Official name | Myeongdong, Namdaemun, Bukchang-dong, Da-dong and Mugyo-dong Special Tourist Zone |
| First designated | **30 March 2000** (Myeongdong, Namdaemun, Bukchang) |
| Expanded | **27 December 2012**, Seoul notice 2012-363, adding Da-dong, Mugyo-dong and part of Cheonggyecheon |
| Area added | 239,295.2 m² (Seoul Shinmun, December 2012) |
| Current area | **872,809.2 m²** (Jung-gu) |
| Boundary | Samil-daero by the Sejong Hotel → Toegye-ro → Shinsegae → Namdaemun Market → The Plaza hotel → City Hall → Cheonggye Plaza → Gwangtonggyo bridge → Janggyo Building → back to the Sejong Hotel |

Subtracting one from the other, the 2000 zone was roughly 630,000 m². The 2012 expansion brought in Seoul Plaza, Cheonggye Plaza and Mugyo-dong's octopus alley, which is why the lawn in front of City Hall is officially part of the "Myeongdong tourist zone".

The rules inside the zone keep shifting. Newspim (June 2026) reports that Seoul revised the district plan for **93,187 m² of Bukchang-dong** in June 2026, easing height and density limits and adding bonuses for tourist accommodation, up to a floor-area ratio of 1,560%. The article says 88% of Bukchang-dong's buildings are over 40 years old. Expect more hotels on Myeongdong's western blocks. Current hotels are in [where to stay in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-hotels-2026-09/).

## Street stalls: name tags, price tags and card readers

Myeongdong's evenings are made by the carts in the middle of the street, and a good part of the district's work over the past decade has been about them.

### 2016: real-name stalls

From **27 June 2016**, Jung-gu ran a **real-name system** for Myeongdong's street stalls. Per the Seoul Shinmun (June 2016):

- **366 vendors** covered. Each gets a road-use permit and pays about ₩1.3 million (about $975) a year
- **One person, one stall, run in person.** Selling, renting or subcontracting a stall is banned and gets the permit cancelled
- Five trading zones, including Myeongdong-gil, Jungang-ro and Chungmuro-gil
- Shifts changed from three to **two, on alternating days**
- Trading starts **at 4 pm on weekdays in summer, 3 pm in winter, and 2 pm on weekends and public holidays**

The goal was to remove **"corporate" stalls**, where one operator ran several carts. Edaily ran the story under the headline "Street-stall heaven Myeongdong".

### 2023: the overcharging row

In summer 2023, as tourists came back after Covid, prices became a story. Newsis (July 2023) reported that some Myeongdong stalls were selling **4 fish-shaped buns (bungeoppang) for ₩5,000 and one grilled squid for ₩12,000**, and the Seoul Shinmun put "3 fried dumplings for ₩5,000" in its headline. Jung-gu met the Myeongdong tourist zone council and promised tighter management and price labelling.

What followed came in steps.

| When | Measure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 October 2023** | 0.42 km² of Myeongdong made a **mandatory price-display zone**. 1,011 shops including the underground arcades; fines up to ₩10 million. **Street stalls excluded** | Edaily, August 2023 |
| February–March 2024 | **Card readers** for stalls; vendors share prices of 10 popular items (bungeoppang, fish cake, grilled squid and so on) monthly and consult the district before raising them; standard hats, masks and gloves | Edaily, February 2024 |
| 1 December 2024 | Price-display zone **expanded** (Jung-gu notice 2024-77) | Gov.kr |
| **18 June–17 July 2025** | **Special crackdown** on Myeongdong stalls: 45 administrative penalties, 136 on-the-spot warnings, for things like trading without a permit, ignoring the alternate-day rule, not displaying the permit and illegal subletting | Gukje News, Money Today, August 2025 |

Money Today reported that the 2025 crackdown even found **a stall still being run under the name of a vendor who had died**; the permit was cancelled on the spot. Nine years in, keeping the "name tag" honest is still work. Gukje News notes that in 2024 Jung-gu became **the first district in Korea to bring card readers and price labelling to street stalls**.

For visitors that boils down to this: a licensed cart should **show its permit and its prices**, and more of them take cards now. If you can't see a price, ask before you order. What the stalls sell, and for how much, is in [where to eat in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-food-2026-09/).

## A street of screens: Myeongdong Square

![Myeongdong-gil on an overcast day: vertical LED ad screens on every lamppost and a large square screen on the right, with cosmetics and fashion shops on both sides and people walking](/images/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/11.webp)

Walk down Myeongdong-gil today and every lamppost carries a vertical screen. This is a **free outdoor advertising zone**. According to Jung-gu, Myeongdong was chosen as one of the **second round of free advertising zones by the Ministry of the Interior and Safety on 28 December 2023**, with screens going up **in three phases over ten years, 2024 to 2033**. Phase one (to 2025) started with four anchor buildings, **Hana Bank, Lotte Young Plaza, Myeongdong Theater and Shinsegae Department Store**, and the streets around Myeongdong-gil. District mayor Kim Gil-sung said he wanted to make Myeongdong "a world-class attraction like New York's Times Square". The district calls the project "Myeongdong Square".

The screens double as a stage. Jung-gu announced a countdown event for **the night of 31 December 2024 in front of Shinsegae's flagship store**. The press release planned for **1,500** pre-registered spectators, **18** screens around Myeongdong showing the broadcast at once, and **33 spots designated as crowd-risk areas** to be managed. Winter media-façade shows and year-end events change every year, so check [things to do in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## The Namsan gondola: from groundbreaking to appeal

Five minutes from Myeongdong Station to the top of Namsan: that was Seoul's promise for the **Namsan gondola**. As of October 2026 it isn't running. The timeline explains why.

| When | What happened | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **5 September 2024** | Groundbreaking at **Yejang Park**, the planned lower station about 200 m from Myeongdong Station. **832 m** to the summit, **25 ten-person cabins**, up to 1,600 people an hour. Target: "spring 2026" | Asia Economy, Hankook Ilbo |
| October 2024 | A court grants an injunction sought by **Korea Cable Car (Hanguk Sakdo Gongeop)**, operator of the existing Namsan cable car; work stops at about 15% complete | Hankook Ilbo |
| **19 December 2025** | Seoul loses at first instance (Seoul Administrative Court): rezoning part of an urban nature park to fit the pylons didn't meet the legal conditions | Kookmin Ilbo |
| **17 September 2026** | Seoul loses the appeal too (Seoul High Court). The city says it is considering going to the Supreme Court | Hankook Ilbo, Financial News |

The sticking point is height. The Hankook Ilbo says the middle pylon is about 35 m, while the limit in an urban nature park zone is 12 m; Seoul changed the zoning, and the courts found that unlawful. Seoul argued the gondola would improve access and end the existing cable car's **64-year monopoly** (Kookmin Ilbo). Bizhankook (September 2026) reports that more than ₩10 billion (about $7.5 million) has already been spent and that guarding the idle site costs ₩170 million a year.

The practical point: **to get up Namsan today, use the existing cable car, a bus, or your feet.** If an older guide or a 2024 article promises a gondola "opening in spring 2026", that plan has lapsed. Ways up Namsan are in [things to do in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/).

## Namdaemun Market: one road over

![Namdaemun Market on a sunny day: shop awnings with clothes and souvenirs on both sides, signs for a shopping arcade, ginseng and glasses shops, and two people eating hotteok in the foreground](/images/myeongdong-guide-2026-09/31.webp)

West of Myeongdong, past Shinsegae's flagship store, is **Namdaemun Market**. Jung-gu calls it a traditional market with "600 years of history". KTO says it spreads out from Sungnyemun Gate with **about 10,000 shops** selling **around 1,700 kinds of goods**, and draws **about 300,000 people a day**. It is both wholesale and retail, so hours vary by shop. If Myeongdong sells what's new, Namdaemun sells everyday things: glasses, kitchenware, children's clothes, ginseng. The noodle and braised-cutlassfish alleys are in [where to eat in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-food-2026-09/), and a walk through the market is in [things to do in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/).

### The evening bus crush at Myeongdong Entrance

Myeongdong is also a commuter hub, with express buses to the southern suburbs. According to the Seoul Shinmun (January 2024), **29 express routes** used the Myeongdong Entrance stop in front of Lotte Department Store. When the city put up queue signs on 28 December 2023, buses backed up in long trains, and Mayor Oh Se-hoon apologised on site on 6 January 2024. ## How to do it

The same streets change by the hour.

1. **Morning** — the cathedral and Namsan first. Before the stalls go up, Myeongdong-gil is calmer.
2. **Lunch** — Namdaemun Market or a Myeongdong side-street restaurant; see [where to eat in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-food-2026-09/).
3. **Afternoon** — shopping on Myeongdong-gil. Tax refunds and money changing are in [things to do in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-things-to-do-2026-09/), and Olive Young has its own [Olive Young shopping guide](/en/posts/olive-young-shopping-2026-09/).
4. **Late afternoon to evening** — stall time: from 2 pm at weekends, 3–4 pm on weekdays. If Myeongdong Theater has a show on, catch an evening performance.
5. **Night** — the Namsan cable car for the view, or one more walk down the street of screens.

If you get lost or need interpreting, the **Myeongdong Tourist Information Center** at 66 Eulji-ro (09:00–18:00, closed on Seollal and Chuseok days) and the **1330 travel hotline** both help in Korean, English, Japanese and Chinese (KTO).

## Where to stay

With two subway lines, airport buses and Namsan, Namdaemun and Gwanghwamun all within walking distance, Myeongdong is where most first-time visitors to Seoul stay. The June 2026 Bukchang-dong plan, with **density bonuses for tourist hotels**, shows the hotel map is still changing. By the count in [where to stay in Myeongdong](/en/posts/myeongdong-hotels-2026-09/), built from Seoul's licensing data, the area has **69 tourist-lodging businesses with 6,399 rooms** (29 tourist hotels and 40 hostels) and 150 urban homestays for foreigners, all of the homestays in Namsan-dong and Hoehyeon-dong.
