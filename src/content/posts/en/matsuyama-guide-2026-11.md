Take the 7 a.m. flight out of Seoul Incheon and you land in Matsuyama at 8:35. The flight is an hour and 35 minutes, the airport bus to town is 15 more, and you can be soaking in Dogo Onsen before lunch. Matsuyama, the capital of Ehime Prefecture on the island of Shikoku, is small enough that a single tram network ties together the castle, the shopping arcades and the onsen district, which is exactly what makes it an easy first trip. This guide collects the numbers you need. Every fare and opening time below is as of November 2026, taken from Matsuyama Airport, the Iyotetsu railway and bus company, Dogo Onsen and Matsuyama Castle's own pages. Prices are in yen; at roughly ¥150 to the US dollar, ¥1,000 is about $6.70.

## Getting there: nonstop from Seoul and Busan

Matsuyama Airport's international timetable lists two daily Jeju Air flights from Incheon: 7C1701 departs 07:00 and arrives 08:35, and 7C1703 departs 13:15 and arrives 14:50. Return flights leave Matsuyama at 09:35 and 15:50, and extra flights are added on some dates between July and October. From Busan, Air Busan flies daily at 16:30, arriving 17:40, and returns at 18:35. Winter schedules can shift, so check the airline before you book.

From the airport, the **Iyotetsu airport limousine bus** leaves in step with flight arrivals.

| Stop | Cash | IC card | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| JR Matsuyama Station | ¥1,000 | ¥980 | about 15 min |
| Matsuyama-shi Station | ¥1,000 | ¥980 | about 20 min |
| Okaido | ¥1,200 | ¥1,180 | — |
| Dogo Onsen | ¥1,200 | ¥1,180 | about 35–43 min |

Children ride for half price. A taxi from the airport to Dogo takes about 29 minutes and costs around ¥3,310, according to a Matsuyama City pamphlet.

## Getting around: the ¥250 tram and the Botchan train

![A new orange low-floor tram and an older orange tram facing each other on tree-lined tracks](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/10.webp)

Iyotetsu's **streetcars** are how you get around. JR Matsuyama Station, Matsuyama-shi Station (Iyotetsu's own terminal), Okaido and Dogo Onsen are all on the network. The fare is flat regardless of distance, and it rose from ¥230 to **¥250** on 1 April 2026. Paying with a national IC card such as Suica or ICOCA, or by other cashless means, knocks ¥20 off, so you pay ¥230. IC cards have worked on every tram and Iyotetsu bus since March 2024, so the card you used in Tokyo or Osaka works here too.

If you will ride often or use the airport bus both ways, the **City Tram & Limousine Bus 1Day ticket** costs ¥2,300 (children ¥1,150). It is sold only through apps (Iyotetsu's Mikyan app and the Jorudan app), not as a paper ticket. A limousine round trip alone is ¥2,000, so a few tram rides make it pay off.

![The steam-style Botchan Ressha locomotive and green carriages running along a city street](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/07.webp)

The **Botchan Ressha** is a replica of the little train in Natsume Soseki's novel *Botchan*. It was suspended in November 2023 for lack of drivers and returned on 20 March 2024. It now runs on weekends and public holidays only (not 30 December–3 January), for ¥1,300 per adult and ¥650 per child, paid on board, IC cards accepted. Departures from Dogo Onsen are at 9:19, 10:48, 13:19 and 14:59.

## Where to stay: three bases

**Around JR Matsuyama Station** is best for day trips, since JR trains to Shimonada, Uchiko and Imabari leave from here. It is 15 minutes from the airport and about 25 minutes by tram from Dogo.

**Okaido and Matsuyama-shi Station** is downtown. The Okaido arcade is 483 m long, and together with the Gintengai arcade to its south it gives you about 1 km of covered shopping street running toward Matsuyama-shi Station. The castle ropeway starts from Ropeway-gai, just north of Okaido, so you can walk to the castle. From Matsuyama-shi Station to Dogo Onsen is about 20 minutes by tram.

**Dogo Onsen** is the ryokan district, a ryokan being a traditional Japanese inn usually with its own bath and dinner. The appeal is walking to the Honkan in a yukata in the evening, and the airport bus stops right there.

Matsuyama's 2026 municipal tax guide confirms the city **does not charge an accommodation tax**, so unlike Kyoto or Tokyo there is no per-person levy on top of your room rate.

## A three-day plan

### Day 1: Matsuyama Castle and Okaido

![The white main keep and turrets of Matsuyama Castle rising above stone walls under a clear sky](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/05.webp)

**Matsuyama Castle** sits on a hill right in the city centre. Entry to the main keep is ¥520 for adults and ¥160 for elementary pupils, 9:00–17:00, extended to 17:30 in August and shortened to 16:30 in December and January, with last entry 30 minutes before closing. It closes on the third Wednesday of December.

![A Matsuyama Castle ropeway gondola climbing a wooded slope beside the single-seat chairlift](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/28.webp)

You can ride up by **ropeway** or by single-seat **chairlift**, both ¥520 return or ¥270 one way. The ropeway runs 8:30–17:30 (18:00 in August, 17:00 in December–January) and the chairlift 8:30–17:00, but the chairlift does not run in rain. The station is about five minutes' walk from Okaido tram stop along Ropeway-gai. If you have time on the way down, the **Ninomaru Historical Garden** below the castle costs ¥200.

![The OKAIDO sign over the entrance of the covered Okaido shopping arcade](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/22.webp)

Spend the afternoon and evening in **Okaido** and **Gintengai**. The roof covers about a kilometre of restaurants, drugstores and souvenir shops, so rain does not matter. The nabeyaki udon shops that Matsuyama's official tourism site recommends are near Gintengai.

### Day 2: Dogo Onsen and Ishiteji

![The three-storey wooden Dogo Onsen Honkan lit up at dusk, with rickshaws parked in front](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/02.webp)

**Dogo Onsen Honkan** is a three-storey wooden public bathhouse. It finished its conservation work and fully reopened on 11 July 2024, and the basic bath went from ¥460 to ¥700 at the same time. Like most Japanese public baths, you wash at the taps before getting in and bathe without swimwear.

| Honkan course | Adult | Child | Hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kami-no-Yu, bath only | ¥700 | ¥350 | 6:00–23:00 |
| Kami-no-Yu, 2nd-floor lounge with tea | ¥1,300 | ¥650 | 6:00–22:00 |
| Tama-no-Yu, 2nd-floor lounge | ¥2,000 | ¥1,000 | 6:00–22:00 |
| Tama-no-Yu, 3rd-floor private room (90 min) | ¥2,500 | ¥1,250 | 6:00–22:00 |
| Yushinden viewing only | ¥500 | ¥250 | 9:00–17:00 |

There are two more bathhouses nearby. The **Asuka-no-Yu** annex charges ¥610 for the ground-floor bath (6:00–23:00), ¥1,280 for the second-floor lounge and ¥1,690 for a private room. **Tsubaki-no-Yu** is ¥500 (children ¥200), 6:30–23:00. If you plan to try all three, the **three-bathhouse ticket** (¥1,440, children ¥680, valid that day and the next) is cheaper than paying separately.

The L-shaped **Dogo Haikara-dori** arcade links Dogo Onsen tram stop with the Honkan, and in the little park in front of the station stands the **Botchan Karakuri Clock**, whose characters from the novel pop out on the hour from 8:00 to 22:00, every 30 minutes on weekends and in peak season.

![Stone lanterns and steps leading up to the main hall of Ishiteji, hung with five-coloured cloth](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/15.webp)

Before or after your bath, walk to **Ishiteji**, 15–20 minutes from Dogo Onsen station, or take an Iyotetsu bus bound for Okudogo from Matsuyama-shi Station for about 20 minutes to the Ishiteji stop. The grounds are free, and the main hall is a nationally designated Important Cultural Property.

### Day 3: Shimonada Station or Ozu

![The open platform shelter of Shimonada Station with blue sea beyond and potted flowers in front](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/17.webp)

Use the last day for a JR trip out of town. Fares are adult one-way.

| Destination | Train | Fare | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shimonada Station | JR Yosan Line local | ¥630 | about 49 min |
| Uchiko | JR limited express, unreserved | ¥1,610 | about 25 min |
| Uchiko | JR local | ¥850 | about 1 hr |
| Ozu | JR limited express, unreserved | ¥1,840 | about 40 min |
| Imabari | JR Ishizuchi limited express, unreserved | ¥1,840 | 36–41 min |
| Imabari | JR local | ¥1,080 | about 1 hr 20 min |

**Shimonada** is an unstaffed station whose platform looks straight out over the Iyonada sea, which has made it a famous photo spot. Local trains are infrequent, so fix your return train before you go. On its operating days, the *Iyonada Monogatari* sightseeing train also runs this way about twice a day.

**Ozu** is an old castle town. Ozu Castle costs ¥550 (¥220 for junior high and younger), 9:00–17:00, and a combined ticket with the riverside villa Garyu Sanso is ¥880.

![The two-storey wooden facade of the Uchiko-za theatre with tall Uchiko-za banners](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/19.webp)

**Uchiko** keeps a street of old merchant houses, but its best-known sight, the wooden **Uchiko-za** kabuki theatre, is closed for earthquake reinforcement until spring 2029 (planned), so you can only see it from outside for now. **Imabari**, the Shikoku end of the Shimanami Kaido cycling route, is also within 40 minutes by limited express.

## What to eat: sea bream, jakoten and mikan

![Uwajima-style taimeshi: sea bream sashimi in soy dressing with seaweed and spring onion over rice](/images/matsuyama-guide-2026-11/23.webp)

Ehime is known for sea bream, and its signature **taimeshi** (sea bream rice) comes in two styles. According to the Ehime tourism association, the Hojo style from north of Matsuyama cooks a whole bream with kelp in the rice pot, while the southern Uwajima style marinates sashimi in soy sauce and raw egg and spoons it over rice; menus usually say which one you are getting. **Jakoten** is a flat fried fish cake made from small Uwa Sea fish ground with skin and bones, and Matsuyama's **nabeyaki udon** comes in a slightly sweet broth in an aluminium pot. For dessert, three-colour **Botchan dango** (bean, egg and green tea) are named after the novel. This is mikan (mandarin) country: the airport has an Orange BAR that pours mikan juice from a tap, and 10FACTORY in town lets you compare juices by variety.

## Practical notes

- **Visas**: Many passport holders, including South Koreans, can visit Japan visa-free for up to 90 days for tourism; check Japan's foreign ministry list for your nationality.
- **Visit Japan Web**: Register immigration and customs details in advance and pass with a QR code. Optional, but faster.
- **Tax-free shopping changed on 1 November 2026**: You now pay the tax-inclusive price in shops and claim the refund at the airport when you leave, within 90 days of purchase. Allow an extra 30 minutes before check-in.
- **Payment**: An IC card covers trams, buses, convenience stores and even the Botchan train. Some small restaurants and temple shops take cash only, so carry some yen.
- **Weather**: Japan Meteorological Agency normals (1991–2020) put the January mean at 6.2°C and August at 28.1°C, with June the wettest month (228.7 mm). Cherry blossoms in Matsuyama typically open around 24 March and peak around 3 April; in 2026 they opened on 24 March and peaked on 4 April.
- **Basics**: Japan is on the same clock as Korea. Power is 100 V with type-A plugs, and there is no tipping.

## Wrapping up

For a first visit, a morning nonstop flight, one airport bus ride and one IC card are all you need. Castle and Okaido on day one, Dogo Onsen and Ishiteji on day two, and a run out to the sea at Shimonada on day three make a relaxed three-day trip. If you are weighing other Japanese cities, see [Osaka for first-timers](/en/posts/osaka-guide-2026-10/) and [Kyoto for first-timers](/en/posts/kyoto-guide-2026-10/).
