On 3 and 4 October 2026, about 480m of Itaewon-ro, from the Noksapyeong Station junction to exit 1 of Itaewon Station, became a street stage for a dance festival. The organiser, the Korea Modern Dance Promotion Association, said in its press release that it took its cue from the street and club dance culture that grew up around Itaewon's "Moon Night" club in the 1980s and 1990s. Three weeks later, on 29 October, comes the **fourth anniversary** of the night 159 people died in an alley just off the same road. Itaewon right now is **a nightlife district finding its feet again, and a neighbourhood that carries a memorial**.

In short: Itaewon is **a place named after a Joseon-era travellers' inn that became a shopping strip beside a US Army base, then in 1997 Seoul's first tourist zone "allowed to stay open at night", and since 2022 a street that has had to learn how to keep crowds safe**. This guide rebuilds that story from district records, legislation and dated reporting. The galleries around Hangangjin Station and the Hannam New Town redevelopment are in the [Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-guide-2026-09/); sights, food and hotels are in [Itaewon things to do](/en/posts/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/), [Itaewon food](/en/posts/itaewon-food-2026-09/) and [Itaewon and Haebangchon hotels](/en/posts/itaewon-hotels-2026-09/).

## Itaewon at a glance

"Itaewon" means different things to different people. In Korea, a *beopjeong-dong* is a legal neighbourhood used in addresses, and a *haengjeong-dong* is an administrative unit with its own community centre; the two do not always match.

The Itaewon 2-dong community centre describes Itaewon-dong as sitting on the southern foot of Namsan, bordered by Hannam-dong to the east, Yongsan-dong to the west and Dongbinggo-dong to the south.

### Where this guide's Itaewon begins and ends

**This blog's boundary**: we split the area by Yongsan-gu's administrative units. **Itaewon 1-dong and 2-dong (legal Itaewon-dong) and Yongsan 2-ga-dong (Haebangchon)** are covered in the [Itaewon guide](/en/posts/itaewon-guide-2026-09/); **the Hannam-dong administrative unit** and the Hannam redevelopment district that runs south of it (including Bogwang-dong and Dongbinggo-dong) are in the [Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-guide-2026-09/). There are three exceptions. First, Usadan-gil and the Seoul Central Mosque have legal Hannam-dong addresses according to the Korea Tourism Organization and Wikipedia, but they sit above exit 3 of Itaewon Station, so they go with Itaewon. Second, the War Memorial of Korea has a Yongsan-dong 1-ga address (on the Samgakji side) but is treated as an Itaewon-side sight. Third, the returned Yongsan Park land such as Yongsan Children's Garden, and the former officers' housing Block 5 site (Namsan Korean Forest Garden), which has an Itaewon-dong address, are covered on the Hannam-dong side. Likewise for the Itaewon Special Tourist Zone: the 1997 designation and the Itaewon-ro stretch are in the Itaewon guide, and its December 2025 expansion to the Hangangjin Station area is in the Hannam-dong guide.

## The name: a travellers' inn

According to the Itaewon 2-dong community centre, **Itaewon (梨泰院) was the name of a Joseon-era *won*, a state-run inn for travellers**. Seoul had four of them on its approaches: Bojewon and Jeongwanwon to the east, Itaewon to the south and Hongjewon to the west. The same page says the area was known for its pear trees.

| Period | Name (Itaewon 2-dong community centre) |
| --- | --- |
| Early Joseon | Seongjeosimni, the ten-*li* belt outside Hanseong's walls |
| 1751 | Itaewon-gye, Dunji-bang, southern Hanseong |
| 1914 | Itaewon-ri, Hanji-myeon, Goyang County, Gyeonggi |
| 1936 | Absorbed into Gyeongseong as Itaewon-jeong |
| 1943 | Placed in Yongsan-gu |
| 1946 | Itaewon-dong |

The interesting part is that **the inn was probably not in today's Itaewon-dong at all**. The community centre says the *won* is "presumed to have stood north of Yongsan High School in today's Yongsan-dong 2-ga", and that Itaewon village stood where the military apartments at the southern mouth of Namsan Tunnel 2 later were, until **in 1906 Japan designated the area as military land, demolished the houses and used it as a firing range**. Korean Wikipedia's Haebangchon article also says the area was the Japanese 20th Division's firing range. In other words, **the name started on the Haebangchon side**, which is one reason this guide covers Haebangchon too.

## From camp town to tourist zone

The community centre goes on: after the Korean War, **US forces stationed around Yongsan and the many embassies that moved in created a shopping street for cheap goods**, and **the 1988 Seoul Olympics made it known worldwide**. The Yongsan History Museum adds that the US Army XXIV Corps set up headquarters at the Yongsan Garrison after 1945, and once a permanent US presence was decided during the war, **a camp town grew up around Itaewon**.

### 25 September 1997: Seoul's first Special Tourist Zone

The National Archives of Korea explains the scheme like this:

- **1993** - Special Tourist Zones are written into the Tourism Promotion Act. The aim: **relax business-hour restrictions to encourage night-time tourism**
- **31 August 1994** - the first five zones: Jeju Island, Gyeongju, Seorak, Yuseong and Haeundae
- **25 September 1997** - Itaewon is designated

All five of the first zones were resorts outside Seoul, and **Itaewon was the first in the capital**. Knowing that the scheme was built to let places stay open late explains, from the paperwork side, why Itaewon has always been an evening neighbourhood. The Korea Tourism Organization now describes the zone as a multinational area where more than 20,000 foreign residents live.

The zone was expanded to the Hangangjin Station area by a Seoul city notice on 26 December 2025. The new extent and area are in the [Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-guide-2026-09/).

### The World Food Street was planned in 2012

The alley behind the Hamilton Hotel became the "World Food Street" through a 2012 district plan. According to Herald Economy (November 2012), Yongsan-gu planned **a 6m-wide, 300m main street with five link lanes totalling 210m**, noting that restaurants and shops from 30 countries were already there. Car-free Fridays to Sundays were part of the plan.

## 29 October 2022, and the fourth anniversary

On the night of 29 October 2022, 159 people died in a crowd crush in the alley beside the Hamilton Hotel. Here are only the official changes that followed.

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 26 October 2023 | **10.29 Memorial Alley** (기억과 안전의 길, "the path of memory and safety") created in the alley, led by the bereaved families' council |
| 21 May 2024 | **Special Act on the 10.29 Itaewon Disaster** (on guaranteeing victims' rights, finding the truth and preventing recurrence) promulgated and in force, Act No. 20427 |
| 15 September 2026 | Amendment in force: the special investigation committee's term extended by a year to **16 September 2027**; victim recognition applications open until 16 March 2028 |

Kyunghyang Shinmun reported that the memorial alley is **about 40m from exit 1 of Itaewon Station**. The words "We still have names we must remember" are set into the ground at the entrance, and the Hamilton Hotel wall carries photographic works and a panel explaining what happened. The artworks and sticky notes are changed every two months. This guide deliberately includes no photo of it.

### Halloween crowd management: what changed in 2025

**31 October 2026 is a Saturday.** As of 6 October we had found no 2026 Halloween safety plan, but this is what applied in 2025.

## Boom and bust: Gyeongnidan-gil and vacancy rates

Gyeongnidan-gil is often called the original "-ridan-gil", the street whose name spawned copycats all over Seoul (Seoul Economic Daily, 2019). But Itaewon's commercial numbers look very different depending on who is counting and which kind of building.

| When | Figure | Measure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 2015 | **8.9%** | Korea Appraisal Board, Itaewon medium-to-large retail vacancy | Seoul Economic Daily, 2019 |
| Q1 2018 | 22.4% | Same measure | Seoul Economic Daily, 2019 |
| Q1 2019 | **24.3%** | Same measure | Seoul Economic Daily, 2019 |
| End 2016 → end 2018 | 1,710 → 1,634 shops | Seoul commercial-district data, operating shops in Itaewon 1-dong | Seoul Economic Daily, 2019 |
| Q3 2025 | Small retail **3.9%** (13.9% a year earlier) / medium-to-large 13.6% (19.9%) | Korea Real Estate Board, Itaewon | Korea Economic Daily, 2025 |
| Q4 2025 | **7.9%** | Cushman & Wakefield, Hannam–Itaewon high-street retail | Yonhap, 2026 |
| Q4 2025 | **37.7%**, highest in Seoul | Korea Real Estate Board, multi-unit retail buildings, Yongsan area including Itaewon | Edaily Market In, 2026 |

So **medium-to-large vacancy nearly tripled between 2015 and 2019**, and in Q3 2025 vacancy in both small and larger shops was lower than a year earlier. Yet in the same quarter, multi-unit retail buildings in Yongsan had the highest vacancy in Seoul. Cushman & Wakefield said global brands opening flagships in Hannam and Itaewon were pushing the shopping area into the side streets. **"Itaewon is back" and "Itaewon is empty" can both be said with numbers**; what matters is which measure is being used.

## Haebangchon: there was once a "Haebang-dong"

![The 108 steps in Haebangchon. On the right, an inclined lift runs up the slope inside a glass enclosure; on the left, one person climbs the stairs](/images/itaewon-guide-2026-09/05.webp)

Haebangchon, "Liberation Village", is not an official name today. But Korean Wikipedia's history of Yongsan-gu's administrative units shows that **when the administrative-unit system began on 18 April 1955, there was a "Haebang-dong"**. It was renamed Yongsan-dong in 1959, Yongsan 2-ga 1-dong in 1970 and Yongsan 2-ga-dong in 1977.

The village is exactly what its name says. The Yongsan History Museum says people who came south from the North after 1945, or lost their homes in the war, **built a village on the Namsan slopes near the Yongsan Garrison**. Wikipedia adds that when the US military government evicted them from former Japanese army quarters, they built shacks on the old firing range above. That is the same ground where the Itaewon inn is thought to have stood.

### Sinheung Market: from three shops to 5,000 visitors a day

| Item | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origins | **1953**, a market for daily necessities in Haebangchon | Yonhap, 2026 |
| Low point | Only **three shops left**; known as the "ghost market" | Hankook Ilbo, 2026 |
| Turnaround | Urban regeneration began about ten years ago. That year the merchants' association head visited building owners one by one to sign a **six-year pact to raise rents no faster than inflation** | Hankook Ilbo, 2026 |
| Now | Up to **5,000 visitors a day** on public holidays (association's figure) | Hankook Ilbo, 2026 |
| 1–3 October 2026 | Yongsan-gu's Sinheung Market Global Local Festival (flea market, a yut-nori "world cup") | Yonhap, 2026 |

In the Hankook Ilbo interview, the association head said that after Gyeongnidan-gil boomed and faded, people warned Haebangchon would "end up like Gyeongnidan". The pact has expired, but he says there has been no big rent rise since. That is his account; we found no official rent data to check it.

### The 108 steps and the inclined lift

An inclined lift opened beside **the 108 steps** on Sinheung-ro, at the bottom of Haebangchon, on 19 November 2018. According to Sports Seoul, it is **53m long, carries 15 people and travels 60m a minute, so the climb takes about a minute**. It cost 2.8 billion won (about $2.1 million) and was the first inclined lift installed in a Korean residential area. In the photo above, the lift runs inside the glass enclosure on the right.

## Noksapyeong: "a field of green grass"

![View north from a footbridge over a wide road lined with fresh green trees. At the end of the road, housing packs a hillside, and N Seoul Tower stands on top of Namsan, dotted with cherry blossom](/images/itaewon-guide-2026-09/04.webp)

According to Korean Wikipedia, Noksapyeong (綠莎坪) means **"a field thick with green grass"**, a name given because almost no one lived here until the end of the Joseon era. Looking north as in the photo, Namsan rises right behind the hill covered by Haebangchon.

The returned US Army land west of the station (Yongsan Park and Yongsan Children's Garden) and its current opening hours are covered in the [Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-guide-2026-09/).

## Usadan-gil and the Seoul Central Mosque

![A green street sign with a mosque illustration reading 'CENTRAL MOSQUE' and '200M' in English and Korean, with a smaller sign pointing toward Usadan-gil below](/images/itaewon-guide-2026-09/07.webp)

The **Seoul Central Mosque** (39 Usadan-ro 10-gil) is, according to the Korea Muslim Federation, **Korea's first mosque: the government provided about 1,500 pyeong (roughly 5,000m²) of land in May 1969, construction began in October 1974 and it opened on 21 May 1976**. Visitors in revealing clothes are asked to **wear garments (skirts, hijabs and so on) from the changing room beside the guard office**. Wikipedia notes it is close to exit 3 of Itaewon Station; follow signs like the one above from Itaewon-ro toward Usadan-gil.

The Korea Tourism Organization describes Usadan-gil as a mix of Pakistani, Turkish, Egyptian, Lebanese and Indian restaurants and an old residential hillside, **where young artists and entrepreneurs began moving in from the early 2010s**.

## How to do it

West to east, getting the climbing done first.

1. **Morning** - Noksapyeong on [[line:6]] up to Haebangchon: the 108-steps lift, Sinheung Market, the Namsan view.
2. **Lunch** - down via Gyeongnidan-gil (Hoenamu-ro) or to Itaewon-ro. How to choose is in [Itaewon food](/en/posts/itaewon-food-2026-09/).
3. **Afternoon** - Usadan-gil and the Seoul Central Mosque from exit 3. An order of sights is in [Itaewon things to do](/en/posts/itaewon-things-to-do-2026-09/).
4. **Evening** - Itaewon-ro and the World Food Street. Pass the memorial alley by exit 1 quietly.
5. **If you have time** - the galleries at Hangangjin; see the [Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-guide-2026-09/).

## Crowds and etiquette

- **At the 10.29 Memorial Alley, slow down and do not photograph people paying their respects.** Do not touch or remove the notes and artworks.
- **On Halloween and weekend nights, keep moving in the World Food Street.** In 2025 barriers channelled people to the right, and district staff kept announcing that standing still was dangerous (Seoul Shinmun).
- **The Seoul Central Mosque is a place of worship.** If your clothes are revealing, you will be asked to wear garments from the changing room.
- **Haebangchon is residential.** The 108 steps and the lanes are residents' routes; do not block them for photos.

## Who it suits

**Good fit:**

- You want a neighbourhood built around world food and evenings
- You want **hill villages and Namsan views**, as in Haebangchon
- You want to see how a city **changed crowd safety** after 2022
- You are curious about **the stories behind official records**: tourist zones, vacancy rates, rent pacts

**Not such a good fit:**

- If hills are hard for you, skip Haebangchon and Gyeongnidan-gil and stick to Itaewon-ro
- If you are expecting a giant Halloween street party, since 2025 this has been **a priority crowd-safety zone**
- If traditional hanok lanes are the goal, try the [Bukchon guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/) or the [Seochon guide](/en/posts/seochon-guide-2026-09/)

## In short

- **Boundary**: Itaewon 1-dong and 2-dong (legal Itaewon-dong) and Yongsan 2-ga-dong (Haebangchon). The Hannam-dong unit and Hannam New Town are in the [Hannam-dong guide](/en/posts/hannam-guide-2026-09/).
- **Name**: a Joseon state inn for travellers, which the district thinks stood north of today's Yongsan High School in Yongsan-dong 2-ga.
