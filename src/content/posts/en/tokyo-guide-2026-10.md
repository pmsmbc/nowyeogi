Tokyo keeps coming up as a first long-haul-free trip for readers in Seoul, Singapore, Hong Kong and Kuala Lumpur for the same reasons: direct flights into an airport that sits almost inside the city, visa-free short stays for many passports, and a transit system that runs on one tap-to-pay card. The part that trips people up is the choice between two airports and half a dozen train brands, so this guide starts there. Every fare and opening time below is as of October 2026, taken from the operators' own pages and from GO TOKYO, the Tokyo Convention and Visitors Bureau site. Prices are in yen; at roughly ¥150 to the US dollar, ¥1,000 is about $6.70.

## Narita or Haneda: pick the airport first

Tokyo has two airports. **Haneda** is on the bay just south of the centre: Keikyu's fastest trains reach Shinagawa in 11 minutes and the Tokyo Monorail reaches Hamamatsucho in 13. **Narita** is out in Chiba Prefecture, about an hour by train to Tokyo Station and about 90 minutes to Shinjuku according to GO TOKYO. If fares are similar, a Haneda arrival saves both time and transport money.

From Seoul, the Gimpo–Haneda pair is the city-centre-to-city-centre route: Korean Air, Asiana, Japan Airlines and ANA all fly it, and direct flights show as roughly 2 hours 5 minutes. Incheon has flights to both Tokyo airports, so check which one your booking lands at. From Southeast Asia and Hong Kong, airlines split between the two as well, and the airport code on the ticket (HND or NRT) is the thing to look for.

The Skyliner is all reserved seating, and the discounted ticket for overseas visitors is sold only online or on board some low-cost carriers (regular ¥2,580, discounted ¥2,310, round trip ¥4,500). The Narita Express is a JR train, so it runs straight through to Tokyo, Shinagawa, Shibuya and Shinjuku without a change, but after JR East's March 2026 fare revision the Shinjuku fare is ¥3,330. A simple rule: hotel in Ueno or Asakusa, take the Skyliner; hotel in Shinjuku or Shibuya, take the N'EX or the limousine bus and keep your luggage in one place.

Haneda needs less thought. From Shinagawa the JR Yamanote Line adds 13 minutes to Tokyo Station, and from Hamamatsucho it is 7 minutes. Taxis run on the meter with a surcharge between 22:00 and 5:00 plus expressway tolls, so for a late arrival check the limousine bus timetable first.

## Transit cards and passes: one Welcome Suica does it

Tokyo's railways are run by JR, Tokyo Metro, Toei and several private companies, but one IC card rides all of them. The tourist **Welcome Suica** card costs from ¥1,000 with no deposit and works for 28 days. If you carry an iPhone, the **Welcome Suica Mobile** app is easier still: it is made by JR East, charges no deposit or issuance fee, tops up through the card linked to Apple Pay, and stays valid for 180 days. The balance cannot be refunded, so spend it down at a convenience store on your last day. It needs iOS 17.2 or later, or an Apple Watch Series 3 or later. A regular Suica card starts at ¥1,000 including a ¥500 deposit.

If you will ride the subway several times a day, the **Tokyo Subway Ticket** is the better deal: ¥1,000 for 24 hours, ¥1,500 for 48 and ¥2,000 for 72 (children half price), unlimited on all nine Tokyo Metro and four Toei lines, 13 in total. It is sold at the airports, subway stations and tourist information centres, and is aimed at overseas visitors and Japanese residents from outside the Kanto region. JR lines are not included, so the [[line:yamanote]] that links Shibuya, Shinjuku, Harajuku and Tokyo Station still goes on your Suica. Metro-only riders have a ¥700 24-hour ticket, and Toei has a ¥700 one-day pass.

## Where to stay: four bases compared

![The red arch of Kabukicho Ichibangai in Shinjuku and a street packed with neon signs at night](/images/tokyo-guide-2026-10/21.webp)

**Shinjuku** is the rail hub where JR, private lines and the subway meet, so everywhere is easy to reach, and the expressway bus terminal where the airport limousines stop is right at the station. The west side has the high-rise hotels and the government building, the east side has Kabukicho and the restaurant streets. It stays bright late into the night; the rowdier alleys are easy to avoid.

**Shibuya** is served by the [[line:yamanote]], [[line:ginza]], [[line:hanzomon]] and [[line:fukutoshin]], and Harajuku, Omotesando and Meiji Jingu are all within walking distance. Pick it for shopping and a younger crowd.

**Ginza and Tokyo Station** have the department stores and Michelin restaurants, with the [[line:ginza]], [[line:marunouchi]] and [[line:hibiya]] overlapping JR. Tsukiji and teamLab Planets in Toyosu are close, and it is the most convenient base if you plan to take the Shinkansen onward. Room rates here are generally reported as the highest of the four.

**Ueno and Asakusa** are the old-town option, with the Skyliner terminus at Ueno and Senso-ji in Asakusa. The [[line:ginza]] and [[line:toeiasakusa]] connect them to the centre, and a Narita arrival makes this the easiest place to haul luggage to. Evenings are quieter, so if late nights matter, stay in Shinjuku or Shibuya instead.

## A 4-day core itinerary

One district per day keeps transit time down. The prices and hours below come from each venue's official pages.

### Day 1: Asakusa and Tokyo Skytree

![Tokyo Skytree rising above the low buildings of Sumida under a clear blue sky](/images/tokyo-guide-2026-10/08.webp)

**Senso-ji** is described as Tokyo's oldest temple and is free to enter. The main hall opens 6:00–17:00, from 6:30 between October and March, while the grounds and the Kaminarimon gate are open round the clock. Go early for the quiet version, or in the daytime if you want the Nakamise shopping street open too. The nearest station is Asakusa on the [[line:ginza]] and [[line:toeiasakusa]].

**Tokyo Skytree** is across the river. Official prices start at ¥3,000 for adults for the combined ticket covering the 350 m Tembo Deck and the 450 m Tembo Galleria, and at ¥1,800 for the Tembo Deck alone, rising on busier dates. Buying at the 4F counter adds ¥500 per ticket, so book on the web or at a convenience store. A Galleria add-on (¥1,400) is sold at machines on the deck on the day. Hours are 10:00–22:00 on weekdays and 9:00–22:00 on weekends and holidays, with last entry an hour before closing; they change by season, so glance at the official calendar.

### Day 2: Meiji Jingu, Harajuku and Shibuya

![The large wooden torii gate on the forest path to Meiji Jingu, with visitors walking beneath it](/images/tokyo-guide-2026-10/14.webp)

**Meiji Jingu** is free and opens at sunrise and closes at sunset. The shrine's monthly table gives 5:40–16:40 for October, 6:40–16:00 for December and roughly 5:00–18:10 in early summer. Facilities inside mostly keep 9:00–16:00, so see the museum or garden in the daytime. It sits right beside JR Harajuku Station, which makes Takeshita Street and Omotesando part of the same walk.

![Shibuya Scramble Crossing seen from above, with people crossing in every direction between the buildings](/images/tokyo-guide-2026-10/06.webp)

Head down to Shibuya in the afternoon. **Shibuya Sky** is the rooftop deck of Shibuya Scramble Square, directly connected to the station. Online tickets are ¥2,700 for entry before 15:00 and ¥3,400 from 15:00; counter prices are ¥3,000 and ¥3,700. It opens 10:00–22:30 with last entry at 21:20 and closes on January 1. The official page notes that when adult tickets sell out online they are not available at the counter either, and the rooftop closes in strong wind or rain. The scramble crossing is right below when you come down.

### Day 3: Tsukiji, Ginza, teamLab and the TMG observatory

**Tsukiji Outer Market** kept its shopping streets after the wholesale market moved to Toyosu in 2018. The market's own guide sets 6:00–9:00 for trade buyers and 9:00–14:00 for the public, with afternoon hours varying by shop. Many shops close on Wednesdays, Sundays and holidays, so check the official calendar before you go. It is one minute from Tsukijishijo Station exit A1 on the [[line:oedo]] or Tsukiji Station exits 1 and 2 on the [[line:hibiya]], and Ginza is a walk away.

There are two **teamLab** museums. **teamLab Planets** in Toyosu opens 8:00–22:00, adults from ¥3,800 depending on the date, three minutes from Shin-Toyosu Station on the Yurikamome line, and the official site says it is scheduled to run until the end of 2027. **teamLab Borderless** in Azabudai Hills opens 8:30–21:00 (until 22:00 on some dates), adults from ¥3,400 with ¥200 added for on-site purchase, two minutes from Kamiyacho Station exit 5 on the [[line:hibiya]]. Both use timed-entry tickets, so booking ahead is the norm.

![Visitors looking out over the city through the tall windows of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatory](/images/tokyo-guide-2026-10/10.webp)

Finish the day at the **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observatories**. Both the North and South decks on the 45th floor, 202 m up, are free and open 9:30–22:00 with entry until 21:30. The South deck closes on the first and third Tuesdays and the North deck on the second and fourth Mondays, so if one is shut, cross to the other tower. Tochomae Station on the [[line:oedo]] connects underground, and it is a walk from the west exit of JR Shinjuku Station.

### Day 4: back to the airport

For Haneda, ride to Shinagawa or Hamamatsucho and change. For Narita, the Skyliner leaves from Ueno and Nippori and the N'EX from Tokyo Station and Shinjuku. From November 2026 tax-free refunds are processed at the airport (see the practical notes), so allow an extra 30 minutes before check-in.

## Food: choose the neighbourhood, not the dish

![Crab, shellfish and other seafood laid out with price tags on a stall at Tsukiji Outer Market](/images/tokyo-guide-2026-10/16.webp)

Tokyo is easier to eat in by area than by menu. Breakfast at **Tsukiji** usually means a seafood rice bowl or grilled skewers eaten standing at the stall, and lunch at any station-front ramen shop or beef-bowl chain keeps the bill low. The monjayaki street in **Tsukishima**, tempura and eel in **Asakusa**, and the yakitori alley of Omoide Yokocho in **Shinjuku** come up constantly in reviews. In Ginza, many high-end restaurants serve lunch courses far cheaper than dinner, which is the usual way to try one on a budget. Convenience-store food gets consistently good reviews, so a late-night meal is never a problem.

## Disney and day trips, only if days are spare

**Tokyo Disneyland and DisneySea** use date-based pricing: the official calendar shows ¥9,400–9,900 for weekdays in September 2026 and ¥11,900 for January 1, 2027. A park takes a full day, so it does not fit a four-day plan; set aside a day if you have five or more, or are travelling with children. Kamakura, Hakone and Nikko are the popular day trips, but this guide covers central Tokyo only and the surroundings will get their own post.

## Practical notes

![Shibuya Scramble Crossing at night, with people crossing between buildings lit by neon signs](/images/tokyo-guide-2026-10/04.webp)

- **Visas**: JNTO's guide says visa exemptions apply to short tourist stays of up to 90 days for passports from a number of countries, and the Japanese embassy in Seoul confirms 90 days for ordinary Korean passports. Paid work is not allowed on a visa-free entry. Check your own passport's terms before booking.
- **Visit Japan Web**: an online pre-registration for immigration and customs. It is optional, but it speeds up customs and the tax-free procedure.
- **Tax-free changes on November 1, 2026**: purchases up to October 31 work as now, with tax deducted at the till. From November 1 you pay the tax-inclusive price in the shop, then before departure present your passport at a tax-free procedure terminal in the international departure lobby (before checking bags) and receive the refund. A green result means you are done; a red one sends you and the goods to the customs counter. The limits stay the same: within 90 days of purchase and at least ¥5,000 before tax per shop per day. Sealed packaging for food and cosmetics is no longer required, and anything used in Japan is not refundable.
- **Paying**: Suica balance covers convenience stores, vending machines and station shops. Small restaurants and market stalls still take cash only in places, so carry ¥10,000–20,000 in notes.
- **Weather**: the rainy season runs from June to mid-July, midsummer passes 35°C with nights rarely under 25°C, and typhoon season starts in August and reaches into October. Autumn colour starts in the Okutama hills in late October and reaches the city in November; winter stays between 1 and 10°C with little snow. Cherry blossoms peak from late March to early April.
- **Basics**: Japan is on the same clock as Korea and one hour ahead of Singapore, Hong Kong and Malaysia. Power is 100 V with type-A plugs. There is no tipping, public bins are rare so carry a small bag for rubbish, and phone calls on trains are frowned upon.

## Wrapping up

For a first visit, land at Haneda, get a Welcome Suica, stay in Shinjuku or Shibuya, and give Asakusa, Shibuya and Tsukiji a day each. Pick one observatory, the free Tokyo Metropolitan Government deck or the paid Shibuya Sky, and build the schedule around whichever teamLab date you can book.
