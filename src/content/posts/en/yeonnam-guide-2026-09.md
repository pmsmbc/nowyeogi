On 1 October 2026, the Seoul Metropolitan Government announced that it had **confirmed a fast-track redevelopment plan for the Mangwon-dong 416-53 area** (Newsis, 1 October 2026). A low-rise neighbourhood about 500m from Mangwon Station will become an apartment complex of up to 29 floors and 1,550 homes. The site sits right next to Mangwon Market and **Mangridan-gil**, the small-shop street along Poeun-ro, so the city says the blocks facing Poeun-ro will be kept to 12-13 floors with small shops at street level. Ten years after this street became a destination, the plan for keeping it is now written into a redevelopment scheme.

The short version: Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong are **two neighbourhoods shaped by a railway and by water**. Yeonnam-dong changed when the Gyeongui Line, opened in 1906, was put underground and a park was laid on top in 2015. Mangwon-dong became the setting for Korea's first class action after a floodgate on the Han River embankment collapsed in 1984. This guide pulls together their history, boundaries and current numbers from official records and dated news reports. Sights and opening details are in [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong things to do](/en/posts/yeonnam-things-to-do-2026-09/), food in [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong food and cafes](/en/posts/yeonnam-food-2026-09/), and accommodation in [where to stay in Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong](/en/posts/yeonnam-hotels-2026-09/).

A quick note on names: Korean neighbourhoods are called *dong*, so Yeonnam-dong is simply "Yeonnam neighbourhood". *Gil* means street or path.

![Yeontral Park on a clear summer day: two old rails still lie on the bed of a shallow channel beside the lawn, and a couple carrying a small dog walks along the path. High-rises near Hongik University Station stand in the distance](/images/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/10.webp)

This is **Yeontral Park**, the nickname for the Yeonnam-dong section of the Gyeongui Line Forest Park, in a photo from Wikimedia Commons. The two lines on the bottom of the channel are **old Gyeongui Line rails**, and according to the Seoul government the water is **groundwater rising from the airport railway tunnel beneath your feet**. Trains went underground and people now walk where they used to run.

"Yeonnam" means **south of Yeonhui**. According to Wikipedia, when Seoul redrew district boundaries in 1975 it moved the part of Yeonhui-dong southwest of the Gyeongui Line into Mapo-gu under this name. That is why Yeonnam-dong's northern edge is still Yeonhui-dong, in a different district. "Mangwon" comes from **Mangwonjeong** (望遠亭, "pavilion for gazing far"), a riverside pavilion rebuilt by Prince Wolsan in the 15th century.

## The stream that once linked them: Segyocheon

There is another reason to cover the two neighbourhoods together: a stream you can no longer see, **Segyocheon**. Opinion News (July 2026) describes it as **a stream that rose in Yeonhui-dong, ran through Yeonnam-dong and Seogyo-dong, and entered the Han River between Hapjeong-dong and Mangwon-dong**. On Kim Jeong-ho's 19th-century map of Seoul, it is drawn flowing from Muak (Ansan) past Segyo-ri and **past Mangwonjeong into the Han**.

- **Where it went**: according to the same article, **Donggyo-ro**, the road through Yeonnam-dong, covers the middle and lower stretches of Segyocheon. Mapo-gu has named the streets around Donggyo-ro and the forest park "Kkirikkiri-gil" 1 to 5.
- **When it disappeared**: a 2015 Seoul government press release says Segyocheon around Yeonnam-dong **vanished from the surface during the 1977 Nanjido embankment works**.
- **How it came back**: the stream in Yeontral Park was, in the city's words, "made using groundwater rising from the underground airport railway" to **recreate the lost Segyocheon**.

In other words, **the water in Yeonnam's park and the stretch of Han River in front of Mangwonjeong were once one stream**. Following its direction is also the easiest way to walk from one neighbourhood to the other (see "Suggested route" below).

## From railway to park

![A rusted steel sign reading 'Gyeongui Line, Yongsan to Sinuiju, since April 3, 1906' in Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese, above a route map showing Yongsan, Seogang, Segyo-ri and Gajwa stations, the Danginri Line (closed 1982) and the Sinchon link line (closed 1975)](/images/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/21.webp)

This sign stands on the Hongdae stretch of the Gyeongui Line Forest Park. It reads **Gyeongui Line, Yongsan to Sinuiju, since 3 April 1906** (Sinuiju is now in North Korea, on the Chinese border). The map shows the old line from Yongsan through Seogang and **Segyo-ri** to Gajwa, and the **Danginri Line (closed 1982)** branching off at Segyo-ri. The Danginri Line story is in the [Hongdae guide](/en/posts/hongdae-guide-2026-09/). Yeonnam-dong is the stretch between Segyo-ri and Gajwa on this map, exactly where the tracks ran.

The railway land was **provided free by the Korea Rail Network Authority**, and Seoul paid the **45.7 billion won** construction bill. The park runs from the Yongsan Culture and Sports Centre to Gajwa Station in Mapo-gu, 10 to 60m wide. Two Seoul press releases give the Yeonnam-dong section as 1,268m and 1,266m, so we use **about 1.27km**.

When the full trail opened in 2016, the city itself wrote that "the Yeonnam-dong section, opened last June, is called 'Yeontral Park' and has become a new Seoul landmark". **The nickname, a play on New York's Central Park, made it into official documents.** The Yeonnam-dong section has a ginkgo-lined path of about 1km starting at the big pine tree outside exit 3, plus the stream described above.

### The next railway park?

The Korea Economic Daily (20 September 2026) used the trail as the starting point for a debate about a "second Yeontral Park". In **October 2024 Seoul announced a plan to put 67.6km of surface railway underground**, including the **Gyeongui Line from Gajwa to Seoul Station**. Gajwa is the northern end of Yeontral Park. The same article notes the plan has not yet been adopted into the national transport ministry's master plan.

## Seoul's Chinese neighbourhood: the 1969 school move

Yeonnam-dong has a story older than the park: its community of **hwagyo**, ethnic Chinese residents of Korea and their descendants.

## After Yeontral Park: rents, zoning and drinking rules

### "Gentrification" was in the 2015 press release

From the Seoul government release issued when the Yeonnam-dong section opened in 2015:

> Yeonnam-dong, where many artists pushed out by large commercial districts such as Hongdae have moved, is an area where newly arrived artists and community groups are joining residents to run flea markets and art classes and build a local economy **in order to address 'gentrification'**.

So **from opening day, Yeonnam-dong was introduced as the place people priced out of Hongdae had moved to**. The city wanted the park to be run by citizens, like New York's Friends of the High Line, through the **Gyeongui Line Forest Keepers** founded in February 2015. The first unit listed was the **Yeonnam-dong Keepers**.

### Rents up 25-30% in a year

| When | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2011-2013 | **Human Town** project around Yeonnam-dong 239-1: instead of demolition, it kept low-rise houses and alleys, spending about 5.4 billion won to bury power lines and repave roads | Yonhap 2022, Edaily 2016 |
| June 2015 | Yeonnam-dong section of the park opens | Seoul |
| May 2016 | Local agents say shop rents rose **25-30% in a year**. One 23.1㎡ shop signed at 100 million won key money and 2 million won a month, up from 1.6 million before the park opened | Edaily, 2016 |
| 21 April 2022 | Seoul converts the Human Town plan into the **Yeonnam-dong District Unit Plan** and **allows cafes, restaurants and other neighbourhood businesses** along the park | Yonhap, 2022 |

"Key money" (*gwonrigeum*) is a premium a new tenant pays the outgoing one for a shop's location and customers, common in Korea. The 2022 decision ran in the opposite direction to Human Town: once visitor numbers jumped, Seoul chose to **spread the shops from the park's centre along its entire length**. The long line of cafes and restaurants beside Yeontral Park today largely dates from after that change.

On housing, the Korea Economic Daily (September 2026) cited two academic studies: apartments within 500m of a park entrance were **on average 4.26% pricier for every 100m closer** (2006-2015 data), and in 2013-14, when the phase including Yeonnam-dong was built, homes within 100m of the park **rose 8.04%**, with smaller rises further away.

### Alcohol-clean zone: not a ban, a "don't bother others" rule

Drinking in parks is generally legal in Korea, which is why the rules here can confuse visitors.

| When | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2017 | Seoul's ordinance on healthy drinking culture designates 22 city-run parks, including this trail, as **alcohol-clean zones** | Asia Economy, 2024 |
| **1 April 2018** | Fines of **up to 100,000 won** (about $75) for drinking and then causing loud noise, odour or other nuisance | Same |
| July 2021 | During COVID-19, Mapo-gu temporarily banned night-time drinking in 173 parks including this trail | MBC, 2021 |
| April 2024 | Only **one sign** about the rule along the whole 6.3km | Asia Economy, 2024 |

So **a can of beer in Yeontral Park is not itself a fineable offence.** The problem is noise, smell and litter afterwards. Homes line both sides of the park, so late-night noise quickly turns into complaints. Korean media have called it the "Gyeongui booze trail" (Herald Economy, 2018) and "Sultral Park", a pun on *sul*, alcohol (Energy Economic News, 2021).

## Mangwon-dong: the neighbourhood where the floodgate broke

### 10:30 am, 2 September 1984

The judgment of the Seoul Civil District Court in case **84GaHap5010**, published by the Korea Law Information Center, is titled "the so-called Mangwon-dong mass flood case". The facts it accepted:

| Item | In the judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Background | After a flood on 19 August 1972, Seoul built the **Mangwon retention basin around 450 Mangwon-dong, around 1973** (54,000㎡) |
| Rain | Heavy rain over central Korea for three days from the afternoon of 31 August 1984 |
| Failure | **Around 10:30 on 2 September 1984** the **sluice box** in the drainage channel between the Han River and the basin **collapsed** |
| Result | River water flowed back, overtopped the basin and left Mangwon-dong **under more than 1m of water until the morning of 4 September** |
| Scale | Seoul gave relief supplies to **11,942 households, 49,358 people** |
| First ruling | **26 August 1987: Seoul held liable** (claims against the contractor rejected) |

The key passage:

> The Mangwon-dong mass flood **did not occur because an untamed natural river overflowed**, but because of a defect inherent in the sluice box of the Mangwon retention basin, an artificial public facility already in place, that is, its lack of the safety it should normally have had.

In plain terms, **the river did not burst its banks; the structure built to hold it back broke.** The court also noted the flood level that year was lower even than the one "just 12 years earlier".

### 1990: the Supreme Court and Korea's first class action

| When | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About a month after the flood | Residents sue the city for damages | Kyunghyang, 2010 |
| 26 Aug 1987 | Residents win at first instance, then again on appeal | Court record, Kyunghyang 2010 |
| **2 August 1990** | **Supreme Court upholds the ruling** in a suit by 22 residents, awarding 200,000 to 2.41 million won each | Kyunghyang, 2010 and 2011 |
| Afterwards | Including follow-up suits, **about 12,000 residents receive 5.32 billion won** | Kyunghyang, 2010 |

Kyunghyang Shinmun credits human-rights lawyer **Cho Young-rae** with effectively designing the case, with lawyers including Park Seung-seo, Lee Se-joong and Park Won-soon (later mayor of Seoul) arguing it. It is remembered as the case that **took Korea's human-rights lawyers from political trials into mass civil litigation**. Wikipedia notes Mangwon-dong was **one of Seoul's chronically flooded areas until the early 1990s**, which is why it saw less development and has more old housing than neighbouring Hapjeong-dong or Sangam-dong.

The retention basin is still central to Mapo's flood defences. **The district mayor who took office on 1 July 2026 spent his first official engagement inspecting the Mangwon retention basin and the Mangwon 1 rainwater pumping station** (Seoul Shinmun, 3 July 2026).

### Mangwonjeong is not in Mangwon-dong

According to the Korea Tourism Organization:

- **1424** Prince Hyoryeong builds a retreat here; when King Sejong visits the next year welcome rain falls, so it is named **Huiujeong** ("pavilion of joyful rain")
- **1484** Prince Wolsan rebuilds it on a larger scale as **Mangwonjeong**
- Renamed under King Yeonsangun, restored to Mangwonjeong after his overthrow
- **1925** lost in the great flood and later river works
- **Construction began June 1988, completed October 1989**; now a Seoul monument

Yet the KTO address for the **Mangwonjeong site is 23 Donggyo-ro 8an-gil, Hapjeong-dong**. **The pavilion that named the neighbourhood now has an address in the next one.** The official Mangwon Hangang Park page also lists it as a "nearby cultural heritage site".

## Mangridan-gil: a small-shop street and 1,550 new homes

"Mangridan-gil" borrows from **Gyeongnidan-gil**, a well-known café street in Itaewon. It refers to the small shops along **Poeun-ro** south of Mangwon Station, and Mapo-gu now uses the name "Mangridan-gil alley shopping street" for official events (Shinailbo, September 2026). The redevelopment zone confirmed this month lies directly to its west.

| When | What | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2021 | First fast-track bid for Mangwon Zone 1 (around 416-53), amid conflict between residents and shopkeepers | Edaily, 2026 |
| After that | Seoul **rejects** the candidacy **twice** | Hankook Ilbo, 2025 |
| 2023 | Approved **on condition** that the zone boundary considers local shops; 68% of residents in favour at the time | Hankook Ilbo, 2025 |
| Aug 2025 | Site of **78,695㎡** with about 1,500 low-rise homes. Opponents claim 11% of owners submitted objections | Hankook Ilbo, 2025 |
| Dec 2025 | City selection committee gives **conditional consent** to the advisory plan | Newspim, 2025 |
| **1 Oct 2026** | Seoul announces the **fast-track plan is confirmed** | Newsis, Seoul Shinmun 2026 |

What the confirmed plan says (Newsis, 1 October 2026):

- **Size**: up to **29 floors, 1,550 homes**, about 500m from Mangwon Station
- **Facing Mangridan-gil**: the edge next to the Poeun-ro shops limited to **12-13 floors**, with small shops along Poeun-ro
- **Traffic**: car entrances on the west and south sides, away from Poeun-ro
- **Park**: about **4,800㎡** in the southeast corner, with a public car park underneath and visitor toilets
- **Walking**: an east-west public walkway linking Mangwon Station, Mangwon Market and the Han River; surrounding roads such as Mangwon-ro 4-gil widened to 15-18m

For visitors: **the plan keeps the Mangridan-gil shops, but the block next door may become a building site in a few years.** "Fast-track" (*sinsok tonghap gihoek*) is a Seoul scheme in which the city drafts the plan to speed up approvals; union formation and zone designation still lie ahead. As of October 2026 the neighbourhood looks as it always has.

Village buses (*maeul* buses) are small green local buses; they take the same T-money card as the subway.

**From Incheon Airport, the [[line:arex]] all-stop train stops directly at Hongik University Station.** The express train runs non-stop to Seoul Station, so take the all-stop service. Hongik University Station is served by **three lines**, which arrived in 1984 ([[line:2]]), 2010 ([[line:arex]]) and 2012 ([[line:gyeongui]]). Exits are spread out by line, so agree on an exit number when meeting someone. Edaily reported in May 2016 that after the park opened, exit 3 became as crowded in the evenings as exit 9, the classic Hongdae meeting point.

## Suggested route

To see both neighbourhoods in a day, **start in Yeonnam-dong and finish by the river**, the same direction the old Segyocheon flowed.

1. **Morning**: Hongik University Station exit 3, then north along Yeontral Park. The stream and the ginkgo path are the heart of this section.
2. **Late morning**: the old Chinese restaurant lanes toward Yeonhui-dong, or the Donggyo-ro area.
3. **Lunch**: Yeonnam-dong lanes. Tips in [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong food and cafes](/en/posts/yeonnam-food-2026-09/).
4. **Afternoon**: move to Mangwon Station for **Mangwon Market** and **Mangridan-gil** (Poeun-ro). A Seoul government article also suggests buying snacks at the market before heading to the river.
5. **Sunset**: **Mangwon Hangang Park**, with Seongsan Bridge to the west and Seoul Battleship Park inside the park. For the Hangang Bus, head to Mangwon pier.
6. **Heading back**: Hapjeong or Mangwon stations. The Mangwonjeong site is a short walk from Hapjeong.

## Crowds and etiquette

- **Homes line both sides of Yeontral Park.** It is an alcohol-clean zone, so drunken noise or odour can be fined. Keep voices down at night.
- **Take your rubbish with you.** In the Han River parks, leaving it behind costs 100,000 won.
- **Weekend afternoons are busiest.** If you are meeting at exit 3, pick a spot a few steps into the park rather than right at the exit.
- **Mangwon Market is a working market.** Don't block shoppers to take photos. According to KTO, opening hours vary by stall.

## Is it for you?

**Good fit if you:**

- Like to explore on foot. Park, lanes and a market lead from Hongik University Station to the river
- Want to come straight from Incheon Airport without changing trains
- Are more curious about **local markets and Han River picnics** than palaces
- Enjoy stories of **hidden infrastructure**, from railway parks to flood control

**Less suited if you:**

- Mainly want palaces and museums: see the [Bukchon guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/) or [Jongno guide](/en/posts/jongno-guide-2026-09/)
- Want clubs and live music late into the night: **central Hongdae** suits you better

## Wrapping up

- **Boundary**: Yeonnam-dong (Yeontral Park, Donggyo-ro, old Chinese restaurants) and Mangwon-dong (Mangwon Market, Mangridan-gil, Mangwon Hangang Park), Mapo-gu. The Hongdae area is covered separately.
- **Railway**: Gyeongui Line opened 1906, undergrounding from 2005, **Yeonnam-dong section (about 1.27km) opened 27 June 2015**, full 6.3km in 2016, built for 45.7 billion won.
- **Water**: the park stream uses airport-railway groundwater to recreate **Segyocheon**, which once flowed from Yeonnam-dong to the Han near Mangwonjeong.
- **Community**: after the 1969 school move, Yeonnam-dong became home to many of Seoul's ethnic Chinese.
- **Change**: rents up 25-30% within a year (2016), cafes and restaurants allowed along the park from 2022, alcohol-clean zone fines since 2018.
- **Mangwon-dong**: retention basin sluice collapsed 2 September 1984; **Supreme Court upheld the ruling on 2 August 1990**; about 12,000 residents compensated.
- **Now**: **1,550-home redevelopment beside Mangridan-gil confirmed on 1 October 2026**. Hangang Bus from Mangwon pier: 3,000 won (at time of writing).

In one line: **Yeonnam-dong buried its railway and gained a park; Mangwon-dong remembers the day its flood defence failed, and is now heading into redevelopment.**
