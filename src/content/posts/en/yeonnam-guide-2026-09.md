Of all the Seoul neighbourhoods this blog has covered, Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong are **the ones shaped by civil engineering rather than by rules**.

The others capped building heights, held down rents or limited the hours tourists may visit. Yeonnam-dong changed when **a railway was put underground and a park laid on top of it**. Mangwon-dong went into the court records when **a floodgate on the Han River embankment gave way**.

![Yeontral Park on a clear summer day, two old rails still lying in the bed of a shallow water channel beside the lawn, people strolling along the path and high-rises near Hongik University Station in the distance](/images/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/10.webp)

The two lines in the bottom of that channel are **the old Gyeongui Line rails**. The water running over them is **groundwater rising from the airport railway tunnel beneath your feet**.

## Yeonnam-dong: where the trains went underground

### How the Gyeongui Line Forest Park came about

Put three Seoul city press releases together and the sequence runs like this.

- **1906** - the original Gyeongui Line opens
- **2005** - work begins on putting the Yongsan-Gajwa stretch underground
- The surface strip left behind is **provided free by the Korea Rail Network Authority**; **Seoul pays the 45.7 billion won** (about $34 million) to build the park
- **2011** - park construction starts; **21 May 2016** - the whole length opens

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Full route | Yongsan-gu Culture and Sports Center to **Gajwa Station**, Mapo-gu |
| Length | **6.3km**, 10-60m wide |
| Area | 102,008㎡ |
| Phase 1 | April 2012, Daeheung-dong, 760m |
| **Phase 2** | **27 June 2015**, **Yeonnam-dong**, Saechang-gogae and Yeomni-dong, 2km in total |
| Phase 3 | 21 May 2016, Wausan-gyo, Sinsu-dong and Wonhyo-ro, 1,446m |

**"Yeontral Park" is the nickname for just one of those sections, the one in Yeonnam-dong** - a play on New York's Central Park. The city's own 2016 release says the Yeonnam-dong section "opened last June, is called 'Yeontral Park' and has become a new Seoul landmark".

### What is in the Yeonnam-dong section

From the city's description:

- **Start**: a **large pine tree outside exit 3** of Hongik University Station
- **Ginkgo avenue**: roughly **1km** along the old track, billed as the section's autumn highlight
- **Stream**: a water channel running from the Yeonnam-dong police box toward the **Hongjecheon** stream

The stream has a second story.

> The stream **uses groundwater that rises from the Airport Railroad in the underground section**, and **recreates the Segyocheon**, a stream that has since disappeared from the Yeonnam-dong area.

According to the city, the Segyocheon **vanished from the surface in 1977 when the Nanjido embankment was built**. In other words, water released by burying a railway is used to draw back a stream that was buried decades earlier.

### "Gentrification" was already in the 2015 press release

This is how Seoul described Yeonnam-dong the week the section opened in 2015.

> Yeonnam-dong, where many artists pushed out by large commercial districts such as Hongdae now live, is an area where newly arrived artists and communities **are working with local residents to tackle 'gentrification'**, running flea markets and art classes and building a neighbourhood economy together.

So **from the day the park opened, Yeonnam-dong was introduced as the place people went after being priced out of Hongdae.** The same release explains how the park was to be run: a non-profit called **Gyeongui Line Forest Keepers**, launched in February 2015, aiming for citizen-led management like New York's Friends of the High Line. The first of its local units listed is the **Yeonnam-dong keepers**.

## Mangwon-dong: where the floodgate failed

### 10:30am, 2 September 1984

You cannot talk about Mangwon-dong without the **1984 Mangwon-dong flood**. The judgment is public on the Korea Law Information Center: Seoul Civil District Court case **84GaHap5010**, whose title literally includes "the so-called Mangwon-dong mass flood case".

Here is what the court found.

| Item | From the judgment |
| --- | --- |
| Background | After the 19 August 1972 flood in Mangwon-dong, Seoul built the **Mangwon retention basin around 450 Mangwon-dong in about 1973** (54,000㎡) |
| Rain | Heavy rain across central Korea for three days from the afternoon of 31 August 1984 |
| Failure | **Around 10:30am on 2 September 1984**, **the sluice box on the drainage culvert** between the Han River and the retention basin **collapsed** |
| Result | River water flowed back, overtopped the basin and left the Mangwon-dong area **more than 1m under water until the morning of 4 September** |
| Scale | Seoul gave relief to **11,942 households, 49,358 people** affected by this flood |
| Ruling | 26 August 1987: **Seoul Metropolitan Government held liable**; the claim against the builder, Hyundai Engineering & Construction, dismissed |

The key sentence in the summary of the ruling:

> The Mangwon-dong mass flood **did not occur because an untamed natural river overflowed**, but because of a defect inherent in the sluice box of the Mangwon retention basin, an artificial public facility already in place.

In plain terms, **the river did not burst its banks; the structure built to hold it back broke.** The court also noted the flood level that year was lower even than the one "just 12 years earlier".

### Mangwonjeong is not in Mangwon-dong

Mangwon-dong takes its name from **Mangwonjeong**, a riverside pavilion. Combining the city's and the Korea Tourism Organization's accounts:

- **1424** - built by Grand Prince Hyoryeong; named **Huiujeong** ("pavilion of welcome rain") after rain fell during a visit by King Sejong
- **1484** - rebuilt by Grand Prince Wolsan and renamed **Mangwonjeong** ("pavilion for gazing far")
- **1925** - lost in the great flood of that year
- **June 1988 to October 1989** - rebuilt

Yet the Korea Tourism Organization lists **the Mangwonjeong site at 23 Donggyo-ro 8an-gil, in Hapjeong-dong**. **The pavilion that gave the neighbourhood its name now has an address in the next neighbourhood.**

## What I had wrong

Four things I thought I knew turned out to be wrong.

**One: Yeontral Park is not 2km long.** The "2km" figure is **the combined length of the three sections opened together in June 2015**. The Yeonnam-dong section alone is about 1.27km, and the whole park is 6.3km. **Two city press releases give the Yeonnam-dong length as 1,268m and 1,266m**, so this guide says about 1.27km.

**Two: Yeonnam-dong did not start out in Mapo-gu.** According to the Encyclopedia of Korean Culture, it was created **in 1975 when part of Yeonhui-dong in Seodaemun-gu was moved into Mapo-gu**, and the name simply means "south of Yeonhui". Yeonhui-dong is still across the park to the northeast.

**Three: the Mangwon flood was not just "too much rain".** As above, the court found **a defect in the sluice box** and made Seoul pay.

**Four: Mangwon-dong is two districts for administrative purposes.** Mapo-gu's table shows **one legal dong, Mangwon-dong, split between two administrative dong, Mangwon 1-dong and Mangwon 2-dong.** Yeonnam-dong is one name for both.

## Where they begin and end

| Name | What it is | For visitors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Yeonnam-dong** | Legal and administrative dong; split from Yeonhui-dong in 1975 | The Yeonnam-dong park section and the lanes either side |
| **Yeontral Park** | Yeonnam-dong section of the park (about 1.27km) | Starts at Hongik University Station exit 3 |
| **Mangwon-dong** | One legal dong, two administrative dong | Mangwon Market, Mangwon Hangang Park |
| **Mangwonjeong** | Seoul monument, address in **Hapjeong-dong** | On the river, next to Mangwon Hangang Park |
| **Hongdae** | Not an administrative name | See the [Hongdae guide](/en/posts/hongdae-guide-2026-09/) |

**The two neighbourhoods are walkable from each other, but they are not one place.** By subway, Yeonnam-dong is **Hongik University Station** on [[line:2]], [[line:gyeongui]] and [[line:arex]], and Mangwon-dong is **Mangwon Station** on [[line:6]]. From Hongik University take [[line:2]] one stop to Hapjeong, then [[line:6]] one stop to Mangwon.

## Getting there

![The site of an old level crossing on the Wausan-gyo section of the Gyeongui Line Forest Park, with a red-and-white barrier, a yellow X-shaped signal and statues of a uniformed man, a mother and a child, an old restaurant alley running off to the left](/images/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/11.webp)

This photo is not Yeonnam-dong but **the old level crossing under Wausan Bridge, southeast of Hongik University Station**. Because the bells went "ttaeng-ttaeng-ttaeng" as the barrier came down for each train, Wausan-ro 32-gil is known as **Ttaeng-ttaeng Street**; the city describes it as a lane of a little over 200m starting opposite the Sanwoollim Theatre. The statues are the station worker and mother-with-baby figures the city installed on this section to recall the railway days of Changjeon-dong.

| Destination | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Yeontral Park** | [[line:2]], [[line:gyeongui]], [[line:arex]] **Hongik University Station exit 3** - the park starts right outside |
| **Mangwon Market** | Walk from [[line:6]] **Mangwon Station** |
| **Mangwon Hangang Park** | [[line:6]] **Mangwon Station exit 1** → village bus [[bus:village:09]] Mapo 09 or [[bus:village:16]] Mapo 16 → get off at the **Mangwon underpass (nadeulmok)** |
| **Mangwon Market ↔ Mangwon underpass** | About 15 minutes on foot |

Village buses are small green neighbourhood buses; they take the same transit cards as city buses. A *nadeulmok* is one of the tunnels through the riverside expressway embankment that lead into the Han River parks.

**From Incheon Airport, AREX all-stop trains ([[line:arex]]) run straight to Hongik University Station.** The express train runs non-stop to Seoul Station, so take the all-stop service. Fares and times are in the [Incheon Airport to Seoul guide](/en/posts/incheon-airport-to-seoul-2026-09/).

If you want to try the **Hangang Bus** river service, there is a **Mangwon pier** in the park; the city listed the adult fare as 3,000 won (about $2.25) in August 2026.

## How to do it

To see both neighbourhoods in one go, **start in Yeonnam-dong and finish at the river**.

1. **Morning** - Hongik University Station exit 3, then north along Yeontral Park. The stream and the ginkgo avenue are the heart of this section.
2. **Lunch** - a restaurant or café in the Yeonnam-dong lanes. How to choose is in the [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong food and cafés](/en/posts/yeonnam-food-2026-09/) guide.
3. **Afternoon** - head to Mangwon-dong for **Mangwon Market**. A city article recommends stopping here for picnic food before going down to the river.
4. **Sunset** - **Mangwon Hangang Park**, on the north bank between Wonhyo and Seongsan bridges, 7.4km long and 422,347㎡ in area.

![Mangwon Hangang Park seen from a hillside, terraced paths among pine trees leading down to the Han River and the red arches of Seongsan Bridge](/images/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/15.webp)

The park also holds **Seoul Battleship Park**, built from three decommissioned naval vessels just downstream of the north end of Seongsan Bridge. The sights of both neighbourhoods are collected in [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong things to do](/en/posts/yeonnam-things-to-do-2026-09/).

### Rules in the Han River parks

Two from the official park rules that visitors most often fall foul of:

- **Take your rubbish home.** Leaving it risks a **100,000 won fine**
- **Park only in designated spaces.** Otherwise a 50,000 won fine; public transport is recommended at weekends and on holidays

![The green entrance of Mangwon Market with its name in Korean letters above, a coffee shop and a rice cake shop on either side and people walking into the covered arcade](/images/yeonnam-guide-2026-09/08.webp)

**Mangwon Market** is at 27 Poeun-ro 6-gil, Mapo-gu. The Korea Tourism Organization notes that **hours differ from stall to stall**, so if you are going late, check the shop you want first.

## Where to stay

Yeonnam-dong is **a residential area one train ride from the airport**. The [Hongdae hotels](/en/posts/hongdae-hotels-2026-09/) guide already suggests Yeonnam-dong over the main streets for light sleepers. Guesthouses, small hotels and options on the Mangwon side are compared in [Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong hotels](/en/posts/yeonnam-hotels-2026-09/).

## Who it suits

**Good fit:**

- You like to travel on foot. Park and lanes link Hongik University Station all the way to the river
- You want to get in from Incheon Airport without changing trains
- A **local market and a Han River picnic** appeal more than palaces
- You are curious about **the infrastructure you cannot see**: disused railways turned into parks, how a city manages flood water

**Not such a good fit:**

- Palaces and museums are your main goal. Jongno and Bukchon are closer; see the [Bukchon guide](/en/posts/bukchon-guide-2026-09/)
- You want clubs and live music until late. The **main Hongdae streets** suit that better than Yeonnam-dong

## Summary

- **Yeontral Park is the Yeonnam-dong section (about 1.27km) of the 6.3km Gyeongui Line Forest Park**, opened on **27 June 2015**.
- The land freed by **putting the Gyeongui Line underground from 2005** was provided free, and Seoul spent **45.7 billion won** on the park.
- The park's stream is **groundwater from the airport railway tunnel**, recreating the lost **Segyocheon**.
- **Yeonnam-dong was split off from Yeonhui-dong in Seodaemun-gu in 1975.**
- **On 2 September 1984 the Mangwon retention basin's sluice box collapsed**, flooding Mangwon-dong over 1m deep; **in 1987 the court held Seoul liable**.
- **Mangwonjeong, which gave Mangwon-dong its name, now has a Hapjeong-dong address.**
- **Hongik University Station exit 3 for Yeontral Park; Mangwon Station exit 1 for the village bus to the river.**

In one line: **Yeonnam-dong got a park by burying a railway, and Mangwon-dong remembers the day the structure holding back the river gave way.**
