One tree, standing by itself in the rice fields outside Wonju (원주), draws cars from across Korea every autumn. The **Bangyeri Ginkgo** (반계리 은행나무) was thought to be about 800 years old when it became a Natural Monument in 1964. A 2024 analysis by the National Institute of Forest Science put its age at **1,317 years**, and Korean media now call it the country's oldest ginkgo. This autumn is the second since a new plaza was built around it, and the one-day festival grows to two weeks. This guide pulls together the city's notices and October 2026 press reports on peak timing, how to get there, parking and viewing etiquette. Dates and bus details are as of 13 October 2026.

## The tree

Reports give the tree a height of 32 m, a trunk girth of 16.27 m and a base girth of 14.5 m. Korea Tourism Organization data lists the crown at 37.5 m across east to west and 31 m north to south. It does not rise as a single trunk. Seven trunks split from the base, so from a distance it reads as a small grove rather than one tree. Long black steel props hold up the heaviest branches, and they are part of the tree's look now.

The age is worth a word. In January 1964 the estimate was 800 to 1,000 years. In 2024 the city of Wonju asked the National Institute of Forest Science to measure it. Coring a protected tree is not allowed, so the institute used LiDAR scans of the trunk and a growth database instead. The answer was 1,317 years, which would place its sprouting in the Unified Silla period.

Several stories are attached to it. One says an ancestor of the Seongju Yi clan planted it. Another says a passing monk drank from a spring here, left his staff in the ground, and the staff grew. Villagers believed a white snake lived inside the trunk and kept the tree from harm, so nobody touched it. A local saying holds that if all the leaves turn yellow at once, the next year's harvest will be good.

## When it peaks in 2026

The **2026 foliage map** released on 8 October by the Korea Tourism Organization and the Korea National Arboretum expects the Bangyeri ginkgo to turn fully yellow in **late October or around 1 November**. Surrounding foliage is forecast to peak around 2 November. For the Gangwon region as a whole, the ginkgo peak is set at 27 October, measured at the provincial arboretum in Chuncheon.

Visitor traffic builds from mid October through early November every year. Last year News1 reported crowds and a film crew at the tree on 23 October, before the leaves had fully turned. Forecasts shift by a few days with the weather, so check the Wonju tourism site or recent photos before you set out. According to Korea Tourism Data Lab figures, the tree was searched in car navigation apps about 45,000 times in 2024, and 90 percent of those searches fell in October and November.

## The new plaza and the 2026 festival

Construction around the tree ran from 2021 and finished in **September 2025** as the Ginkgo Plaza. Wonju Today reports a site of about 16,200 m² with a lawn, 170 parking spaces, a 162 m pedestrian path, a tensile-roof outdoor stage and a nursery plot for the tree's successors. Young ginkgos were planted along the roads.

Until last year the festival was a single day. On Sunday 2 November 2025 it ran 10:30 to 16:00 with folk drumming and nanta percussion, a brass ensemble from the Wonju Philharmonic, an autumn concert, a photo exhibition and craft booths for wish cards and calligraphy. For 2026 a committee of 16 Munmak residents has taken over, and the city says the festival stretches to **two weeks** with a resident-run food market and local produce stalls. A vendor recruitment notice gave the festival period as **Monday 26 October to Sunday 8 November 2026**, which matches the end date of the extra bus service below. Whether stage events run every day has not been announced, so check the Wonju tourism site for the programme.

## Practical notes

**Location and access**: 42 Banjeo-ri 2-gil, Munmak-eup, Wonju. The plaza's lot address is 1495-1 Bangye-ri. It is open at all times, and both entry and parking are free. A low protective fence circles the tree, so you walk around it on the plaza rather than up to the trunk.

**Public transport**: No bus runs straight from central Wonju. From Wonju Intercity Bus Terminal or Wonju Station, take a city bus bound for Munmak to the **Munmak-eup Community Service Center** (문막읍행정복지센터), then change to **community bus No. 1**, which runs from the centre past the Bangyeri ginkgo to Daedun-ri. The Wonju Facilities Management Corporation is adding **8 extra round trips on weekends and public holidays from 17 October to 8 November**, with the usual 12 weekday runs unchanged. Wonju's city bus numbers change often, so check Naver Map or Kakao Map on the day. Wonju itself is roughly an hour from Seoul's Cheongnyangni Station by KTX.

**Driving and parking**: Search "반계리 은행나무" in your navigation app and it routes to the plaza car park. The plaza has 170 spaces, and in March the city reported a plan to lease nearby private land for temporary festival parking, bringing the total to around 500. Even so, the access road backs up on peak weekends. One local report counted 38 minutes to cover 1.4 km. Arriving before 9 am or coming on a weekday is far easier.

**Viewing etiquette**: Do not climb the tree, step inside the fence or walk on the soil over the roots. A full loop outside the fence gives you both the whole-tree view and the spots where you stand under the branches looking up. The plaza sits in open farmland with almost no shade, so bring a layer for early November mornings.

**Nearby**: **Sogeumsan Grand Valley** is a 15-minute drive. A cable car takes you up to a suspension bridge about 100 m above the valley floor and a second, longer swaying bridge. The ginkgo and Sogeumsan make an easy half-day pairing.

## Wrapping up

The Bangyeri ginkgo is a one-sight destination, but the one sight is enormous. The 2026 peak is forecast for the turn of October into November, the festival is longer than before, and weekend buses have been added, so getting there is easier than in past years. The road, though, is as narrow as ever, so an early start or the Munmak bus transfer remains the safest plan on a weekend.
