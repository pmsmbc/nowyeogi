Search for "Seoul subway map PDF" and you will mostly find re-uploads of unclear age. The original is easier to get than you might think: Seoul Metro, the city's main subway operator, publishes the network map in English, Japanese, Simplified Chinese and Korean as both PDF and JPG, under a licence that allows free reuse with credit. The English and Japanese files are below, straight from the official source.

The Chinese and Korean editions, and any newer edition, are on the [Seoul Open Data Plaza dataset page](https://data.seoul.go.kr/dataList/OA-22535/F/1/datasetView.do). According to the dataset description, the maps are refreshed with the latest information once a year.

## What the English map looks like

![The full English Seoul Metro Map](/images/seoul-subway-map-2026-11/01.webp)

The map is titled "Seoul Metro Map" and fits Lines 1 to 9 together with the wider metropolitan network, including the Gyeongui-Jungang Line, AREX (the Airport Railroad), the Sinbundang Line and the Suin-Bundang Line, on a single sheet. It uses Seoul's redesigned map style, which the city's design office describes as the first overhaul in 40 years. One noticeable feature is that transfer stations are drawn as stacked circles, like a traffic light.

![Close-up of the English map around Seoul Station](/images/seoul-subway-map-2026-11/03.webp)

## The Japanese edition

![The full Japanese edition of the Seoul Metro Map](/images/seoul-subway-map-2026-11/02.webp)

The Japanese map has an identical layout, with station names in katakana. That makes it handy for mixed groups: everyone can point to the same spot on their own language's version. One quirk worth knowing is that in the September 2025 Japanese edition, Line 6 station 632 (Beotigogae) is labelled ハンガンジン, the same as neighbouring 631 Hangangjin. The English edition gets it right.

## PDF or JPG?

- **For your phone, take the JPG.** At 5,102 pixels square, station names stay sharp when you zoom in your photo app, and it works offline, which helps on the stretches underground where mobile data drops out.
- **For printing, take the PDF.** The page is set up at roughly 90 cm square, so choose "fit to page" when printing. A4 works, but the text gets small; A3 is easier to read.
- **Sending it on:** Messaging apps such as KakaoTalk, LINE and WhatsApp compress photos, which can blur the station names. Send the original-quality file or share the download link instead.

## Reading station numbers

The number inside each circle is the station number, and it is the most reliable way to navigate if Korean names are unfamiliar. On Lines 1 to 9 the first digit is the line: Seoul Station is 133 on [[line:1]] and 426 on [[line:4]]. Other lines use a letter prefix, so Seoul Station on [[line:arex]] is A01 and Sinsa on the [[line:sinbundang]] is D04. The same numbers appear on station signs and on the screens inside trains. Where several circles are stacked together, you can change lines.

## Practical notes

- **Licence:** The Seoul Open Data Plaza lists the files under Korea Open Government License (KOGL) Type 1, which allows reuse, including commercial use and modification, as long as you credit the source. The copyright holder is the Seoul Metropolitan Government and the provider is Seoul Metro.
- **Check the edition date:** As of writing, the latest files are the 29 September 2025 edition. Sections that opened after that date may be missing, so if a station you need is not on the map, check the dataset page for a newer edition.
- **Route planning:** For fastest or fewest-transfer routes between two stations, Seoul Metro's [Cyber Station](http://www.seoulmetro.co.kr/kr/cyberStation.do) route finder is useful.
- **Help:** The Seoul Metro call centre number printed at the top left of the map is 1577-1234.

## Wrapping up

For a trip to Seoul, a JPG saved to your phone and an A3 print of the PDF for your room cover most needs. If you are travelling with Japanese-speaking friends, give them the Japanese edition and mention the Beotigogae label.
