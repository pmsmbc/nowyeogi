A good many people come to Ikseon-dong **because they want to sleep in a hanok**.

But what a hanok stay actually is is not well known. **What the law requires, and what it does not**, is different from what most people assume. That comes first.

![Inside a hanok room, the wooden floor visible between paper-screen doors and timber posts](/images/ikseon-things-to-do-2026-09/05.webp)

## Hanok stays are concentrated in Jongno-gu

By the Seoul Tourism Organization's Seoul Stay listing there are **133 hanok stays** in Seoul, and the distribution is extreme.

| District | Hanok stays |
| --- | --- |
| **Jongno-gu** | **100** |
| Eunpyeong-gu | 18 |
| Seongbuk-gu | 7 |
| Dongdaemun-gu | 5 |
| Jung-gu | **0** |
| Seongdong-gu | **0** |

**Jung-gu, which contains Euljiro, has none, and neither does Seongdong-gu, which contains Seongsu-dong.** To sleep in a hanok you effectively have to come to Jongno-gu.

That said, **there are fewer right by Ikseon-dong than you might expect.** Within 500m of Jongno 3-ga Station there are 6, while **within 500m of Anguk Station there are 21**. The Bukchon side has far more.

## What the hanok-stay business standard actually requires

This is probably the most useful part of the post.

A hanok stay is registered as **a hanok-stay business under the Tourism Promotion Act**. It is not a hotel business but a tourist-use facility business. Reading the registration standard clears up a lot of misunderstandings.

### What the law does not require

**A private bathroom is not compulsory.** This is all the provision says.

> Where overnight stays are provided, **convenience facilities such as a bath or shower shall be provided** so that users are not inconvenienced

There is no wording requiring one per room. **Shared toilets are legal**, and there are in fact hanok stays near Jongno 3-ga Station listed as having shared toilets.

**Nor is there any rule that you have to sleep on the floor.** There is no provision at all about beds, ondol or bedding mats. Some hanok stays state **"four queen-size beds"** outright.

"Sleeping in a hanok means sleeping on a mat on the floor" is **industry practice, not a legal requirement.** If you want a bed, you can find one.

### What the law does require

- **At least one fire extinguisher**, and **a standalone smoke alarm** in every room
- **A carbon monoxide alarm** as well, if heating is individual
- Total floor area of the sleeping space **under 230 square metres**
- **Disinfection at least once a month**, and regular laundering of bedding
- **Ventilation** (exempt if natural ventilation through windows is possible)
- **A manager on duty during business hours**
- **Posting the tariff and keeping to it**

**Hanok in winter often have individual heating, so in practice a carbon monoxide alarm should be there.** Take a look when you check in.

**There is no requirement for a 24-hour front desk.** But a manager must be present during business hours.

**If the rate on your booking screen differs from the rate on site**, that is a breach of the registration standard.

## What the accommodation around Ikseon-dong actually is

Setting hanok aside, here is what ordinary accommodation looks like, on the Ministry of the Interior and Safety licensing data.

**Jongno-gu as a whole has 368 businesses with 10,258 rooms**, third in Seoul, after Jung-gu (25.0%) and Gangnam-gu (10.3%).

But **the seven neighbourhoods within walking distance of Ikseon-dong** - Ikseon, Nakwon, Donui, Myo, Gwonnong, Waryong and Bongik - look different. There are **74 businesses with 2,664 rooms**, made up like this.

| Business type | Rooms | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Yeogwan** | **886** | **33.3%** |
| Registered tourist hotel | 760 | 28.5% |
| General hotel | 448 | 16.8% |
| Lodging business (residence) | 419 | 15.7% |

**Old-style yeogwan inns come first.** Across Jongno-gu as a whole registered tourist hotels come first, but not in this area. The data confirms that Jongno 3-ga is a district of old inns.

Businesses licensed in 1968, 1970 and 1977 are still recorded as trading.

### What is opening now is mostly residence-type lodging

**Of the 15 new businesses since January 2025, 12 are "residence"-type serviced lodging**, averaging 9.1 rooms. Small residence-type lodgings are increasing fast.

**Having "hotel" in the name has nothing to do with tourist hotel grading.** The Seongsu-dong and Euljiro posts made the same point, and it holds here too.

### Do not mix the two sets of figures

An important caveat. **Not one hanok stay is included in the 368 businesses and 10,258 rooms above.**

The hanok-stay business is **a registration under the Tourism Promotion Act**, while the data above covers only businesses **notified under the Public Health Control Act**. The governing laws are different. Cross-checking the 133 Seoul Stay hanok against the list above produced **zero matching names**.

## You will pass through Donui-dong

![An Ikseon-dong alley of low continuous hanok rooflines, with a wooden deck laid in front of a mint-green shop](/images/ikseon-guide-2026-09/06.webp)

Right next to Ikseon-dong, **in Donui-dong, there is a jjokbang district** - an area of jjokbang, single tiny rooms. It is written here because knowing in advance is better.

These are Jongno-gu's published figures. **As of 31 July 2026, 490 of the 671 jjokbang residents in Jongno-gu live in Donui-dong.** 90% are men.

The municipal Donui-dong jjokbang counselling centre provides **a laundry room, showers, toilets and a heatwave and cold-wave shelter**, built separately. That the centre has to build them separately means **the rooms themselves do not have them**.

In summer Jongno-gu runs **heatwave safety lodging** in partnership with hotels, up to 10 days per person.

**This is not a place to go and look at.** It is where 490 people live. Walking from exit 4 of Jongno 3-ga Station into Ikseon-dong takes you past it, and it is better to know in advance and pass quietly than to come upon it unawares and raise a camera.

The request Jongno-gu posts on its Ikseon-dong page applies here just as it does there.

> Do not startle or disturb residents by shouting or taking photographs carelessly

For the record, **jjokbang are not registered lodging businesses.** They are not in the figures above, and they are not something a traveller can book.

## There is an airport bus

Many people think Jongno means taking the AREX and changing. It does not.

The airport limousine [[bus:6002]] stops at Jongno 3-ga.

- **Boarding** - 30m from exit 11, in front of the jewellery wholesale arcade
- **Alighting** - in front of exit 15, by the Lotteria
- **First bus 04:13, last bus 20:45**
- Adult 17,000 won (about $12.75)

**The last bus is at 20:45**, nearly an hour later than Euljiro's 19:50.

For the Bukchon and Anguk side, where the hanok stays are concentrated, the route is [[bus:6011]]. It stops at the Changdeokgung (Biwon) stop and in front of Hana Bank at exit 1 of Anguk Station, with a first bus at 04:25 and a last bus at 20:42.

By rail, take [[line:arex]] to Seoul Station and change to [[line:1]].

## Where to book

- **You want to sleep in a hanok** - the Anguk Station side has more than three times the choice Ikseon-dong does
- **You want to sleep cheaply** - a third of the rooms in this area are in yeogwan
- **You want a registered tourist hotel** - there are some in Ikseon-dong itself, and 3,840 rooms across Jongno-gu
- **You are in and out of the airport** - the 6002's last bus at 20:45 leaves reasonable room

## What could not be verified

**There is no official source for the average room rate in Jongno-gu.** That is why no figures appear here.

**Complete data on hanok-stay businesses could not be obtained.** The Seoul Stay listing stands in for it, and since that is what the city promotes, it may not be every registered business in the country.

**Some exit numbers for Nakwon Arcade, Jongmyo Shrine and Gwangjang Market** had no official basis that could be found. Only the confirmed ones are written above.

## Wrapping up

- **100 of Seoul's 133 hanok stays are in Jongno-gu.** Jung-gu and Seongdong-gu have none.
- **Neither a private bathroom nor sleeping on the floor is a legal requirement.** Some hanok have beds.
- **A fire extinguisher and standalone smoke alarms are compulsory**, plus a CO alarm where heating is individual.
- **33% of the rooms within walking distance of Ikseon-dong are yeogwan.**
- **What is opening now is mostly residence-type lodging.** Check, even when the name says hotel.
- **Hanok stays do not appear in the general lodging statistics.** The governing law is different.
- **The airport bus is route 6002**, stopping at Jongno 3-ga, with a last bus at 20:45.
