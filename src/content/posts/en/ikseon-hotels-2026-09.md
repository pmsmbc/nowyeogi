Search for a room near Ikseon-dong's hanok lanes and the first results are usually hotels with "Insadong" in the name. Follow their licensed addresses, though, and many of them turn out to be in **Nagwon-dong and Ikseon-dong**, on the Jongno 3-ga side of Insadong. This post counts every licensed place to stay in Ikseon-dong and the six small neighbourhoods around it, using the City of Seoul's official licensing data.

- **27 tourist hotels and hostels with 1,148 rooms** — four tourist hotels, one family hotel, one small hotel and 21 hostels
- **74 general lodging businesses with 2,664 rooms** — the biggest group is **33 yeogwan (traditional budget inns) with 886 rooms**
- **14 hanok stays** — 10 of them in **Gwonnong-dong**, between Changdeokgung Palace and Jongmyo Shrine
- just **5** foreign-tourist homestays

In short: **the big hotels are in Nagwon-dong, the hanok in Gwonnong-dong, and the small hostels along one street on the edge of the Ikseon-dong lanes.**

## How these were counted

Seoul Open Data Plaza publishes four licensing datasets: tourist accommodation (관광숙박업), general lodging (숙박업), foreign-tourist urban homestays (외국인관광 도시민박업) and hanok experience businesses (한옥체험업). From each, only entries marked "operating" whose legal-dong address is one of the **seven neighbourhoods below** were kept. Tourist accommodation and lodging are the 24 September 2026 update, homestays 27 September and hanok 23 September — the same data and method as the [Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-hotels-2026-09/), [Dongdaemun](/en/posts/dongdaemun-hotels-2026-10/) and [Euljiro](/en/posts/euljiro-hotels-2026-09/) posts. A "dong" is a legal neighbourhood unit, much smaller than a district.

The two main datasets come from different laws. Tourist accommodation is registered under the **Tourism Promotion Act**; general lodging is reported under the **Public Health Control Act**. Many tourist hotels and hostels appear in both, so **26 of the 74 lodging entries share an address with a tourist-accommodation entry**, and the remaining **48 places with 1,518 rooms appear only in the lodging data**. Hanok stays are a third, separate licence (a "tourist-use facility" under the Tourism Promotion Act) and are not mixed into either table.

## Donui-dong and Myo-dong: small hotels, and a jjokbang district

Donui-dong and Myo-dong are two small neighbourhoods between Ikseon-dong and Jongno 3-ga Station.

- **The May Hotel** (Donui-dong, Supyo-ro 26-gil 10) was registered in May 2025 as a **small hotel** with **25 rooms** on floors 7–10. The lodging data lists the same address as Hotel The May, a general hotel licensed in 1986 with 42 rooms, so the two datasets disagree
- Donui-dong's hostels are Ikseon Atelier (Donhwamun-ro 9ga-gil 19, 10 rooms, 2025) and Sodamon Jongno (Donhwamun-ro 9-gil 6, 2 rooms, 2026)
- In Myo-dong, one building at Donhwamun-ro 56-1 holds two hostels registered on the same day in March 2026: Stay Ann Ikseon (floors 2–3, 9 rooms) and Kimstel Korea Jongno (floors 4–5, 13 rooms)
- Lodging-only entries: eight in Donui-dong (M&Lucky Hotel 45 rooms, T 32, Lemon Tree Hotel 26 and others) and one in Myo-dong (Onu Stay Ikseon, a yeogwan from 1977 with 18 rooms)

**Donui-dong also has a jjokbang district** — very small single rooms let cheaply as housing. According to Jongno-gu's published figures, **490 of the district's 671 jjokbang residents lived in Donui-dong as of 31 July 2026** (about 90% of them men), and the city-run Donui-dong Jjokbang Counselling Centre (Donhwamun-ro 9ga-gil 20-2) provides **laundry, showers, toilets and a heat-and-cold shelter** for them. Jjokbang are not licensed lodging, are not in any of the counts above, and cannot be booked by travellers.

If you stay in Donui-dong you may walk through these lanes. People live here, so pass quietly. Jongno-gu's own alley-walking guidance asks visitors **not to startle or disturb residents with loud voices or indiscriminate photography**.

## Hanok stays: what the rules require and what they don't

The [Jongno](/en/posts/jongno-hotels-2026-09/) and [Bukchon](/en/posts/bukchon-hotels-2026-09/) posts point to this section. The source is **Annex 1, item 4(g) ("sa") of the Enforcement Decree of the Tourism Promotion Act**, the registration standards for tourism businesses, in the version in force from 4 August 2026.

Article 2 of the decree defines a hanok experience business as one that **equips a hanok with facilities suitable for tourists to experience staying there**, or with facilities for traditional games and crafts. It is not a hotel licence; it is classed as a **tourist-use facility business**.

### What the standard requires

- The building must be a **hanok** meeting the standard set by the Minister of Land (designated heritage hanok and registered "excellent architectural assets" are exempt)
- The space used for stays must be **under 230 m² of total floor area** (exceptions for heritage hanok, registered architectural assets, and hanok-village houses or old family homes designated by local ordinance)
- If stays are offered, there must be **convenience facilities such as a bathroom or shower** so guests are not inconvenienced
- **At least one fire extinguisher** in or near the rooms; if stays are offered, a **stand-alone smoke alarm in every room**, plus a **carbon monoxide alarm** where heating is individual
- Any cooking facilities installed to gas, LPG and fire-safety rules
- A supply of tap water or water meeting drinking-water standards
- The capacity to **disinfect at least once a month**, clean rooms and bathrooms as needed and **wash bedding regularly**
- **Ventilation** (not needed where windows allow natural airflow)
- Bath water meeting the Public Health Control Act's bathing-water quality standard
- **A manager on site during business hours**
- If stays are offered, **a rate card posted at the reception and on any online interface** used for business, **and those rates must be honoured**

The last item relates to the amendment of 4 August 2026: its transitional provision gave existing hanok businesses **one month from that date** to meet it.

### What the standard does not require

- **A private bathroom for each room** — the text only says "a bathroom or shower, etc." Ask whether the bathroom is en suite or shared before you book
- **Anything about beds, ondol or floor mattresses** — there is no rule that you must sleep on the floor, and none requiring a bed
- **A 24-hour front desk** — a manager is required only "during business hours". If you'll arrive late, tell the host in advance
- **Foreigners-only status** — the definition says "tourists", so unlike foreign-tourist urban homestays, hanok stays are open to Korean guests too

## Summary

- Across Ikseon, Nagwon, Donui, Myo, Gwonnong, Waryong and Bongik-dong: **27 tourist hotels and hostels (1,148 rooms)**, **74 lodging businesses (2,664 rooms)**, **14 hanok stays** and 5 homestays.
- Jongno-gu's largest tourist hotel by registered rooms, **ibis Ambassador Insadong (363)**, is in Ikseon-dong; Orakai Insadong Suites, Moxy, N285 and Best Western are in **Nagwon-dong**.
- **15 of the 21 hostels were registered in 2025 or later**; all six in Ikseon-dong are on Samil-daero 32ga-gil.
- Of the 48 lodging-only places, **33 are yeogwan**, concentrated on Supyo-ro 22-gil and 26-gil in Nagwon-dong.
- **10 of the 14 hanok stays are on the Yulgok-ro 10-gil side of Gwonnong-dong**; inside Ikseon-dong there is just one, registered in September 2026.
- Korea's hanok-stay standard has **no rule on private bathrooms, beds or a 24-hour desk**; rates must be posted at reception and online.
- Airport buses: **6002 from Jongno 3-ga, last 20:45**; **6011 from Changdeokgung 20:42 and Anguk 20:45**. Overnight buses leave from Dongdaemun and Gwanghwamun.
